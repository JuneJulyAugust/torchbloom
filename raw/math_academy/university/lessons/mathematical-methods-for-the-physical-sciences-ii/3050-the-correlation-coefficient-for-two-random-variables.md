### Introduction

Let $X$ and $Y$ be two random variables with covariance $\textrm{Cov}[X, Y]$ and standard deviations $\textrm {SD}[X]$ and $\textrm {SD}[Y].$

The **correlation coefficient of $X$ and $Y,$** also known as the **Pearson correlation coefficient**, is denoted $\rho$ (the Greek letter "rho") and is defined as

$$


\rho = \dfrac{\textrm{Cov}[X, Y] }{\textrm {SD}[X]\cdot \textrm {SD}[Y]}.


$$

Note the following:

- The correlation coefficient satisfies

- If $X$ and $Y$ are independent random variables, then The reverse implication does not always hold. In other words, $\rho=0$ does *not* imply that $X$ and $Y$ are independent.

- Unlike the covariance, $\rho$ is a *dimensionless* quantity. This means we'll get the same answer regardless of the units used to measure $X$ and $Y.$

- Also unlike the covariance, $\rho$ tells the strength of the correlation between $X$ and $Y.$ We typically have the following rule of thumb: If $|\rho| \gt 0.7,$ the correlation is **strong,** and the data generated by our two random variables closely follow a straight line. If $0.3\leq |\rho| \leq 0.7,$ the correlation is **weak.** The data generated by our random variables follow a straight line, but many are far from the line. If $|\rho| \lt 0.3,$ then the correlation is **negligible.** No (linear) correlation exists between the data points generated by the random variables.

Let's get some practice computing the correlation coefficient.

### Example: Finding a Correlation Coefficient Given Variances and Covariance

#### Question

The variances of the random variables $X$ and $Y$ are $25$ and $36,$ respectively. Given that $\textrm{Cov}[X,Y] = 18,$ find the correlation coefficient of $X$ and $Y.$

#### Explanation

Recall that the correlation coefficient of the random variables $X$ and $Y$ is defined as

$$


\rho = \dfrac{\textrm{Cov}[X, Y] }{\textrm {SD}[X] \cdot \textrm {SD}[Y]},


$$

where

- $\textrm{Cov}[X, Y]$ is the covariance, and

- $\textrm {SD}[X]$ and $\textrm {SD}[Y]$ are the standard deviations of $X$ and $Y.$

In our case, we have

$$


\begin{aligned}SD[𝑋] & =\sqrt{√Var[𝑋]}=\sqrt{√25}=5, \\ SD[𝑋] & =\sqrt{√Var[𝑌]}=\sqrt{√36}=6.\end{aligned}


$$

Substituting our data in the formula, we obtain

$$


\begin{aligned}𝜌 & =\frac{Cov[𝑋,𝑌]}{SD[𝑋]⋅SD[𝑋]} \\ & =\frac{18}{5⋅6} \\ & =\frac{3}{5}.\end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Finding the Correlation Coefficient of Two Discrete Random Variables

#### Question

The joint probability mass function $f(x,y)$ for the discrete random variables $X$ and $Y$ is shown above. Given that

$$


\textrm {E}[X]=0.4\qquad \textrm {E}[Y]=0.6, \qquad \textrm {SD}[X] \approx 0.4899, \qquad \textrm {SD}[Y] \approx 0.4899,


$$

find the correlation coefficient of $X$ and $Y.$ Round your answer to $2$ decimal places.

#### Explanation

Recall that the correlation coefficient of random variables $X$ and $Y$ is defined as

$$


\rho = \dfrac{\textrm{Cov}[X, Y] }{\textrm {SD}[X] \cdot \textrm {SD}[Y]},


$$

where

- $\textrm{Cov}[X, Y]$ is the covariance, and

- $\textrm {SD}[X]$ and $\textrm {SD}[Y]$ are the standard deviations of $X$ and $Y.$

First, by applying the rule of the lazy statistician, we have

$$


\begin{aligned}E[𝑋𝑌] & =\underset{𝑥∈𝑆_{𝑋}}{∑}\underset{𝑦∈𝑆_{𝑌}}{∑}𝑥𝑦\,𝑓(𝑥,𝑦) \\ & =0⋅0⋅0.1+0⋅1⋅0.5 \\ & =\,+1⋅0⋅0.3+1⋅1⋅0.1 \\ & =0.1.\end{aligned}


$$

Then, we compute the covariance and get

$$


\begin{aligned}Cov[𝑋,𝑌] & =E[𝑋𝑌]−E[𝑋]⋅E[𝑌] \\ & =0.1−(0.4)(0.6) \\ & =−0.14.\end{aligned}


$$

Therefore, the correlation coefficient is

$$


\begin{aligned}𝜌 & =\frac{Cov[𝑋,𝑌]}{SD[𝑋]⋅SD[𝑌]} \\ & ≈\frac{−0.14}{0.4899⋅0.4899} \\ & ≈−0.58,\end{aligned}


$$

rounded to $2$ decimal places.

### Example: Finding the Correlation Coefficient of Two Continuous Random Variables

#### Question

$$


\begin{aligned}2(𝑥+𝑦), & \,0≤𝑦≤𝑥≤1 \\ 0, & \,otherwise\end{aligned}


$$

The joint probability density function $f(x,y)$ for the continuous random variables $X$ and $Y$ is shown above. Given that

$$


\textrm {E}[X]= \dfrac{3}{4},\qquad \textrm {E}[Y]= \dfrac{5}{12}, \qquad \textrm {SD}[X]\approx 0.1936, \qquad \textrm {SD}[Y]\approx 0.2444,


$$

find the correlation coefficient of $X$ and $Y.$ Round your answer to $2$ decimal places.

#### Explanation

Recall that the correlation coefficient of the random variables $X$ and $Y$ is defined as

$$


\rho = \dfrac{\textrm{Cov}[X, Y] }{\textrm {SD}[X] \cdot \textrm {SD}[Y]},


$$

where

- $\textrm{Cov}[X, Y]$ is the covariance, and

- $\textrm {SD}[X]$ and $\textrm {SD}[Y]$ are the standard deviations of $X$ and $Y.$

First, by applying the rule of the lazy statistician, we have

$$


\begin{aligned}E[𝑋𝑌] & =∬_{ℝ^{2}}𝑥𝑦\,𝑓(𝑥,𝑦)\,d𝑥d𝑦 \\ & =∫_{10}^{}[∫_{𝑥0}^{}𝑥𝑦⋅2(𝑥+𝑦)\,d𝑦]d𝑥 \\ & =∫_{10}^{}[∫_{𝑥0}^{}(2𝑥^{2}𝑦+2𝑥𝑦^{2})\,d𝑦]d𝑥 \\ & =∫_{10}^{}[𝑥^{2}𝑦^{2}+\frac{2}{3}𝑥𝑦^{3}]_{𝑦=𝑥𝑦=0}^{}d𝑥 \\ & =∫_{10}^{}(𝑥^{4}+\frac{2}{3}𝑥^{4})\,d𝑥 \\ & =∫_{10}^{}\frac{5}{3}𝑥^{4}\,d𝑥 \\ & =[\frac{1}{3}𝑥^{5}]_{10}^{} \\ & =\frac{1}{3}.\end{aligned}


$$

Then, we compute the covariance and get

$$


\begin{aligned}Cov[𝑋,𝑌] & =E[𝑋𝑌]−E[𝑋]⋅E[𝑌] \\ & =\frac{1}{3}−(\frac{3}{4})(\frac{5}{12}) \\ & =\frac{1}{48}.\end{aligned}


$$

Therefore, the correlation coefficient is

$$


\begin{aligned}𝜌 & =\frac{Cov[𝑋,𝑌]}{SD[𝑋]⋅SD[𝑌]} \\ & ≈\frac{(\frac{1}{48})}{48} \\ & ≈0.44,\end{aligned}


$$

rounded to $2$ decimal places.
