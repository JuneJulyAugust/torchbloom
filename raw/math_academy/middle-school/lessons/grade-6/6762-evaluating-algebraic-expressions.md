## Lesson

### Introduction

To evaluate an algebraic expression, we replace the variable with a given value and then simplify the resulting numerical expression.

For example, suppose we want to evaluate the expression $2x^2$ when $x=3.$ This means we replace $x$ with $3$ everywhere it appears.

After replacing the variable, we simplify using the order of operations, so exponents are evaluated before multiplication and addition. In this case, we have

$$



\begin{aligned}2𝑥^{2} & = \\ 2×3^{2} & = \\ 2×9 & = \\ 18 & .\end{aligned}



$$

When an expression contains an exponent, remember that the exponent tells us how many times the base is multiplied by itself. For instance, $3^2$ means $3\times3,$ and $3^3$ means $3\times3\times3.$

In this lesson, you will practice evaluating expressions by carefully replacing variables with given values and simplifying step by step.

### Example: Evaluating Expressions Containing Squares or Cubes

#### Question

If $r=\dfrac57,$ then $r^2=$

#### Explanation

Replacing $r$ with $\dfrac57$ in the expression, we get

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑟^{2} & = \\ (\frac{5}{7})^{2} & = \\ \frac{5}{7}×\frac{5}{7} & = \\ \frac{5×5}{7×7} & = \\ \frac{25}{49} & .\end{aligned}



$$

### Example: Evaluating Expressions Containing Addition or Multiplication

#### Question

If $k=4,$ then $3k^2=$

#### Explanation

Replacing $k$ with $4$ in the expression, we get

$$



\begin{aligned}3𝑘^{2} & = \\ 3×𝑘^{2} & = \\ 3×4^{2} & = \\ 3×16 & = \\ 48 & .\end{aligned}



$$

### Example: Evaluating Expressions Containing Two Terms

#### Question

If $n=4,$ then $3n^2 + 5=$

#### Explanation

Replacing $n$ with $4$ in the expression, we get

$$



\begin{aligned}3𝑛^{2}+5 & = \\ 3×𝑛^{2}+5 & = \\ 3×4^{2}+5 & = \\ 3×16+5 & = \\ 48+5 & = \\ 53 & .\end{aligned}



$$

### Evaluating Expressions in Real-World Contexts

In real-world situations, algebraic expressions are often used to model quantities such as distance, cost, or time. Evaluating the expression means replacing the variable with a given value and interpreting the result in the context of the situation.

Suppose the total cost (in dollars) of renting a bike for $t$ hours is given by the expression $12t + 5.$ The value $5$ represents a fixed fee, and $12t$ represents the hourly cost.

To find the total cost of renting the bike for $3$ hours, we replace $t$ with $3$ and simplify.

$$



\begin{aligned}12𝑡+5 & = \\ 12×3+5 & = \\ 36+5 & = \\ 41 & .\end{aligned}



$$

The numerical result tells us the value of the quantity, and the context tells us how to interpret it. In this case, the total cost of renting the bike for $3$ hours is $41.$

When evaluating expressions in real-world contexts, always remember to

- replace the variable with the given value,

- simplify using the order of operations, and

- interpret the final value using the appropriate units.

### Example: Word Problems

#### Question

The amount of paint (in liters) used to cover a wall is given by the expression $\dfrac12w^2+\dfrac34,$ where $w$ is the width of the wall in meters. How much paint is used when the width of the wall is $\dfrac54\,\text{m}?$

#### Explanation

To determine how much paint is used when the width of the wall is $\dfrac54\,\text{m},$ we evaluate the expression at $w=\dfrac54.$

First, replacing $w$ with $\dfrac54$ in the expression, we obtain

$$



\begin{aligned}\frac{1}{2}𝑤^{2}+\frac{3}{4} & = \\ \frac{1}{2}×𝑤^{2}+\frac{3}{4} & = \\ \frac{1}{2}×(\frac{5}{4})^{2}+\frac{3}{4} & = \\ \frac{1}{2}×\frac{25}{16}+\frac{3}{4} & .\end{aligned}



$$

Then, evaluating the resulting expression, we get

$$



\begin{aligned}\frac{1}{2}×\frac{25}{16}+\frac{3}{4} & = \\ \frac{25}{32}+\frac{3}{4} & = \\ \frac{25}{32}+\frac{24}{32} & = \\ \frac{49}{32} & .\end{aligned}



$$

Therefore, the amount of paint used is $\dfrac{49}{32}\,\text{L}.$
