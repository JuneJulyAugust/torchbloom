## Lesson

### Introduction

Sometimes, after substituting the value of a variable into another equation, we may still need to use algebra to solve that equation.

For example, suppose that $b = a + 2$ and $b = 5,$ and we want to solve for $a.$

To combine these two equations, we can substitute the value $\color{blue}b=5$ into the equation $b=a+2,$ as follows:

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑏 & =𝑎+2 \\ 5 & =𝑎+2\end{aligned}



$$

Now, we need to solve the resulting equation for $a.$

To isolate $a,$ we need to perform the *opposite of adding $2$* to $a,$ which is *subtracting* $2$ from $a.$ So we subtract $2$ from both sides of the equation and simplify:

$$



\begin{aligned}5−2 & =𝑎+2−2 \\ 3 & =𝑎+0 \\ 3 & =𝑎\end{aligned}



$$

Therefore, the solution is $a=3.$

### Example: Solving for the Independent Variable Using Addition or Subtraction

#### Question

Find the value of $q$ given that $p = q + 6$ and $p = 19.$

#### Explanation

To find the value of $q,$ we first substitute $p = 19$ into this equation:

$$



19 = q + 6



$$

Now, we solve for $q$ by subtracting $6$ from both sides of this equation:

$$



\begin{aligned}19 & =𝑞+6 \\ 19−6 & =𝑞+6−6 \\ 13 & =𝑞+0 \\ 13 & =𝑞 \\ 𝑞 & =13\end{aligned}



$$

### Example: Solving for the Independent Variable Using Multiplication or Division

#### Question

Find the value of $w$ given that $z = \dfrac{w}{4}$ and $z = 7.$

#### Explanation

To find the value of $w,$ we first substitute $z = 7$ into this equation:

$$



7 = \dfrac{w}{4}



$$

Now, we solve for $w$ by multiplying both sides of this equation by $4\mathbin{:}$

$$



\begin{aligned}7 & =\frac{𝑤}{4} \\ 7×4 & =\frac{𝑤}{4}×4 \\ 28 & =\frac{4𝑤}{4} \\ 28 & =𝑤 \\ 𝑤 & =28\end{aligned}



$$

### Example: Solving Contextual Two-Variable Equations

#### Question

At the beginning of the year, there were $n$ students in the sixth-grade class. There are currently $m$ students in this class. The relationship between $n$ and $m$ is given by the equation $m = n + 7.$ If there are currently $38$ students in the sixth-grade class, how many students did this class have at the beginning of the year?

#### Explanation

If there are currently $38$ students in the sixth-grade class, we have that $m = 38.$ So, we substitute $m = 38$ into the equation $m = n + 7$ and get:

$$



38 = n + 7



$$

We solve for $n$ by subtracting $7$ from both sides of this equation:

$$



\begin{aligned}38 & =𝑛+7 \\ 38−7 & =𝑛+7−7 \\ 31 & =𝑛+0 \\ 31 & =𝑛\end{aligned}



$$

Therefore, there were $31$ students in the sixth-grade class at the beginning of the year.

### Equations Requiring Two-Step Solutions

Next, suppose we would like to solve for $x$ given that

$$



y+2=\dfrac{x}{8}



$$

and $y=4.$

To find the value of $x,$ we first substitute $y = 4$ into this equation:

$$



\begin{aligned}4+2 & =\frac{𝑥}{8} \\ 6 & =\frac{𝑥}{8}\end{aligned}



$$

Now, we solve for $x$ by multiplying both sides of this equation by $8{:}$

$$



\begin{aligned}6 & =\frac{𝑥}{8} \\ 6×8 & =\frac{𝑥}{8}×8 \\ 48 & =\frac{8𝑥}{8} \\ 48 & =𝑥 \\ 𝑥 & =48\end{aligned}



$$

### Example: Solving for the Independent Variable Using Two Steps

#### Question

Solve for $a$ if $3a = b + 5$ and $b = 10.$

#### Explanation

To find the value of $a,$ we first substitute $b = 10$ into this equation:

$$



\begin{aligned}3𝑎 & =10+5 \\ 3𝑎 & =15\end{aligned}



$$

Now, we solve for $a$ by dividing both sides of this equation by $3{:}$

$$



\begin{aligned}3𝑎 & =15 \\ \frac{3𝑎}{3} & =\frac{15}{3} \\ \frac{3𝑎}{3} & =5 \\ 𝑎 & =5\end{aligned}



$$
