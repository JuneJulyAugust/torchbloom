## Lesson

### Introduction

When simplifying a linear expression, our goal is to combine like terms so that the expression has fewer terms and is easier to work with.

Recall that like terms are terms that have the same variable part. Because the variable parts represent the same quantity, the terms can be added or subtracted.

For example, consider the expression $8x - 3x.$ The terms $8x$ and $-3x$ are like terms, so we can combine them by adding their coefficients:

$$



\begin{aligned}8𝑥−3𝑥 & = \\ \underset{coefficients}{\underset{}{(8−3)}}𝑥 & = \\ 5𝑥 & \end{aligned}



$$

When combining like terms:

- add the coefficients if the terms are being added, and

- subtract the coefficients if one term is being subtracted.

In this lesson, you will practice simplifying expressions by combining like terms step by step.

Sometimes, a term may appear without a written number in front of the variable. For example, $x$ means we are adding $1x,$ and $-x$ means we are subtracting $1x.$

### Example: Combining Two Like Terms

#### Question

$9x - 4x =$

#### Explanation

We have two like terms, $9x$ and $-4x.$

To collect these like terms, we combine them into a single term by subtracting their coefficients:

$$



\begin{aligned}9𝑥−4𝑥 & = \\ (9−4)𝑥 & = \\ 5𝑥 & \end{aligned}



$$

### Example: Combining Multiple Groups of Like Terms

#### Question

$6a - 2a + 5a + 3 =$

#### Explanation

We have three like terms, $6a,$ $-2a,$ and $5a.$

To collect these like terms, we combine them into a single term by adding their coefficients:

$$



\begin{aligned}6𝑎−2𝑎+5𝑎+3 & = \\ (6−2+5)𝑎+3 & = \\ 9𝑎+3 & \end{aligned}



$$

### Rearranging Terms to Group Like Terms

Sometimes, like terms are not written next to each other. In these cases, we first rearrange the expression so that like terms are grouped together, and then we combine them.

For example, consider the expression $12r - 5 + 3r.$ The two variable terms, $12r$ and $3r,$ are like terms, but they are separated by a constant.

We can rewrite the expression so that the like terms are together:

$$



\begin{aligned}12𝑟−5+3𝑟 & = \\ \underset{𝑟 terms}{\underset{}{12𝑟+3𝑟}}−5 & \end{aligned}



$$

Now that the like terms are grouped, we combine them by adding their coefficients:

$$



\begin{aligned}\underset{}{12𝑟+3𝑟}−5 & = \\ (12+3)𝑟−5 & = \\ 15𝑟−5 & \end{aligned}



$$

Rearranging terms makes it easier to see which terms can be combined before simplifying the expression.

### Example: Rearranging and Combining Like Terms

#### Question

$14m - 6 - 5m =$

#### Explanation

We rearrange the terms so that the like terms, $14m$ and $-5m,$ are together.

$$



\begin{aligned} 14m - 6 - 5m &= \\[5pt] \underbrace{14m - 5m}_{m \text{ terms}} - 6 & \end{aligned}



$$

Then, we combine the like terms:

$$



\begin{aligned} \underbrace{14m - 5m} - 6 &= \\[5pt] (14 - 5)m - 6 &= \\[5pt] 9m - 6 \end{aligned}



$$

### Simplifying Expressions with More Than One Variable

Some linear expressions contain terms with different variables. Even though these terms may look similar, they are **not** like terms and cannot be combined with each other.

For example, consider the expression $6x - 2x + 5y + y.$ The $x$ terms and the $y$ terms represent different quantities, so they must be combined separately.

$$



\begin{aligned}6𝑥−2𝑥+5𝑦+𝑦 & = \\ \underset{𝑥 terms}{\underset{}{6𝑥−2𝑥}}+\underset{𝑦 terms}{\underset{}{5𝑦+𝑦}} & \end{aligned}



$$

We now combine each group of like terms:

$$



\begin{aligned}\underset{}{\underset{}{6𝑥−2𝑥}}+\underset{}{\underset{}{5𝑦+𝑦}} & = \\ (6−2)𝑥+(5+1)𝑦 & = \\ 4𝑥+6𝑦 & \end{aligned}



$$

When simplifying expressions with more than one variable, always group and combine like terms one variable at a time.

### Example: Combining Multiple Groups of Like Terms

#### Question

$4m + 3m + 6n - 2n - 5 =$

#### Explanation

We have two groups of two like terms, $4m$ and $3m,$ and $6n$ and $-2n.$

To collect these like terms, we combine each group into a single term, adding their coefficients:

$$



\begin{aligned} \underbrace{4m + 3m}_{m \text{ terms}}\,+\, \underbrace{6n - 2n}_{n \text{ terms}} - 5 &= \\[5pt] (4+3)m + (6 - 2)n - 5 &= \\[5pt] 7m + 4n - 5 \end{aligned}



$$
