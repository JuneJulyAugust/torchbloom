### Introduction

In many real-world situations, we want to describe quantities that must stay *below*, *above*, or *at least* some value.

For example:

- A budget must stay *below* a certain amount.

- A factory must produce *at least* a certain number of items.

- A container must hold *no more than* a certain weight.

Situations like these are modeled using *inequalities*.

For example, suppose Liam originally had $s$ stickers. He gave $4$ of his stickers to a friend, and now he has fewer than $20$ stickers. Which inequality represents this situation?

We are told that Liam originally had $s$ stickers.

Since he gave $4$ stickers to a friend, the number of stickers he has now is

$$



s - 4.



$$

Because Liam now has *fewer than* $20$ stickers, this quantity must satisfy the following inequality:

$$



\begin{aligned}Liam's stickers after giving some away & <20 \\ 𝑠−4 & <20\end{aligned}



$$

Therefore, the correct inequality is

$$



s - 4 < 20



$$

### Example: Modeling Using Strict Inequalities

#### Question

Each notebook costs $4$ dollars. A student buys $x$ notebooks, and the total cost is less than $30$ dollars. Which inequality represents this situation?

#### Explanation

If each notebook costs $4$ dollars and a student buys $x$ notebooks, then the total cost can be written as

$$



4x.



$$

Because the total cost is ** $30$ dollars, this quantity must satisfy the following inequality:

$$



\begin{aligned}Total cost & <30 \\ 4𝑥 & <30\end{aligned}



$$

Therefore, the correct inequality is

$$



4x < 30.



$$

### Example: Modeling Using Non-Strict Inequalities

#### Question

A bakery makes $x$ trays of muffins each morning. Each tray contains $6$ muffins. The bakery must bake at least $72$ muffins in the morning. Which inequality represents this situation?

#### Explanation

We are told that the bakery makes $x$ trays each morning.

Since each tray contains $6$ muffins, the total number of muffins baked can be written as

$$



6x.



$$

Because the bakery must bake ** $72$ muffins, this quantity must satisfy the following inequality:

$$



\begin{aligned}Total muffins baked & ≥72 \\ 6𝑥 & ≥72\end{aligned}



$$

Therefore, the correct inequality is

$$



6x \geq 72.



$$

### Example: Modeling Using One-Step Inequalities With Fractions

#### Question

Each batch of paint uses $\dfrac{3}{4}$ liter of blue dye. If an artist mixes $x$ batches, the total amount of blue dye used is less than $6$ liters. Which inequality represents this situation?

#### Explanation

Each batch requires $\dfrac{3}{4}$ liter of blue dye. If the artist mixes $x$ batches, then the total amount of blue dye used can be written as

$$



\dfrac{3}{4}x.



$$

Because the total amount of blue dye used is ** $6$ liters, this quantity must satisfy the following inequality:

$$



\begin{aligned}Total dye used & <6 \\ \frac{3}{4}𝑥 & <6\end{aligned}



$$

Therefore, the correct inequality is

$$



\dfrac{3}{4}x < 6.



$$
