### Example: Dividing Proper Fractions When the Result is a Whole Number

#### Question

How many $\dfrac{5}{12}\,\text{m}$ long boards can be cut from a $\dfrac{5}{3}\,\text{m}$ plank of wood?

#### Explanation

To determine the number of boards, we need to divide the length of the plank by the length of one board:

$$



\dfrac53 \div \dfrac{5}{12}



$$

Dividing a fraction by another fraction is equivalent to multiplying the first fraction by the reciprocal of the second fraction.

The reciprocal of $\dfrac{5}{12}$ is $\dfrac{12}{5}.$ So, we have

$$



\dfrac53 \div \dfrac{5}{12} = \dfrac{\color{blue}5}{\color{red}3} \times \dfrac{\color{red}12}{\color{blue}5}.



$$

Notice that the first fraction’s denominator ($\color{red}3$) divides the second fraction’s numerator ($\color{red}12$), and the second fraction’s denominator ($\color{blue}5$) divides the first fraction’s numerator ($\color{blue}5$).

So, we can simplify our problem by first swapping the denominators:

$$



\dfrac{\color{blue}5}{\color{blue}5} \times \dfrac{\color{red}12}{\color{red}3}



$$

Next, we simplify each fraction:

$$



\dfrac55 = 5 \div 5 = 1 \qquad\text{and}\qquad \dfrac{12}{3} = 12 \div 3 = 4



$$

Finally, we conclude that

$$



\dfrac55 \times \dfrac{12}{3} = 1 \times 4 = 4.



$$

Therefore, the plank can be cut into $4$ boards.

### Example: Dividing Proper Fractions When the Result is an Improper Fraction

#### Question

A container holds $\dfrac{5}{18}\,\text{kg}$ of clay. Each small sculpture requires $\dfrac{2}{27}\,\text{kg}$ of clay. How many sculptures can be made with the clay in the container? Give your answer as an **** in its simplest form.

#### Explanation

To determine how many sculptures can be made, we need to divide the total amount of clay by the amount used for each sculpture:

$$



\dfrac{5}{18} \div \dfrac{2}{27}



$$

Dividing a fraction by another fraction is equivalent to multiplying the first fraction by the reciprocal of the second fraction.

The reciprocal of $\dfrac{2}{27}$ is $\dfrac{27}{2}.$ So, we have

$$



\dfrac{5}{18} \div \dfrac{2}{27} = \dfrac{5}{\color{red}18} \times \dfrac{\color{blue}27}{2}.



$$

Notice that the first fraction’s denominator ($\color{red}18$) and the second fraction’s numerator ($\color{blue}27$) have a common factor of $9.$

So, we can simplify our problem by first swapping the denominators:

$$



\dfrac{5}{2} \times \dfrac{\color{blue}27}{\color{red}18}



$$

Next, we reduce the second fraction to its lowest terms by dividing the numerator and denominator by $9{:}$

$$



\dfrac{5}{2} \times \dfrac{27 \div 9}{18 \div 9} = \dfrac{5}{2} \times \dfrac{3}{2}



$$

Finally, to multiply two fractions, we multiply the numerators, and we multiply the denominators:

$$



\dfrac{5}{2} \times \dfrac{3}{2} = \dfrac{15}{4}



$$

Therefore, $\dfrac{15}{4}$ sculptures can be made.

### Example: Dividing Proper Fractions and Expressing the Solution as a Mixed Number

#### Question

How many $\dfrac34\,\text{kg}$ bags of flour can be filled using $\dfrac{7}{6}\,\text{kg}$ of flour? Give your answer as a **** in its simplest form.

#### Explanation

To determine the number of bags that can be filled, we need to divide the total amount of flour by the amount in one bag:

$$



\dfrac76 \div \dfrac34



$$

Dividing a fraction by another fraction is equivalent to multiplying the first fraction by the reciprocal of the second fraction.

The reciprocal of $\dfrac34$ is $\dfrac43.$ So, we have

$$



\dfrac76 \div \dfrac34 = \dfrac76 \times \dfrac43.



$$

Now, we multiply fractions. We multiply the numerators, and we multiply the denominators:

$$



\dfrac76 \times \dfrac43 = \dfrac{7 \times 4}{6 \times 3} = \dfrac{28}{18}



$$

Next, we simplify the improper fraction:

$$



\dfrac{28}{18} = \dfrac{14}{9}



$$

Finally, we write the resulting improper fraction as a mixed number:

$$



\dfrac{14}{9} = 1\,\text{R}5 = 1\,\dfrac59



$$

Therefore, $1\,\dfrac59$ bags of flour can be filled.
