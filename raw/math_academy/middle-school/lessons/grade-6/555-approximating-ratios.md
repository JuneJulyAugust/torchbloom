### Introduction

We can use what we know about rounding numbers to approximate ratios. We round the quantities to a given degree first, before setting up the ratio.

Look at the following table, which shows the number of girls in a ballet school with blonde or black hair.

Rounding to the nearest ten, let's determine the approximate ratio of girls with blonde hair to girls with black hair.

First, let's approximate the number of girls with blonde or black hair by rounding to the nearest ten.

- Number of girls with blonde hair: $33 \longrightarrow 30$

- Number of girls with black hair: $58 \longrightarrow 60$

Therefore, the ratio of the number of girls with blonde hair to the number of girls with black hair is approximately

$$



\dfrac{30}{60}.



$$

We can simplify this ratio by dividing both numbers by $10$:

$$



\dfrac{30 \div 10}{60 \div 10} = \dfrac{3}{6}



$$

Then, we divide by $3$:

$$



\dfrac{3 \div 3}{6 \div 3} = \dfrac{1}{2}



$$

Therefore, the ratio of the number of girls with blonde hair to the number of girls with black hair is approximately $\dfrac{1}{2}.$

### Example: Approximating Ratios by Rounding to the Nearest Ten

#### Question

The following table shows the number of children in a park who like baseball.

Rounding to the nearest ten, find the approximate ratio of children who like baseball to those who don't.

#### Explanation

First, let's approximate the number of children who like baseball and those who don't like it by rounding to the nearest ten.

- Children who like baseball: $43 \longrightarrow 40$

- Children who don't like baseball: $69 \longrightarrow 70$

Therefore, the ratio of children who like baseball to those who don't is approximately

$$



\dfrac{40}{70} \, .



$$

We can simplify this ratio by dividing the numerator and denominator by $10\mathbin{:}$

$$



\dfrac{40 \div 10}{70 \div 10} = \dfrac{4}{7}



$$

Therefore, the ratio of children who like baseball to those who don't is approximately $\dfrac{4}{7},$ or $4:7.$

### Example: Approximating Ratios by Rounding to the Nearest Hundred

#### Question

The following table shows the number of boys and girls in a school.

Rounding the given values to the nearest hundred, determine the approximate ratio of the number of boys to the total number of students (boys and girls).

#### Explanation

First, let's approximate the number of boys and girls by rounding to the nearest hundred.

- Number of boys: $441 \longrightarrow 400$

- Number of girls: $569 \longrightarrow 600$

Thus, the total number of students is approximately $400 + 600 = 1,000.$

Therefore, the ratio of the number of boys to the total number of students is approximately

$$



\dfrac{400}{1,000} \, .



$$

We can simplify this ratio by dividing both numbers by $100$:

$$



\dfrac{400 \div 100}{1,000 \div 100} = \dfrac{4}{10}



$$

Then, we divide by $2$:

$$



\dfrac{4 \div 2}{10 \div 2} = \dfrac{2}{5}



$$

Therefore, the ratio of the number of boys to the total number of students is approximately $\dfrac{2}{5}.$

### Finding a Value Given a Ratio

Now, let's see how we can find a value given the ratio and another value.

Suppose at a school library, there are some science books and $396$ history books. The ratio of the number of science books to the number of history books is $3: 4.$ By rounding the number of history books to the nearest ten, approximate the number of science books in the library.

First, let's approximate the number of history books:

$$



396 \longrightarrow 400



$$

The ratio of the number of science books to the number of history books is

$$



\dfrac{\color{blue}3}{\color{red}4}.



$$

Here,

- the numerator represents the number of science books, and

- the denominator represents the number of history books.

We can make a denominator of $400$ by multiplying the numerator and denominator by $100{:}$

$$



\dfrac{3 \times 100}{4 \times 100} = \dfrac{300}{400}



$$

Therefore, there are approximately $300$ science books in the library.

### Example: Finding a Value Given a Ratio by Rounding an Amount

#### Question

The ratio of the number of orchids to the number of roses in a garden is $1: 3.$ In the garden, there are $84$ orchids. Rounding the number of orchids to the nearest ten, approximate the number of roses in the garden.

#### Explanation

First, let's approximate the number of orchids:

$$



84 \longrightarrow 80



$$

We are told that ratio of the number of orchids to the number of roses in the garden is $1:3,$ which can be expressed as the fraction

$$



\dfrac{\color{blue}1}{\color{red}3} \, .



$$

The numerator represents the number of orchids and the denominator represents the number of roses.

We know that there are approximately $80$ orchids, so we want to make a numerator of $80.$ We can do this by multiplying the numerator and denominator by $80\mathbin{:}$

$$



\dfrac{1 \times 80}{3 \times 80} = \dfrac{80}{240}



$$

Therefore, there are approximately $240$ roses in the garden.

### Example: Finding a Value by Approximating Ratios

#### Question

The number of pears and apples in Peter's greengrocer is shown in the table below.

There are approximately $60$ pears in Maria's greengrocer. The ratio of the number of apples to the number of pears is equal in both greengrocers. By rounding the given data to the nearest ten, approximate the number of apples that are in Maria's greengrocer.

#### Explanation

First, let's round the number of pears and apples in Peter's greengrocer:

- Apples: $42 \longrightarrow 40$

- Pears: $81 \longrightarrow 80$

Therefore, the ratio of the number of apples to the number of pears (expressed as a fraction) is approximately

$$



\dfrac{40}{80} \, .



$$

The numerator represents the number of apples, and the denominator represents the number of pears.

We can simplify this ratio by dividing both numbers by $10$:

$$



\dfrac{40 \div 10}{80 \div 10} = \dfrac{4}{8}



$$

Then, we divide by $4$:

$$



\dfrac{4 \div 4}{8 \div 4} = \dfrac{1}{2}



$$

We know that there are approximately $60$ pears in Maria's greengrocer, so we want to make a denominator of $60.$ We can do this by multiplying the numerator and denominator by $30\mathbin{:}$

$$



\dfrac{1 \times 30}{2 \times 30} = \dfrac{30}{60}



$$

Therefore, there are approximately $30$ apples in Maria's greengrocer.
