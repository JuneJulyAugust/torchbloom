## Lesson

### Introduction

Recall that to *solve* an equation, we isolate the variable by *reversing* the operations applied to it.

If a number is *subtracted* from the variable, we use the inverse operation, which is *addition*.

Whatever operation we perform on one side of the equation, we must perform the same operation on the other side to keep the equation balanced.

For example, suppose we want to solve for $x$ given that

$$



x - 11 = 14.



$$

To isolate $x,$ we need to perform the opposite of subtracting $11$ from $x,$ which is adding $11$ to $x.$

So, we add $11$ to both sides of the equation:

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑥−11 & =14 \\ 𝑥−11+11 & =14+11\end{aligned}



$$

Next, we simplify:

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑥+0 & =25 \\ 𝑥 & =25\end{aligned}



$$

So, the solution to the equation is $x = 25.$

### Example: Solving a One-Step Subtraction Equation

#### Question

Solve for $t$ given that $t - 3.7 = 5.6.$

#### Explanation

To isolate $t,$ we need to perform the opposite of subtracting $3.7$ from $t,$ which is adding $3.7$ to $t.$

So, we add $3.7$ to both sides of the equation:

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑡−3.7 & =5.6 \\ 𝑡−3.7+3.7 & =5.6+3.7\end{aligned}



$$

Next, we simplify:

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑡+0 & =9.3 \\ 𝑡 & =9.3\end{aligned}



$$

### Example: Solving a One-Step Subtraction Equation With Fractions

#### Question

Solve for $z$ given that $z - \dfrac{5}{12} = \dfrac{1}{6}.$

#### Explanation

To isolate $z,$ we need to perform the opposite of subtracting $\dfrac{5}{12}$ from $z,$ which is adding $\dfrac{5}{12}$ to $z.$

So, we add $\dfrac{5}{12}$ to both sides of the equation:

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑧−\frac{5}{12} & =\frac{1}{6} \\ 𝑧−\frac{5}{12}+\frac{5}{12} & =\frac{1}{6}+\frac{5}{12}\end{aligned}



$$

Next, we simplify:

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑧+0 & =\frac{1}{6}+\frac{5}{12} \\ 𝑧 & =\frac{2}{12}+\frac{5}{12} \\ & =\frac{2+5}{12} \\ & =\frac{7}{12}\end{aligned}



$$

### One-Step Subtraction Equations With Unknown Subtrahend

Sometimes the variable is not the number being subtracted from, but the number being subtracted.

In other words, instead of an equation of the form

$$



y - 6 = 14,



$$

we may have an equation of the form

$$



6 - y = 14.



$$

In this case, the variable is the *subtrahend*.

To isolate the variable, we subtract the constant from both sides of the equation. This creates a negative variable (for example, $-y$), and then we determine the value of the variable by finding the opposite of that number.

For example, let’s solve for $y$ given that

$$



6 - y = 14.



$$

To isolate $y,$ we subtract $6$ from both sides of the equation:

$$



\begin{aligned}6−𝑦 & =14 \\ 6−𝑦−6 & =14−6 \\ 6−6−𝑦 & =14−6\end{aligned}



$$

Next, we simplify:

$$



\begin{aligned}0−𝑦 & =8 \\ −𝑦 & =8\end{aligned}



$$

Notice that $-y$ (the opposite of $y$) is equal to $8.$

Thus, $y$ must be equal to the opposite of $8,$ which is $-8.$

Therefore, $y = -8.$

### Example: Solving a One-Step Subtraction Equation With an Unknown Subtrahend

#### Question

Solve for $x$ given that $4.8 - x = 9.1.$

#### Explanation

To isolate $x,$ we subtract $4.8$ from both sides of the equation:

$$



\begin{aligned}4.8−𝑥 & =9.1 \\ 4.8−𝑥−4.8 & =9.1−4.8 \\ 4.8−4.8−𝑥 & =9.1−4.8\end{aligned}



$$

Next, we simplify:

$$



\begin{aligned}0−𝑥 & =4.3 \\ −𝑥 & =4.3\end{aligned}



$$

Notice that $-x$ (the opposite of $x$) is equal to $4.3.$ Thus, $x$ must be equal to the opposite of $4.3,$ which is $-4.3.$

Therefore, $x = -4.3.$

### Example: Solving a One-Step Subtraction Equation With Fractions and Unknown Subtrahend

#### Question

Solve for $x$ given that $\dfrac{9}{10} - x = \dfrac{17}{10}.$

#### Explanation

To isolate $x,$ we subtract $\dfrac{9}{10}$ from both sides of the equation:

$$



\begin{aligned}\frac{9}{10}−𝑥 & =\frac{17}{10} \\ \frac{9}{10}−𝑥−\frac{9}{10} & =\frac{17}{10}−\frac{9}{10} \\ \frac{9}{10}−\frac{9}{10}−𝑥 & =\frac{17}{10}−\frac{9}{10}\end{aligned}



$$

Next, we simplify:

$$



\begin{aligned}0−𝑥 & =\frac{17}{10}−\frac{9}{10} \\ −𝑥 & =\frac{17−9}{10} \\ −𝑥 & =\frac{8}{10} \\ −𝑥 & =\frac{4}{5}\end{aligned}



$$

Notice that $-x$ (the opposite of $x$) is equal to $\dfrac{4}{5}.$ Thus, $x$ must be equal to the opposite of $\dfrac{4}{5},$ which is $-\dfrac{4}{5}.$

Therefore, $x = -\dfrac{4}{5}.$
