## Lesson

### Introduction

A **linear expression** is an algebraic expression that contains a variable, but the variable is only multiplied by a number or divided by a number.

Some examples of linear expressions are $3n+5,$ $x-\dfrac12,$ and $\dfrac{y}{5}+2.$

To **evaluate** an algebraic expression, we replace the variable with the value that it represents and then simplify.

For example, suppose we are given the expression $3n+5$ and we know that $n=6.$ Then $n$ represents the number $6,$ so we replace $n$ with $6{:}$

$$



\begin{aligned}3𝑛+5 & = \\ 3×𝑛+5 & = \\ 3×6+5 & = \\ 18+5 & = \\ 23 & .\end{aligned}



$$

So, when $n=6,$ the value of the expression $3n+5$ is $23.$

### Expressions With Multiplication and Division

Now, suppose $h=35.$ Let's compute $\dfrac{h}7.$

As before, replacing $h$ with $35$ in the expression, we get

$$



\begin{aligned}\frac{ℎ}{7} & = \\ ℎ÷7 & = \\ 35÷7 & = \\ 5 & .\end{aligned}



$$

### Example: Evaluating Linear Expressions Involving Multiplication and Division

#### Question

If $p=\dfrac34,$ then $4p=$

#### Explanation

Replacing $p$ with $\dfrac34$ in the expression, we get

$$



\begin{aligned}4𝑝 & = \\ 4×𝑝 & = \\ \frac{4}{1}×\frac{3}{4} & = \\ \frac{12}{4} & = \\ 3 & .\end{aligned}



$$

### Combining Different Operations in Expressions

We can combine several arithmetic operations in linear expressions.

For example, given that $m = 6,$ let's compute $2m + 5.$

Replacing $m$ with $6$ in the expression, we get

$$



\begin{aligned}2𝑚+5 & = \\ 2×6+5 & = \\ 12+5 & = \\ 17 & .\end{aligned}



$$

### Example: Evaluating Linear Expressions

#### Question

If $k = 30,$ then $\dfrac{k}5 - 3 =$

#### Explanation

Replacing $k$ with $30$ in the expression, we get

$$



\begin{aligned}\frac{𝑘}{5}−3 & = \\ 𝑘÷5−3 & = \\ 30÷5−3 & = \\ 6−3 & = \\ 3 & .\end{aligned}



$$

### Evaluating Linear Expressions in Context

Finally, let's see how we can evaluate linear expressions in context.

Suppose the temperature (in degrees Celsius) after $t$ hours of a liquid mixture as it cools is given by the expression $38 - 4t.$ What is the temperature of the mixture after $\dfrac52$ hours?

To determine the temperature after $\dfrac{5}{2}$ hours, we replace $t$ with $\dfrac52$ and evaluate the resulting expression:

$$



\begin{aligned}38−4𝑡 & = \\ 38−4×𝑡 & = \\ 38−\frac{4}{1}×\frac{5}{2} & = \\ 38−\frac{4}{2}×\frac{5}{1} & = \\ 38−2×5 & = \\ 38−10 & = \\ 28 & \end{aligned}



$$

Therefore, the temperature after $\dfrac52$ hours is $28\,^\circ\text{C}.$

### Example: Evaluating Linear Expressions: Word Problems

#### Question

The distance (in kilometers) that a delivery drone travels after $t$ hours is given by the expression $14 - 3t.$ What distance does the drone travel after $\dfrac{5}{2}$ hours?

#### Explanation

To determine the distance after $\dfrac{5}{2}$ hours, we replace $t$ with $\dfrac52$ and evaluate the resulting expression:

$$



\begin{aligned}14−3𝑡 & = \\ 14−3×𝑡 & = \\ 14−\frac{3}{1}×\frac{5}{2} & = \\ 14−\frac{15}{2} & = \\ \frac{28}{2}−\frac{15}{2} & = \\ \frac{13}{2} & .\end{aligned}



$$

Therefore, the distance the drone travels after $\dfrac{5}{2}$ hours is $\boxed{\dfrac{13}{2}}$ kilometers.
