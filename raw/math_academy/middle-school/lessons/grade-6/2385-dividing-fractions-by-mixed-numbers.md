## Lesson

### Introduction

Remember the following rule for dividing fractions:

Dividing one fraction by another is equivalent to multiplying the first fraction by the reciprocal of the second fraction.

We can use this rule with mixed numbers, too. To illustrate, let's use this rule to divide a fraction by a mixed number:

$$



\dfrac{1}{5}\div 1\,\dfrac{1}{3}



$$

First, we write $1 \,\dfrac{1}{3}$ as an improper fraction:

$$



1 \,\dfrac{1}{3}=\dfrac{(1 \times 3) + 1}{3}= \dfrac{4}{3}



$$

Therefore, our problem now is to work out the following:

$$



\dfrac{1}{5}\div \dfrac{4}{3}



$$

Recall that dividing one fraction by another is equivalent to multiplying the first fraction by the reciprocal of the second fraction.

The reciprocal of $\dfrac{\color{blue}4}{\color{red}3}$ is $\dfrac{\color{red}3}{\color{blue}4}.$ So, we have:

$$



\dfrac{1}{5} \div \dfrac{\color{blue}4}{\color{red}3} = \dfrac{1}{5} \times \dfrac{\color{red}3}{\color{blue}4}



$$

Now, we multiply the fractions. We multiply the numerators, and we multiply the denominators:

$$



\dfrac{1}{5} \times \dfrac{3}{4} = \dfrac {1\times 3} {5\times 4} = \dfrac{3}{20}



$$

Therefore,

$$



\dfrac{1}{5}\div 1\,\dfrac{1}{3} = \dfrac{3}{20} \, .



$$

### Example: Dividing a Fraction by a Mixed Number

#### Question

Divide $\dfrac{2}{3}\div 1\,\dfrac{2}{5}.$

#### Explanation

First, we write $1 \,\dfrac{2}{5}$ as an improper fraction:

$$



1 \,\dfrac{2}{5}=\dfrac{(1 \times 5) + 2}{5}= \dfrac{7}{5}



$$

Therefore, our problem now is to work out the following:

$$



\dfrac{2}{3}\div \dfrac{7}{5}



$$

Dividing one fraction by another is equivalent to multiplying the first fraction by the reciprocal of the second fraction.

The reciprocal of $\dfrac{\color{blue}7}{\color{red}5}$ is $\dfrac{\color{red}5}{\color{blue}7}.$ So, we have:

$$



\dfrac{2}{3} \div \dfrac{\color{blue}7}{\color{red}5} = \dfrac{2}{3} \times \dfrac{\color{red}5}{\color{blue}7}



$$

Now, we multiply the fractions. We multiply the numerators, and we multiply the denominators:

$$



\dfrac{2}{3} \times \dfrac{5}{7} = \dfrac {2\times 5} {3\times 7} = \dfrac{10}{21}



$$

Therefore,

$$



\dfrac{2}{3}\div 1\,\dfrac{2}{5} = \dfrac{10}{21} \, .



$$

### Example: Dividing a Fraction by a Mixed Number When Simplification is Needed: Finding the Missing Numerator

#### Question

What is the missing number in the following equality?

$$



0



$$

#### Explanation

First, we write $1 \,\dfrac{3}{8}$ as an improper fraction:

$$



1 \,\dfrac{3}{8}=\dfrac{(1 \times 8) + 3}{8}= \dfrac{11}{8}



$$

Therefore, our problem now is to work out the following:

$$



\dfrac{1}{4}\div \dfrac{11}{8}



$$

Dividing one fraction by another is equivalent to multiplying the first fraction by the reciprocal of the second fraction.

The reciprocal of $\dfrac{\color{blue}11}{\color{red}8}$ is $\dfrac{\color{red}8}{\color{blue}11}.$ So, we have:

$$



\dfrac{1}{4} \div \dfrac{\color{blue}11}{\color{red}8} = \dfrac{1}{4} \times \dfrac{\color{red}8}{\color{blue}11}



$$

Notice that the first fraction's denominator ($4$) and the second fraction's numerator ($\color{red}8$) have a common factor of $4.$

Therefore, we can simplify our problem by first swapping the denominators:

$$



\dfrac{1}{\color{blue}11} \times \dfrac{\color{red}8}{4}



$$

Next, we reduce the second fraction to its lowest terms by dividing the numerator and denominator by $4.$

$$



\begin{aligned}\frac{1}{11}×\frac{8÷4}{4÷4} & =\frac{1}{11}×\frac{2}{1}\end{aligned}



$$

To multiply two fractions, we multiply the numerators, and we multiply the denominators:

$$



\dfrac{1}{11} \times \dfrac{2}{1} = \dfrac{1 \times 2}{11 \times 1} = \dfrac{\boxed{\color{blue}2}}{11}



$$

Therefore,

$$



\dfrac{1}{4}\div 1\,\dfrac{3}{8} = \dfrac{\boxed{\color{blue}2}}{11}.



$$

We conclude that the missing number is $2.$

#### Question

$\dfrac{1}{4}\div 1\,\dfrac{7}{8}=$

#### Explanation

First, we write $1 \,\dfrac{7}{8}$ as an improper fraction:

$$



1 \,\dfrac{7}{8}=\dfrac{(1 \times 8) + 7}{8}= \dfrac{15}{8}

\dfrac{1}{4} \div \dfrac{\color{blue}15}{\color{red}8} = \dfrac{1}{4} \times \dfrac{\color{red}8}{\color{blue}15}



$$

Notice that the first fraction's denominator ($4$) and the second fraction's numerator ($\color{red}8$) have a common factor of $4.$

Therefore, we can simplify our problem by first swapping the denominators:

$$



\dfrac{1}{\color{blue}15} \times \dfrac{\color{red}8}{4}



$$

Next, we reduce the second fraction to its lowest terms by dividing the numerator and denominator by $4.$

$$



\begin{aligned}\frac{1}{15}×\frac{8÷4}{4÷4} & =\frac{1}{15}×\frac{2}{1}\end{aligned}

$$



\dfrac{1}{15} \times \dfrac{2}{1} = \dfrac{1 \times 2}{15 \times 1} = \dfrac{2}{15}
