## Lesson

### Introduction

Recall the following important rule for dividing fractions:

*Dividing one fraction by another is equivalent to multiplying the first fraction by the reciprocal of the second fraction*.

We can use this rule with whole and mixed numbers, too!

To illustrate, let's solve the following division problem:

$$



2 \,\dfrac{1}{5} \div 2



$$

First, we need to write $2 \,\dfrac{1}{5}$ as an improper fraction:

$$



2 \,\dfrac{1}{5}=\dfrac{(2 \times 5) + 1}{5}= \dfrac{11}{5}



$$

Therefore, our problem now is to determine the value of

$$



\dfrac{11}{5} \div 2.



$$

Dividing a fraction by a whole number is equivalent to multiplying the fraction by the reciprocal of the whole number.

The reciprocal of $\color{blue}2$ is $\dfrac{1}{{\color{blue}{2}}}.$ So, we have

$$



\dfrac{11}{5}\, {\color{red}\div} \,\, {\color{blue}2} = \dfrac{11}{5}\, {\color{red}\times} \,\dfrac{1}{\color{blue}2}.



$$

Now, we multiply the fractions. We multiply the numerators, and we multiply the denominators:

$$



\dfrac{11}{5}\times \dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac {11\times 1} {5\times 2} = \dfrac{11}{10}



$$

Therefore,

$$



2 \,\dfrac{1}{5} \div 2 = \dfrac{11}{10}.



$$

Finally, expressing our result as a mixed number, we conclude that

$$



2 \,\dfrac{1}{5} \div 2 = 1\dfrac{1}{10}.



$$

### Example: Dividing a Mixed Number by a Whole Number

#### Question

Calculate the value of $2 \,\dfrac{1}{2} \div 4.$

#### Explanation

First, we write $2 \,\dfrac{1}{2}$ as an improper fraction:

$$



2 \,\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{(2 \times 2) + 1}{2}= \dfrac{5}{2}



$$

Therefore, our problem now is to determine the value of

$$



\dfrac{5}{2} \div 4.



$$

Dividing a fraction by a whole number is equivalent to multiplying the fraction by the reciprocal of the whole number.

The reciprocal of $\color{blue}4$ is $\dfrac{1}{{\color{blue}{4}}}.$ So, we have

$$



\dfrac 5 2\, {\color{red}\div} \,\, {\color{blue}4} = \dfrac 5 2\, {\color{red}\times} \,\dfrac{1}{\color{blue}4}.



$$

Now, we multiply the fractions. We multiply the numerators, and we multiply the denominators:

$$



\dfrac 5 2 \times \dfrac{1}{4} = \dfrac {5\times 1} {2\times 4} = \dfrac{5}{8}



$$

Therefore,

$$



2 \,\dfrac{1}{2} \div 4 = \dfrac{5}{8} \, .



$$

### Example: Dividing a Mixed Number by a Whole Number When Simplification Is Required

#### Question

Calculate the value of $2 \,\dfrac{2}{3} \div 6.$

#### Explanation

First, we write $2 \,\dfrac{2}{3}$ as an improper fraction:

$$



2 \,\dfrac{2}{3}=\dfrac{(2 \times 3) + 2}{3}= \dfrac{8}{3}



$$

Therefore, our problem now is to determine the value of

$$



\dfrac{8}{3} \div 6.



$$

Dividing a fraction by a whole number is equivalent to multiplying the fraction by the reciprocal of the whole number.

The reciprocal of $\color{blue}6$ is $\dfrac{1}{{\color{blue}{6}}}.$ So, we have

$$



\dfrac{8}{3}\, {\color{red}\div} \, {\color{blue}6} = \dfrac{8}{3}\, {\color{red}\times} \,\dfrac{1}{\color{blue}6}.



$$

Notice that the first fraction's numerator ($8$) and the second fraction's denominator ($\color{blue}6$) have a common factor of $2.$

Therefore, we can simplify our problem by first swapping the denominators:

$$



\dfrac{8}{\color{blue}6} \times \dfrac{1}{3}



$$

Next, we reduce the first fraction to its lowest terms by dividing the numerator and denominator by $2.$

$$



\begin{aligned}\frac{8÷2}{6÷2}×\frac{1}{3} & =\frac{4}{3}×\frac{1}{3}\end{aligned}



$$

To multiply two fractions, we multiply the numerators, and we multiply the denominators:

$$



\dfrac{4}{3} \times \dfrac{1}{3} = \dfrac{4 \times 1}{3 \times 3} = \dfrac{4}{9}



$$

Therefore,

$$



2 \,\dfrac{2}{3} \div 6 = \dfrac{4}{9}.



$$

### Example: Dividing a Mixed Number by a Whole Number When the Result Is a Mixed Number

#### Question

Calculate the value of $7 \,\dfrac{1}{3} \div 4.$

#### Explanation

First, we write $7 \,\dfrac{1}{3}$ as an improper fraction:

$$



7 \,\dfrac{1}{3}=\dfrac{(7 \times 3) + 1}{3}= \dfrac{22}{3}



$$

Therefore, our problem now is to determine the value of

$$



\dfrac{22}{3} \div 4.



$$

Dividing a fraction by a whole number is equivalent to multiplying the fraction by the reciprocal of the whole number.

The reciprocal of $\color{blue}4$ is $\dfrac{1}{{\color{blue}{4}}}.$ So, we have

$$



\dfrac{22}{3}\, {\color{red}\div} \, {\color{blue}4} = \dfrac{22}{3}\, {\color{red}\times} \,\dfrac{1}{\color{blue}4}.



$$

Notice that the first fraction's numerator ($22$) and the second fraction's denominator ($\color{blue}4$) have a common factor of $2.$

Therefore, we can simplify our problem by first swapping the denominators:

$$



\dfrac{22}{\color{blue}4} \times \dfrac{1}{3}



$$

Next, we reduce the first fraction to its lowest terms by dividing the numerator and denominator by $2.$

$$



\begin{aligned}\frac{22÷2}{4÷2}×\frac{1}{3} & =\frac{11}{2}×\frac{1}{3}\end{aligned}



$$

To multiply two fractions, we multiply the numerators, and we multiply the denominators:

$$



\dfrac{11}{2} \times \dfrac{1}{3} = \dfrac{11 \times 1}{2 \times 3} = \dfrac{11}{6}



$$

Finally, we write the resulting improper fraction as a mixed number:

$$



\dfrac{11}{6} = 1\,\text{R}\,5 = 1\,\dfrac{5}{6}



$$

Therefore,

$$



7 \,\dfrac{1}{3} \div 4 = 1\,\dfrac{5}{6}.



$$
