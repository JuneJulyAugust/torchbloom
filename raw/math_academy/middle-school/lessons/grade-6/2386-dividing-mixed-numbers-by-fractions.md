## Lesson

### Introduction

Remember the following rule for dividing fractions:

Dividing one fraction by another is equivalent to multiplying the first fraction by the reciprocal of the second fraction.

We can use this rule with mixed numbers, too. To illustrate, let's use this rule to divide a mixed number by a fraction:

$$



1\,\dfrac{2}{3}\div \dfrac{1}{4}



$$

First, we write $1 \,\dfrac{2}{3}$ as an improper fraction:

$$



1 \,\dfrac{2}{3}=\dfrac{(1 \times 3) + 2}{3}= \dfrac{5}{3}



$$

Therefore, our problem now is to work out the following:

$$



\dfrac 5 3\div \dfrac 1 4



$$

Remember that dividing one fraction by another is equivalent to multiplying the first fraction by the reciprocal of the second fraction.

The reciprocal of $\dfrac{\color{blue}1}{\color{red}4}$ is $\dfrac{\color{red}4}{\color{blue}1}.$ So, we have:

$$



\dfrac{5}{3} \div \dfrac{\color{blue}1}{\color{red}4} = \dfrac{5}{3} \times \dfrac{\color{red}4}{\color{blue}1}



$$

Now, we multiply the fractions. We multiply the numerators, and we multiply the denominators:

$$



\dfrac{5}{3} \times \dfrac{4}{1} = \dfrac {5\times 4} {3\times 1} = \dfrac{20}{3}



$$

Finally, we write our improper fraction as a mixed number:

$$



\dfrac{20}{3} =6\,\text{R} 2 = 6\,\dfrac{2}{3}



$$

Therefore,

$$



1\,\dfrac{2}{3}\div \dfrac{1}{4} = 6\,\dfrac{2}{3} \, .



$$

### Example: Dividing a Mixed Number by a Fraction

#### Question

What is the missing digit in the following equality?

$$



0



$$

#### Explanation

First, we write $1 \,\dfrac{5}{6}$ as an improper fraction:

$$



1 \,\dfrac{5}{6}=\dfrac{(1 \times 6) + 5}{6}= \dfrac{11}{6}



$$

Therefore, our problem now is to work out the following:

$$



\dfrac {11}{6}\div \dfrac{2}{5}



$$

Dividing one fraction by another is equivalent to multiplying the first fraction by the reciprocal of the second fraction.

The reciprocal of $\dfrac{\color{blue}2}{\color{red}5}$ is $\dfrac{\color{red}5}{\color{blue}2}.$ So we have:

$$



\dfrac{11}{6} \div \dfrac{\color{blue}2}{\color{red}5} = \dfrac{11}{6} \times \dfrac{\color{red}5}{\color{blue}2}



$$

Now, we multiply the fractions. We multiply the numerators, and we multiply the denominators:

$$



\dfrac{11}{6} \times \dfrac{5}{2} = \dfrac {11\times 5} {6\times 2} = \dfrac{\boxed{\color{blue}55}}{12}



$$

Therefore,

$$



\dfrac {11}{6}\div \dfrac{2}{5} = \dfrac{\boxed{\color{blue}55}}{12} \, .



$$

We conclude that the missing number is $55.$

### Example: Dividing a Mixed Number by a Fraction When Simplification is Required

#### Question

What is the missing number in the following equality?

$$



0



$$

#### Explanation

First, we write $2 \,\dfrac{1}{6}$ as an improper fraction:

$$



2 \,\dfrac{1}{6}=\dfrac{(2 \times 6) + 1}{6}= \dfrac{13}{6}



$$

Therefore, our problem now is to work out the following:

$$



\dfrac{13}{6} \div \dfrac{2}{3}



$$

Dividing one fraction by another is equivalent to multiplying the first fraction by the reciprocal of the second fraction.

The reciprocal of $\dfrac{\color{blue}2}{\color{red}3}$ is $\dfrac{\color{red}3}{\color{blue}2}.$ So, we have:

$$



\dfrac{13}{6} \div \dfrac{\color{blue}2}{\color{red}3} = \dfrac{13}{6} \times \dfrac{\color{red}3}{\color{blue}2}



$$

Notice that the first fraction's denominator ($6$) and the second fraction's numerator ($\color{red}3$) have a common factor of $3.$

Therefore, we can simplify our problem by first swapping the denominators:

$$



\dfrac{13}{\color{blue}2} \times \dfrac{\color{red}3}{6}



$$

Next, we reduce the second fraction to its lowest terms by dividing the numerator and denominator by $3.$

$$



\begin{aligned}\frac{13}{2}×\frac{3÷3}{6÷3} & =\frac{13}{2}×\frac{1}{2}\end{aligned}



$$

To multiply two fractions, we multiply the numerators, and we multiply the denominators:

$$



\dfrac{13}{2} \times \dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{13 \times 1}{2 \times 2} = \dfrac{\boxed{\color{blue}13}}{4}



$$

Therefore,

$$



2\,\dfrac{1}{6}\div \dfrac{2}{3}= \dfrac{\boxed{\color{blue}13}}{4}.



$$

We conclude that the missing number is $13.$

### Example: Dividing a Mixed Number by a Fraction When the Result is a Mixed Number

#### Question

Divide $1\,\dfrac{2}{5}\div \dfrac{3}{10}.$

#### Explanation

First, we write $1 \,\dfrac{2}{5}$ as an improper fraction:

$$



1 \,\dfrac{2}{5}=\dfrac{(1 \times 5) + 2}{5}= \dfrac{7}{5}



$$

Therefore, our problem now is to work out the following:

$$



\dfrac{7}{5}\div \dfrac{3}{10}



$$

Dividing one fraction by another is equivalent to multiplying the first fraction by the reciprocal of the second fraction.

The reciprocal of $\dfrac{\color{blue}3}{\color{red}10}$ is $\dfrac{\color{red}10}{\color{blue}3}.$ So, we have:

$$



\dfrac{7}{5} \div \dfrac{\color{blue}3}{\color{red}10} = \dfrac{7}{5} \times \dfrac{\color{red}10}{\color{blue}3}



$$

Notice that the first fraction's denominator ($5$) and the second fraction's numerator ($\color{red}10$) have a common factor of $5.$

Therefore, we can simplify our problem by first swapping the denominators:

$$



\dfrac{7}{\color{blue}3} \times \dfrac{\color{red}10}{5}



$$

Next, we reduce the second fraction to its lowest terms by dividing the numerator and denominator by $5.$

$$



\begin{aligned}\frac{7}{3}×\frac{10÷5}{5÷5} & =\frac{7}{3}×\frac{2}{1}\end{aligned}



$$

To multiply two fractions, we multiply the numerators, and we multiply the denominators:

$$



\dfrac{7}{3} \times \dfrac{2}{1} = \dfrac{7 \times 2}{3 \times 1} = \dfrac{14}{3}



$$

Finally, we write our improper fraction as a mixed number:

$$



\dfrac{14}{3} = 4\,\text{R}\,2 = 4\,\dfrac{2}{3}



$$

Therefore,

$$



1\,\dfrac{2}{5}\div \dfrac{3}{10} = 4\,\dfrac{2}{3}.



$$
