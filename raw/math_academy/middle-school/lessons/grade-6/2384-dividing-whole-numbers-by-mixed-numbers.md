## Lesson

### Introduction

Remember the following rule for dividing fractions:

Dividing one fraction by another is equivalent to multiplying the first fraction by the reciprocal of the second fraction.

We can use this rule with mixed numbers, too. To illustrate, let's use this rule to divide a whole number by a mixed number:

$$



4 \div 2\,\dfrac{1}{3}



$$

First, we write $2 \,\dfrac{1}{3}$ as an improper fraction:

$$



2 \,\dfrac{1}{3}=\dfrac{(2 \times 3) + 1}{3}= \dfrac{7}{3}



$$

We also write $4$ as an improper fraction:

$$



4=\dfrac{4}{1}



$$

Therefore, our problem now is to work out the following:

$$



\dfrac 4 1\div \dfrac 7 3



$$

Remember that dividing one fraction by another is equivalent to multiplying the first fraction by the reciprocal of the second fraction.

The reciprocal of $\dfrac{\color{blue}7}{\color{red}3}$ is $\dfrac{\color{red}3}{\color{blue}7}.$ So, we have:

$$



\dfrac 4 1 \div \dfrac{\color{blue}7}{\color{red}3} = \dfrac 4 1 \times \dfrac{\color{red}3}{\color{blue}7}



$$

Now, we multiply the fractions. We multiply the numerators, and we multiply the denominators:

$$



\dfrac 4 {1} \times \dfrac{3}{7} = \dfrac {4\times 3} {1\times 7} = \dfrac{12}{7}



$$

Therefore,

$$



4 \div 2\,\dfrac{1}{3} = \dfrac{12}{7} \, .



$$

### Example: Dividing a Whole Number by a Mixed Number

#### Question

What is $12 \div 1\,\dfrac{1}{5}?$

#### Explanation

First, we write $1 \,\dfrac{1}{5}$ as an improper fraction:

$$



1 \,\dfrac{1}{5}=\dfrac{(1 \times 5) + 1}{5}= \dfrac{6}{5}



$$

We also write $12$ as an improper fraction:

$$



12=\dfrac{12}{1}



$$

Therefore, our problem now is to work out the following:

$$



\dfrac {12} 1\div \dfrac {6} 5



$$

Dividing one fraction by another is equivalent to multiplying the first fraction by the reciprocal of the second fraction.

The reciprocal of $\dfrac{\color{blue}6}{\color{red}5}$ is $\dfrac{\color{red}5}{\color{blue}6}.$ So, we have:

$$



\dfrac {12} 1 \div \dfrac{\color{blue}6}{\color{red}5} = \dfrac {12} 1 \times \dfrac{\color{red}5}{\color{blue}6}



$$

Now, we multiply the fractions. We multiply the numerators, and we multiply the denominators:

$$



\dfrac {12} {1} \times \dfrac{5}{6} = \dfrac {12\times 5} {1\times 6} = \dfrac{60}{6}



$$

Finally, we simplify:

$$



60\div 6=10



$$

Therefore,

$$



12 \div 1\,\dfrac{1}{5} = 10 \, .



$$

### Example: Dividing a Whole Number by a Mixed Number When Simplification is Needed

#### Question

What is the missing digit in the following equality?

$$



0



$$

#### Explanation

First, we write $2\,\dfrac{1}{4}$ as an improper fraction:

$$



2\,\dfrac{1}{4}=\dfrac{(2 \times 4) + 1}{4}= \dfrac{9}{4}



$$

We also write $3$ as an improper fraction:

$$



3=\dfrac{3}{1}



$$

Therefore, our problem now is to work out the following:

$$



\dfrac{3}{1}\div \dfrac{9}{4}



$$

Dividing one fraction by another is equivalent to multiplying the first fraction by the reciprocal of the second fraction.

The reciprocal of $\dfrac{\color{blue}9}{\color{red}4}$ is $\dfrac{\color{red}4}{\color{blue}9}.$ So, we have:

$$



\dfrac{3}{1} \div \dfrac{\color{blue}9}{\color{red}4} = \dfrac{3}{1} \times \dfrac{\color{red}4}{\color{blue}9}



$$

Notice that the first fraction's numerator ($3$) and the second fraction's denominator ($\color{blue}9$) have a common factor of $3.$

Therefore, we can simplify our problem by first swapping the denominators:

$$



\dfrac{3}{\color{blue}9} \times \dfrac{4}{1}



$$

Next, we reduce the first fraction to its lowest terms by dividing the numerator and denominator by $3.$

$$



\begin{aligned}\frac{3÷3}{9÷3}×\frac{4}{1} & =\frac{1}{3}×\frac{4}{1}\end{aligned}



$$

To multiply two fractions, we multiply the numerators, and we multiply the denominators:

$$



\dfrac{1}{3} \times \dfrac{4}{1} = \dfrac{1 \times 4}{3 \times 1} = \dfrac{4}{3}



$$

Therefore,

$$



3\div 2\,\dfrac{1}{4} = \dfrac{4}{3}.



$$

We conclude that the missing number is ${\color{blue}{4}}.$

### Example: Dividing a Whole Number by a Mixed Number When the Result is a Mixed Number

#### Question

$5 \div 2\,\dfrac{1}{7}=$

#### Explanation

First, we write $2\,\dfrac{1}{7}$ as an improper fraction:

$$



2\,\dfrac{1}{7}=\dfrac{(2 \times 7) + 1}{7}= \dfrac{15}{7}



$$

We also write $5$ as an improper fraction:

$$



5=\dfrac{5}{1}



$$

Therefore, our problem now is to work out the following:

$$



\dfrac{5}{1}\div \dfrac{15}{7}



$$

Dividing one fraction by another is equivalent to multiplying the first fraction by the reciprocal of the second fraction.

The reciprocal of $\dfrac{\color{blue}15}{\color{red}7}$ is $\dfrac{\color{red}7}{\color{blue}15}.$ So, we have:

$$



\dfrac{5}{1} \div \dfrac{\color{blue}15}{\color{red}7} = \dfrac{5}{1} \times \dfrac{\color{red}7}{\color{blue}15}



$$

Notice that the first fraction's numerator ($5$) and the second fraction's denominator ($\color{blue}15$) have a common factor of $5.$

Therefore, we can simplify our problem by first swapping the denominators:

$$



\dfrac{5}{\color{blue}15} \times \dfrac{7}{1}



$$

Next, we reduce the first fraction to its lowest terms by dividing the numerator and denominator by $5.$

$$



\begin{aligned}\frac{5÷5}{15÷5}×\frac{7}{1} & =\frac{1}{3}×\frac{7}{1}\end{aligned}



$$

To multiply two fractions, we multiply the numerators, and we multiply the denominators:

$$



\dfrac{1}{3} \times \dfrac{7}{1} = \dfrac{1 \times 7}{3 \times 1} = \dfrac{7}{3}



$$

Finally, we write our improper fraction as a mixed number:

$$



\dfrac{7}{3} = 2\,\textrm{R}\,1 = 2\,\dfrac{1}{3}



$$

Therefore,

$$



5 \div 2\,\dfrac{1}{7}=2\,\dfrac{1}{3}.



$$
