## Lesson

### Introduction

Many real-world situations involve an unknown quantity that we can determine using an equation. By translating a real-world description into an equation, we can solve for the unknown value.

For example, a concert ticket costs a base fee of $18$ plus a service charge of $s$ dollars. If the total price of the ticket is $45,$ what is the value of $s?$

The total cost is made up of two parts:

- the base ticket fee of $18,$ and

- the service charge of $s$ dollars.

So, the total cost can be written as

$$



s + 18.



$$

Since the total ticket price is $45,$ we equate this expression to $45.$

Therefore, the equation is

$$



s + 18 = 45.



$$

To isolate $s,$ we need to perform the opposite of adding $18,$ which is subtracting $18.$

So, we subtract $18$ from both sides of the equation:

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑠+18 & =45 \\ 𝑠+18−18 & =45−18\end{aligned}



$$

Next, we simplify:

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑠+0 & =27 \\ 𝑠 & =27\end{aligned}



$$

Therefore, the service charge is $27.$

### Example: Modeling Using One-Step Addition and Subtraction Equations

#### Question

A water tank contained $w$ liters of water at the beginning of the day. After $9.6$ liters were drained, $14.7$ liters remained. What is the value of $w$?

#### Explanation

The tank started with $w$ liters of water. Then, $9.6$ liters were drained, so the amount of water remaining can be written as

$$



w - 9.6.



$$

Since $14.7$ liters remained, we equate this expression to $14.7.$

Therefore, the equation is

$$



w - 9.6 = 14.7.



$$

To isolate $w,$ we need to perform the opposite of subtracting $9.6$ from $w,$ which is adding $9.6$ to $w.$

So, we add $9.6$ to both sides of the equation:

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑤−9.6 & =14.7 \\ 𝑤−9.6+9.6 & =14.7+9.6\end{aligned}



$$

Next, we simplify:

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑤+0 & =24.3 \\ 𝑤 & =24.3\end{aligned}



$$

Therefore, the tank originally contained $24.3$ liters of water.

### Example: Modeling Using One-Step Multiplication and Division Equations

#### Question

A bakery places $6$ muffins into each box. If the bakery packs a total of $48$ muffins, how many boxes did the bakery fill?

#### Explanation

Each box holds $6$ muffins. If the bakery fills $b$ boxes, then the total number of muffins packed can be written as

$$



6b.



$$

Since the bakery packed a total of $48$ muffins, we equate this expression to $48.$

Therefore, the equation is

$$



6b = 48.



$$

To isolate $b,$ we divide both sides of the equation by $6{:}$

$$



\begin{aligned}6𝑏 & =48 \\ \frac{6𝑏}{6} & =\frac{48}{6}\end{aligned}



$$

Next, we simplify:

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑏 & =8\end{aligned}



$$

Therefore, the bakery filled $8$ boxes.

### Example: Modeling Using One-Step Equations With Fractions

#### Question

A ribbon of length $L$ meters is cut into $6$ equal pieces. If each piece is $\dfrac{2}{3}$ of a meter long, what is the length of the ribbon?

#### Explanation

If a ribbon of length $L$ meters is cut into $6$ equal pieces, then the length of each piece can be written as

$$



\dfrac{L}{6}.



$$

Since each piece is $\dfrac{2}{3}$ of a meter long, we equate this expression to $\dfrac{2}{3}.$

Therefore, the equation is

$$



\dfrac{L}{6} = \dfrac{2}{3}.



$$

To isolate $L,$ we multiply both sides of the equation by $6{:}$

$$



\begin{aligned}\frac{𝐿}{6} & =\frac{2}{3} \\ \frac{𝐿}{6}×6 & =\frac{2}{3}×6\end{aligned}



$$

Next, we simplify:

$$



\begin{aligned}𝐿 & =4\end{aligned}



$$

Therefore, the length of the ribbon is $4$ meters.
