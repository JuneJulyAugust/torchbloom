## Lesson

### Introduction

Remember the following rule for dividing fractions:

Dividing one fraction by another is equivalent to multiplying the first fraction by the reciprocal of the second fraction.

We can use this rule with mixed numbers, too. To illustrate, let's use this rule to divide two mixed numbers:

$$



1 \,\dfrac{1}{2} \div 1 \,\dfrac{3}{5}



$$

First, we write $1 \,\dfrac{1}{2}$ as an improper fraction:

$$



1 \,\dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{(1 \times 2) + 1}{2}= \dfrac{3}{2}



$$

We also write $1 \,\dfrac{3}{5}$ as an improper fraction:

$$



1 \,\dfrac{3}{5}=\dfrac{(1 \times 5) + 3}{5}= \dfrac{8}{5}



$$

Therefore, our problem now is to work out the following:

$$



\dfrac{3}{2} \div \dfrac{8}{5}.



$$

Dividing one fraction by another is equivalent to multiplying the first fraction by the reciprocal of the second fraction.

The reciprocal of $\dfrac{\color{blue}8}{\color{red}5}$ is $\dfrac{\color{red}5}{\color{blue}8}.$ So we have:

$$



\dfrac{3}{2} \div \dfrac{\color{blue}8}{\color{red}5} = \dfrac{3}{2} \times \dfrac{\color{red}5}{\color{blue}8}



$$

Now, we multiply the fractions. We multiply the numerators, and we multiply the denominators:

$$



\dfrac{3}{2} \times \dfrac{5}{8} = \dfrac {3 \times 5}{2 \times 8} = \dfrac{15}{16}



$$

Therefore,

$$



1 \,\dfrac{1}{2} \div 1 \,\dfrac{3}{5} = \dfrac{15}{16} \, .



$$

### Example: Dividing Mixed Numbers

#### Question

What is the missing digit in the following equality?

$$



0



$$

#### Explanation

First, we write $1 \,\dfrac{2}{5}$ as an improper fraction:

$$



1 \,\dfrac{2}{5} = \dfrac{(1 \times 5) + 2}{5}= \dfrac{7}{5}



$$

We also write $1 \,\dfrac{1}{2}$ as an improper fraction:

$$



1 \,\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{(1 \times 2) + 1}{2}= \dfrac{3}{2}



$$

Therefore, our problem now is to work out the following:

$$



\dfrac{7}{5} \div \dfrac{3}{2}



$$

Dividing one fraction by another is equivalent to multiplying the first fraction by the reciprocal of the second fraction.

The reciprocal of $\dfrac{\color{blue}3}{\color{red}2}$ is $\dfrac{\color{red}2}{\color{blue}3}.$ So, we have:

$$



\dfrac{7}{5} \div \dfrac{\color{blue}3}{\color{red}2} = \dfrac{7}{5} \times \dfrac{\color{red}2}{\color{blue}3}



$$

Now, we multiply the fractions. We multiply the numerators, and we multiply the denominators:

$$



\dfrac{7}{5} \times \dfrac{2}{3} = \dfrac{7 \times 2}{5 \times 3} = \dfrac{\boxed{\color{blue}14}}{15}



$$

Therefore,

$$



1 \, \dfrac{2}{5} \div 1 \, \dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{\boxed{\color{blue}14}}{15} \, .



$$

We conclude that the missing number is $14.$

### Example: Dividing Mixed Numbers When Simplification is Required

#### Question

What is the missing digit in the following equality?

$$



0



$$

#### Explanation

First, we write $1 \,\dfrac{1}{2}$ as an improper fraction:

$$



1 \,\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{(1 \times 2) + 1}{2}= \dfrac{3}{2}



$$

We also write $2 \,\dfrac{1}{4}$ as an improper fraction:

$$



2 \,\dfrac{1}{4}=\dfrac{(2 \times 4) + 1}{4}= \dfrac{9}{4}



$$

Therefore, our problem now is to work out the following:

$$



\dfrac{3}{2} \div \dfrac{9}{4}



$$

Dividing one fraction by another is equivalent to multiplying the first fraction by the reciprocal of the second fraction.

The reciprocal of $\dfrac{\color{blue}9}{\color{red}4}$ is $\dfrac{\color{red}4}{\color{blue}9}.$ So, we have:

$$



\dfrac{3}{2} \div \dfrac{\color{blue}9}{\color{red}4} = \dfrac{3}{2} \times \dfrac{\color{red}4}{\color{blue}9}



$$

Notice that the first fraction's denominator ($2$) and the second fraction's numerator ($\color{red}4$) have a common factor of $2.$

Therefore, we can simplify our problem by first swapping the denominators:

$$



\dfrac{3}{\color{blue}9} \times \dfrac{\color{red}4}{2}



$$

Next, we reduce the second fraction to its lowest terms by dividing the numerator and denominator by $2.$

$$



\begin{aligned}\frac{3}{9}×\frac{4÷2}{2÷2} & =\frac{3}{9}×\frac{2}{1}\end{aligned}



$$

Now, we reduce the first fraction to its lowest terms by dividing the numerator and denominator by $3.$

$$



\begin{aligned}\frac{3÷3}{9÷3}×\frac{2}{1} & =\frac{1}{3}×\frac{2}{1}\end{aligned}



$$

To multiply two fractions, we multiply the numerators, and we multiply the denominators:

$$



\dfrac{1}{3} \times \dfrac{2}{1} = \dfrac{1 \times 2}{3 \times 1} = \dfrac{2}{\boxed{\color{blue}3}}



$$

Therefore,

$$



1 \,\dfrac{1}{2} \div 2 \,\dfrac{1}{4} = \dfrac{2}{\boxed{\color{blue}3}}.



$$

We conclude that the missing number is $3.$

### Example: Dividing Mixed Numbers When the Result is a Mixed Number

#### Question

What is the value of $2 \, \dfrac{1}{2} \div 1 \, \dfrac{1}{6}?$

#### Explanation

First, we write $2 \,\dfrac{1}{2}$ as an improper fraction:

$$



2 \,\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{(2 \times 2) + 1}{2}= \dfrac{5}{2}



$$

We also write $1 \,\dfrac{1}{6}$ as an improper fraction:

$$



1 \,\dfrac{1}{6}=\dfrac{(1 \times 6) + 1}{6}= \dfrac{7}{6}



$$

Therefore, our problem now is to work out the following:

$$



\dfrac{5}{2} \div \dfrac{7}{6}



$$

Dividing one fraction by another is equivalent to multiplying the first fraction by the reciprocal of the second fraction.

The reciprocal of $\dfrac{\color{blue}7}{\color{red}6}$ is $\dfrac{\color{red}6}{\color{blue}7}.$ So we have:

$$



\dfrac{5}{2} \div \dfrac{\color{blue}7}{\color{red}6} = \dfrac{5}{2} \times \dfrac{\color{red}6}{\color{blue}7}



$$

Notice that the first fraction's denominator ($2$) and the second fraction's numerator ($\color{red}6$) have a common factor of $2.$

Therefore, we can simplify our problem by first swapping the denominators:

$$



\dfrac{5}{\color{blue}7} \times \dfrac{\color{red}6}{2}



$$

Next, we reduce the second fraction to its lowest terms by dividing the numerator and denominator by $2.$

$$



\begin{aligned}\frac{5}{7}×\frac{6÷2}{2÷2} & =\frac{5}{7}×\frac{3}{1}\end{aligned}



$$

To multiply two fractions, we multiply the numerators, and we multiply the denominators:

$$



\dfrac{5}{7} \times \dfrac{3}{1} = \dfrac{5 \times 3}{7 \times 1} = \dfrac{15}{7}



$$

Finally, we write our improper fraction as a mixed number:

$$



\dfrac{15}{7} = 2\,\text{R}\,1 = 2\,\dfrac{1}{7}



$$

Therefore,

$$



2 \,\dfrac{1}{2} \div 1 \,\dfrac{1}{6} = 2 \,\dfrac{1}{7}.



$$
