### Introduction

If the area of a rectangle is known and one side length is given, the missing side length can be found by dividing the area by the known side length. This idea works whether the area, the side length, or both are fractions.

For example, let's determine the missing side length in the area model below.

To find the missing side length, we divide the area by the length of the known side:

$$



\dfrac57 \div \dfrac25



$$

Dividing a fraction by another fraction is equivalent to multiplying the first fraction by the reciprocal of the second fraction.

The reciprocal of $\dfrac25$ is $\dfrac52.$ So, we have

$$



\dfrac57 \div \dfrac25 = \dfrac57 \times \dfrac52.



$$

Now, we multiply fractions by multiplying the numerators and multiplying the denominators:

$$



\dfrac57 \times \dfrac52 = \dfrac{5 \times 5}{7 \times 2} = \dfrac{25}{14}



$$

Therefore, the missing side length is $\dfrac{25}{14}.$

### Example: Cases When The Area is a Fraction

#### Question

Determine the missing side length in the area model below. Give your answer in its simplest form.

#### Explanation

We find the length of one side of the rectangle by dividing the area by the length of the other side:

$$



\dfrac{6}{7} \div 3



$$

Dividing a fraction by a whole number is equivalent to multiplying the fraction by the reciprocal of that whole number.

The reciprocal of $3$ is $\dfrac13.$ So, we have

$$



\dfrac67 \div 3 = \dfrac67 \times \dfrac13.



$$

Now, we multiply fractions. We multiply the numerators, and we multiply the denominators:

$$



\dfrac67 \times \dfrac13 = \dfrac{6 \times 1}{7 \times 3} = \dfrac6{21}



$$

Finally, we simplify our result by dividing the numerator and denominator by $3{:}$

$$



\dfrac6{21} = \dfrac{6 \div 3}{21 \div 3} = \dfrac27



$$

### Example: Cases When a Side is a Fraction

#### Question

Determine the missing side length in the area model below. Give your answer as an **** in its simplest form.

#### Explanation

We find the length of one side of the rectangle by dividing the area by the length of the other side:

$$



5 \div \dfrac23



$$

Dividing a whole number by a fraction is equivalent to multiplying the whole number by the reciprocal of the fraction.

The reciprocal of $\dfrac23$ is $\dfrac32.$ So, we have

$$



5 \div \dfrac23 = \dfrac51 \div \dfrac23 = \dfrac51 \times \dfrac32.



$$

Now, we multiply fractions. We multiply the numerators, and we multiply the denominators:

$$



\dfrac51 \times \dfrac32 = \dfrac{5 \times 3}{1 \times 2} = \dfrac{15}{2}



$$

### Example: Cases When The Area and a Side are Fractions: Integer Result

#### Question

Determine the missing side length in the area model below. Give your answer in its simplest form.

#### Explanation

We find the length of one side of the rectangle by dividing the area by the length of the other side:

$$



\dfrac{5}{6} \div \dfrac{1}{6}



$$

Dividing a fraction by another fraction is equivalent to multiplying the first fraction by the reciprocal of the second fraction.

The reciprocal of $\dfrac16$ is $\dfrac61.$ So, we have

$$



\dfrac56 \div \dfrac16 = \dfrac56 \times \dfrac61.



$$

Now, we multiply fractions. We multiply the numerators, and we multiply the denominators:

$$



\dfrac56 \times \dfrac61 = \dfrac{5 \times 6}{6 \times 1} = \dfrac{30}{6}



$$

Finally, we simplify:

$$



\dfrac{30}{6} = 30 \div 6 = 5



$$

### Example: Cases When The Area and a Side are Fractions: Fractional Result

#### Question

Determine the missing side length in the area model below. Give your answer as an **** in its simplest form.

#### Explanation

We find the length of one side of the rectangle by dividing the area by the length of the other side:

$$



\dfrac65 \div \dfrac49



$$

Dividing a fraction by another fraction is equivalent to multiplying the first fraction by the reciprocal of the second fraction.

The reciprocal of $\dfrac49$ is $\dfrac94.$ So, we have

$$



\dfrac65 \div \dfrac49 = \dfrac{\color{blue}6}{5} \times \dfrac9{\color{red}4}.



$$

Notice that the first fraction’s numerator ($\color{blue}6$) and the second fraction’s denominator ($\color{red}4$) have a common factor of $2.$

So, we can simplify our problem by first swapping the denominators:

$$



\dfrac{\color{blue}6}{\color{red}4} \times \dfrac95



$$

Next, we reduce the first fraction to its lowest terms by dividing the numerator and denominator by $2{:}$

$$



\dfrac{{\color{blue}6} \div 2}{{\color{red}4} \div 2} \times \dfrac95 = \dfrac32 \times \dfrac95



$$

Finally, we multiply fractions. We multiply the numerators, and we multiply the denominators:

$$



\dfrac32 \times \dfrac95 = \dfrac{3 \times 9}{2 \times 5} = \dfrac{27}{10}



$$
