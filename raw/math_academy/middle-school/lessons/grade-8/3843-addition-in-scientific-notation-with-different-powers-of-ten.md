## Lesson

### Introduction

In this lesson, we'll learn how to add numbers in scientific notation with different powers of ten.

For example, let's compute the following sum:

$$


(4\times 10^{\color{blue}{5}}) + (2 \times 10^{\color{red}{4}})


$$

Notice that the two numbers in our sum have different powers of ten (${\color{blue}{5}}$ and ${\color{red}{4}},$ respectively).

To add these two numbers, we use the following steps:

- **Step 1**: Identify the smaller number in the sum.

- **Step 2**: Rewrite the smaller number to have the same power of ten as the larger number.

- **Step 3**: Add the two numbers as usual.

In our sum, the *second* number $(2\times 10^{\color{red}{4}})$ is the smaller of the two because it has the smallest power of ten.

So, we rewrite this number so that its power of ten is ${\color{blue}{5}},$ as follows:

$$


\begin{aligned}2×10^{4} & = \\ 2×10^{5−1} & = \\ 2×10^{−1+5} & = \\ 2×10^{−1}×10^{5} & = \\ 0.2\,×10^{5} & \end{aligned}


$$

Now, carrying out the addition, we get

$$


\begin{aligned}(4×10^{5})+(2×10^{4}) & = \\ (4×10^{5})+(0.2×10^{5}) & = \\ (4+0.2)×10^{5} & = \\ 4.2×10^{5}. & \end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Adding Numbers With Different Positive Powers of Ten

#### Question

Express $\left(2.4 \times 10^{7}\right) + \left(3.7 \times 10^{8} \right)$ in scientific notation.

#### Explanation

The first number is the smaller of the two, so we rewrite it to have the same power of $10$ as the first number, as follows:

$$


\begin{aligned}2.4×10^{7} & = \\ 2.4×10^{8−1} & = \\ 2.4×10^{−1+8} & = \\ 2.4×10^{−1}×10^{8} & = \\ 0.24×10^{8} & \end{aligned}


$$

Now, carrying out the addition, we get

$$


\begin{aligned}(2.4×10^{7})+(3.7×10^{8}) & = \\ (0.24×10^{8})+(3.7×10^{8}) & = \\ (0.24\,+3.7)×10^{8} & = \\ 3.94\,×10^{8}. & \end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Adding Numbers With Different Negative Powers of Ten

#### Question

Express $\left(3 \times 10^{-4}\right) + \left(5 \times 10^{-3} \right)$ in scientific notation.

#### Explanation

The first number is the smaller of the two, so we rewrite it to have the same power of $10$ as the second number, as follows:

$$


\begin{aligned}3×10^{−4} & = \\ 3×10^{−3−1} & = \\ 3×10^{−1−3} & = \\ 3×10^{−1}×10^{−3} & = \\ 0.3×10^{−3} & \end{aligned}


$$

Now, carrying out the addition, we get

$$


\begin{aligned}(3×10^{−4})+(5×10^{−3}) & = \\ (0.3\,×10^{−3})+(5\,×10^{−3}) & = \\ (0.3+5)×10^{−3} & = \\ 5.3\,×10^{−3}. & \end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Adding Numbers Where the Powers Differ by More Than One

#### Question

Express $\left(7 \times 10^{10} \right) + \left(2\times 10^{12} \right)$ in scientific notation.

#### Explanation

The first number is the smaller of the two, so we rewrite it to have the same power of $10$ as the second number, as follows:

$$


\begin{aligned}7×10^{10} & = \\ 7×10^{12−2} & = \\ 7×10^{−2+12} & = \\ 7×10^{−2}×10^{12} & = \\ 0.07×10^{12} & \end{aligned}


$$

Now, carrying out the addition, we get

$$


\begin{aligned}(7×10^{10})+(2×10^{12}) & = \\ (0.07\,×10^{12})+(2\,×10^{12}) & = \\ (0.07+2)×10^{12} & \\ 2.07\,×10^{12}. & \end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Word Problems

#### Question

Astronomers observe the motion of Mars' two moons, Phobos and Deimos, and use these observations to approximate their masses. The mass of Phobos is approximately $1.07\times 10^{16}\,\text{kg},$ while the mass of Deimos is approximately $1.5 \times 10^{15}\,\text{kg}.$ What is the total mass of Mars' two moons?

#### Explanation

We need to write the following expression in scientific notation:

$$


1.07 \times 10^{16} + 1.5 \times 10^{15}


$$

The second number is the smaller of the two, so we rewrite it to have the same power of $10$ as the first number, as follows:

$$


\begin{aligned}1.5×10^{15} & = \\ 1.5×10^{16−1} & = \\ 1.5×10^{−1+16} & = \\ 1.5×10^{−1}×10^{16} & = \\ 0.15×10^{16} & \end{aligned}


$$

Now, carrying out the addition, we get

$$


\begin{aligned}(1.07×10^{16})+(1.5×10^{15}) & = \\ (1.07×10^{16})+(0.15×10^{16}) & = \\ (1.07+0.15)×10^{16} & = \\ 1.22×10^{16}. & \end{aligned}


$$

Therefore, the total mass of the two moons is $1.22 \times 10^{16} \,\text{kg}.$
