## Lesson

### Introduction

Sometimes, when we are choosing an appropriate unit, we are not given any formula to use.

This often occurs when we measure the **rate of change** of something. A rate of change measures how much something changes as we change something else.

In such cases, we can use a quotient to work out an appropriate unit. The numerator is what we measure the change of, and the denominator is what we change:

$$


\dfrac{\color{blue}\text{what we measure the change of}}{\color{red}\text{what we change}}


$$

For instance, suppose that a newly opened store wants to measure the rate at which the number of customers increases each day.

We *measure the change* of the number of customers, as *we change* the number of days, so the most appropriate unit is

$$


\dfrac{\color{blue}\text{customers}}{\color{red}\text{day}},


$$

or, customers per day.

### Example: Choosing Appropriate Units When Base Units Are Not Required

#### Question

A tank is being filled with water. What is an appropriate unit for measuring the rate of change of water volume in the tank, as time goes on?

#### Explanation

We ** of the volume as ** the amount of time. Consequently, any appropriate unit for measuring the rate of change of water volume in the tank must take the form

$$


\dfrac{\text{unit of volume}}{\text{unit of time}}.


$$

Some examples of appropriate units include $\dfrac{\text{liters}}{\text{hour}},$ $\dfrac{\text{m}^3}{\text{min}},$ and $\dfrac{\text{cm}^3}{\text{s}}.$

### Example: Choosing Appropriate Units When Base Units Are Required

#### Question

Density $D$ is the rate that mass $M$ changes as volume $V$ changes. What are the units of density in terms of base units?

#### Explanation

We ** in the mass, as ** the volume. Consequently, any appropriate unit must take the form

$$


\dfrac{\text{unit of mass}}{\text{unit of volume}}.


$$

The base unit for mass is $\text{kg},$ and the base unit for volume is $\text{m}^3.$ Therefore, the units of density are

$$


\dfrac{\text{kg}}{\text{m}^3},


$$

or, kilograms per cubic meter.
