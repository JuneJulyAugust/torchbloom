## Lesson

### Introduction

If we're given a number in scientific notation and we want to convert it back to **standard notation**, all we need to do is evaluate the expression.

To illustrate, let's convert the number $3.25\times 10^{3}$ back to standard notation.

Notice that the decimal part $3.25$ is multiplied by $10^3,$ which is a power of $10.$ So, to multiply $3.25$ by $10^3,$ we take the decimal part and move the decimal point $3$ places to the right.

Therefore,

$$

3.25 \times 10^{3} = 3.25 \times 1\,000 = 3\,250.

$$

**Note:** In this example, the exponent in the power of $10$ was $3,$ which is positive. When the exponent in the power of $10$ is negative, we need to move the decimal point to the left when converting from scientific notation to standard notation.

Let's see a few more examples.

### Example: Expressing a Number Given in Scientific Notation With a Positive Exponent in Standard Notation

#### Question

Write the number $5.17 \times 10^{4}$ in standard notation.

#### Explanation

To change a number from scientific notation to standard notation, we take the decimal part and move the decimal point to the left or right, depending on the sign of the power of $10.$

In this case, the power of $10$ is $4,$ which is positive. So, we move the decimal point $4$ places to the right. Therefore,

$$

5.17 \times 10^{4} = 51\,700.

$$

### Example: Expressing a Number Given in Scientific Notation With a Negative Exponent in Standard Notation

#### Question

Write the number $5.87 \times 10^{-5}$ in standard notation.

#### Explanation

To change a number from scientific notation to standard notation, we move the decimal point to the left or right, depending on the sign of the power of $10.$

In this case, the power of $10$ is $-5,$ which is negative. So, we move the decimal point $5$ places to the left. Therefore,

$$

5.87 \times 10^{-5}= 0.000\,058\,7.

$$

### Example: Expressing Negative Numbers in Standard Notation

#### Question

Write the number $-2.43 \times 10^{4}$ in standard notation.

#### Explanation

To change a number from scientific notation to standard notation, we move the decimal point to the left or right, depending on the sign of the power of $10.$

In this case, the power of $10$ is $4,$ which is positive. So, we move the decimal point $4$ places to the right. Therefore,

$$

- 2.43 \times 10^{4} = -24\,300.

$$

### Example: Converting to Standard Notation: Word Problems

#### Question

Red blood cells are disk-shaped cells that are found in our blood and are responsible for transferring oxygen to different parts of the body. A typical red blood cell has a diameter of around $7 \times 10^{-6}$ $\textrm{m}.$ What is this length in standard notation?

#### Explanation

To change a number from scientific notation to standard notation, we take the decimal part and move the decimal point to the left or right, depending on the sign of the power of $10.$

In this case, the power of $10$ is $-6,$ which is negative. So, we move the decimal point $6$ places to the left:

$$

7 \times 10^{-6} = 0.000\,007

$$

Therefore, the diameter of a red blood cell is around $0.000\,007$ $\textrm{m}$.
