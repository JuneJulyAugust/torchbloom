## Lesson

### Introduction

If we have rational expressions on both sides of an equation, say,

$$

\dfrac{{\color{red}x-1}}{{\color{blue}2}} = \dfrac{{\color{blue}2x-1}}{{\color{red}5}},

$$

then we can eliminate the rational expressions by applying **cross-multiplication**:

- we cross-multiply the left-hand side numerator $({\color{red}x-1})$ with the right-hand side denominator $({\color{red}5}),$ and

- we cross-multiply the left-hand side denominator $({\color{blue}2})$ with the right-hand side numerator $({\color{blue}2x-1}).$

Doing this, we get the following equation:

$$

({\color{red}x-1}) \cdot ({\color{red}5}) = ({\color{blue}2}) \cdot ({\color{blue}2x - 1})

$$

After we've cross-multiplied, we proceed as usual to solve the equation:

$$

\begin{aligned}(𝑥−1)⋅5 & =2⋅(2𝑥−1) \\ 5𝑥−5 & =4𝑥−2 \\ 5𝑥−5+5 & =4𝑥−2+5 \\ 5𝑥 & =4𝑥+3 \\ 5𝑥−4𝑥 & =4𝑥+3−4𝑥 \\ 𝑥 & =3\end{aligned}

$$

**Note:** Cross-multiplication might seem magical, but it is really just a shortcut for multiplying both sides of the equation by each denominator:

$$

\begin{aligned}\frac{𝑥−1}{2} & =\frac{2𝑥−1}{5} \\ 2⋅\frac{𝑥−1}{2} & =2⋅\frac{2𝑥−1}{5} \\ 2⋅\frac{𝑥−1}{2} & =2⋅\frac{2𝑥−1}{5} \\ 𝑥−1 & =2⋅\frac{2𝑥−1}{5} \\ (𝑥−1)⋅5 & =2⋅\frac{2𝑥−1}{5}⋅5 \\ (𝑥−1)⋅5 & =2⋅\frac{2𝑥−1}{5}⋅5 \\ (𝑥−1)⋅(5) & =(2)⋅(2𝑥−1)\end{aligned}

$$

### Example: Cross-Multiplying with One Binomial

#### Question

Solve the equation $\dfrac{2x+5}{30} = \dfrac{1}{2}.$

#### Explanation

First, we cross-multiply:

$$

\begin{aligned}\frac{2𝑥+5}{30} & =\frac{1}{2} \\ (2𝑥+5)⋅2 & =30⋅1\end{aligned}

$$

Then, we proceed as usual to solve the equation:

$$

\begin{aligned}(2𝑥+5)⋅2 & =30⋅1 \\ 4𝑥+10 & =30 \\ 4𝑥+10−10 & =30−10 \\ 4𝑥 & =20 \\ \frac{4𝑥}{4} & =\frac{20}{4} \\ 𝑥 & =5\end{aligned}

$$

### Example: Cross-Multiplying with Two Binomials

#### Question

Solve for $x$, where $\dfrac{6-2x}{2} = \dfrac{6-x}{3}.$

#### Explanation

First, we cross-multiply:

$$

\begin{aligned}\frac{6−2𝑥}{2} & =\frac{6−𝑥}{3} \\ (6−2𝑥)⋅3 & =2⋅(6−𝑥)\end{aligned}

$$

Then, we proceed as usual to solve the equation:

$$

\begin{aligned}(6−2𝑥)⋅3 & =2⋅(6−𝑥) \\ 18−6𝑥 & =12−2𝑥 \\ 18−6𝑥−18 & =12−2𝑥−18 \\ −6𝑥 & =−2𝑥−6 \\ −6𝑥+2𝑥 & =−2𝑥−6+2𝑥 \\ −4𝑥 & =−6 \\ \frac{−4𝑥}{−4} & =\frac{−6}{−4} \\ 𝑥 & =\frac{6}{4} \\ 𝑥 & =\frac{3}{2}\end{aligned}

$$

### Cross-Multiplying with a Negative Sign

Consider the following equation:

$$

\dfrac{x+1}{2} = -\dfrac{x}{3}

$$

Even though one of the fractions is negative, we can still use cross-multiplication. All we have to do is transfer the negative into the numerator of the fraction, and proceed with cross-multiplication:

$$

\begin{aligned}\frac{𝑥+1}{2} & =−\frac{𝑥}{3} \\ \frac{𝑥+1}{2} & =\frac{−𝑥}{3} \\ (𝑥+1)⋅3 & =2⋅(−𝑥)\end{aligned}

$$

Then, we proceed as usual to solve the equation:

$$

\begin{aligned}(𝑥+1)⋅3 & =2⋅(−𝑥) \\ 3𝑥+3 & =−2𝑥 \\ 3𝑥+3−3𝑥 & =−2𝑥−3𝑥 \\ 3 & =−5𝑥 \\ \frac{3}{−5} & =\frac{−5𝑥}{−5} \\ −\frac{3}{5} & =𝑥\end{aligned}

$$

Therefore, $x = -\dfrac{3}{5}.$

### Example: Cross-Multiplying with a Negative Sign

#### Question

Solve for $u$, where $\dfrac{u+2}{3} = - \dfrac{3-u}{2}.$

#### Explanation

We transfer the negative into the numerator of the fraction, and proceed with cross-multiplication:

$$

\begin{aligned}\frac{𝑢+2}{3} & =−\frac{3−𝑢}{2} \\ \frac{𝑢+2}{3} & =\frac{−(3−𝑢)}{2} \\ \frac{𝑢+2}{3} & =\frac{−3+𝑢}{2} \\ (𝑢+2)⋅2 & =3⋅(−3+𝑢)\end{aligned}

$$

Then, we proceed as usual to solve the equation:

$$

\begin{aligned}(𝑢+2)⋅2 & =3⋅(−3+𝑢) \\ 2𝑢+4 & =−9+3𝑢 \\ 2𝑢+4−2𝑢 & =−9+3𝑢−2𝑢 \\ 4 & =−9+𝑢 \\ 4+9 & =−9+𝑢+9 \\ 13 & =𝑢\end{aligned}

$$

Therefore, $u=13.$
