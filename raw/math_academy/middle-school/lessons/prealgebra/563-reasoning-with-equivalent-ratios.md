## Lesson

### Introduction

Sometimes, we are given a ratio and want to find an amount. In these cases, it's helpful to use equivalent fractions.

For example, if the ratio of cows to sheep on Margaret's farm is $11:7$, and she has $22$ cows, how many sheep are there on the farm?

To solve this, we start with the ratio ${\color{blue}11}:{\color{red}7}$ and write it as a fraction:

$$

\dfrac{\color{blue}11}{\color{red}7}

$$

In this fraction, the numerator represents the number of cows and the denominator represents the number of sheep.

Since there are $22$ cows on the farm, we would like a numerator of $22.$ We make this by multiplying the numerator and denominator by $2\mathbin{:}$

$$

\dfrac{{\color{blue}11}\times 2}{{\color{red}7}\times 2} = \dfrac{{\color{blue}22}}{{\color{red}14}}

$$

The denominator is now the number of sheep on the farm if there are $22$ cows. Therefore, on Margaret's farm, there are $\color{red}14$ sheep.

### Example: Using a Ratio to Compute a Denominator

#### Question

Adam has a cedar tree and willow tree in his garden. The ratio of the height of the cedar tree to the height of a willow tree is $8:5.$ If the height of the cedar tree is $24$ meters, what is the height of the willow tree?

#### Explanation

We start with the ratio ${\color{blue}8}:{\color{red}5}$ and write it as a fraction:

$$

\dfrac{{\color{blue}8}}{{\color{red}5}}

$$

In this fraction, the numerator represents the height of the cedar tree and the denominator represents the height of the willow tree.

We can make a numerator of $24$ by multiplying the numerator and denominator by $3$:

$$

\dfrac{{\color{blue}8}\times 3}{{\color{red}5}\times 3} = \dfrac{{\color{blue}24}}{{\color{red}15}}

$$

Therefore, if the height of the cedar tree is $24$ meters, the height of the willow tree is $15$ meters.

### Example: Using a Ratio to Compute a Numerator

#### Question

The ratio of sand to crushed stone in concrete is $\dfrac{3}{2}.$ If Daniel has $80$ kilograms of crushed stone available to make concrete, what amount of sand should he use?

#### Explanation

We are given that the ratio of sand to crushed stone in concrete is $\dfrac{3}{2}.$

In this fraction, the numerator represents the amount of sand and the denominator represents the amount of crushed stone.

We can make a denominator of $80$ by multiplying the numerator and denominator by $40\mathbin{:}$

$$

\dfrac{{\color{blue}3}\times 40}{{\color{red}2}\times 40} = \dfrac{{\color{blue}120}}{{\color{red}80}}

$$

Therefore, for $80$ kilograms of crushed stone, Daniel must use $120$ kilograms of sand.
