## Lesson

### Introduction

Suppose that we want to solve the equation

$$

\dfrac{2}{3}x = 8.

$$

This equation has a **fractional coefficient** of $\dfrac{2}{3}.$ The term $\dfrac{2}{3}x$ means that $x$ is being multiplied by $2$ and divided by $3.$

We can solve this problem by applying the multiplication principle, twice. First, we multiply both sides by ${\color{blue}{3}}$ to get rid of the fraction:

$$

\begin{aligned} \dfrac{2}{3}x &= 8 \\[5pt] {\color{blue}{3}}\cdot \dfrac{2}{3}x &={\color{blue}{3}}\cdot 8 \\[5pt] \color{blue}{3} \cdot \dfrac{2}{3}x &={\color{blue}{3}}\cdot 8 \\2x &=24 \end{aligned}

$$

Then, we divide both sides by ${\color{red}{2}}$ to isolate $x\mathbin{:}$

$$

\begin{aligned} 2x &=24\\[5pt] \dfrac{2x}{{\color{red}{2}}} &=\dfrac{24}{{\color{red}{2}}} \\[5pt] \dfrac{2x}{\color{red}{2}} &=\dfrac{24}{{\color{red}{2}}} \\x &=12 \end{aligned}

$$

### Example: Solving One-Step Linear Equations Without the Reciprocal

#### Question

Solve for $x,$ if $-\dfrac{3}{4}x = 12.$

#### Explanation

First, we multiply both sides by $\color{blue}4$ to get rid of the fraction:

$$

\begin{aligned}−\frac{3}{4}𝑥 & =12 \\ 4⋅(−\frac{3}{4}𝑥) & =4⋅12 \\ 4⋅(−\frac{3}{4}𝑥) & =48 \\ −3𝑥 & =48\end{aligned}

$$

Then, we divide both sides of the equation by ${\color{red}-3}$ to isolate $x\mathbin{:}$

$$

\begin{aligned}−3𝑥 & =48 \\ \frac{−3𝑥}{−3} & =\frac{48}{−3} \\ \frac{−3𝑥}{−3} & =−16 \\ 𝑥 & =−16\end{aligned}

$$

### Using the Reciprocal

When solving equations with fractional coefficients, there is a shortcut! We can complete division and multiplication in a single step by multiplying both sides of the equation by the reciprocal of the fractional coefficient.

For example, to solve the equation $\dfrac{2x}{5}= 6,$ we first express the fraction as a coefficient:

$$

 \begin{aligned} \dfrac{2}{5}x &= 6 \end{aligned}

$$

The fractional coefficient is $\dfrac{2}{5}$, and its reciprocal is $\left(\dfrac 2 5\right)^{-1} = {\color{blue}{\dfrac 5 2}}.$ Because the product of a fraction and its reciprocal is equal to $1,$ we can multiply both sides of the equation by the reciprocal to isolate $x\mathbin{:}$

$$

 \begin{aligned} \dfrac{2}{5}x &= 6 \\[5pt] \underbrace{ {\color{blue}{\dfrac 5 2}} \cdot \dfrac{2}{5} }_{\large =\, 1} x &= {\color{blue}{\dfrac 5 2}} \cdot 6 \\[5pt] 1 x &= \dfrac{5\cdot 6}{2} \\[5pt] x &= \dfrac{30}{2} \\[5pt] x &= 15 \end{aligned}

$$

### Example: Solving One-Step Linear Equations With the Reciprocal

#### Question

If $-\dfrac{3}{2}t = 24$, then what is the value of $t?$

#### Explanation

We multiply both sides of the equation by the reciprocal of $-\dfrac{3}{2},$ which is

$$

\left( -\dfrac{3}{2} \right)^{-1} = -\dfrac{2}{3}.

$$

This gives

$$

\begin{aligned}\begin{aligned}−\frac{3}{2}𝑡 & =24 \\ −\frac{2}{3}⋅(−\frac{3}{2}𝑡) & =−\frac{2}{3}⋅24 \\ 1𝑡 & =−\frac{2}{3}⋅3⋅8 \\ 𝑡 & =−16.\end{aligned}\end{aligned}

$$

### Example: Solving Two-Step Linear Equations With the Reciprocal

#### Question

Solve the equation $\dfrac{3x}{4}-5 = -8.$

#### Explanation

First, we isolate the fractional term $\dfrac{3x}{4}$ by adding $5$ to both sides:

$$

\begin{aligned} \dfrac{3x}{4}-5 &=-8\\[5pt] \dfrac{3x}{4}-5+5 &=-8+5\\[5pt] \dfrac{3x}{4}+0 &=-3 \\[5pt] \dfrac{3x}{4} &=-3 \end{aligned}

$$

The fractional coefficient is $\dfrac{3}{4},$ so we multiply by its reciprocal $\left(\dfrac{3}{4} \right)^{-1} = \dfrac{4}{3}.$ This gives

$$

\begin{aligned} \dfrac{3}{4}x &=-3 \\[5pt] \dfrac{4}{3} \cdot \dfrac{3}{4}x &= \dfrac{4}{3} \cdot (-3) \\[5pt] 1 x &= - \dfrac{4}{3 } \cdot 3 \\[5pt] x &= -4. \end{aligned}

$$
