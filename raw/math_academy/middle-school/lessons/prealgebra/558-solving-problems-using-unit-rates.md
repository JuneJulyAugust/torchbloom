### Introduction

Unit rates can be used to solve many real-world problems in which we are given two quantities and wish to find what happens to one quantity when we vary the other.

For example, we might know the total cost of a specific number of items and be asked to find the cost for a different number of the same item.

To solve a problem using unit rates, the first step is to compute the unit rate by dividing the two given quantities. Then, we can use the unit rate to calculate the desired quantity.

Let's see some examples!

### Example: Problem Solving Using the Ratio of Two Integers

#### Question

If a machine at a factory takes $6$ hours to complete $2$ batches of refractory bricks, how many hours does it take to complete $10$ batches?

#### Explanation

To find the number of hours it takes the machine to complete $10$ batches of refractory bricks, we first find the unit rate of hours per batch and then multiply the unit rate by $10$ batches.

To find the unit rate of hours per batch, we divide the number of hours, $6,$ by the number of batches, $2,$ as follows:

$$

\begin{aligned}6÷2 & =3\end{aligned}

$$

So, the unit rate is $3$ hours per batch. To find the number of hours it takes the machine to complete $10$ batches, we multiply the unit rate by $10$ batches and get

$$

\begin{aligned}3×10 & =30.\end{aligned}

$$

Therefore, it takes the machine $30$ hours to complete $10$ batches.

### Example: Problem Solving Using the Ratio of a Fraction and an Integer

#### Question

At a certain sports center, there are $5$ equal-size pools. If a pump can empty $\dfrac{3}{2}$ of a pool in $15$ minutes, then how many minutes will it take the pump to empty all $5$ pools?

#### Explanation

To find the number of minutes that the pump takes to empty $5$ pools, we can first find the unit rate of minutes per pool, and then multiply the unit rate by $5$ pools.

To find the unit rate of minutes per pool, we divide the number of minutes, $15,$ by the total pool volume emptied, $\dfrac{3}{2},$ as follows:

$$

15 \div \dfrac{3}{2}

$$

To divide by a fraction, we multiply by the fraction's reciprocal:

$$

\begin{aligned}15÷\frac{3}{2} & =15×\frac{2}{3} \\ & =\frac{15}{1}×\frac{2}{3} \\ & =\frac{15×2}{1×3} \\ & =\frac{30}{3} \\ & =30÷3 \\ & =10\end{aligned}

$$

So, the unit rate is $10$ minutes per pool. To find the number of minutes that the pump takes to empty $5$ pools, we multiply the unit rate by $5$ pools and get

$$

\begin{aligned}10×5 & =50.\end{aligned}

$$

Therefore, the pump will take $50$ minutes to empty $5$ pools.

### Example: Problem Solving Using the Ratio of Two Fractions

#### Question

Ava works in a coffee store. She earns a bonus of $\dfrac 3 4$ of a dollar for each $\dfrac 3 8$ liters of coffee sold. How much of a bonus will Ava earn if she sells $18$ liters of coffee?

#### Explanation

To find the number of dollars that Ava earns if she sells $18$ liters of coffee, we can first find the unit rate of dollars per liter, and then multiply the unit rate by $18$ liters.

To find the unit rate of dollars per liter, we divide the number of dollars, $\dfrac{3}{4},$ by the number of liters, $\dfrac{3}{8},$ as follows:

$$

\dfrac{3}{4} \div \dfrac{3}{8}

$$

Dividing one fraction by another is equivalent to multiplying the fraction by the reciprocal of the second fraction:

$$

\begin{aligned}\frac{3}{4}÷\frac{3}{8} & =\frac{3}{4}×\frac{8}{3} \\ & =\frac{3×8}{4×3} \\ & =\frac{24}{12} \\ & =24÷12 \\ & =2\end{aligned}

$$

So, the unit rate is $2$ dollars per liter. To find the number of dollars that Ava earns selling $18$ liters, we multiply the unit rate by $18$ liters and get

$$

\begin{aligned}2×18 & =36.\end{aligned}

$$

Therefore, Ava earns $36$ dollars if she sells $18$ liters of coffee.
