## Lesson

### Introduction

A **unit rate** is a ratio of two quantities stated as some amount of one quantity per unit of the other quantity.

For example, suppose that Carl can run $100$ meters in $20$ seconds. The unit rate is how many meters Carl can run in $1$ second. To compute Carl's unit rate, we divide the number of meters by the number of seconds:

$$



100 \div 20 = 5



$$

So, the unit rate is $5$ meters per second. In other words, Carl can run $5$ meters in $1$ second.

Unit rates do not always come out to whole numbers. For example, suppose Jamie can run $100$ meters in $15$ seconds. To compute Jamie's unit rate, we again divide the number of meters by the number of seconds:

$$



\begin{aligned}100÷15 & =\frac{100}{15} \\ & =\frac{100÷5}{15÷5} \\ & =\frac{20}{3}\end{aligned}



$$

Writing the resulting improper fraction as a mixed number, we get

$$



\dfrac{20}{3} = 6 \, \text{R} 2 = 6 \, \dfrac{2}{3}.



$$

Therefore, Jamie can run $6 \, \dfrac{2}{3}$ meters per second.

### Example: Calculating a Unit Rate as the Quotient of an Integer and a Fraction

#### Question

Jamie's family drove $15$ miles on $\dfrac{3}{4}$ of a gallon of gas. What is the unit rate of miles per gallon of gas?

#### Explanation

To compute the unit rate of miles per gallon of gas, we divide the number of miles, $15,$ by the number of gallons of gas, $\dfrac{3}{4},$ as follows:

$$



15 \div \dfrac{3}{4}



$$

To divide by a fraction, we multiply by the fraction's reciprocal:

$$



\begin{aligned}15÷\frac{3}{4} & =15×\frac{4}{3} \\ & =\frac{15}{1}×\frac{4}{3} \\ & =\frac{15×4}{1×3} \\ & =\frac{60}{3} \\ & =20\end{aligned}



$$

Therefore, the unit rate is $20$ miles per gallon of gas.

### Example: Calculating a Unit Rate as the Quotient of a Fraction and an Integer

#### Question

Bill measured $\dfrac{1}{3}$ tablespoon of olive oil and found that it weighed $4$ grams. How many tablespoons is $1$ gram of olive oil?

#### Explanation

To find the number of tablespoons in $1$ gram of olive oil, we can compute the unit rate of tablespoons per gram olive oil. We do this by dividing the number of tablespoons, $\dfrac{1}{3},$ by the number of grams, $4,$ as follows:

$$



\dfrac{1}{3} \div 4



$$

Dividing a fraction by a whole number is equivalent to multiplying the fraction by the reciprocal of that whole number:

$$



\begin{aligned}\frac{1}{3}÷4 & =\frac{1}{3}×\frac{1}{4} \\ & =\frac{1×1}{3×4} \\ & =\frac{1}{12}\end{aligned}



$$

Therefore, there is $\dfrac{1}{12}$ of a tablespoon in $1$ gram of olive oil.

### Example: Calculating a Unit Rate as the Quotient of a Fraction and a Fraction

#### Question

Martha swam $\dfrac{3}{4}$ of a mile in $\dfrac{2}{3}$ of an hour. How many miles can Martha swim in $1$ hour?

#### Explanation

To find the number of miles that Martha can swim in $1$ hour, we can compute the unit rate of miles per hour. We do this by dividing the number of miles, $\dfrac{3}{4},$ by the number of hours, $\dfrac{2}{3},$ as follows:

$$



\dfrac{3}{4} \div \dfrac{2}{3}



$$

Dividing a fraction by another fraction is equivalent to multiplying the first fraction by the reciprocal of the second fraction:

$$



\begin{aligned}\frac{3}{4}÷\frac{2}{3} & =\frac{3}{4}×\frac{3}{2} \\ & =\frac{3×3}{4×2} \\ & =\frac{9}{8}\end{aligned}



$$

Finally, we write the resulting improper fraction as a mixed number:

$$



\dfrac{9}{8} = 1 \, \text{R} 1 = 1 \, \dfrac{1}{8}



$$

Therefore, Martha can swim $1 \, \dfrac{1}{8}$ miles in $1$ hour.
