### Introduction

When we compare prices, it is often helpful to know the *unit price*. Knowing it helps us:

- compare different products,

- determine the total cost for a given amount,

- decide which option is the better deal.

For example, suppose Lena wants to buy some apples. The price of apples at a nearby market is $1.20/\mathrm{lb}.$ How much would Lena pay for $4$ pounds of apples?

The unit price is $1.20$ per pound.

Since Lena is buying $4$ pounds, we multiply the unit price by the number of pounds:

$$



\begin{aligned}1.20×4 & =4.80\end{aligned}



$$

Therefore, Lena would pay $4.80$ for $4$ pounds of apples.

Whenever we know the unit price and the quantity, we can find the total cost by multiplying:

$$



\text{Total Cost} = \text{Unit Price} \times \text{Quantity}.



$$

### Example: Calculating a Total Price from a Unit Price

#### Question

Micah is going to the store to buy some sunflower seeds. How much would it cost to buy $\dfrac{1}{4}$ of a pound of sunflower seeds?

#### Explanation

The price of sunflower seeds is $ 5 / \text{lb}.$ To find the cost of $\dfrac{1}{4}$ of a pound of sunflower seeds, we multiply:

$$



\begin{aligned}5×\frac{1}{4} & =\frac{5}{1}×\frac{1}{4} \\ & =\frac{5×1}{1×4} \\ & =\frac{5}{4} \\ & =5÷4 \\ & =1.25\end{aligned}



$$

Therefore, it would cost $ 1.25$ to buy $\dfrac{1}{4}$ of a pound of sunflower seeds.

### Determining the Best Deal by Computing Unit Prices

Unit prices can be used to quantify how good of a deal a particular purchase is.

For example, suppose that a store sells two packs of shirts:

- $3$ shirts for $12$

- $5$ shirts for $15$

To determine which pack is the better deal, we can compare their unit prices.

- For the pack of $3$ shirts for $12,$ dividing the total cost by the total number of shirts gives So, the unit price is $4.$

- For the pack of $5$ shirts for $15,$ dividing the total cost by the total number of shirts gives So, the unit price is $3.$

Now we interpret the unit prices in the context of the problem.

- In the pack of $3$ shirts for $12,$ each shirt costs $4.$

- In the pack of $5$ shirts for $15,$ each shirt costs $3.$

Based on the above, we conclude that the pack of $5$ shirts for $15$ is the better deal because it has a lower unit price (meaning that each shirt costs less).

### Example: Determining the Best Deal by Computing Unit Prices

#### Question

At the store, there are three different boxes of corn flakes cereal.

- Box $A$ contains $18$ ounces of cereal, and costs $ 3.60 \,.$

- Box $B$ contains $15$ ounces of cereal, and costs $ 3.30 \,.$

- Box $C$ contains $20$ ounces of cereal, and costs $ 3.80 \,.$

Which box of cereal is the best deal?

#### Explanation

The best deal is the box with the lowest unit price. So, we need to compute the unit prices for all three boxes. To compute the unit price, we divide the price by the total number of ounces.

- Dividing the total cost by the number of ounces in box $A,$ we get So, the unit price of box $A$ is $ 0.20.$

- Dividing the total cost by the number of ounces in box $B,$ we get So, the unit price of box $B$ is $ 0.22.$

- Dividing the total cost by the number of ounces in box $C,$ we get So, the unit price of box $C$ is $ 0.19.$

From the above, we see that box $C$ has the lowest unit price. Therefore, box $C$ is the best deal.

### Reciprocal Unit Prices

For every unit price in "dollars per item", there is also a **reciprocal unit price** in "items per dollar".

For example, suppose that a store sells fabric for $2$ per yard. Taking the reciprocal, we have

$$



2 \,\text{dollars per yard} \quad\rightarrow\quad \dfrac{1}{2} \,\text{yards per dollar} .



$$

So, if we have $1,$ then we can buy $\dfrac{1}{2}$ of a yard of fabric.

### Example: Working With a Reciprocal Unit Price

#### Question

Andy sells homemade lemonade for $ 4$ per liter. Beth wants to buy some lemonade from Andy, but she only has $ 1.$ How much lemonade can Beth buy?

#### Explanation

We are given the unit rate of $ 4$ per liter (dollars per liter). To find the amount of lemonade that Beth can buy for $ 1,$ we need to find the unit rate in liters per dollar. So, we take the reciprocal:

$$



4 \,\text{dollars per liter} \quad\rightarrow\quad \dfrac{1}{4} \,\text{liters per dollar}



$$

So, the unit rate is $\dfrac{1}{4}$ liters per dollar. Therefore, Beth can buy $\dfrac{1}{4}$ of a liter of lemonade.
