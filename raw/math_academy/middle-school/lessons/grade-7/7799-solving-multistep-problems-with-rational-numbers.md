## Lesson

### Introduction

In this lesson, we will solve multistep problems with rational numbers. Sometimes, before we evaluate an expression, it helps to rewrite a fraction as a decimal or rewrite a decimal as a fraction.

For example, let's rewrite the expression below so that both numbers are in decimal form before evaluating.

$$



\dfrac{7}{10} + 0.6



$$

We want to rewrite the fraction as a decimal.

In our case, $\dfrac{7}{10}$ means $7 \div 10.$ So, we compute

$$



7 \div 10 = 0.7.



$$

Therefore, the expression can be rewritten as

$$



\dfrac{7}{10} + 0.6 = 0.7 + 0.6.



$$

Now, we can evaluate the expression:

$$



0.7 + 0.6 = 1.3.



$$

Let's see more examples.

### Example: Rewriting Rational Numbers in Equivalent Forms

#### Question

Rewrite the expression below so that both numbers are in fraction form before evaluating.

$$



\dfrac{3}{5} \times 0.4



$$

#### Explanation

We want to rewrite the decimal as a fraction.

In our case, $0.4$ is the same as $\dfrac{4}{10}.$

Now, we simplify and get

$$



\dfrac{4}{10} = \dfrac{4 \div 2}{10 \div 2} = \dfrac{2}{5}.



$$

Therefore, the expression can be rewritten as

$$



\dfrac{3}{5} \times 0.4 = \dfrac{3}{5} \times \boxed{\dfrac{2}{5}}.



$$

### Multistep Problems With Positive and Negative Rational Numbers

Now, suppose that the temperature changes by $-6.25$ degrees, then by $+4.5$ degrees, and then by $-2.75$ degrees.

To find the resulting change, we combine the changes in order.

In our case,

- $-6.25$ means the temperature decreases by $6.25,$

- $+4.5$ means the temperature increases by $4.5,$ and

- $-2.75$ means the temperature decreases by $2.75.$

So, we compute

$$



\begin{aligned}−6.25+4.5−2.75 & =−1.75−2.75 \\ & =−4.5.\end{aligned}



$$

Therefore, the final change is $-4.5$ degrees.

### Example: Solving Multistep Problems With Positive and Negative Rational Numbers

#### Question

A drone starts at $5\dfrac{1}{4}$ feet, descends $2.5$ feet, and then rises $\dfrac{2}{3}$ foot. What is the drone's final elevation?

#### Explanation

We start with the initial elevation.

In our case,

- starting at $5\dfrac{1}{4}$ feet means we begin with $5\dfrac{1}{4},$

- descending $2.5$ feet means we subtract $2.5,$ and

- rising $\dfrac{2}{3}$ foot means we add $\dfrac{2}{3}.$

Also, notice that

$$



2.5 = 2\dfrac{5}{10} = \dfrac{25}{10} = \dfrac{5}{2}.



$$

So, we compute

$$



\begin{aligned}5\frac{1}{4}−2.5+\frac{2}{3} & =\frac{21}{4}−\frac{5}{2}+\frac{2}{3} \\ & =\frac{63}{12}−\frac{30}{12}+\frac{8}{12} \\ & =\frac{63−30+8}{12} \\ & =\frac{41}{12} \\ & =3\frac{5}{12}.\end{aligned}



$$

Therefore, the drone's final elevation is $3\dfrac{5}{12}$ feet.

### Number Sense for Arithmetic Operations

We can use number sense to check whether an answer is reasonable before computing exactly.

Here are some helpful rules.

- When we add a positive number, the result gets larger.

- When we subtract a positive number, the result gets smaller.

For example, suppose a student claims that $4\dfrac{1}{2}+2\dfrac{3}{4}=3.$ Let's determine whether this claim is reasonable without computing.

Since $2\dfrac{3}{4}$ is a positive number, adding it to $4\dfrac{1}{2}$ should make the result larger than $4\dfrac{1}{2}.$

But $3$ is less than $4\dfrac{1}{2},$ so the claim cannot be correct!

### Number Sense for Multiplication and Division

We can also use number sense to check reasonableness in multiplication and division.

- When we multiply a positive number by a positive number greater than $1,$ the result gets larger. by a positive number less than $1,$ the result gets smaller.

- When we divide a positive number by a positive number greater than $1,$ the result gets smaller. by a positive number less than $1,$ the result gets larger.

For example, suppose a student claims that $6 \div \dfrac{1}{2} = 3.$ Let's determine whether this claim is reasonable without computing.

Since $6$ is positive and $\dfrac{1}{2}$ is a positive number less than $1,$ dividing $6$ by $\dfrac{1}{2}$ should make the result larger than $6.$

But $3$ is less than $6,$ so the claim cannot be correct!

### Example: Checking Rational-Number Solutions for Reasonableness

#### Question

A student claims that $3\dfrac{1}{2} \div 0.5=1\dfrac{3}{4}.$ Is the answer reasonable?

#### Explanation

To check whether the answer is reasonable, we can think about what division means.

In our case, we start with $3\dfrac{1}{2}$ and divide by $0.5.$

When we divide by a positive number less than $1,$ the result should be greater than the starting number.

But $1\dfrac{3}{4}$ is less than $3\dfrac{1}{2},$ so this answer cannot be reasonable.

Therefore, the correct answer is the following:

No, because the result must be greater than $3\dfrac{1}{2}.$

### Reasoning With Rounding

We can also use rounding to decide whether an answer is reasonable. Rounding gives us an estimate, which helps us quickly check whether a claimed answer makes sense.

A student saves $7\dfrac{1}{2}$ dollars on Monday and $6\dfrac{3}{4}$ dollars on Tuesday. The student claims that the total amount saved is about $10$ dollars. Is that reasonable?

To check whether the answer is reasonable, we can estimate.

In our case, $7\dfrac{1}{2}$ dollars is about $8$ dollars, and $6\dfrac{3}{4}$ dollars is about $7$ dollars.

So, the total amount saved is about

$$



\begin{aligned}8+7 & =15.\end{aligned}



$$

So, the total should be about $15$ dollars, not about $10$ dollars.

Therefore, no, the student's claim is not reasonable.

### Example: Checking Rational-Number Solutions for Reasonableness With Rounding

#### Question

A picture frame is $11\dfrac{1}{2}$ inches wide and a photo is $4\dfrac{1}{4}$ inches wide. A student says each side border is about $2$ inches. Is that reasonable?

#### Explanation

To check whether the answer is reasonable, we can estimate.

In our case, the picture frame is about $12$ inches wide, and the photo is about $4$ inches wide.

So, the extra space is about

$$



\begin{aligned}12−4 & =8.\end{aligned}



$$

Since this extra space is split between $2$ side borders, each border is about

$$



\begin{aligned}8÷2 & =4.\end{aligned}



$$

So, each side border should be about $4$ inches, not about $2$ inches.

Therefore, the correct answer is the following:

No, because the border is about $4$ inches.
