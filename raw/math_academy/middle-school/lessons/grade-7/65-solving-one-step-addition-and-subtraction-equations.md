## Lesson

### Introduction

Solving a linear equation involves **isolating** the variable on one side of the equation.

This can often be accomplished using the **addition principle**. The addition principle tells us that adding (or subtracting) the same number on both sides of an equation does not change its solution.

For example, suppose that we want to solve the equation

$$



x + 4 = 5.



$$

The variable $x$ is not isolated, because we also have a $+4$ on the left-hand side.

To isolate $x$, we can perform the *opposite* operation, which is *subtracting* $4$ from $x.$ This way, the $+4$ and $-4$ will cancel out to $0,$ which will leave $x$ alone. Remember to do this to *both* sides of the equation!

$$



\begin{aligned} x + 4 &= 5 \\x+4\,{\color{red}{-}}\,{\color{red}{4}} &=5\,{\color{red}{-}}\,{\color{red}{4}} \\x+(4-4) &=(5-4) \\x + 0 &= 1 \\x &= 1. \end{aligned}



$$

So $x=1$ is our solution. Let's check that our solution is correct by plugging it back into the original equation:

$$



\begin{aligned} x + 4 &= 5 \\1 + 4&= 5 \\5 &=5 \;{\color{green}{\checkmark}} \end{aligned}



$$

### Example: Solving Linear Equations that Require Subtraction

#### Question

Find the solution to the equation $16 + x =10.$

#### Explanation

First, let's rewrite the left-hand-side so that it's similar to what we've seen before.

$$



x + 16 = 10



$$

To isolate $x$, we need to perform the ** of adding $16$ to $x$, which is ** $16$ from $x.$ Remember to perform the same operation on the right-hand side.

$$



\begin{aligned} x + 16 &= 10 \\x + 16 - 16 &= 10 - 16 \\x + 0 &= -6 \\x &= -6 \end{aligned}



$$

### Example: Solving Linear Equations that Require Addition

#### Question

Solve the equation $x-8 =6.$

#### Explanation

To isolate $x$, we need to perform the ** of subtracting $8$ from $x$, which is ** $8$ to $x.$ Remember to perform the same operation on the right-hand side.

$$



\begin{aligned} x-8 &= 6 \\x-8+8 &= 6+8 \\x+0 &= 14 \\x &= 14 \end{aligned}



$$

### Example: Solving Linear Equations Containing Negative Constants

#### Question

What is the solution to the equation $-15 = p - 12?$

#### Explanation

First, let's swap the left and right-hand sides so that the variable is on the left-hand side.

$$



p-12 = -15



$$

To isolate $p$, we need to perform the ** of subtracting $12$ from $p$, which is ** $12$ to $p.$ Remember to perform the same operation on the right-hand side.

$$



\begin{aligned} p-12 &= -15\\p-12 + 12 &= -15 + 12\\p+0 &=-3\\p &=-3 \end{aligned}



$$

### Example: Solving Linear Equations Containing Non-Integer Constants

#### Question

Solve the equation $\dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{3}{4} + z.$

#### Explanation

First, let's swap the left and right-hand sides so that the variable is on the left-hand side.

$$



\begin{aligned}\frac{1}{2} & =\frac{3}{4}+𝑧 \\ \frac{3}{4}+𝑧 & =\frac{1}{2} \\ 𝑧+\frac{3}{4} & =\frac{1}{2}\end{aligned}



$$

To isolate $z$, we need to perform the ** of adding $\dfrac{3}{4}$ to $z$, which is ** $\dfrac{3}{4}$ from $z.$ Remember to perform the same operation on the right-hand side.

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑧+\frac{3}{4} & =\frac{1}{2} \\ 𝑧+\frac{3}{4}−\frac{3}{4} & =\frac{1}{2}−\frac{3}{4} \\ 𝑧+0 & =\frac{2}{4}−\frac{3}{4} \\ 𝑧 & =\frac{2−3}{4} \\ 𝑧 & =−\frac{1}{4}\end{aligned}



$$
