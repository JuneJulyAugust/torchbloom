## Lesson

### Introduction

Remember that a *unit rate* is a ratio of two quantities stated as some amount of one quantity per unit of the other quantity.

In this lesson, we will consider unit rates from ratios that involve *mixed numbers*.

For example, suppose that a man can run $33\,\dfrac{1}{3}$ meters in $\dfrac{1}{12}$ of a minute. To compute the man's unit rate in meters per minute, we divide the number of meters by the number of minutes, as follows:

$$



33\,\dfrac{1}{3} \div \dfrac{1}{12}



$$

First, we write $33\,\dfrac{1}{3}$ as an improper fraction:

$$



33\,\dfrac{1}{3} = \dfrac{(33 \times 3) + 1}{3} = \dfrac{100}{3}



$$

So, our problem now is to work out the following division:

$$



\dfrac{100}{3} \div \dfrac{1}{12}



$$

Dividing one fraction by another is equivalent to multiplying the first fraction by the reciprocal of the second fraction.

The reciprocal of $\dfrac{\color{blue}{1}}{\color{red}{12}}$ is $\dfrac{\color{red}{12}}{\color{blue}{1}}.$ So we have:

$$



\dfrac{100}{3} \div \dfrac{\color{blue}{1}}{\color{red}{12}} = \dfrac{100}{3} \times \dfrac{\color{red}{12}}{\color{blue}{1}}



$$

Notice that the first fraction's denominator ($3$) and the second fraction's numerator ($\color{red}{12}$) have a common factor of $3.$

Therefore, we can simplify our problem by first swapping the denominators:

$$



\dfrac{100}{\color{blue}{1}} \times \dfrac{\color{red}{12}}{3}



$$

Next, we reduce the second fraction to its lowest terms by dividing the numerator and denominator by $3.$

$$



\begin{aligned}\frac{100}{1}×\frac{12÷3}{3÷3} & =\frac{100}{1}×\frac{4}{1}\end{aligned}



$$

To multiply two fractions, we multiply the numerators, and we multiply the denominators:

$$



\dfrac{100}{1} \times \dfrac{4}{1} = \dfrac{100 \times 4}{1 \times 1} = 400



$$

Therefore, the man's unit rate is $400$ meters per minute.

### Example: Calculating a Unit Rate as the Quotient of a Fraction and a Mixed Number

#### Question

Adam painted $\dfrac{3}{4}$ of a fence in $2 \, \dfrac{2}{3}$ hours. What is Adam's unit rate in fences per hour?

#### Explanation

To compute the unit rate of fences per hour, we divide the number of fences, $\dfrac{3}{4},$ by the number of hours, $2 \,\dfrac{2}{3},$ as follows:

$$



\dfrac{3}{4} \div 2 \,\dfrac{2}{3}



$$

First, we write $2 \,\dfrac{2}{3}$ as an improper fraction:

$$



2 \,\dfrac{2}{3} = \dfrac{(2 \times 3) + 2}{3} = \dfrac{8}{3}



$$

Therefore, our problem now is to work out the following:

$$



\dfrac{3}{4} \div \dfrac{8}{3}



$$

Dividing one fraction by another is equivalent to multiplying the first fraction by the reciprocal of the second fraction.

The reciprocal of $\dfrac{\color{blue}{8}}{\color{red}{3}}$ is $\dfrac{\color{red}{3}}{\color{blue}{8}}.$ So we have:

$$



\dfrac{3}{4} \div \dfrac{\color{blue}{8}}{\color{red}{3}} = \dfrac{3}{4} \times \dfrac{\color{red}{3}}{\color{blue}{8}}



$$

Notice that the first fraction's numerator ($3$) and the second fraction's denominator ($\color{blue}{8}$) do not have a common factor greater than $1.$

Also, the first fraction's denominator ($4$) and the second fraction's numerator ($\color{red}{3}$) do not have a common factor greater than $1.$

So, we cannot simplify by swapping the denominators first.

Next, we multiply the fractions. We multiply the numerators, and we multiply the denominators:

$$



\dfrac{3}{4} \times \dfrac{3}{8} = \dfrac{3 \times 3}{4 \times 8} = \dfrac{9}{32}



$$

Therefore, the unit rate is $\dfrac{9}{32}$ fences per hour.

### Example: Calculating a Unit Rate as the Quotient of a Mixed Number and a Fraction

#### Question

Allison walked $\dfrac{2}{3}$ of a mile in $3 \, \dfrac{1}{2}$ hours. How long does it take Allison to walk $1$ mile?

#### Explanation

To find the number of hours that it takes for Allison to walk $1$ mile, we can compute the unit rate of hours per mile. We do this by dividing the number of hours, $3 \,\dfrac{1}{2},$ by the number of miles, $\dfrac{2}{3},$ as follows:

$$



3 \,\dfrac{1}{2} \div \dfrac{2}{3}



$$

First, we write $3 \,\dfrac{1}{2}$ as an improper fraction:

$$



3 \,\dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{(3 \times 2) + 1}{2} = \dfrac{7}{2}



$$

Therefore, our problem now is to work out the following:

$$



\dfrac{7}{2} \div \dfrac{2}{3}



$$

Dividing one fraction by another is equivalent to multiplying the first fraction by the reciprocal of the second fraction.

The reciprocal of $\dfrac{\color{blue}{2}}{\color{red}{3}}$ is $\dfrac{\color{red}{3}}{\color{blue}{2}}.$ So we have:

$$



\dfrac{7}{2} \div \dfrac{\color{blue}{2}}{\color{red}{3}} = \dfrac{7}{2} \times \dfrac{\color{red}{3}}{\color{blue}{2}}



$$

Notice that the first fraction's numerator ($7$) and the second fraction's denominator ($\color{blue}{2}$) do not have a common factor greater than $1.$

Also, the first fraction's denominator ($2$) and the second fraction's numerator ($\color{red}{3}$) do not have a common factor greater than $1.$

So, we cannot simplify by swapping the denominators first.

Next, we multiply the fractions. We multiply the numerators, and we multiply the denominators:

$$



\dfrac{7}{2} \times \dfrac{3}{2} = \dfrac{7 \times 3}{2 \times 2} = \dfrac{21}{4}



$$

Finally, we write the resulting improper fraction as a mixed number:

$$



\dfrac{21}{4} = 5 \,\textrm{R}\,1 = 5 \,\dfrac{1}{4}



$$

Therefore, it takes Allison $5 \,\dfrac{1}{4}$ hours to walk $1$ mile.

### Example: Calculating a Unit Rate as the Quotient of Mixed Numbers

#### Question

A machine produces $7\,\dfrac{1}{2}$ meters of fabric in $2\,\dfrac{1}{2}$ hours. What is the unit rate of meters per hour in this situation?

#### Explanation

To compute the unit rate of meters per hour, we divide the number of meters, $7\,\dfrac{1}{2},$ by the number of hours, $2\,\dfrac{1}{2},$ as follows:

$$



7\,\dfrac{1}{2} \div 2\,\dfrac{1}{2}



$$

First, we write each mixed number as an improper fraction:

$$



\begin{aligned}7\,\frac{1}{2} & =\frac{(7×2)+1}{2}=\frac{15}{2} \\ 2\,\frac{1}{2} & =\frac{(2×2)+1}{2}=\frac{5}{2}\end{aligned}



$$

Therefore, our problem now is to compute:

$$



\dfrac{15}{2} \div \dfrac{5}{2}



$$

Dividing one fraction by another is equivalent to multiplying by the reciprocal.

The reciprocal of $\dfrac{\color{blue}{5}}{\color{red}{2}}$ is $\dfrac{\color{red}{2}}{\color{blue}{5}}.$ So we have:

$$



\dfrac{15}{2} \div \dfrac{\color{blue}{5}}{\color{red}{2}} = \dfrac{15}{2} \times \dfrac{\color{red}{2}}{\color{blue}{5}}



$$

Notice that the first fraction's denominator ($2$) and the second fraction's numerator ($\color{red}{2}$) have a common factor of $2.$

Also, the first fraction's numerator ($15$) and the second fraction's denominator ($\color{blue}{5}$) have a common factor of $5.$

Therefore, we can simplify our problem by first swapping the denominators:

$$



\dfrac{15}{\color{blue}{5}} \times \dfrac{\color{red}{2}}{2}



$$

Next, we reduce both fractions to lowest terms:

$$



\begin{aligned}\frac{15÷5}{5÷5}×\frac{2÷2}{2÷2} & =\frac{3}{1}×\frac{1}{1}\end{aligned}



$$

To multiply two fractions, we multiply the numerators, and we multiply the denominators:

$$



\dfrac{3}{1} \times \dfrac{1}{1} = \dfrac{3 \times 1}{1 \times 1} = 3



$$

Therefore, the unit rate is $3$ meters per hour.
