## Lesson

### Introduction

In previous lessons, we learned how to apply percentage increases and decreases. We can refer to percentage increases and decreases collectively as **percentage change.** In this lesson, we'll learn how to find what percentage change is applied to a number to get another number.

To set the stage, let's first recall how to calculate a percentage increase in a retail situation.

Suppose that a fast-food meal in a particular restaurant costs $10.$ The manager then decides to increase the price by $20\%.$ What is the new price of the meal?

Recall that *increasing* the price of the meal by $20\%$ means that we must apply a new percentage of

$$



100\% + 20\% = 120\%.



$$

Thus, the new price is $120\%$ of the old price.

Expressing $120\%$ as a decimal, we have

$$



120\% = \dfrac{120}{100} = 1.2.



$$

Now, we multiply the original price by $1.2{:}$

$$



10 \times 1.2 = 12



$$

So, the price of the meal after the increase is $12.$

### Percentage Change

Now, let's consider the reverse problem. Suppose we know that the original price of the meal was $10$ and the new price is $12.$ We know that the manager applied a $20\%$ increase, but how can we determine this percentage change from the initial and final prices alone?

To compute a percentage change, we use the following formula:

$$



\text{percentage change} = \dfrac{|\text{new value} - \text{original value}|}{\text{original value}} \times 100\%



$$

Note the following:

- This formula defines percentage change as the ratio of the absolute change in a quantity (the numerator) relative to its original value (the denominator), expressed as a percentage.

- Note the presence of the absolute value in the numerator. This allows us to apply it to both percentage increases and percentage decreases, as percentage change should always be positive.

Now, in our situation, we have the following information:

- $\text{new value} = 12$

- $\text{original value} = 10$

So, to compute the percentage change, we use the given formula as follows:

$$



\begin{aligned}percentage change & =\frac{|new value−original value|}{original value}×100\% \\ & =\frac{|12−10|}{10}×100\% \\ & =\frac{|2|}{10}×100\% \\ & =\frac{2}{10}×100\% \\ & =\frac{1}{5}×100\% \\ & =0.2×100\% \\ & =20\%.\end{aligned}



$$

So, the percentage increase is $20\%,$ exactly as we saw earlier.

### Example: Calculating a Percentage Increase

#### Question

A school had $50$ students in its initial year. The following year, it had $80$ students. What was the percentage increase in the number of students between the first and second years?

#### Explanation

$$



\text{percentage change} = \dfrac{|\text{new value} - \text{original value}|}{\text{original value}} \times 100\%.



$$

We have the following information:

- $\text{new value} = 80$ students

- $\text{original value} = 50$ students

Substituting these values into our formula, we get

$$



\begin{aligned}percentage change & =\frac{|80−50|}{50}×100\% \\ & =\frac{|30|}{50}×100\% \\ & =\frac{30}{50}×100\% \\ & =\frac{3}{5}×100\% \\ & =0.6×100\% \\ & =60\%.\end{aligned}



$$

So, the percentage increase is $60\%.$

### Example: Calculating a Percentage Increase From a Description

#### Question

A positive number is quadruple another positive number. What is the percentage increase from the smaller to the larger number?

#### Explanation

We will use the formula

$$



\text{percentage change} = \dfrac{|\text{new value} - \text{original value}|}{\text{original value}} \times 100\%.



$$

Let $x$ be the original value. Then, we have the following information:

- $\text{new value} = 4x$

- $\text{original value} = x$

Substituting these values into our formula, we get

$$



\begin{aligned}percentage change & =\frac{|4𝑥−𝑥|}{𝑥}×100\% \\ & =\frac{|3𝑥|}{𝑥}×100\% \\ & =\frac{3𝑥}{𝑥}×100\% \\ & =\frac{3𝑥}{𝑥}×100\% \\ & =3×100\% \\ & =300\%.\end{aligned}



$$

Therefore, the smaller number is increased by $300\%.$

### Example: Calculating a Percentage Decrease

#### Question

Bruce made $24$ muffins for his family last week, later realizing that he made too many. So this week, Bruce only made $15$ muffins. What is the percentage decrease in the number of muffins Bruce produced between the first and second weeks?

#### Explanation

We will use the formula

$$



\text{percentage change} = \dfrac{|\text{new value} - \text{original value}|}{\text{original value}} \times 100\%.



$$

We have the following information:

- $\text{new value} = 15$ muffins

- $\text{original value} = 24$ muffins

Substituting these values into our formula, we get

$$



\begin{aligned}percentage change & =\frac{|15−24|}{24}×100\% \\ & =\frac{|−9|}{24}×100\% \\ & =\frac{9}{24}×100\% \\ & =\frac{3}{8}×100\% \\ & =0.375×100\% \\ & =37.5\%.\end{aligned}



$$

So, the percentage decrease is $37.5\%.$

### Percentage Error

In real-world applications, it's common to estimate values. However, estimation often leads to **error**, which is the difference between a quantity's estimated and actual values.

For example, if a real estate agent estimates that a house is worth $180\,000$ but its actual market value is $150\,000,$ then the total error in the agent's estimate is

$$



\text{error} = 180\,000 - 150\,000 = 30\,000.



$$

Is this an acceptable amount of error?

Errors by themselves are often difficult to interpret. For example, an error of $1$ centimeter is acceptable when designing a parking lot, but such an error could have serious consequences when performing medical procedures.

To resolve this, it's often more helpful to compute the **percentage error**. The formula for the percentage error is given by

$$



\text{percentage error} = \dfrac{|\text{estimate} - \text{exact value}|}{\text{exact value}} \times 100\%.



$$

Notice that this is virtually identical to the formula for percentage change. The only difference is the terminology used.

Let's go back to our real estate question. The estimated value is $180\,000,$ and the exact value is $150\,000.$ Therefore,

$$



\begin{aligned}percentage error & =\frac{|estimate−exact value|}{exact value}×100\% \\ & =\frac{|180\,000−150\,000|}{150\,000}×100\% \\ & =\frac{|30\,000|}{150\,000}×100\% \\ & =\frac{30\,000}{150\,000}×100\% \\ & =\frac{3}{15}×100\% \\ & =0.2×100\% \\ & =20\%.\end{aligned}



$$

Therefore, the percentage error in the agent's estimation is $20\%,$ which is relatively high.

### Example: Calculating Percentage Error

#### Question

Jan is completing a research project. She initially estimated it would take her $27$ weeks to complete the project. However, it took her only $20$ weeks. What is the percentage error in her original estimate?

#### Explanation

We will use the formula

$$



\text{percentage error} = \dfrac{|\text{estimate} - \text{exact value}|}{\text{exact value}} \times 100\%.



$$

We have the following information:

- $\text{estimate} = 27$ weeks

- $\text{exact value} = 20$ weeks

Substituting these values into our formula, we get

$$



\begin{aligned}percentage error & =\frac{|27−20|}{20}×100\% \\ & =\frac{|7|}{20}×100\% \\ & =\frac{7}{20}×100\% \\ & =0.35×100\% \\ & =35\%.\end{aligned}



$$

So, the percentage error is $35\%.$
