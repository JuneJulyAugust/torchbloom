## Lesson

### Introduction

In this lesson, we will learn how to construct and solve inequalities in context.

Suppose that Carol earns $20$ per hour and needs at least $ 360$ each week to cover her living expenses. What's the minimum number of hours Carol needs to work each week to cover her expenses?

Suppose Carol works $h$ hours. Then

- she will earn $20h$ dollars in total, and

- this amount of money should be *greater or equal to* $360$ because she needs to earn *at least* this amount.

So, the number of hours that Carol must work must satisfy the following inequality:

$$



20h \geq 360



$$

To solve this inequality, we must divide both sides by $20.$ This gives

$$



\begin{aligned}20ℎ & ≥360 \\ \frac{20ℎ}{20} & ≥\frac{360}{20} \\ \frac{20ℎ}{20} & ≥18 \\ ℎ & ≥18.\end{aligned}



$$

Therefore, Carol needs to work for *at least* $18$ hours per week to cover her expenses.

### Example: Modeling With Non-Strict Inequalites

#### Question

Jenny has a hot dog stand in a park, where she makes a profit of $3$ per hot dog. She needs to make a profit of at least $540$ per week to continue operating. How many hot dogs does Jenny need to sell weekly to continue working?

#### Explanation

Jenny charges $ 3$ per hot dog. So, if she sells $H$ hot dogs, she makes a total of $3H$ dollars.

Since Jenny must make ** $ 540,$ the following inequality must be satisfied:

$$



3H \geq 540



$$

To solve for $H,$ we divide both sides by $3\mathbin{:}$

$$



\begin{aligned}3𝐻 & ≥540 \\ \frac{3𝐻}{3} & ≥\frac{540}{3} \\ \frac{3𝐻}{3} & ≥180 \\ 𝐻 & ≥180\end{aligned}



$$

So, to continue operating, Jenny must sell ** $180$ hot dogs.

### Example: Modeling With Strict Inequalites

#### Question

A box of nails costs $ 8.$ How many boxes of nails a person can buy for less than $ 440?$

#### Explanation

To buy $B$ boxes of nails, the person needs to spend $8B$ dollars.

Since the person has ** $ 440,$ the following inequality must be satisfied:

$$



8B \lt 440



$$

To solve for $B,$ we divide both sides of the inequality by $8{:}$

$$



\begin{aligned}8𝐵 & <440 \\ \frac{8𝐵}{8} & <\frac{440}{8} \\ \frac{8𝐵}{8} & <55 \\ 𝐵 & <55\end{aligned}



$$

Thus, the person can buy ** $55$ boxes of nails.

### Example: Interpreting Solutions With Fractions

#### Question

Erika wants to spend at least $ 115$ on a pair of sports shoes. If Erika has a job where she earns $14$ for each full hour worked, how many full hours must Erika work to buy a pair of sports shoes?

#### Explanation

Erika earns $14$ for each full hour worked. So if she works $H$ full hours, she makes $14H$ dollars.

Since she wants to spend ** $ 115$ on the shoes, the following inequality must be satisfied:

$$



14H \ge 115



$$

To solve for $H,$ we divide both sides of the inequality by $14{:}$

$$



\begin{aligned}14𝐻 & ≥115 \\ \frac{14𝐻}{14} & ≥\frac{115}{14} \\ \frac{14𝐻}{14} & ≥\frac{115}{14} \\ 𝐻 & ≥\frac{115}{14} \\ 𝐻 & ≥8\,\frac{3}{14}\end{aligned}



$$

Now, $H$ must be an integer. Therefore, we conclude that Erika must work at least $9$ full hours.
