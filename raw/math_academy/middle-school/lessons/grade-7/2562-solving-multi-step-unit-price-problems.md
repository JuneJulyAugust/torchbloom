### Introduction

In real life, we often need to determine the cost of buying quantities of several different items. If we know the unit prices of the items we want to buy, then we can compute the cost.

For example, suppose that at the store, cinnamon costs $2$ per ounce and nutmeg costs $3$ per ounce. If we want to compute the total cost of buying $4$ ounces of cinnamon and $2$ ounces of nutmeg, then we can find the cost for each item and then add the results.

- The price of cinnamon is $2$ per ounce. To find the cost of $4$ ounces of cinnamon, we multiply: So, $4$ ounces of cinnamon will cost $8.$

- The price of nutmeg is $3$ per ounce. To find the cost of $2$ ounces of nutmeg, we multiply: So, $2$ ounces of nutmeg will cost $6.$

Adding up the individual costs, we get

$$



8 + 6 = 14.



$$

Therefore, it will cost $14$ to buy $4$ ounces of cinnamon and $2$ ounces of nutmeg.

### Example: Calculating a Total Price from a Unit Price

#### Question

Sonja is going to the store to buy some groceries. How much would it cost to buy $\dfrac{1}{2}$ of a pound of raisins and $\dfrac{3}{4}$ of a pound of oatmeal?

#### Explanation

Let's compute the cost for each item that Sonja buys.

- The price of raisins is $ 4 / \text{lb}.$ To find the cost of $\dfrac{1}{2}$ of a pound of raisins, we multiply: Therefore, it would cost $ 2$ to buy $\dfrac{1}{2}$ of a pound of raisins.

- The price of oatmeal is $ 2 / \text{lb}.$ To find the cost of $\dfrac{3}{4}$ of a pound of oatmeal, we multiply: Therefore, it would cost $ 1.50$ to buy $\dfrac{3}{4}$ of a pound of oatmeal.

Adding up the individual prices, we get

$$



2 + 1.50 = 3.50 \, .



$$

Therefore, it would cost $ 3.50$ to buy $\dfrac{1}{2}$ of a pound of raisins and $\dfrac{3}{4}$ of a pound of oatmeal.

### Example: Computing the Total Cost With Unit Conversions

#### Question

Emma wants to buy $18$ feet of ribbon. If the cost of the ribbon is $2.40$ for $4$ yards, then how much will it cost for Emma to buy $18$ feet of ribbon?

Assume that there are $3$ feet in $1$ yard.

#### Explanation

First, we convert from yards to feet. There are $3$ feet in each yard, so we multiply the number of yards by $3\mathbin{:}$

$$



\begin{aligned}4×3 & =12\end{aligned}



$$

So $4$ yards is the same as $12$ feet. Therefore, the store sells $12$ feet of ribbon for $2.40.$

Now, to find the price per foot, we divide the total price by $12\mathbin{:}$

$$



\begin{aligned}2.40÷12 & =\frac{2.4}{12} \\ & =\frac{2.4×10}{12×10} \\ & =\frac{24}{120} \\ & =\frac{24÷12}{120÷12} \\ & =\frac{2}{10} \\ & =0.20\end{aligned}



$$

So the ribbon costs $0.20$ per foot.

Finally, to find the cost of $18$ feet of ribbon, we multiply the number of feet by $0.20\mathbin{:}$

$$



0.20 \times 18 = 3.60



$$

Therefore, if Emma buys $18$ feet of ribbon, it will cost $3.60.$

### Working With Reciprocal Unit Prices

Previously, we have computed how much a particular combination of items will cost. However, we sometimes want to determine how much of an item we can get with a fixed amount of money.

For example, suppose that we go to the store with $10$ and want to buy as much rope as we can. If rope costs $2$ per meter, then how much rope can we buy?

The first step is to compute the reciprocal unit price of meters of rope per dollar. Starting with the unit price of $2$ per meter and taking the reciprocal, we have

$$



2 \,\textrm{dollars per meter} \quad\rightarrow\quad \dfrac{1}{2} \,\textrm{meters per dollar} .



$$

Now, to find the number of meters of rope that $10$ can buy, we multiply the number of dollars by $\dfrac{1}{2}\mathbin{:}$

$$



\begin{aligned}10×\frac{1}{2} & =\frac{10}{1}×\frac{1}{2} \\ & =\frac{10×1}{1×2} \\ & =\frac{10}{2} \\ & =10÷2 \\ & =5\end{aligned}



$$

Therefore, we can buy $5$ meters of rope for $10.$

### Example: Calculating Quantities Using Reciprocal Unit Prices

#### Question

A smoothie stand sells fruit smoothies for $ 2.40$ per cup. Sofia wants to buy as many smoothies as she can. If Sofia has $ 24,$ how many cups of smoothies can she buy?

#### Explanation

We are given the unit rate of $ 2.40$ per cup (dollars per cup). To find the amount of smoothies that Sofia can buy for $ 24,$ we first need to find the unit rate in cups per dollar. So, we take the reciprocal:

$$



2.40\,\textrm{dollars per cup}\quad\rightarrow\quad 1 \div 2.40 \,\textrm{cups per dollar}



$$

We have

$$



\begin{aligned}1÷2.40 & =\frac{1}{2.4} \\ & =\frac{1×10}{2.4×10} \\ & =\frac{10}{24} \\ & =\frac{10÷2}{24÷2} \\ & =\frac{5}{12}\end{aligned}



$$

The unit rate is $\dfrac{5}{12}$ of a cup per dollar. To find the number of cups of smoothies that $ 24$ can buy, we multiply the number of dollars by $\dfrac{5}{12}\mathbin{:}$

$$



\begin{aligned}24×\frac{5}{12} & =\frac{24}{1}×\frac{5}{12} \\ & =\frac{24×5}{1×12} \\ & =\frac{120}{12} \\ & =120÷12 \\ & =10\end{aligned}



$$

Therefore, Sofia will get $10$ cups of smoothies for $24.$
