## Lesson

### Introduction

As we already know, to evaluate numerical expressions with a mix of different operations, we must follow the order of operations (PEMDAS).

The same rules apply to numerical expressions with negative numbers. For example, let's compute the value of

$$



(-2) \times (-3)^2.



$$

To evaluate this expression, we use PEMDAS. First, we compute the exponents:

$$



(-2)\,\times\overbrace{(-3)^2}^{\large\color{blue} 9} = (-2) \times {\color{blue} 9}



$$

Then, we multiply:

$$



(-2) \times 9 = -18



$$

Therefore,

$$



(-2) \times (-3)^2 = -18.



$$

### Example: Evaluating Expressions With Stand-Alone Exponents

#### Question

Calculate $(-3)^3 \div 6$.

#### Explanation

To evaluate this expression, we use PEMDAS. First, we compute the exponents:

$$



\begin{aligned}\underset{−27}{\underset{}{(−3)^{3}}}÷6=(−27)÷6\end{aligned}



$$

Then, we divide:

$$



\begin{aligned}(−27)÷6 & = \\ \frac{(−27)}{6} & = \\ −\frac{27}{6} & = \\ −\frac{27÷3}{6÷3} & = \\ −\frac{9}{2} & \end{aligned}



$$

### Example: Evaluating Expressions With Exponents Outside Parentheses

#### Question

Evaluate $(1-4)^2 \times \dfrac{1}{12}.$

#### Explanation

To evaluate this expression, we use PEMDAS. First, we perform the operation inside the parentheses:

$$



\begin{aligned}(\underset{−3}{\underset{}{1−4}})^{2}×\frac{1}{12} & =(−3)^{2}×\frac{1}{12}\end{aligned}



$$

Next, we compute the exponents:

$$



\begin{aligned}\underset{9}{\underset{}{(−3)^{2}}}×\frac{1}{12} & =9×\frac{1}{12}\end{aligned}



$$

Finally, we multiply:

$$



\begin{aligned}9×\frac{1}{12} & = \\ \frac{9}{1}×\frac{1}{12} & = \\ \frac{9×1}{1×12} & = \\ \frac{9}{12} & = \\ \frac{9÷3}{12÷3} & = \\ \frac{3}{4} & \end{aligned}



$$

### Example: Evaluating Expressions With Exponents Inside Parentheses

#### Question

What is the value of $\left(5 \div (-2)^2 \right) \times \left(-\dfrac{4}{5} \right).$

#### Explanation

To evaluate this expression, we use PEMDAS. First, consider the expression inside the parenthesis:

$$



\begin{aligned}5÷(−2)^{2}\end{aligned}



$$

Applying PEMDAS to this expression, we obtain:

$$



\begin{aligned}5÷(−2)^{2} & = \\ 5÷4 & = \\ \frac{5}{4} & \end{aligned}



$$

Finally, we multiply:

$$



\begin{aligned}(\overset{\overset{5÷(−2)^{2}}{}}{\frac{5}{4}})×(−\frac{4}{5}) & = \\ \frac{5}{4}×(−\frac{4}{5}) & = \\ −\frac{5}{4}×\frac{4}{5} & = \\ −\frac{5×4}{4×5} & = \\ −\frac{5×4}{4×5} & = \\ −1 & \end{aligned}



$$
