### Introduction

The **product rule for exponents** states that when we multiply two or more exponents that have the same base, we add the exponents:

$$



a^n\cdot a^m=a^{n+m}



$$

For example,

$$



a^2\cdot a^3 = a^{2+3} = a^5.



$$

To see why the product rule is true, let's take the above example and work it out again, the long way.

For the two factors $a^2$ and $a^3$ we have $a^2=a\cdot a$ and $a^3= a\cdot a\cdot a.$ If we now substitute these two expressions into $a^2\cdot a^3$, we get the same result:

$$



\begin{aligned} a^2\cdot a^3&=\\(a\cdot a) \cdot (a\cdot a\cdot a)&=\\a\cdot a\cdot a\cdot a\cdot a&=\\a^5 \end{aligned}



$$

The product rule is just a shortcut for the process above.

### Example: Using the Product Rule with Two Terms

#### Question

Simplify $a^{9}\cdot a^{7}.$

#### Explanation

We add the exponents, as follows:

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑎^{9}⋅𝑎^{7} & = \\ 𝑎^{9+7} & = \\ 𝑎^{16} & \end{aligned}



$$

### Example: Using the Product Rule with Three or More Terms

#### Question

Find $n$ such that $x^2\cdot x^3 \cdot x^4 = x^n$ is correct.

#### Explanation

We add the exponents, as follows:

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑥^{2}⋅𝑥^{3}⋅𝑥^{4} & = \\ 𝑥^{2+3+4} & = \\ 𝑥^{9} & \end{aligned}



$$

Therefore $x^{n}=x^9$, so $n=9.$

### Example: Using the Product Rule with Multiple Variables

#### Question

Simplify $x\left(x^3 y^5\right) \left(x^2 y^9\right).$

#### Explanation

Here we have two variables, $x$ and $y.$ We will start by grouping the two variables separately:

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑥(𝑥^{3}𝑦^{5})(𝑥^{2}𝑦^{9}) & = \\ 𝑥^{1}⋅𝑥^{3}⋅𝑦^{5}⋅𝑥^{2}⋅𝑦^{9} & = \\ (𝑥^{1}⋅𝑥^{3}⋅𝑥^{2})(𝑦^{5}⋅𝑦^{9}) & \end{aligned}



$$

This way, we can apply the product rule to the two variables independently:

$$



\begin{aligned}(𝑥^{1}⋅𝑥^{3}⋅𝑥^{2})(𝑦^{5}⋅𝑦^{9}) & = \\ 𝑥^{(1+3+2)}⋅𝑦^{(5+9)} & = \\ 𝑥^{6}𝑦^{14} & \end{aligned}



$$
