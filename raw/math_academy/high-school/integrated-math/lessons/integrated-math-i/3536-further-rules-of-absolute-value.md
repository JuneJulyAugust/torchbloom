## Lesson

### Introduction

In this lesson, we'll extend our knowledge of absolute value.

Let's first remind ourselves of the rules of absolute value:

- $|{-a}| = |a|$

- $|a \cdot b| = |a| \cdot |b|$

- $\left| \dfrac{a}{b} \right| = \dfrac{|a|}{|b|}$

Recall that the last two rules are known as the **distributive properties.**

### Example: Simplifying Absolute Value Expressions

#### Question

Simplify the expression $|{-3z}|-5\cdot|4z|.$

#### Explanation

Firstly, since $|{-a}| = |a|,$ we can write our expression as

$$



|3z|-5\cdot|4z|.



$$

Then, we distribute the absolute value over the multiplication, and simplify:

$$



\begin{aligned}|3𝑧|−5⋅|4𝑧| & = \\ |3|⋅|𝑧|−5⋅|4|⋅|𝑧| & = \\ 3⋅|𝑧|−5⋅4⋅|𝑧| & = \\ 3⋅|𝑧|−20⋅|𝑧| & = \\ 3|𝑧|−20|𝑧| & = \\ −17|𝑧| & \end{aligned}



$$

### The Absolute Value of a Square

We know that the absolute value operation does nothing to a number if it is non-negative. For example:

$$



\begin{aligned}|0| & =0 \\ |1| & =1 \\ |2| & =2 \\ |3| & =3 \\ & ⋮\end{aligned}



$$

We can summarize this fact as follows:

$$



|x| = x \quad \text{ if } \quad x \geq 0



$$

We also know that $x^2$ is non-negative for any value of $x$. Therefore,

$$



\left|x^2\right| = x^2



$$

where $x$ is *any* real number.

### Example: Simplifying the Absolute Value of a Square

#### Question

Simplify the expression $\left| - \dfrac{x^2}{10} \right|.$

#### Explanation

Firstly, since $|{-a}| = |a|,$ we can write our expression as

$$



\left| \dfrac{x^2}{10} \right|.



$$

Then, we distribute the absolute value over the multiplication and division:

$$



\begin{aligned}\frac{𝑥^{2}}{10} & = \\ \frac{𝑥^{2}}{|10|} & = \\ \frac{𝑥^{2}}{10} & \end{aligned}



$$

Since $x^2 \geq 0$ for any real $x,$ we have that $\left|x^2\right|=x^2.$ Therefore,

$$



\begin{aligned}\frac{𝑥^{2}}{10} & =\frac{𝑥^{2}}{10}.\end{aligned}



$$

### The Absolute Value of a Negative Variable

We know that $|x|=x$ if $x \geq 0.$ But what if $x < 0?$

If a variable is known to be negative, then the absolute value changes it to positive. To turn a negative number into a positive number, we multiply by $-1.$ Therefore, we have

$$



|x| = -x \quad\textrm{for}\quad x < 0.



$$

To convince ourselves that this is indeed true, let's verify it in the case when $x=-2.$ Substituting this value into the above, we get

$$



\begin{aligned}|−2| & =−(−2)=2.\,✓\end{aligned}



$$

### Example: Evaluating an Absolute Value Expression with a Domain Restriction

#### Question

Given that $x\lt 0,$ simplify $|{-6x}|+2x.$

#### Explanation

Firstly, since $|{-a}| = |a|,$ we can write our expression as

$$



|6x|+2x.



$$

Then, we distribute the absolute value over the multiplication. This gives

$$



\begin{aligned}|6𝑥|+2𝑥 & = \\ |6|⋅|𝑥|+2𝑥 & = \\ 6⋅|𝑥|+2𝑥 & = \\ 6|𝑥|+2𝑥 & .\end{aligned}



$$

Now, since $x \lt 0,$ we have that $|x|=-x.$ Therefore,

$$



\begin{aligned}6|𝑥|+2𝑥 & = \\ 6(−𝑥)+2𝑥 & = \\ −6𝑥+2𝑥 & = \\ −4𝑥 & .\end{aligned}



$$
