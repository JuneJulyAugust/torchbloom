### Introduction

Consider the following system of equations:

$$



\begin{aligned}5𝑥+2𝑦=−1 \\ −4𝑥−3𝑦=−2\end{aligned}



$$

Suppose we want to eliminate the variable $x$ from the system using the elimination method.

First, notice that the coefficient of $x$ in the *first* equation is ${\color{red}{5}},$ while in the *second* equation it is $-{\color{blue}{4}}.$

So, if we multiply the *first* equation by ${\color{blue}{4}}$ and the *second* equation by ${\color{red}{5}},$ then the $x$-coefficient in the first and second equations respectively will become $20$ and $-20,$ and these will cancel when we add the equations.

$$



\begin{aligned}(\,5𝑥+2𝑦=−1\,)\,×(4) \\ (\,−4𝑥−3𝑦=−2\,)\,×(5)\end{aligned}



$$

Now, when we add the two equations, the variable $x$ will be eliminated:

$$



\begin{aligned}20𝑥+8𝑦 & =−4 \\ −20𝑥−15𝑦 & =−10 \\ (8𝑦−15𝑦) & =(−4−10) \\ −7𝑦 & =−14 \\ 𝑦 & =2\end{aligned}



$$

Therefore, we have successfully eliminated the variable $x$ from the system.

$$



\begin{aligned}3𝑎+2𝑏=−2 \\ 4𝑎−3𝑏=3\end{aligned}



$$

Describe the steps needed to eliminate the variable $a$ from the system:

To eliminate $a,$ multiply the first equation by $44$ the second by $44$ and add the equations.

#### Explanation

Notice that the coefficient of $a$ in the first equation is ${\color{red}{3}},$ while in the second equation it is ${\color{blue}{4}}.$

If we multiply the first equation by ${\color{blue}{4}}$ and the second equation by $-{\color{red}{3}},$ then the $a$-coefficient in the first and second equations will become $12$ and $-12,$ and these will cancel when we add the equations.

$$



\begin{aligned}(\,3𝑎+2𝑏=−2\,)\,×(4) \\ (\,4𝑎−3𝑏=3\,)\,×(−3)\end{aligned}



$$

\begin{aligned}12𝑎+8𝑏 & =−8 \\ −12𝑎+9𝑏 & =−9 \\ (8𝑏+9𝑏) & =(−8−9) \\ 17𝑏 & =−17\end{aligned}



$$

### Example: Solving a System of Equations by Eliminating the First Variable

#### Question

Find the solution $(x,y)$ to the following system of equations by eliminating the variable $x{:}$

$$



\begin{aligned}2𝑥−3𝑦=−7 \\ −3𝑥+4𝑦=8\end{aligned}



$$

#### Explanation

Notice that the $x$-coefficient in the first equation is ${\color{red}2},$ while in the second equation it is $-{\color{blue}3}.$

If we multiply the first equation by ${\color{blue}3}$ and the second equation by ${\color{red}2},$ then the $x$-coefficient in the first and second equations will become $6$ and $-6,$ and these will cancel when we add the equations.

$$



\begin{aligned}(\,2𝑥−3𝑦=−7\,)\,×(3) \\ (\,−3𝑥+4𝑦=8\,)\,×(2)\end{aligned}



$$

Now, when we add the two equations, the variable $x$ will be eliminated:

$$



\begin{aligned}6𝑥−9𝑦 & =−21 \\ −6𝑥+8𝑦 & =16 \\ (8𝑦−9𝑦) & =(16−21) \\ −𝑦 & =−5 \\ 𝑦 & =5\end{aligned}



$$

So, the solution must have $y=5.$ However, we still need to find $x.$ To do this, we substitute $y=5$ back into one of the equations. Let's choose the first equation:

$$



\begin{aligned}2𝑥−3𝑦 & =−7 \\ 2𝑥−3(5) & =−7 \\ 2𝑥−15 & =−7 \\ 2𝑥 & =8 \\ 𝑥 & =4\end{aligned}



$$

Therefore, the solution to the system of equations is $x=4$ and $y=5$, which can be written as $(4,5).$

### Example: Solving a System of Equations by Eliminating the Second Variable

#### Question

Find the solution $(x,y)$ to the system below by eliminating the variable $y.$

$$



\begin{aligned}4𝑥+3𝑦=3 \\ 5𝑥+4𝑦=5\end{aligned}



$$

#### Explanation

Notice that the coefficient of $y$ in the first equation is ${\color{red}3},$ while in the second equation it is ${\color{blue}4}.$

If we multiply the first equation by ${\color{blue}4}$ and the second equation by $-{\color{red}3},$ then the $y$-coefficient in the first and second equations will become $-12$ and $12,$ and these will cancel when we add the equations.

$$



\begin{aligned}(\,4𝑥+3𝑦=3\,)\,×(4) \\ (\,5𝑥+4𝑦=5\,)\,×(−3)\end{aligned}



$$

Now, when we add the two equations, the variable $y$ will be eliminated:

$$



\begin{aligned}16𝑥+12𝑦 & =12 \\ −15𝑥−12𝑦 & =−15 \\ (16𝑥−15𝑥) & =(12−15) \\ 𝑥 & =−3\end{aligned}



$$

So, the solution must have $x=-3.$ However, we still need to find $y.$ To do this, we substitute $x=-3$ back into one of the equations. Let's choose the first equation:

$$



\begin{aligned}4𝑥+3𝑦 & =3 \\ 4(−3)+3𝑦 & =3 \\ −12+3𝑦 & =3 \\ 3𝑦 & =15 \\ 𝑦 & =5\end{aligned}



$$

Therefore, the solution to the system of equations is $x=-3$ and $y=5,$ which can be written as $(-3,5).$
