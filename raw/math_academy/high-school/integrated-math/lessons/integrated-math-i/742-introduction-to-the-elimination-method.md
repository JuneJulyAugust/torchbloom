### Introduction

Suppose that we want to solve, *without using graphs*, the following system of linear equations:

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑥+𝑦=1 \\ 5𝑥−𝑦=11\end{aligned}



$$

To solve the system, we must combine the equations into a single equation that can be solved using algebraic manipulation. One way to combine the equations is to use the **elimination method.**

Notice that the coefficient of $y$ in the first equation is $1,$ and the coefficient of $y$ in the second equation is $-1.$ Since these numbers add to zero, we can *add* the two equations, *eliminate* the variable $y,$ and solve the resulting equation for $x\mathbin{:}$

$$



\begin{aligned}\begin{matrix}𝑥+𝑦 & =1 \\ 5𝑥−𝑦 & =11 \\ (𝑥+5𝑥) & =(1+11) \\ 6𝑥 & =12 \\ 𝑥 & =2\end{matrix}\end{aligned}



$$

So, the solution must have $x=2.$ However, we still need to find $y.$ To do this, we substitute $x=2$ back into one of the equations. Let's choose the first equation:

$$



\begin{aligned}\begin{matrix}𝑥+𝑦 & =1 \\ (2)+𝑦 & =1 \\ 𝑦 & =−1\end{matrix}\end{aligned}



$$

Therefore, the solution to the system of equations is $x=2$ and $y=-1,$ which can be expressed as $(2, -1).$

### Example: Eliminating the Second Variable

#### Question

Find the solution $(a,b)$ to the following system of equations using the elimination method:

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑎+𝑏=3 \\ 4𝑎−𝑏=7\end{aligned}



$$

#### Explanation

Notice that the coefficient of $b$ in the first equation is $1,$ and the coefficient of $b$ in the second equation is $-1.$

Since these numbers add to zero, we can add the two equations, ** the variable $b,$ and solve the resulting equation for $a\mathbin{:}$

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑎+𝑏 & =3 \\ 4𝑎−𝑏 & =7 \\ (𝑎+4𝑎) & =(3+7) \\ 5𝑎 & =10 \\ 𝑎 & =2\end{aligned}



$$

So, the solution must have $a=2.$ However, we still need to find $b.$ To do this, we substitute $a=2$ back into one of the equations. Let's choose the first equation:

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑎+𝑏 & =3 \\ (2)+𝑏 & =3 \\ 𝑏 & =1\end{aligned}



$$

Therefore, the solution to the system of equations is $a=2, \,b=1,$ which can be expressed as $(2, 1).$

### Example: Eliminating the First Variable

#### Question

Find the solution $(a,b)$ to the following system of equations using the elimination method:

$$



\begin{aligned}3𝑎−𝑏=2 \\ −3𝑎+2𝑏=−1\end{aligned}



$$

#### Explanation

Notice that the coefficient of $a$ in the first equation is $3,$ and the coefficient of $a$ in the second equation is $-3.$

Since these numbers add to zero, we can add the two equations, ** the variable $a,$ and solve the resulting equation for $b\mathbin{:}$

$$



\begin{aligned}3𝑎−𝑏 & =2 \\ −3𝑎+2𝑏 & =−1 \\ (−𝑏+2𝑏) & =(2+(−1)) \\ 𝑏 & =1\end{aligned}



$$

So, the solution must have $b=1.$ However, we still need to find $a.$ To do this, we substitute $b=1$ back into one of the equations. Let's choose the first equation:

$$



\begin{aligned}3𝑎−𝑏 & =2 \\ 3𝑎−(1) & =2 \\ 3𝑎 & =3 \\ 𝑎 & =1\end{aligned}



$$

Therefore, the solution to the system of equations is $a=1, \,b=1,$ which can be expressed as $\left(1,1\right).$

### Eliminating Variables From Systems of Linear Equations

Now, suppose that we want to solve the following system of linear equations using elimination:

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑥+2𝑦=1 \\ 𝑥−𝑦=−2\end{aligned}



$$

As it stands, adding the two equations will not eliminate any of the variables.

However, suppose we want to eliminate the variable $x$. We begin by multiplying the second equation by $\color{blue}-1$, which gives the following equivalent system:

$$



\begin{aligned}\,(𝑥+2𝑦=1 \\ (\,𝑥−𝑦=−2\,)\,×(−1)\end{aligned}



$$

Then, we add the two resulting equations together. This eliminates the variable $x,$ making it possible to solve for $y\mathbin{:}$

$$



\begin{aligned}\begin{matrix}𝑥+2𝑦 & =1 \\ −𝑥+𝑦 & =2 \\ (2𝑦+𝑦) & =(1+2) \\ 3𝑦 & =3 \\ 𝑦 & =1\end{matrix}\end{aligned}



$$

So, the solution must have $y=1.$ However, we still need to find $x.$ To do this, we substitute $y=1$ back into one of the equations. Let's choose the second equation:

$$



\begin{aligned}\begin{matrix}𝑥−𝑦 & =−2 \\ 𝑥−(1) & =−2 \\ 𝑥 & =−1\end{matrix}\end{aligned}



$$

Therefore, the solution to the system of equations is $x=-1$ and $y=1,$ which can be expressed as $(-1, 1).$

### Example: Determining the Result of a Transformation

#### Question

Consider the following system of equations.

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑥+𝑦=3 \\ 6𝑥−2𝑦=−2\end{aligned}



$$

The variable $y$ can be eliminated from the system using the following two steps:

1. Multiply the first equation by $2$ and obtain

2. Add the multiplied equation to the second equation to get

What's the missing expression?

#### Explanation

Let's follow the steps provided:

****: Multiplying the first equation by ${\color{blue}{2}}$ yields the following equivalent system:

$$



\begin{aligned}(\,𝑥+𝑦=3\,)\,×(2) \\ 6𝑥−2𝑦=−2\end{aligned}



$$

****: Next, we add these two equations together:

$$



 \begin{aligned} 2x + 2y & = 6\\[5pt] 6x - 2y & = -2\\[5pt] \hline (2x + 6x) &= (6 - 2)\\[5pt] {\color{red}8x} &= 4 \end{aligned}



$$

Therefore, the missing expression is ${\color{red}8x}.$

### Example: Solving a System of Equations

#### Question

Consider the following system of equations.

$$



\begin{aligned}3𝑥−2𝑦=9 \\ 𝑥−3𝑦=−4\end{aligned}



$$

The variable $x$ can be eliminated from the system using the following two steps:

1. Multiply the second equation by $-3$ and obtain

2. Add the multiplied equation to the first equation to get

Find the missing expression, and hence find the solution $(x,y)$ of the system.

#### Explanation

Let's follow the steps provided:

****: Multiplying the second equation by ${\color{blue}{-3}}$ yields the following equivalent system:

$$



\begin{aligned}\,(3𝑥−2𝑦=9 \\ (\,𝑥−3𝑦=−4\,)\,×(−3)\end{aligned}



$$

****: Next, we add these two equations together, ** the variable $x,$ and solve the resulting equation for $y{:}$

$$



 \begin{aligned} 3x - 2y & = 9\\[5pt] -3x + 9y & = 12\\[5pt] \hline (9y - 2y) & = (9 + 12)\\[5pt] {\color{red}7y} & = 21\\[5pt] y & = 3 \end{aligned}



$$

So, the solution must have $y = 3.$ However, we still need to find $x.$ To do this, we substitute $y = 3$ back into one of the equations. Let's choose the second equation:

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑥−3𝑦 & =−4 \\ 𝑥−3(3) & =−4 \\ 𝑥−9 & =−4 \\ 𝑥 & =5\end{aligned}



$$

Therefore, the solution to the system of equations is $x=5, \,y = 3,$ which can be expressed as $(5,3).$
