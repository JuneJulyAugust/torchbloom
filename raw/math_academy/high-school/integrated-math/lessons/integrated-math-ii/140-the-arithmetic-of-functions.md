### Example: Finding the Sum or Difference of Two Functions

#### Question

Given $f(x) = 2x + 7$ and $g(x) = 5x - 24,$ find $(f - g)(x).$

#### Explanation

The notation $(f-g)(x)$ means the difference of the functions $f(x)$ and $g(x).$

To find the difference of the two functions, we subtract $g(x)$ from $f(x)\mathbin{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned} (f - g)(x) &= f(x) - g(x) \\[3pt] &=(2x + 7) - (5x - 24)\\[3pt] &= (2x-5x) + (7-(-24))\\[3pt] &= -3x+31 \end{aligned}


$$

### Evaluating the Sum or Difference of Two Functions

When we're asked to evaluate the sum of two functions at a particular point, there are two methods that we can use. We could either evaluate both functions at the point and sum the results. Or, we could sum the two functions and then evaluate the result at the point.

To demonstrate, let's find the $(f+g)(1)$ for $f(x) = x^2 -1$ and $g(x) = 2x-3.$ The notation $(f+g)(x)$ means the sum of the functions $f(x)$ and $g(x).$

**Method 1**

To evaluate $(f + g)(1),$ we find $f(1)$ and $g(1),$ and then add the results:

First, we find $f(1)$ and $g(1)\mathbin{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑓(1) & =(1)^{2}−1=0 \\ 𝑔(1) & =2(1)−3=−1\end{aligned}


$$

Now, we add the results:

$$


\begin{aligned}(𝑓+𝑔)(1) & =𝑓(1)+𝑔(1) \\ & =0+(−1) \\ & =−1\end{aligned}


$$

**Method 2**

First, we find $(f+g)(x)$ by adding the two functions:

$$


\begin{aligned}(𝑓+𝑔)(𝑥) & =(𝑥^{2}−1)+(2𝑥−3) \\ & =𝑥^{2}+2𝑥−4\end{aligned}


$$

Now, we evaluate $(f + g)(1)\mathbin{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}(𝑓+𝑔)(1) & =(1)^{2}+2(1)−4=−1\end{aligned}


$$

Both these methods work fine, but method $1$ is usually faster.

Finally, note that we can evaluate the difference between two functions using the same methods.

### Example: Evaluating the Sum or Difference of Two Functions

#### Question

Find $(f + g)(-1)$ for $f(x) = 1 -2x$ and $g(x) = 4x + 3.$

#### Explanation

The notation $(f+g)(x)$ means the sum of the functions $f(x)$ and $g(x).$

****

To evaluate $(f + g)(-1),$ we find $f(-1)$ and $g(-1),$ and then add the results.

First, we find $f(-1)$ and $g(-1)\mathbin{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑓(−1) & =1−2(−1)=3 \\ 𝑔(−1) & =4(−1)+3=−1\end{aligned}


$$

Now, we add the results:

$$


\begin{aligned}(𝑓+𝑔)(−1) & =𝑓(−1)+𝑔(−1) \\ & =3+(−1) \\ & =2\end{aligned}


$$

****

First, we find $(f+g)(x)$ by adding the two functions:

$$


\begin{aligned}(𝑓+𝑔)(𝑥) & =(1−2𝑥)+(4𝑥+3) \\ & =2𝑥+4\end{aligned}


$$

Now, we evaluate $(f + g)(-1)\mathbin{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}(𝑓+𝑔)(−1) & =−2+4=2\end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Finding the Product of Two Functions

#### Question

If $f(x) = 2x - 3$ and $g(x) = x + 6,$ then $(fg)(x)=$

#### Explanation

The notation $(fg)(x)$ means the product of the functions $f(x)$ and $g(x).$

To find the product of the two functions $f$ and $g,$ we multiply them together:

$$


\begin{aligned} (fg)(x) &= f(x)\cdot g(x) \\[3pt] &= (2x - 3)(x + 6) \\[3pt] &= 2x^2 + 12x -3x - 18 \\[3pt] &= 2x^2 + 9x - 18 \end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Evaluating the Quotient of Two Functions

#### Question

Find $(f/g)(-1)$ for $f(x) = 2x +5$ and $g(x) = x^3+2.$

#### Explanation

The notation $(f/g)(x)$ means the quotient of the functions $f(x)$ and $g(x).$

****

To evaluate $(f/g)(-1),$ we find $f(-1)$ and $g(-1),$ and then divide the results:

First, we find $f(-1)$ and $g(-1)\mathbin{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑓(−1) & =2(−1)+5=3 \\ 𝑔(−1) & =(−1)^{3}+2=1\end{aligned}


$$

Now, we divide the results:

$$


(f/g)(-1) = \dfrac{f(-1)}{g(-1)} = \dfrac{3}{1} =3


$$

****

First, we find $(f/g)(x)$ by dividing $f$ by $g\mathbin{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}(𝑓/𝑔)(𝑥)=\frac{2𝑥+5}{𝑥^{3}+2}\end{aligned}


$$

Now, we evaluate $(f/g)(-1)\mathbin{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}(𝑓/𝑔)(−1) & =\frac{2(−1)+5}{(−1)^{3}+2}=\frac{3}{1}=3\end{aligned}


$$
