## Lesson

### Introduction

We can use the method of completing the square to solve *any* quadratic equation, even if the equation does not factor.

To illustrate, suppose we want to solve the following equation:

$$


x^2+2x-8=0


$$

We can do so by following the five steps below:

**Step 1.** Move the constant term to the right-hand side:

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑥^{2}+2𝑥−8 & =0 \\ 𝑥^{2}+2𝑥 & =8\end{aligned}


$$

**Step 2.** Complete the square on the left-hand side:

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑥^{2}+2𝑥 & =8 \\ 𝑥^{2}+2⋅1⋅𝑥 & =8 \\ 𝑥^{2}+2⋅1⋅𝑥+1^{2}−1^{2} & =8 \\ (𝑥^{2}+2⋅1⋅𝑥+1^{2})−1 & =8 \\ (𝑥+1)^{2}−1 & =8\end{aligned}


$$

**Step 3.** Isolate the perfect square by adding $1$ to both sides. This gives

$$


\begin{aligned}(𝑥+1)^{2} & =9.\end{aligned}


$$

**Step 4.** We now solve the equation using the square root method:

$$


\begin{aligned}(𝑥+1)^{2} & =9 \\ \sqrt{(𝑥+1)^{2}} & =\sqrt{9} \\ |𝑥+1| & =3 \\ 𝑥+1 & =±3 \\ 𝑥 & =−1±3\end{aligned}


$$

**Step 5.** Finally, we simplify the two solutions:

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑥_{1} & =−1−3=−4 \\ 𝑥_{2} & =−1+3=2\end{aligned}


$$

Therefore, the solutions are $x=-4$ and $x=2.$

### Example: Solving a Quadratic Equation Given a Corresponding Perfect Square

#### Question

Solve the equation $x^2 -10x = -24.$

**

#### Explanation

Using the hint, we have

$$


x^2 -10x +25 = (x-5)^2.


$$

First, we complete the square on the left-hand side:

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑥^{2}−10𝑥 & =−24 \\ 𝑥^{2}−2⋅5⋅𝑥 & =−24 \\ 𝑥^{2}−2⋅5⋅𝑥+5^{2}−5^{2} & =−24 \\ (𝑥−5)^{2}−25 & =−24\end{aligned}


$$

Next, we isolate the perfect square by adding $25$ to both sides:

$$


\begin{aligned}(𝑥−5)^{2}−25+25 & =−24+25 \\ (𝑥−5)^{2} & =1\end{aligned}


$$

We now solve the equation using the square root method:

$$


\begin{aligned}(𝑥−5)^{2} & =1 \\ \sqrt{(𝑥−5)^{2}} & =\sqrt{1} \\ |𝑥−5| & =1 \\ 𝑥−5 & =±1 \\ 𝑥 & =5±1\end{aligned}


$$

Finally, we simplify the two solutions:

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑥_{1} & =5−1=4 \\ 𝑥_{2} & =5+1=6\end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Solving a Quadratic Equation When the Solutions Are Rational

#### Question

Write the equation $x^2 + 6x +5= 0$ in the form $(x+p)^2=q,$ and hence solve the equation.

#### Explanation

First, we move the constant term to the right-hand side and complete the square on the left-hand side:

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑥^{2}+6𝑥+5 & =0 \\ 𝑥^{2}+6𝑥 & =−5 \\ 𝑥^{2}+2⋅3⋅𝑥 & =−5 \\ 𝑥^{2}+2⋅3⋅𝑥+3^{2}−3^{2} & =−5 \\ (𝑥^{2}+2⋅3⋅𝑥+3^{2})−9 & =−5 \\ (𝑥+3)^{2}−9 & =−5\end{aligned}


$$

Then, we isolate the perfect square:

$$


\begin{aligned}(𝑥+3)^{2}−9 & =−5 \\ (𝑥+3)^{2} & =−5+9 \\ (𝑥+3)^{2} & =4\end{aligned}


$$

So, $p=3$ and $q=4.$ Now, we solve the equation using the square-root method:

$$


\begin{aligned}(𝑥+3)^{2} & =4 \\ \sqrt{(𝑥+3)^{2}} & =\sqrt{4} \\ |𝑥+3| & =2 \\ 𝑥+3 & =±2 \\ 𝑥 & =−3±2\end{aligned}


$$

Finally, we simplify the two solutions:

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑥_{1} & =−3+2=−1 \\ 𝑥_{2} & =−3−2=−5\end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Solving a Quadratic Equation When the Solutions Are Irrational

#### Question

Solve the equation $x^2-2x-1=0.$

#### Explanation

First, we move the constant term to the right-hand side and complete the square on the left-hand side:

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑥^{2}−2𝑥−1 & =0 \\ 𝑥^{2}−2𝑥 & =1 \\ 𝑥^{2}−2⋅1⋅𝑥 & =1 \\ 𝑥^{2}−2⋅1⋅𝑥+1^{2}−1^{2} & =1 \\ (𝑥^{2}−2𝑥+1)−1 & =1 \\ (𝑥−1)^{2}−1 & =1\end{aligned}


$$

Then, we isolate the perfect square and solve the equation using the square-root method:

$$


\begin{aligned}(𝑥−1)^{2}−1 & =1 \\ (𝑥−1)^{2} & =2 \\ \sqrt{(𝑥−1)^{2}} & =\sqrt{2} \\ |𝑥−1| & =\sqrt{2} \\ 𝑥−1 & =±\sqrt{2} \\ 𝑥 & =1±\sqrt{2}\end{aligned}


$$

Therefore, our solutions are

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑥_{1} & =1+\sqrt{2}, \\ 𝑥_{2} & =1−\sqrt{2}.\end{aligned}


$$
