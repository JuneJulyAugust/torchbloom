## Lesson

### Introduction

Sometimes we can simplify a radical expression by factoring the expression inside the radical.

For instance, let's try to simplify the expression

$$


\sqrt{a^2+2a+1}.


$$

We can begin by factoring the expression under the radical, as follows:

$$


\begin{aligned}\sqrt{√𝑎^{2}+2𝑎+1} & = \\ \sqrt{√𝑎^{2}+2⋅1⋅𝑎+1} & = \\ \sqrt{√(𝑎+1)^{2}} & \end{aligned}


$$

Now, using the fact that $\sqrt{a^2} = |a|,$ we can simplify our expression even further. This gives

$$


\sqrt{(a+1)^2} = |a+1|.


$$

### Example: Simplifying the Square Root of a Perfect Square by Factoring

#### Question

Given that $x\geq1,$ simplify $\sqrt{4x^2 - 8x + 4}.$

#### Explanation

To simplify the radical expression, we factor the expression inside the radical and then simplify using the identity $\sqrt{a^2} = |a|,$ as follows:

$$


\begin{aligned}\sqrt{√4𝑥^{2}−8𝑥+4} & =\sqrt{√4⋅(𝑥^{2}−2𝑥+1)} \\ & =\sqrt{√4}⋅\sqrt{√𝑥^{2}−2𝑥+1} \\ & =2⋅\sqrt{√𝑥^{2}−2𝑥+1} \\ & =2⋅\sqrt{√𝑥^{2}−2⋅1⋅𝑥+1^{2}} \\ & =2⋅\sqrt{√(𝑥−1)^{2}} \\ & =2|𝑥−1|\end{aligned}


$$

Now, since $x \geq 1,$ we have that

$$


x-1 \geq 0 \quad \Longrightarrow\quad |x-1| = x-1.


$$

Therefore, our expression simplifies to $2(x-1).$

### Example: Simplifying a Square Root by Factoring a Higher Order Polynomial

#### Question

Simplify $\sqrt{4x^4y +12x^4}.$

#### Explanation

To simplify the radical expression, we factor the expression inside the radical and then simplify using the identity $\sqrt{a^2} = |a|,$ as follows:

$$


\begin{aligned} \sqrt{4x^4y +12x^4} &= \sqrt{4x^4(y+3)} \\[3pt] &= \sqrt{4x^4} \cdot \sqrt{y+3} \\[3pt] &= \sqrt{\left(2x^2\right)^2} \cdot \sqrt{y+3} \\[3pt] &= |2x^2| \sqrt{y+3} \end{aligned}


$$

Now, since $x^2 \geq0,$ we have that

$$


2x^2 \geq 0 \quad \Longrightarrow\quad |2x^2| = 2x^2.


$$

Therefore, our expression simplifies to $2x^2\sqrt{y + 3}.$

### Example: Simplifying the Square Root of a Rational Expression

#### Question

Simplify $\sqrt{\dfrac{x^2-4x+4}{x^2+6x+9}}.$

#### Explanation

To simplify the expression, we first factor the numerator and denominator under the radical:

$$


\begin{aligned}\sqrt{√\frac{𝑥^{2}−4𝑥+4}{𝑥^{2}+6𝑥+9}} & =\sqrt{√\frac{𝑥^{2}+2⋅𝑥⋅(−2)+(−2)^{2}}{𝑥^{2}+2⋅𝑥⋅3+3^{2}}} \\ & =\sqrt{√\frac{(𝑥−2)^{2}}{(𝑥+3)^{2}}}\end{aligned}


$$

Then, by applying the quotient rule for radicals and the rules of absolute value, we get

$$


\begin{aligned}\sqrt{√\frac{(𝑥−2)^{2}}{(𝑥+3)^{2}}} & =\frac{\sqrt{√(𝑥−2)^{2}}}{\sqrt{√(𝑥+3)^{2}}} \\ & =\frac{|𝑥−2|}{|𝑥+3|} \\ & =\frac{𝑥−2}{𝑥+3}.\end{aligned}


$$
