### Introduction

To find the least common multiple ($\textrm{LCM}$) of two monomials, we find the least common multiple of the constants and the least common multiple of the variable part. Then, we multiply both results together.

For example, let's find the least common multiple of the monomials $4 a^2$ and $10a^3.$

First, we find $\textrm{LCM}(4,10)\mathbin{:}$

- Multiples of $4\mathbin{:}$ $\quad 4, 8, 12,16,{\color{blue}20}, \ldots$

- Multiples of $10\mathbin{:}$ $\quad 10, {\color{blue}20}, 30, 40 \ldots$

So, $\textrm{LCM}(4,10) = 20.$

Then, to find the $\textrm{LCM}$ of $a^2$ and $a^3,$ we raise $a$ to the largest power that appears in the expressions, which is $3.$ Therefore,

$$


\textrm{LCM}(a^2,a^3) = {\color{red}{a^3}}.


$$

Finally, we multiply all of our results together:

$$


\textrm{LCM}(4a^2,10a^3) ={\color{blue}{20}}\cdot {\color{red}{a^3}}= 20a^3


$$

### Example: Finding the LCM of Two Monomials that Differ by a Constant Factor

#### Question

Find the least common multiple of $2x^2$ and $5x^2.$

#### Explanation

To find the least common multiple ($\textrm{LCM}$) of two monomials, we find the least common multiple of the constants and the least common multiple of the variable parts. Then, we multiply both results together.

First, we find $\textrm{LCM}(2,5)\mathbin{:}$

- Multiples of $2\mathbin{:}$ $\quad 2, 4, 6, 8, {\color{blue}10}, \ldots$

- Multiples of $5\mathbin{:}$ $\quad 5, {\color{blue}10}, 15, \ldots$

So, $\textrm{LCM}(2,5) = {\color{blue}{10}}.$

Since the variable part is the same in the expressions, we have

$$


\textrm{LCM}(x^2,x^2) = {\color{red}{x^2}}.


$$

Finally, we multiply all of our results together:

$$


\textrm{LCM}(2x^2,5x^2) = {\color{blue}{10}}\cdot {\color{red}{x^2}} = 10x^2


$$

### Example: Finding the LCM of Two Single-Variable Monomials

#### Question

Find the least common multiple of the monomials $2x$ and $3x^2.$

#### Explanation

To find the least common multiple ($\textrm{LCM}$) of two monomials, we find the least common multiple of the constants and the least common multiple of the variable parts. Then, we multiply both results together.

First, we find $\textrm{LCM}(2,3)\mathbin{:}$

- Multiples of $2\mathbin{:}$ $\quad 2, 4, {\color{blue}6}, 8, \ldots$

- Multiples of $3\mathbin{:}$ $\quad 3, {\color{blue}6}, 9, \ldots$

So, $\textrm{LCM}(2,3) = 6.$

Then, to find the $\textrm{LCM}$ of $x$ and $x^2,$ we raise $x$ to the largest power that appears in the expressions, which is $2.$ Therefore,

$$


\textrm{LCM}(x,x^2) = {\color{red}{x^2}}.


$$

Finally, we multiply all of our results together:

$$


\textrm{LCM}(2x,3x^2) ={\color{blue}{6}}\cdot {\color{red}{x^2}}= 6x^2


$$

### Example: Finding the LCM of Two Multivariable Monomials

#### Question

Find the least common multiple of the monomials $3xy$ and $5x^2y.$

#### Explanation

To find the least common multiple ($\textrm{LCM}$) of two monomials, we find the least common multiple of the constants and the least common multiple of the variable parts. Then, we multiply both results together.

First, we find $\textrm{LCM}(3,5)\mathbin{:}$

- Multiples of $3\mathbin{:}$ $\quad 3, 6, 9,12,{\color{blue}15}, \ldots$

- Multiples of $5\mathbin{:}$ $\quad 5,10, {\color{blue}15}, 20, \ldots$

So, $\textrm{LCM}(3,5) = 15.$

Then, to find the $\textrm{LCM}$ of $xy$ and $x^2y,$ we find the largest powers for $x$ and $y$ that appear in the expressions:

- For $x$ and $x^2,$ the largest power is ${\color{blue}{2}}.$

- For $y$ and $y,$ the largest power is ${\color{red}{1}}.$

Now, we raise $x$ and $y$ to their corresponding largest powers. Therefore,

$$


\textrm{LCM}(xy,x^2y) =x^{\color{blue}{2}}\cdot y^{\color{red}{1}}=x^2y.


$$

Finally, we multiply all of our results together:

$$


\textrm{LCM}(3xy,5x^2y) = 15\cdot x^2y = 15x^2y


$$

### Example: Finding the LCM of Three Monomials

#### Question

Find the least common multiple of $2w,$ $3w^3$ and $8w^2.$

#### Explanation

To find the least common multiple ($\textrm{LCM}$) of three monomials, we find the least common multiple of the constants and the least common multiple of the variable parts. Then, we multiply both results together.

First, we find $\textrm{LCM}(2,3,8)\mathbin{:}$

- Multiples of $2\mathbin{:}$ $\quad 2, 4, 6,8, \ldots, 22,{\color{blue}24}, \ldots$

- Multiples of $3\mathbin{:}$ $\quad 3,6,9,12,15, 18, 21, {\color{blue}24}, 27, \ldots$

- Multiples of $8\mathbin{:}$ $\quad 8, 16, {\color{blue}24}, 32, \ldots$

So, $\textrm{LCM}(2,3,8) = {\color{blue}{24}}.$

Then, to find the $\textrm{LCM}$ of $w,$ $w^3,$ and $w^2,$ we raise $w$ to the largest power that appears in any of the expressions, which is $3.$ Therefore,

$$


\textrm{LCM}(w, w^3,w^2) = {\color{red}{w^3}}.


$$

Finally, we multiply all of our results together:

$$


\textrm{LCM}(2w, 3w^3,8w^2) = {\color{blue}{24}}\cdot {\color{red}{w^3}} = 24w^3


$$
