## Lesson

### Introduction

When two fractions have the same denominator, we add the numerators. For example,

$$


\dfrac 1 7 + \dfrac 5 7 = \dfrac{1+5}{7} = \dfrac 6 7.


$$

In this case, both fractions were over a common denominator of $7$.

It works the same with rational expressions! So suppose that we want to add

$$


\dfrac{1}{x} + \dfrac{5}{x}.


$$

Because both fractions have the same denominator of $x,$ we can add the numerators as before:

$$


\dfrac{1}{x} + \dfrac{5}{x} = \dfrac{1+5}{x} = \dfrac{6}{x}


$$

Let's see some more examples!

### Example: Adding Rational Expressions With a Common Denominator

#### Question

Simplify $\dfrac{2+a}{2a} + \dfrac{3+2a}{2a}.$

#### Explanation

We have a common denominator of $2a,$ so we add the numerators and simplify:

$$


\begin{aligned}\frac{2+𝑎}{2𝑎}+\frac{3+2𝑎}{2𝑎} & = \\ \frac{2+𝑎+3+2𝑎}{2𝑎} & = \\ \frac{2+3+𝑎+2𝑎}{2𝑎} & = \\ \frac{5+3𝑎}{2𝑎} & \end{aligned}


$$

### Adding Rational Expressions Without Common Denominators

Remember that in order to add fractions with different denominators, like $\dfrac 1 4 + \dfrac 1 8,$ we need to put the fractions over a **common denominator**, as follows:

$$


\begin{aligned}\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{8} & = \\ \frac{2}{8}+\frac{1}{8} & = \\ \frac{3}{8} & \end{aligned}


$$

It works the same in algebraic rational expressions! To add two fractions, we need to put each fraction over a common denominator. Then, we add the numerators.

### Example: Adding Rational Expressions in One Variable With Common Factors in the Denominators

#### Question

Given that $\dfrac 3 {2x}=a$ and $\dfrac y x=b,$ what is $a+b?$

#### Explanation

We wish to add the following fractions:

$$


a+b = \dfrac 3 {2x} + \dfrac y x


$$

In order to add the two fractions, we need them to have a common denominator. Here, we can put both fractions over a common denominator of $2x$ if we multiply the top and bottom of the second fraction by $2\mathbin{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned} \dfrac 3 {2x} + \dfrac y x &= \\[5pt] \dfrac 3 {2x} + \dfrac 2 2 \cdot \dfrac y x &= \\[5pt] \dfrac 3 {2x} + \dfrac {2y} {2x} &= \\[5pt] \dfrac {3 + 2y} {2x} \end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Adding Rational Expressions in Multiple Variables With Common Factors in the Denominators

#### Question

Simplify $\dfrac{y}{x(1+y)} + \dfrac{y}{1+y}.$

#### Explanation

In order to add the two fractions, we need them to have a common denominator. Here, we can put both fractions over a common denominator of $x(y+1)$ if we multiply the top and bottom of the second fraction by $x\mathbin{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}\frac{𝑦}{𝑥(1+𝑦)}+\frac{𝑦}{1+𝑦} & = \\ \frac{𝑦}{𝑥(1+𝑦)}+\frac{𝑥}{𝑥}⋅\frac{𝑦}{1+𝑦} & = \\ \frac{𝑦}{𝑥(1+𝑦)}+\frac{𝑥𝑦}{𝑥(1+𝑦)} & = \\ \frac{𝑦+𝑥𝑦}{𝑥(1+𝑦)} & = \\ \frac{𝑦(1+𝑥)}{𝑥(1+𝑦)} & \end{aligned}


$$
