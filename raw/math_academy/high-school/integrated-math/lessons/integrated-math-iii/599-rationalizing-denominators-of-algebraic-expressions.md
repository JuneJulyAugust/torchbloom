### Introduction

When simplifying expressions, we prefer not to leave a radical in the denominator. To eliminate a radical in an expression's denominator, we can use a process known as **rationalizing the denominator**.

To rationalize the denominator, we need to multiply both the numerator and denominator by the radical that we wish to remove from the denominator.

For instance, to rationalize the denominator in the expression

$$


\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x}},


$$

all we need to do is multiply both the numerator and denominator by $\sqrt{x}$ and simplify:

$$


\begin{aligned}\frac{1}{\sqrt{√𝑥}} & = \\ \frac{1}{\sqrt{√𝑥}}\,⋅\,\frac{\sqrt{√𝑥}}{\sqrt{√𝑥}} & = \\ \frac{\sqrt{√𝑥}}{(\sqrt{√𝑥})^{2}} & = \\ \frac{\sqrt{√𝑥}}{𝑥} & \end{aligned}


$$

We see that the final expression no longer has a radical in the denominator. Thus, the denominator has been rationalized.

**Note:** Because we multiplied the numerator and denominator by the same amount, the resulting expression $\dfrac{\sqrt{x}}{x}$ is equivalent to the original fraction $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x}}.$ They are equivalent, even though they are written differently.

### Example: Rationalizing a Variable Fraction

#### Question

Rationalize $\dfrac{x}{2\sqrt{y}}.$

#### Explanation

We need to remove the radical $\sqrt{y}$ from the denominator. To do this, we multiply both the numerator and denominator by $\sqrt{y},$ and simplify:

$$


\begin{aligned}\frac{𝑥}{2\sqrt{√𝑦}} & = \\ \frac{𝑥}{2\sqrt{√𝑦}}⋅\frac{\sqrt{√𝑦}}{\sqrt{√𝑦}} & = \\ \frac{𝑥⋅\sqrt{√𝑦}}{2\sqrt{√𝑦}⋅\sqrt{√𝑦}} & = \\ \frac{𝑥\sqrt{√𝑦}}{2(\sqrt{√𝑦})^{2}} & = \\ \frac{𝑥\sqrt{√𝑦}}{2𝑦} & \end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Rationalizing a Variable Fraction Using the Quotient Rule For Radicals

#### Question

Rationalize the denominator of $\sqrt{\dfrac{3}{8a}}.$

#### Explanation

First, we apply the quotient rule and put the numerator and denominator under separate radicals:

$$


\begin{aligned}\sqrt{√\frac{3}{8𝑎}} & =\frac{\sqrt{√3}}{\sqrt{√8𝑎}}\end{aligned}


$$

Now, we need to remove the radical $\sqrt{8a}$ from the denominator. To do this, we multiply both the numerator and denominator by $\sqrt{8a}$ and simplify:

$$


\begin{aligned}\frac{\sqrt{√3}}{\sqrt{√8𝑎}}⋅\frac{\sqrt{√8𝑎}}{\sqrt{√8𝑎}} & = \\ \frac{\sqrt{√24𝑎}}{(\sqrt{√8𝑎})^{2}} & = \\ \frac{\sqrt{√4⋅6𝑎}}{8𝑎} & = \\ \frac{2\sqrt{√6𝑎}}{8𝑎} & = \\ \frac{\sqrt{√6𝑎}}{4𝑎} & \end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Rationalizing a Variable Fraction With More Than Two Terms

#### Question

Express $\dfrac{\sqrt{y}+2}{\sqrt{z+1}}$ without radicals in the denominator.

#### Explanation

We need to remove the radical $\sqrt{z+1}$ from the denominator. To do this, we multiply both the numerator and denominator by $\sqrt{z+1}$ and simplify:

$$


\begin{aligned}\frac{\sqrt{√𝑦}+2}{\sqrt{√𝑧+1}} & = \\ \frac{\sqrt{√𝑦}+2}{\sqrt{√𝑧+1}}⋅\frac{\sqrt{√𝑧+1}}{\sqrt{√𝑧+1}} & = \\ \frac{(\sqrt{√𝑦}+2)\sqrt{√𝑧+1}}{(\sqrt{√𝑧+1})^{2}} & = \\ \frac{\sqrt{√𝑦}⋅\sqrt{√𝑧+1}+2⋅\sqrt{√𝑧+1}}{𝑧+1} & = \\ \frac{\sqrt{√𝑦⋅(𝑧+1)}+2\sqrt{√𝑧+1}}{𝑧+1} & = \\ \frac{\sqrt{√𝑦𝑧+𝑦}+2\sqrt{√𝑧+1}}{𝑧+1} & \end{aligned}


$$
