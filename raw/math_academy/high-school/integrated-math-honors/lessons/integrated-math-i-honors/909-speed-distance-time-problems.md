### Introduction

If an object moves at a constant speed for some length of time, we can calculate how far it travels using the formula

$$


\text{distance} = \text{speed} \times \text{time}.


$$

We can use this formula to determine how long it takes for two objects to meet.

For example, suppose a car leaves London for Glasgow, driving at $70$ miles per hour. At the same time, another car departs Glasgow for London, driving at $60$ miles per hour. If the distance between Glasgow and London is $416$ miles, how long until the cars drive past each other?

Let $T$ be the number of hours from when the cars left to when they meet.

We fill in a speed-time-distance table for each vehicle:

When the cars cross paths, they will have driven a combined distance of $416$ miles. Therefore, we can set the sum of the distances equal to $416$ and solve for time, as follows:

$$


\begin{aligned}70𝑇+60𝑇 & =416 \\ 130𝑇 & =416 \\ \frac{130𝑇}{130} & =\frac{416}{130} \\ 𝑇 & ≈3.2\end{aligned}


$$

So, the cars drive past each other after about $3.2$ hours.

### Example: Modeling Two Objects Traveling in Opposite Directions

#### Question

John and Mary leave their office simultaneously, heading directly away from each other. If John is walking at $7\,\text{km/hr}$ and Mary walks at $5\,\text{km/hr}$, how long will it take for them to be $3\,\text{km}$ apart?

#### Explanation

Let $T$ be the time it takes for John and Mary to be $3\,\text{km}$ apart.

We fill in a speed-time-distance table:

After $T$ hours, they will have traveled a combined distance of $3\,\text{km}$. Therefore, we can set the sum of the distances equal to $3$ and solve for time:

$$


\begin{aligned}5𝑇+7𝑇 & =3 \\ 12𝑇 & =3 \\ 𝑇 & =\frac{3}{12} \\ 𝑇 & =\frac{1}{4}\end{aligned}


$$

It will take $\dfrac{1}{4}$ hours for John and Mary to be $3\,\text{km}$ apart. If we convert this time to minutes, we get

$$


\begin{aligned}\frac{1}{4}\,hr & =\frac{1}{4}\,hr×\frac{60\,min}{hr} \\ & =\frac{1}{4}\,hr×\frac{60\,min}{hr} \\ & =\frac{1}{4}×60\,min \\ & =15\,min.\end{aligned}


$$

Therefore, it will take $15$ minutes for them to be $3\,\text{km}$ apart.
