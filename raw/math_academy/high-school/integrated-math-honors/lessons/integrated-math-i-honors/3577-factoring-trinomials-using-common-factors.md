## Lesson

### Introduction

Suppose we want to factor the polynomial

$$


8x^2 + 24x + 16.


$$

At first glance, it looks tricky because the coefficients are rather large!

However, notice that all the terms have a common factor of $8.$ If we first factor out an $8$ from each of the terms, then we can cut the factoring down to something more manageable:

$$


\begin{aligned} 8x^2 + 24x + 16 &= \\\[5pt] 8(x^2 + 3x + 2) \end{aligned}


$$

Now, we need to factor the polynomial $x^2 + 3x + 2$ in the parentheses.

The constant term $(2)$ has a single factor pair, namely

$$


(1, 2).


$$

Notice that the numbers in this factor pair indeed sum to the linear term's coefficient $(1+2=3).$

So, we can write the linear term as $3x=x+2x$ and factor as usual:

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑥^{2}+3𝑥+2 & = \\ 𝑥^{2}+𝑥+2𝑥+2 & = \\ (𝑥^{2}+𝑥)+(2𝑥+2) & = \\ 𝑥(𝑥+1)+2(𝑥+1) & = \\ (𝑥+2)(𝑥+1) & \end{aligned}


$$

Therefore, our original expression factors as follows:

$$


\begin{aligned}8𝑥^{2}+24𝑥+16 & = \\ 8(𝑥^{2}+3𝑥+2) & = \\ 8(𝑥+2)(𝑥+1) & \end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Factoring a Trinomial Whose Terms Have a Common Factor

#### Question

Factor $3x^2 - 21x + 30.$

#### Explanation

First, notice that we can factor out a $3$ from each of the terms:

$$


\begin{aligned}3𝑥^{2}−21𝑥+30 & = \\ 3(𝑥^{2}−7𝑥+10) & \end{aligned}


$$

Now, we just need to factor the polynomial $x^2-7x+10$ in parentheses.

We start by listing the factor pairs of the constant term $(10),$ but only the negative factor pairs since the coefficient of the linear term $(-7)$ is negative.

$$


\begin{aligned}(−1,−10),\,(−2,−5)\end{aligned}


$$

Now we find which factor pair adds up to the coefficient of the linear term $(-7)\mathbin{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}(−1)+(−10) & =−11 & × \\ (−2)+(−5) & =−7 & ✓\end{aligned}


$$

Using the factor pair identified above, we write the linear term as $-7x=-2x-5x,$ and factor:

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑥^{2}−7𝑥+10 & = \\ 𝑥^{2}−2𝑥−5𝑥+10 & = \\ (𝑥^{2}−2𝑥)+(−5𝑥+10) & = \\ 𝑥(𝑥−2)−5(𝑥−2) & = \\ (𝑥−5)(𝑥−2) & \end{aligned}


$$

Therefore, our original expression factors as follows:

$$


\begin{aligned} 3\left(x^2-7x+10\right)&=\\\[5pt] 3(x-5)(x-2) \end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Factoring a Trinomial With a Negative Quadratic Term

#### Question

Factor the expression $-2x^2-22x-48.$

#### Explanation

First, notice that we can factor out a $-2$ from each of the terms:

$$


\begin{aligned}−2𝑥^{2}−22𝑥−48 & = \\ −2(𝑥^{2}+11𝑥+24) & \end{aligned}


$$

Now, we just need to factor the polynomial $x^2+11x+24$ in parentheses.

First, we find the factor pairs of the constant term $(24)\mathbin{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}(1,24),\,(2,12),\,(3,8),\,(4,6)\end{aligned}


$$

Now, we find which factor pair adds up to the coefficient of the linear term $(11)\mathbin{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}1+24 & =25 & × \\ 2+12 & =14 & × \\ 3+8 & =11 & ✓ \\ 4+6 & =10 & ×\end{aligned}


$$

Using the factor pair identified in the previous step, write the linear term as $11x=3x+8x,$ and then factor the expression completely:

$$


\begin{aligned} -2\left(x^2+11x+24\right)&=\\\[5pt] -2\left(x^2+3x+8x+24\right)&=\\\[5pt] -2\left((x^2+3x)+(8x+24)\right)&=\\\[5pt] -2\Bigl(x(x+3)+8(x+3)\Bigr)&=\\\[5pt] -2(x+8)(x+3) \end{aligned}


$$
