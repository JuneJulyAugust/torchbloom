## Lesson

### Introduction

Suppose that we want to solve the following trigonometric equation:

$$


3\tan^2 x - 1 =0, \qquad 0 \leq x < 2\pi


$$

To solve this equation, we perform the following three steps:

**Step 1**: Solve for the trigonometric function.

First, we rearrange the equation and isolate $\tan^2{x}{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}3tan^{2}⁡𝑥−1 & =0 \\ 3tan^{2}⁡𝑥 & =1 \\ tan^{2}⁡𝑥 & =\frac{1}{3}\end{aligned}


$$

Taking the square root on both sides of the equation, we have

$$


\begin{aligned}tan^{2}⁡𝑥 & =\frac{1}{3} \\ tan⁡𝑥 & =±\sqrt{\frac{1}{3}} \\ tan⁡𝑥 & =±\frac{\sqrt{3}}{3}.\end{aligned}


$$

**Step 2**: Solve the resulting two equations.

Our equations are

$$


\tan{x} = -\dfrac{\sqrt3}3 \qquad \text{and}\qquad \tan{x} = \dfrac{\sqrt3}3.


$$

We solve each equation separately.

- First, we solve $\tan{x} = -\dfrac{\sqrt3}{3}.$ The solutions to this equation for $0\leq x < 2\pi$ are $x=\dfrac{5\pi}{6},\dfrac{11\pi}{6}.$

- Then, we solve $\tan{x} = \dfrac{\sqrt3}{3}.$ The solutions to this equation for $0\leq x < 2\pi$ are $x=\dfrac{\pi}{6},\dfrac{7\pi}{6}.$

**Step 3**: Form the solution set by combining all the solutions found in step 2.

Combining the solutions from both equations, the solutions to the original equation (in ascending order) are

$$


x_1 = \dfrac{\pi}{6},\qquad x_2 = \dfrac{5\pi}{6},\qquad x_3 = \dfrac{7\pi}{6},\qquad x_4 = \dfrac{11\pi}{6}.


$$

### Example: Solving a Quadratic Trigonometric Equation Containing Tangent Using the Square Root Method

#### Question

Solve the equation $\tan^2{x}=3$ for $-\pi < x \leq \pi.$

#### Explanation

Taking the square root of both sides of the equation, we have

$$


\begin{aligned}tan^{2}⁡𝑥 & =3 \\ tan⁡𝑥 & =±\sqrt{3}\end{aligned}


$$

So, we have to solve the following two equations:

$$


\tan{x} = -\sqrt 3, \qquad \tan{x} = \sqrt3


$$

Let's solve each equation separately.

- First, we solve $\tan{x} = -\sqrt 3.$ The solutions to this equation for $-\pi < x \leq \pi$ are $x = -\dfrac{\pi}{3}, \dfrac{2\pi}{3}.$

- Then, we solve $\tan{x} = \sqrt 3.$ The solutions to this equation for $-\pi < x \leq \pi$ are $x=\dfrac{\pi}{3}, -\dfrac{2\pi}{3}.$

Combining all solutions from both equations, we get

$$


x_1 = -\dfrac{2\pi}{3}, \qquad x_2 = -\dfrac{\pi}{3}, \qquad x_3 = \dfrac{\pi}{3}, \qquad x_4 = \dfrac{2\pi}{3}.


$$

### Example: Solving a Quadratic Trigonometric Equation Containing Cotangent Using the Square Root Method

#### Question

Given that $x_1$ and $x_2$ are the solutions to the equation $25\cot^2 x - 1 = 0$ for $0 ^\circ \leq x < 180^\circ,$ what is the numerical value of $x_1\cdot x_2,$ rounded to the nearest integer?

#### Explanation

First, we rearrange the equation and isolate $\cot^2{x}{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}25cot^{2}⁡𝑥−1 & =0 \\ cot^{2}⁡𝑥 & =\frac{1}{25}\end{aligned}


$$

Taking the square root of both sides of the equation, we have

$$


\begin{aligned}cot^{2}⁡𝑥 & =\frac{1}{25} \\ cot⁡𝑥 & =±\sqrt{\frac{1}{25}} \\ cot⁡𝑥 & =±\frac{1}{5}.\end{aligned}


$$

So, we have to solve the following two equations:

$$


\cot x = -\dfrac{1}{5}, \qquad \cot x = \dfrac{1}{5}


$$

Let's solve each equation separately.

- First, we solve $\cot{x} = -\dfrac1 5.$ The solution to this equation for $0 ^\circ \leq x < 180^\circ$ is $x\approx 101.310^\circ.$

- Then, we solve $\cot{x} = \dfrac1 5.$ The solution to this equation for $0 ^\circ \leq x < 180^\circ$ is $x\approx 78.690^\circ.$

Combining all solutions from both equations, we get

$$


x_1 \approx 78.690^\circ, \quad x_2 \approx 101.310^\circ.


$$

Finally,

$$


x_1\cdot x_2 \approx 78.690 \cdot 101.310 \approx 7\,972,


$$

rounded to the nearest integer.
