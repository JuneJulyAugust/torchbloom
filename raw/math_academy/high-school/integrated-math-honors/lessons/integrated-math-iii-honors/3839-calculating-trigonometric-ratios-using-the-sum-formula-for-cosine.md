## Lesson

### Introduction

We can compute trigonometric ratios using the sum and difference formulas for cosine together with the Pythagorean identity.

Let's suppose that for the angles $u$ and $v,$ we have

$$


\sin u = \dfrac{1}{3}, \qquad \sin v = \dfrac {3}{5}, \qquad \cos v = -\dfrac{4}{5}.


$$

How can we find the value of $\cos\left(u - v\right)$ given that $u$ lies in the second quadrant?

First, we recall the difference formula for cosine:

$$


\cos(u - v) = \cos u \cos v + \sin u \sin v


$$

We know the values of $\sin{u}$, $\sin{v}$, and $\cos{v}.$ To find the missing value of $\cos{u}$, we can use the Pythagorean identity:

$$


\sin^2 u + \cos^2 u = 1 \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \cos^2 u = 1- \sin^2 u


$$

Substituting our values into the Pythagorean identity, we get

$$


\begin{aligned}cos^{2}⁡𝑢 & =1−sin^{2}⁡𝑢 \\ & =1−(\frac{1}{3})^{2} \\ & =1−\frac{1}{3^{2}} \\ & =1−\frac{1}{9} \\ & =\frac{9}{9}−\frac{1}{9} \\ & =\frac{8}{9}.\end{aligned}


$$

Therefore,

$$


\begin{aligned}cos⁡𝑢 & =±\sqrt{√\frac{8}{9}} \\ & =±\frac{2\sqrt{√2}}{3}.\end{aligned}


$$

We're given that $u$ lies in the second quadrant, where cosine is negative. So, we disregard the positive value, and we have

$$


\cos u= -\dfrac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}.


$$

We can now apply the difference formula:

$$


\begin{aligned} \cos(u - v) &= \cos u \cos v + \sin u \sin v \\\[5pt] &= \left( -\dfrac{2\sqrt{2}}{3} \right) \left(-\dfrac{4}{5}\right) + \left(\dfrac{1}{3}\right) \left(\dfrac{3}{5}\right) \\\[5pt] &= \dfrac{8\sqrt{2}}{15} + \dfrac{3}{15} \\\[5pt] &= \dfrac{8\sqrt{2}+3}{15} \end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Computing the Cosine of a Composite Angle Using Angles in the First Quadrant

#### Question

Suppose that for the angles $u$ and $v,$ we have

$$


\sin u = \dfrac {4} {5}, \qquad \cos u = \dfrac {3} {5}, \qquad \sin v = \dfrac {9} {15}.


$$

Given that $v$ lies in the first quadrant, find the value of $\cos\left(u - v\right).$

#### Explanation

First, we recall the difference formula for cosine:

$$


\cos(u - v) = \cos u \cos v + \sin u \sin v


$$

We know the values of $\sin{u}$, $\cos{u}$, and $\sin{v}.$ To find the missing value of $\cos{v,}$ we can use the Pythagorean identity:

$$


\sin^2 v + \cos^2 v= 1 \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \cos^2 v = 1- \sin^2 v


$$

Substituting our values into the Pythagorean identity, we get

$$


\begin{aligned}cos^{2}⁡𝑣 & =1−sin^{2}⁡𝑣 \\ & =1−(\frac{9}{15})^{2} \\ & =1−\frac{9^{2}}{15^{2}} \\ & =1−\frac{81}{225} \\ & =\frac{225}{225}−\frac{81}{225} \\ & =\frac{144}{225}.\end{aligned}


$$

Therefore,

$$


\begin{aligned}cos⁡𝑣 & =±\sqrt{√\frac{144}{225}} \\ & =±\frac{12}{15}.\end{aligned}


$$

We're given that $v$ lies in the first quadrant, where cosine is positive. So, we disregard the negative value, and we have

$$


\cos v= \dfrac{12}{15}.


$$

We can now apply the difference formula:

$$


\begin{aligned}cos⁡(𝑢−𝑣) & =cos⁡𝑢cos⁡𝑣+sin⁡𝑢sin⁡𝑣 \\ & =(\frac{3}{5})(\frac{12}{15})+(\frac{4}{5})(\frac{9}{15}) \\ & =\frac{36}{75}+\frac{36}{75} \\ & =\frac{72}{75} \\ & =\frac{24}{25}\end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Computing the Cosine of a Composite Angle Using Angles in the Second, Third, and Fourth Quadrants

#### Question

Suppose that for the angles $u$ and $v,$ we have

$$


\sin u = -\dfrac {4} {5}, \qquad \cos v = \dfrac {15} {17}, \qquad \sin v = \dfrac {8} {17}.


$$

Given that $u$ lies in the third quadrant, find the value of $\cos\left(u + v\right).$

#### Explanation

First, we recall the sum formula for cosine:

$$


\cos(u + v) = \cos u \cos v - \sin u \sin v


$$

We know the values of $\sin{u}$, $\cos{v}$, and $\sin{v}.$ To find the missing value of $\cos{u,}$ we can use the Pythagorean identity:

$$


\sin^2 u + \cos^2 u= 1 \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \cos^2 u = 1- \sin^2 u


$$

Substituting our values into the Pythagorean identity, we get

$$


\begin{aligned}cos^{2}⁡𝑢 & =1−sin^{2}⁡𝑢 \\ & =1−(−\frac{4}{5})^{2} \\ & =1−\frac{4^{2}}{5^{2}} \\ & =1−\frac{16}{25} \\ & =\frac{25}{25}−\frac{16}{25} \\ & =\frac{9}{25}.\end{aligned}


$$

Therefore,

$$


\begin{aligned}cos⁡𝑢 & =±\sqrt{√\frac{9}{25}} \\ & =±\frac{3}{5}.\end{aligned}


$$

We're given that $u$ lies in the third quadrant, where cosine is negative. So, we disregard the positive value, and we have

$$


\cos u= -\dfrac{3}{5}.


$$

We can now apply the sum formula:

$$


\begin{aligned}cos⁡(𝑢+𝑣) & =cos⁡𝑢cos⁡𝑣−sin⁡𝑢sin⁡𝑣 \\ & =(−\frac{3}{5})(\frac{15}{17})−(−\frac{4}{5})(\frac{8}{17}) \\ & =−\frac{45}{85}+\frac{32}{85} \\ & =−\frac{13}{85}\end{aligned}


$$
