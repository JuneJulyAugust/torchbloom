## Lesson

### Introduction

Suppose we want to find all of the solutions to the equation

$$


0≤𝑥<2𝜋.


$$

To calculate a solution, we can reduce the equation to a more convenient form by dividing through by $\cos x,$ and then use the identity $\tan x = \dfrac{\sin x}{\cos x}$ to simplify. This gives

$$


\begin{aligned}sin⁡𝑥−\sqrt{3}cos⁡𝑥 & =0 \\ \frac{sin⁡𝑥}{cos⁡𝑥}−\frac{\sqrt{3}cos⁡𝑥}{cos⁡𝑥} & =0 \\ \frac{sin⁡𝑥}{cos⁡𝑥}−\frac{\sqrt{3}cos⁡𝑥}{cos⁡𝑥} & =0 \\ \frac{sin⁡𝑥}{cos⁡𝑥}−\sqrt{3} & =0 \\ tan⁡𝑥−\sqrt{3} & =0.\end{aligned}


$$

Now, we rearrange the equation and isolate $\tan x\mathbin{:}$

$$


\tan x = \sqrt{3}


$$

We can immediately calculate the principal value $x\mathbin{:}$

$$


x = \arctan(\sqrt{3}) = \dfrac{\pi}{3}


$$

Since $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ lies inside the given domain, our first solution is $x_1 = \dfrac{\pi}{3}.$

To find the other solution in the given interval, we add $\pi\mathbin{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑥_{2} & =𝑥_{1}+𝜋 \\ & =\frac{𝜋}{3}+𝜋 \\ & =\frac{4𝜋}{3}\end{aligned}


$$

So the solutions are $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3}, \dfrac{4\pi}{3}.$

### Example: Solving Trigonometric Equations Using the Sin-Cos-Tan Identity

#### Question

Calculate, in degrees to the nearest degree, the solutions to the equation $5\sin x = -7\cos x$ for $0^\circ \leq x < 360^\circ.$

#### Explanation

We divide each term of the equation by $\cos x$ and use the identity $\tan x = \dfrac{\sin x}{\cos x}.$

$$


\begin{aligned}5sin⁡𝑥 & =−7cos⁡𝑥 \\ \frac{5sin⁡𝑥}{cos⁡𝑥} & =−\frac{7cos⁡𝑥}{cos⁡𝑥} \\ \frac{5sin⁡𝑥}{cos⁡𝑥} & =−\frac{7cos⁡𝑥}{cos⁡𝑥} \\ 5⋅\frac{sin⁡𝑥}{cos⁡𝑥} & =−7 \\ 5tan⁡𝑥 & =−7\end{aligned}


$$

Now, we rearrange the equation and isolate $\tan x\mathbin{:}$

$$


\tan x = -\dfrac{7}{5}


$$

The length of the interval for $x$ is $2\pi$, so we will likely require $2$ solutions.

We can immediately calculate the principal value $x\mathbin{:}$

$$


x = \arctan\left(-\dfrac{7}{5}\right) \approx -54.46


$$

This value is outside the domain $0^\circ \leq x < 360^\circ.$ To get a value that's inside the domain, we add $180^\circ\mathbin{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑥_{1} & =arctan⁡(−\frac{7}{5})+180^{∘} \\ & ≈−54.46^{∘}+180^{∘} \\ & =125.54^{∘}\end{aligned}


$$

To find the other solution inside the given interval, we add $180^\circ$ to $x_1.$ Therefore,

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑥_{2} & =𝑥_{1}+180^{∘} \\ & ≈125.54^{∘}+180^{∘} \\ & =305.54^{∘}.\end{aligned}


$$

So the solutions, rounded to the nearest degree, are $x= 126^\circ, 306^\circ.$

### Solving Trigonometric Equations Using the Sin-Cos-Tan Identity Under a Modified Domain

Let's find all the solutions to the equation

$$


−𝜋<𝑥≤𝜋.


$$

Notice that this time, the domain is $-\pi < x \leq \pi.$

First, we divide each term of the equation by $\cos x$ and use the identity $\tan x = \dfrac{\sin x}{\cos x}$ to simplify. This gives

$$


\begin{aligned}sin⁡𝑥−cos⁡𝑥 & =0 \\ \frac{sin⁡𝑥}{cos⁡𝑥}−\frac{cos⁡𝑥}{cos⁡𝑥} & =0 \\ \frac{sin⁡𝑥}{cos⁡𝑥}−\frac{cos⁡𝑥}{cos⁡𝑥} & =0 \\ \frac{sin⁡𝑥}{cos⁡𝑥}−1 & =0 \\ tan⁡𝑥−1 & =0.\end{aligned}


$$

Now, we rearrange the equation and isolate $\tan x\mathbin{:}$

$$


\tan x = 1


$$

We can immediately calculate the principal value $x\mathbin{:}$

$$


x = \arctan(1) = \dfrac{\pi}{4}


$$

Since $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ lies inside the given domain, our first solution is $x_1 = \dfrac{\pi}{4}.$

To find the other solution in the given interval, we *subtract* $\pi.$ Hence:

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑥_{2} & =𝑥_{1}−𝜋 \\ & =\frac{𝜋}{4}−𝜋 \\ & =−\frac{3𝜋}{4}\end{aligned}


$$

So the solutions are $x = -\dfrac{3\pi}{4}, \dfrac{\pi}{4}.$

### Example: Solving Trigonometric Equations Using the Sin-Cos-Tan Identity Under a Modified Domain

#### Question

Calculate, in degrees to the nearest degree, the solutions to the equation $3\sin x + 12\cos x = 0$ for $-180^\circ < x \leq 180^\circ.$

#### Explanation

We divide each term of the equation by $\cos x$ and use the identity $\tan x = \dfrac{\sin x}{\cos x}.$

$$


\begin{aligned}3sin⁡𝑥+12cos⁡𝑥 & =0 \\ \frac{3sin⁡𝑥}{cos⁡𝑥}+\frac{12cos⁡𝑥}{cos⁡𝑥} & =0 \\ \frac{3sin⁡𝑥}{cos⁡𝑥}+\frac{12cos⁡𝑥}{cos⁡𝑥} & =0 \\ 3⋅\frac{sin⁡𝑥}{cos⁡𝑥}+12 & =0 \\ 3tan⁡𝑥+12 & =0\end{aligned}


$$

Now, we rearrange the equation and isolate $\tan x\mathbin{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}3tan⁡𝑥 & =−12 \\ tan⁡𝑥 & =−4\end{aligned}


$$

We can immediately calculate the principal value $x\mathbin{:}$

$$


x = \arctan(-4) \approx -75.96^\circ


$$

Since this value lies inside the given domain, our first solution is $x_1 = -75.96^\circ.$

To find the other solution in the given interval, we ** $180^\circ.$ Hence,

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑥_{2} & =𝑥_{1}+180^{∘} \\ & ≈−75.96^{∘}+180^{∘} \\ & =104.04^{∘}.\end{aligned}


$$

So the solutions, rounded to the nearest degree, are $x= -76^\circ, 104^\circ.$
