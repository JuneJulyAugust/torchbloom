## Lesson

### Introduction

Suppose we are given the equation $\log_3 x = 4.$ Can we determine $x?$

Recall that the inverse operation of the logarithm is exponentiation. So, to isolate $x,$ we must exponentiate both sides of this equation.

The base of the logarithm is $3,$ so we use a base of $3$ for the exponentiation as well:

$$



\begin{aligned}log_{3}⁡𝑥 & =4 \\ 3^{(log_{3}⁡𝑥)} & =3^{4}\end{aligned}



$$

The equation above may look complicated, but since exponentiation is the inverse function of the logarithm, we know that $3^{(\log_3{x})}=x.$ Therefore, we get

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑥 & =3^{4} \\ 𝑥 & =81.\end{aligned}



$$

We conclude that $x = 81$ is the solution.

### Example: Solving Logarithmic Equations

#### Question

If $\log_2 x = 5,$ then what is the value of $x?$

#### Explanation

To remove the logarithm, we exponentiate both sides of the equation. The base of the logarithm is $2,$ so we use a base of $2$ for the exponentiation as well:

$$



\begin{aligned}log_{2}⁡𝑥 & =5 \\ 2^{log_{2}⁡𝑥} & =2^{5}\end{aligned}



$$

Now, since exponentiation is the inverse function of the logarithm, we know that $2^{\log_2 x} = x.$

Therefore, we get

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑥 & =2^{5} \\ 𝑥 & =32.\end{aligned}



$$

### Example: Solving Logarithmic Equations With Linear Arguments

#### Question

If $\log_7 (2x + 1) = 1,$ then what is $x?$

#### Explanation

To remove the logarithm, we exponentiate both sides of the equation. The base of the logarithm is $7,$ so we use this base for the exponentiation as well:

$$



\begin{aligned}log_{7}⁡(2𝑥+1) & =1 \\ 7^{log_{7}⁡(2𝑥+1)} & =7^{1}\end{aligned}



$$

Now, since exponentiation is the inverse function of the logarithm, we know that $7^{\log_7 (2x + 1)} = 2x+1.$

Therefore, we get

$$



\begin{aligned}2𝑥+1 & =7 \\ 2𝑥 & =6 \\ 𝑥 & =3.\end{aligned}



$$

### Example: Solving Logarithmic Equations by First Rearranging the Equation

#### Question

Given that $2\log_5 (x-3)+1 = 5,$ then what is the value of $x?$

#### Explanation

First, we make $\log_5 (x-3)$ the subject of the equation by moving everything else to the right-hand side. This gives

$$



\begin{aligned}2log_{5}⁡(𝑥−3)+1 & =5 \\ 2log_{5}⁡(𝑥−3) & =4 \\ log_{5}⁡(𝑥−3) & =2.\end{aligned}



$$

To remove the logarithm, we exponentiate both sides of the equation. The base of the logarithm is $5,$ so we use a base of $5$ for the exponentiation as well:

$$



\begin{aligned}log_{5}⁡(𝑥−3) & =2 \\ 5^{log_{5}⁡(𝑥−3)} & =5^{2}\end{aligned}



$$

Now, since exponentiation is the inverse function of the logarithm, we know that $5^{\log_5 (x - 3)} = x-3.$

Therefore, we get

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑥−3 & =5^{2} \\ 𝑥−3 & =25 \\ 𝑥 & =28\end{aligned}



$$

### Example: Solving Logarithmic Equations by Combining Logarithmic Terms

#### Question

If $7\log_6{x} - 3\log_6{x} = 2,$ then what is the value of $x?$

#### Explanation

First, we simplify our equation by collecting the like logarithmic terms, and isolating the logarithm, as follows:

$$



\begin{aligned}7log_{6}⁡𝑥−3log_{6}⁡𝑥 & =2 \\ (7−3)log_{6}⁡𝑥 & =2 \\ 4log_{6}⁡𝑥 & =2 \\ log_{6}⁡𝑥 & =\frac{1}{2}\end{aligned}



$$

To remove the logarithm, we exponentiate both sides of the equation. The base of the logarithm is $6,$ so we use a base of $6$ for the exponentiation as well:

$$



\begin{aligned}log_{6}⁡𝑥 & =\frac{1}{2} \\ 6^{log_{6}⁡𝑥} & =6^{1/2}\end{aligned}



$$

Now, since exponentiation is the inverse function of the logarithm, we know that $6^{\log_6 x} = x.$

Therefore, we get

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑥 & =6^{1/2} \\ 𝑥 & =\sqrt{6}.\end{aligned}



$$
