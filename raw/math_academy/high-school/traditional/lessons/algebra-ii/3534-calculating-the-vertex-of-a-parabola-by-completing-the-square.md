## Lesson

### Introduction

Suppose we are given the parabola

$$



x^2 - 4x - 8y - 28 = 0,



$$

whose graph is shown below. How can we find the vertex of the parabola?

![Instructional graphic](../../../../lesson-assets/algebra-ii/topic-3534/53f1b0f157e752ce.png)

Recall that the standard equation of a *vertical* parabola that has its vertex at $(h,k)$ is

$$



(x-h)^2 = 4p(y-k),



$$

where $p$ is a constant.

To write our equation in standard form, we group the $x$-terms on the left-hand side and complete the square, as follows:

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑥^{2}−4𝑥−8𝑦−28 & =0 \\ 𝑥^{2}−4𝑥 & =8𝑦+28 \\ (𝑥−2)^{2}−4 & =8𝑦+28 \\ (𝑥−2)^{2} & =8𝑦+32 \\ (𝑥−2)^{2} & =8(𝑦+4) \\ (𝑥−2)^{2} & =4(2)(𝑦−(−4))\end{aligned}



$$

Therefore, the coordinates of the vertex are $(h,k) = ({\color{blue}2},{\color{red}-4}).$

### Example: Calculating the Vertex of a Vertical Parabola by Completing the Square

#### Question

Calculate the coordinates of the vertex of the parabola $5x^2 -10x - 4y - 7 = 0.$

#### Explanation

The standard equation of an upward- or downward-opening parabola that has its vertex at $(h,k)$ is

$$



(x-h)^2 = 4p(y-k),



$$

where $p$ is a constant.

To write the given equation in this form, we need to group the $x$-terms on the left-hand side and complete the square, as follows:

$$



\begin{aligned}5𝑥^{2}−10𝑥−4𝑦−7 & =0 \\ 5𝑥^{2}−10𝑥 & =4𝑦+7 \\ 5[𝑥^{2}−2𝑥] & =4𝑦+7 \\ 5[(𝑥−1)^{2}−1] & =4𝑦+7 \\ 5(𝑥−1)^{2}−5 & =4𝑦+7 \\ 5(𝑥−1)^{2} & =4𝑦+7+5 \\ 5(𝑥−1)^{2} & =4𝑦+12 \\ 5(𝑥−1)^{2} & =4(𝑦+3) \\ (𝑥−1)^{2} & =\frac{4}{5}(𝑦+3) \\ (𝑥−1)^{2} & =4(\frac{1}{5})(𝑦+3)\end{aligned}



$$

Therefore, the coordinates of the vertex are $(h,k) = (1,-3).$

### Example: Calculating the Vertex of a Horizontal Parabola by Completing the Square

#### Question

Calculate the coordinates of the vertex of the parabola $y^2 - 8x - 6y + 33 = 0$.

#### Explanation

The standard equation of a left- or right-opening parabola that has its vertex at $(h,k)$ is

$$



(y-k)^2 = 4p(x-h),



$$

where $p$ is a constant.

To write the given equation in this form, we need to group the $y$-terms on the left-hand side and complete the square, as follows:

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑦^{2}−8𝑥−6𝑦+33 & =0 \\ 𝑦^{2}−6𝑦 & =8𝑥−33 \\ (𝑦−3)^{2}−9 & =8𝑥−33 \\ (𝑦−3)^{2} & =8𝑥−33+9 \\ (𝑦−3)^{2} & =8𝑥−24 \\ (𝑦−3)^{2} & =8(𝑥−3) \\ (𝑦−3)^{2} & =4(2)(𝑥−3)\end{aligned}



$$

Therefore, the coordinates of the vertex are $(h,k) = (3,3).$
