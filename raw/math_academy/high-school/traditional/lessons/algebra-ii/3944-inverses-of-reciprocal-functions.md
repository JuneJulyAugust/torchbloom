## Lesson

### Introduction

In this lesson, we'll learn how to compute inverses of reciprocal functions.

As an example, let's consider the function $f(x),$ given by

$$



f(x) = \dfrac{7}{2x+4}.



$$

To find the inverse $f^{-1}(x),$ we start by replacing $f(x)$ with $y{:}$

$$



y = \dfrac{7}{2x+4}



$$

Now, interchanging $x$ and $y$ gives

$$



x = \dfrac{7}{2y+4}.



$$

Then, we make $y$ the subject of the equation as follows:

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑥 & =\frac{7}{2𝑦+4} \\ 𝑥(2𝑦+4) & =7 \\ 2𝑥𝑦+4𝑥 & =7 \\ 2𝑥𝑦 & =7−4𝑥 \\ 𝑦 & =\frac{7−4𝑥}{2𝑥}\end{aligned}



$$

Therefore, the inverse function is

$$



f^{-1}(x) = \dfrac{7 - 4x}{2x}.



$$

### Example: Finding the Inverse of a Reciprocal Function

#### Question

The function $f(x) = \dfrac{4}{(x-1)^2}$ is defined for $x >1.$ Find $f^{-1}(x).$

#### Explanation

To find $f^{-1}(x),$ we start by replacing $f(x)$ with $y{:}$

$$



y = \dfrac{4}{(x-1)^2}



$$

Now, interchanging $x$ and $y$ gives

$$



x = \dfrac{4}{(y-1)^2}.



$$

Then, we make $y$ the subject of the equation, as follows:

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑥 & =\frac{4}{(𝑦−1)^{2}} \\ 𝑥⋅(𝑦−1)^{2} & =4 \\ (𝑦−1)^{2} & =\frac{4}{𝑥} \\ \sqrt{(𝑦−1)^{2}} & =\sqrt{\frac{4}{𝑥}} \\ |𝑦−1| & =\frac{2}{\sqrt{𝑥}} \\ 𝑦−1 & =\frac{2}{\sqrt{𝑥}} \\ 𝑦 & =\frac{2}{\sqrt{𝑥}}+1\end{aligned}



$$

Note that we used the fact that for $y > 1,$ we have

$$



|y-1| = y-1.



$$

Therefore, the inverse function is

$$



f^{-1}(x) = \dfrac{2}{\sqrt{x}} + 1.



$$

### Example: Finding the Inverse of a Simple Rational Function

#### Question

Find the inverse of the function $f(x) = \dfrac{2-x}{x+3}.$

#### Explanation

To find $f^{-1}(x),$ we start by replacing $f(x)$ with $y{:}$

$$



y = \dfrac{2-x}{x+3}



$$

Now, interchanging $x$ and $y$ gives

$$



x = \dfrac{2-y}{y+3}.



$$

Then, we make $y$ the subject of the equation, as follows:

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑥 & =\frac{2−𝑦}{𝑦+3} \\ 𝑥(𝑦+3) & =2−𝑦 \\ 𝑥𝑦+3𝑥 & =2−𝑦 \\ 𝑥𝑦+𝑦 & =2−3𝑥 \\ 𝑦(𝑥+1) & =2−3𝑥 \\ 𝑦 & =\frac{2−3𝑥}{𝑥+1}\end{aligned}



$$

Therefore, the inverse function is

$$



f^{-1}(x) = \dfrac{2-3x}{x+1}.



$$

### Example: Determining an Unknown Value Given the Inverse of a Rational Function

#### Question

The function $f(x),$ defined as

$$



f(x) = \dfrac{kx + 6}{x-4}, \qquad x\neq 4



$$

has an inverse function given by

$$



f^{-1}(x) = \dfrac{4x+6}{x-2}, \qquad x\neq 2.



$$

What is the value of $k?$

#### Explanation

To find $f^{-1}(x),$ we start by replacing $f(x)$ with $y{:}$

$$



y = \dfrac{kx + 6}{x-4}



$$

Now, interchanging $x$ and $y$ gives

$$



x = \dfrac{ky+6}{y-4}.



$$

Then, we make $y$ the subject of the equation, as follows:

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑥 & =\frac{𝑘𝑦+6}{𝑦−4} \\ 𝑥(𝑦−4) & =𝑘𝑦+6 \\ 𝑥𝑦−4𝑥 & =𝑘𝑦+6 \\ 𝑥𝑦−𝑘𝑦 & =4𝑥+6 \\ 𝑦(𝑥−𝑘) & =4𝑥+6 \\ 𝑦 & =\frac{4𝑥+6}{𝑥−𝑘}\end{aligned}



$$

Therefore, the inverse function is

$$



f^{-1}(x) = \dfrac{4x+6}{x-k}.



$$

Comparing our answer with the given expression, we conclude that $k=2.$
