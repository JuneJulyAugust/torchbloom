### Introduction

Given an equation involving complex numbers, we can always equate real and imaginary parts to give two separate equations.

For example, suppose we're given the equation

$$



2 + 9 \text{i} = 2x + 3y \text{i}.



$$

The real part of $2 + 9 \text{i}$ is $2,$ and the real part of $2x + 3y \text{i}$ is $2x.$ Equating the real parts, we get

$$



\begin{aligned}2 & =2𝑥 \\ 𝑥 & =1.\end{aligned}



$$

The imaginary part of $2 + 9 \text{i}$ is $9,$ and the imaginary part of $2x + 3y \text{i}$ is $3y.$ Equating the imaginary parts, we get

$$



\begin{aligned}9 & =3𝑦 \\ 𝑦 & =3.\end{aligned}



$$

Therefore, $x=1$ and $y=3.$

### Example: Equating Real and Imaginary Parts of a Complex Number to Solve For an Unknown Quantity

#### Question

Given that $3+2\text{i} = a + \text{i}b,$ calculate the value of $a$ and the value of $b.$

#### Explanation

First, we equate the real parts:

$$



3 = a



$$

Then, we equate the imaginary parts:

$$



2 = b



$$

Therefore, $a=3$ and $b=2.$

### Example: Solving for Unknowns By Computing a Quotient and Equating Real and Imaginary Parts

#### Question

Given that $3+5\text{i} = (p+\text{i}q)(1+\text{i}),$ calculate $p$ and $q.$

#### Explanation

First, we divide both sides of the equation by $1+\text{i}$ to isolate the variables:

$$



\begin{aligned}3+5i & =(𝑝+i𝑞)(1+i) \\ \frac{3+5i}{1+i} & =𝑝+i𝑞\end{aligned}



$$

Now, to simplify the fraction on the left-hand side, we multiply the numerator and denominator by the conjugate of the denominator:

$$



\begin{aligned}\frac{(3+5i)(1−i)}{(1+i)(1−i)} & =𝑝+i𝑞 \\ \frac{3−3i+5i−5i^{2}}{1^{2}−i^{2}} & =𝑝+i𝑞 \\ \frac{3+2i−5(−1)}{1−(−1)} & =𝑝+i𝑞 \\ \frac{8+2i}{2} & =𝑝+i𝑞 \\ 4+i & =𝑝+i𝑞\end{aligned}



$$

Now, we can equate the real parts and the imaginary parts separately.

- Equating the real parts, we get $4=p.$

- Equating the imaginary parts, we get $1=q.$

Therefore, $p=4$ and $q=1.$
