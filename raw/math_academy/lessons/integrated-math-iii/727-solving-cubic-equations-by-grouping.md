## Lesson

### Introduction

We have learned how to solve quadratic equations by factoring. Just like quadratic equations, some (but not all) cubic equations can also be solved by factoring.

To solve a cubic equation by factoring, we just need to follow three steps:

1. Write the equation with $0$ on one side

2. Factor the expression on the other side

3. Set each factor equal to $0$ and solve

To factor the cubic, we can often use the method of grouping.

**Note:** The cubic equations in the following examples can all be factored, but keep in mind that most cubic equations cannot be!

### Example: Solving Cubic Equations by Grouping

#### Question

Solve $x^3+x^2-9x-9=0.$

#### Explanation

We proceed to factor the left-hand side by grouping:

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑥^{3}+𝑥^{2}−9𝑥−9 & = \\ (𝑥^{3}+𝑥^{2})−(9𝑥+9) & = \\ 𝑥^{2}(𝑥+1)−9(𝑥+1) & = \\ (𝑥+1)⋅𝑥^{2}−(𝑥+1)⋅9 & = \\ (𝑥+1)(𝑥^{2}−9) & \end{aligned}


$$

Then, we solve $(x+1)(x^2-9) = 0$ by setting each factor equal to $0.$

- Setting the factor $(x+1)$ equal to $0,$ we get

- Setting the factor $(x^2-9)$ equal to $0,$ we get

Therefore, the solutions are $x=-1, \, \pm 3.$

### Example: Solving Cubic Equations With Leading Coefficients by Grouping

#### Question

Solve $2x^3+3x^2-8x-12=0.$

#### Explanation

We proceed to factor the left side by grouping:

$$


\begin{aligned}2𝑥^{3}+3𝑥^{2}−8𝑥−12 & = \\ (2𝑥^{3}+3𝑥^{2})−(8𝑥+12) & = \\ 𝑥^{2}(2𝑥+3)−4(2𝑥+3) & = \\ (2𝑥+3)⋅𝑥^{2}−(2𝑥+3)⋅4 & = \\ (2𝑥+3)(𝑥^{2}−4) & \end{aligned}


$$

Then, we solve $(2x+3)(x^2-4) = 0$ by setting each factor equal to $0.$

- Setting the factor $(2x+3)$ equal to $0,$ we get

- Setting the factor $(x^2-4)$ equal to $0,$ we get

Therefore, the solutions are $x=-2, \; -\dfrac{3}{2}, \; 2.$

### Example: Solving Cubic Equations With Negative Leading Coefficients by Grouping

#### Question

Solve $-x^3-4x^2+5x+20=0.$

#### Explanation

First, we multiply the equation by $-1$ to make the coefficient of the cubic term positive. This gives

$$


x^3+4x^2-5x-20=0.


$$

We then proceed to factor the left-hand side by grouping:

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑥^{3}+4𝑥^{2}−5𝑥−20 & =0 \\ 𝑥^{2}(𝑥+4)−5(𝑥+4) & =0 \\ (𝑥^{2}−5)(𝑥+4) & =0\end{aligned}


$$

Then, we solve $(x^2-5)(x+4)=0$ by setting each factor equal to $0.$

- Setting the factor $(x^2-5)$ equal to $0,$ we get

- Setting the factor $(x+4)$ equal to $0,$ we get

Therefore, the solutions are $x=-4, \pm \sqrt 5.$

### Example: Solving Cubic Equations by Manipulating the Equation and Then Grouping

#### Question

Solve $8x^3+3=4x^2+6x.$

#### Explanation

First, we subtract $4x^2$ and $6x$ from both sides to make the right side $0.$ To prepare for factoring by grouping, we make sure to write the resulting cubic in standard form.

$$


8x^3-4x^2-6x+3=0


$$

Next, we factor by grouping. Notice that when grouping the smaller terms, we factor out the negative sign.

$$


\begin{aligned}8𝑥^{3}−4𝑥^{2}−6𝑥+3 & = \\ (8𝑥^{3}−4𝑥^{2})−(6𝑥−3) & = \\ 4𝑥^{2}(2𝑥−1)−3(2𝑥−1) & = \\ (2𝑥−1)⋅4𝑥^{2}−(2𝑥−1)⋅3 & = \\ (2𝑥−1)(4𝑥^{2}−3). & \end{aligned}


$$

Finally, we set each factor equal to $0$ and solve.

- Setting the factor $(2x-1)$ equal to $0,$ we get

- Setting the factor $(4x^2-3)$ equal to $0,$ we get

Therefore, the solutions are $x= -\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}, \; \dfrac12, \; \dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}.$
