## Lesson

### Introduction

Remember that when we multiply a decimal by a power of ten, we shift the decimal place to the right by the number of zeros in the power of ten.

When the power of ten is written in exponent form, the exponent tells us the number of places to shift the decimal point.

For instance, to find $47 \times 10^{1}$, we first rewrite $10^1$ as $1$ followed by a block of $1$ zero:

$$
1
$$

Since we're multiplying by $10$ and $10$ has $\color{blue} 1$ zero, we move the decimal point $\color{blue}1$ place to the right.

$$
47.0 \rightarrow 470
$$

Therefore,

$$
47 \times 10^{1} = 470 \, .
$$

### Example: Multiplying a Two-Digit Decimal by a Power of Ten

#### Question

Find an expression equivalent to $9.8 \times 10^{2}.$

#### Explanation

First, we rewrite $10^2$ as $1$ followed by a block of $2$ zeros:

$$
2
$$

Since we're multiplying by $100$ and $100$ has $\color{blue} 2$ zeros, we move the decimal point $\color{blue}2$ places to the right:

$$
9.8 \rightarrow 980.0 = 980
$$

Therefore,

$$
9.8 \times 10^{2} = 980 \, .
$$
