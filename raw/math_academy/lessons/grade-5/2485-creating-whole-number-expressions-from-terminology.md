### Introduction

We can express operations in a variety of different ways.

For example, $4+2$ can be written as:

- Add $4$ and $2$

- The **sum** of $4$ and $2$

- The **total** of $4$ and $2$

Likewise, the expression $4-2$ can be written as:

- $4$ minus $2$

- Subtract $2$ from $4$

- The **difference** of $4$ and $2$

### Using Special Words to Represent Multiplication and Division

We also have some special words that represent multiplication and division.

The expression $4\times 2$ can be written as:

- $2$ multiplied by $4$ (or $4$ multiplied by $2$)

- The **product** of $2$ and $4$ (or the product of $4$ and $2$)

Likewise, the expression $4\div 2$ can be written as:

- The **quotient** of $4$ and $2$

- Divide $4$ by $2$

- $4$ divided by $2$

### Using Parentheses

When we have two operations within an expression, we can use parentheses to indicate which operation comes first.

For example, consider the statement "*the sum of $3$ and $4,$ multiplied by $5.$*"

Let's break it down:

**Step 1.** "*The sum of $3$ and $4$*" means:

$$
3 + 4
$$

**Step 2.** Since this operation comes first, we use parentheses:

$$
(3+4)
$$

**Step 3.** The "*... multiplied by $5$*" means that we should multiply the above expression by $5.$ So we get:

$$
(3+4) \times 5
$$

### Example: Using Special Words with Two Operations

#### Question

What expression represents "**"?

#### Explanation

"**" means:

$$
12 \div 6
$$

Since this operation comes first, we use parentheses:

$$
(12 \div 6)
$$

"**" means that we should add $3$ to the above expression. So we get:

$$
(12 \div 6) + 3
$$
