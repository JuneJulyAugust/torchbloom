## Lesson

### Introduction

It's important to interpret quotients and remainders when they arise in real-life problems.

Consider the following situation:

*Clare must pack $64$ candy bars into boxes. Each box can contain $5$ candy bars. She wishes to create as many fully-filled boxes as possible.*

Let's answer the following questions:

1. How many fully-filled boxes will there be?

2. How many candy bars will there be in the partially-filled box?

3. How many boxes will there be in total?

Since the $64$ candy bars are distributed into boxes containing $5$ bars each, we start by dividing $64$ by $5$ using the long division method:

The result of the long division is

$$
64 \div 5 = {\color{blue}{12}}\,\text{R}\,{\color{red}{4}}.
$$

Let's now answer our questions by interpreting this result:

- The quotient of the division is ${\color{blue}{12}}.$ This means that there will be ${\color{blue}{12}}$ fully-filled boxes.

- The remainder of the division is ${\color{red}{4}}.$ This means that there will be ${\color{red}{4}}$ candy bars in the partially-filled box.

- Since we have ${\color{blue}{12}}$ fully-filled boxes and one partially-filled box, there will be ${\color{blue}{12}}+1 = 13$ boxes in total.

Let's see some more examples.

### Example: Interpreting Remainders

#### Question

A manufacturer of bias tape must package $9,252$ yards of tape into rolls containing $5$ yards each. After packaging, how many yards of tape will be left over?

#### Explanation

To determine how many yards of the tape will be left, we need to find the remainder of $9,252\div 5.$

To divide $9,252$ by $5,$ we use long division:

Therefore,

$$
9,252 \div 5 = 1,850 \,\text{R}\, {\color{blue}{2}}.
$$

So, $1,850$ rolls of tape will be packaged into rolls, and $\color{blue}2$ yards will be left over.

### Example: Interpreting Quotients

#### Question

An architect orders $563$ boards for a project. If the boards they need come in packs of $4,$ how many fully-filled packs will they receive?

#### Explanation

To find out the number of fully-filled packs the architect will receive, we need to compute the quotient of $563 \div 4.$

To divide $563$ by $4,$ we use long division:

Therefore,

$$
563 \div 4 = {\color{blue}{140}}\,\text{R}\,3.
$$

So, the architect will receive ${\color{blue}{140}}$ fully-filled packs.

### Example: Adjusting a Quotient When a Remainder is Present

#### Question

Deborah, a clothing company employee, has to pack $112$ shirts into boxes. If each box can hold up to $6$ shirts, what is the smallest number of boxes that she needs to pack the shirts?

#### Explanation

To figure out the smallest number of boxes Deborah needs, we need to divide $112\div 6.$

To divide $112$ by $6,$ we use long division:

Therefore,

$$
112 \div 6 = 18 \,\text{R}\, {\color{blue}{4}}.
$$

This means that Deborah will fill $18$ boxes and have ${\color{blue}{4}}$ shirts left over.

To hold the leftover shirts, Deborah will need an additional box. Therefore, she will need $18+1 =19$ boxes in total.
