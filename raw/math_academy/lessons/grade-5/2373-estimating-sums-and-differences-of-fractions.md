## Lesson

### Introduction

We can *estimate* fraction addition and subtraction by rounding each fraction to a **benchmark**. Usually, we use the following benchmarks:

$$
0,\qquad \dfrac{1}{2}, \qquad 1.
$$

When comparing fractions with benchmarks, there are typically three cases:

- If the numerator is *much smaller* than the denominator, such as with $\dfrac{1}{6},$ we round the fraction to $0$.

- If the numerator is *about one half* of the denominator, such as with $\dfrac{3}{8},$ we round the fraction to $\dfrac 1 2$.

- If the numerator is *very close* to the denominator, such as with $\dfrac{5}{6},$ we round the fraction to $1$.

Let's check out some examples.
