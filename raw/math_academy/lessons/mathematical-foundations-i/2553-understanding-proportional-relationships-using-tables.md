### Introduction

Two variables, $x$ and $y,$ are said to have a **proportional relationship** if their ratio is constant. That is, for any $(x,y)$ pair, we have

$$


k = \dfrac{y}{x}


$$

where $k$ is a fixed number called the **constant of proportionality** or **constant of variation.**

### Example: Computing a Constant of Proportionality

#### Question

The table above shows a proportional relationship. Assuming that $y=kx$ for each pair of values, what is the constant of proportionality?

#### Explanation

We're told that $x$ and $y$ have a proportional relationship.

To find the constant of proportionality, we first need to choose a point $(x,y)$ from the table. Let's choose the point $(6,4),$ which corresponds to the first row of the table.

Now, we can find the constant of proportionality by computing the ratio of the $y$-coordinate to the $x$-coordinate, as follows:

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑘 & =\frac{𝑦}{𝑥} \\ & =\frac{4}{6} \\ & =\frac{4÷2}{6÷2} \\ & =\frac{2}{3}\end{aligned}


$$

Therefore, the constant of proportionality is $k=\dfrac{2}{3}.$

### Finding the Equation of a Proportional Relationship

When the variables $x$ and $y$ form a proportional relationship, the ratio of any $(x,y)$ pair is constant:

$$


\dfrac y x = k


$$

Using the relationship between multiplication and division, we can rewrite this equation above as follows:

$$


y = kx


$$

### Example: Finding a Proportional Relationship Equation

#### Question

Merlin is making sandwiches for a party. There is a proportional relationship between the time $x$ (in minutes) he spends preparing sandwiches and the number of sandwiches $y$ he makes.

What is the equation for the relationship between $x$ and $y?$

#### Explanation

We're told that $x$ and $y$ have a proportional relationship.

Every proportional relationship can be written in the form $y=kx,$ where $k$ is the constant of proportionality.

To compute the constant of proportionality, we first need to choose a point $(x,y)$ from the table. Let's choose the point $(5,4),$ which corresponds to the first row of the table.

Now, we can find the constant of proportionality as follows:

$$


k = \dfrac{y}{x} = \dfrac{4}{5}


$$

Therefore, the equation relating $x$ and $y$ is

$$


y = \dfrac{4}{5} x.


$$

### Example: Interpreting Data From Proportional Relationships

#### Question

Jill likes to listen to music while she runs. The following table shows a proportional relationship between the distance $d$ (in kilometers) she runs and the number of songs $s$ she listens to.

Which of the following statements are true?

1. The unit rate is $\dfrac32$ songs per kilometer.

2. If Jill runs for $1$ kilometer, she'll listen to $1.5$ songs.

3. If Jill runs for $12$ kilometers, she'll listen to $15$ songs.

#### Explanation

We're told that $d$ and $s$ have a proportional relationship.

To find the constant of proportionality, we first need to choose a point $(d,s)$ from the table. Let's choose the point $(2,3),$ which corresponds to the first row of the table.

We can find the constant of proportionality as follows:

$$


k = \dfrac{s}{d} = \dfrac{3}{2}


$$

The units of $k$ are "songs per kilometer" since we divided the number of songs $s$ by the distance $d.$ Therefore, the relationship between $d$ and $s$ is

$$


s=\dfrac32 d.


$$

With that in mind, let's look at each statement.

- Statement I is true. We found above that $k =\dfrac32$ songs per kilometer.

- Statement II is true. We know that $k=\dfrac32,$ which means that if Jill runs for $1$ kilometer, she'll listen to $\dfrac32 = 1.5$ songs.

- Statement III is false. If we substitute $d=12$ into the relationship $s=\dfrac32 d,$ we get which means that she'll listen to $18$ songs if she runs for $12$ kilometers.

Therefore, the correct answer is "I and II only."
