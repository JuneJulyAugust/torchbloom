### Example: Factoring Trinomial With Positive Coefficients

#### Question

Factor the quadratic expression $10a^2 + 11a + 3.$

#### Explanation

We must factor the quadratic expression

$$


10a^2 + 11a + 3.


$$

First, we find two numbers that

- multiply to the product of the quadratic coefficient and the constant ($10 \times 3 = 30$), and

- add to the linear coefficient ($11$).

Since all coefficients are positive, we only need to consider the positive factor pairs of $30.$

The positive factor pairs of $30$ are

$$


(1,30), \quad (2,15), \quad (3,10), \quad (5,6).


$$

Notice the numbers in the factor pair $(5,6)$ add to $11{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}5+6 & =11\,✓\end{aligned}


$$

Then, we write the linear term as $5a + 6a,$ and factor the result by grouping:

$$


\begin{aligned}10𝑎^{2}+11𝑎+3 & = \\ 10𝑎^{2}+5𝑎+6𝑎+3 & = \\ (10𝑎^{2}+5𝑎)+(6𝑎+3) & = \\ 5𝑎(2𝑎+1)+3(2𝑎+1) & = \\ (5𝑎+3)(2𝑎+1) & \end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Factoring Trinomials With Negative Constants

#### Question

Factor the quadratic expression $2c^2 + 15c - 8.$

#### Explanation

We must factor the quadratic expression

$$


2c^2 + 15c - 8.


$$

First, we find two numbers that

- multiply to the product of the quadratic coefficient and the constant ($2 \times -8 = -16$), and

- add to the linear coefficient ($15$).

First, we find the factor pairs of $-16{:}$

$$


(1,-16),\quad (-1,16), \quad (2,-8), \quad (-2,8), \quad (4,-4)


$$

Notice the numbers in the factor pair $(-1,16)$ add to $15{:}$

$$


(-1) + 16 = 15 \quad {\color{green}\checkmark}


$$

Then, rewrite the linear term as $- c+16c,$ and factor the result by grouping:

$$


\begin{aligned}2𝑐^{2}+15𝑐−8 & = \\ 2𝑐^{2}−𝑐+16𝑐−8 & = \\ (2𝑐^{2}−𝑐)+(16𝑐−8) & = \\ 𝑐(2𝑐−1)+8(2𝑐−1) & = \\ (2𝑐−1)(𝑐+8) & \end{aligned}


$$

### Trinomials With Negative Linear Coefficients and Positive Constants

To factor a quadratic expression, we must find a factor pair of the *product* of the quadratic coefficient and the constant that adds to the linear coefficient.

With that in mind, let's factor the following trinomial:

$$


{\color{red}{2}}y^2 {\color{purple}{\,-\,7}}y + {\color{blue}{3}}


$$

Notice that,

- the product of the quadratic coefficient and the constant, ${\color{red}{2}} \times {\color{blue}{3}} = 6$, is *positive*, while

- the coefficient of the linear term $({\color{purple}{-7}})$ is *negative*.

Since the linear coefficient is negative and the product of the quadratic coefficient and constant is positive, we need to consider the *negative* factor pairs of $6.$

The negative factor pairs of $6$ are

$$


(-1,-6), \quad (-2,-3).


$$

For each pair, their *product* is positive, and their sum is *negative*, which is what we want.

Now, we determine which of these factor pairs sums to the coefficient of the linear term $({\color{purple}{-7}}){:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}(−1)+(−6) & =−7\,✓ \\ (−2)+(−3) & =−5\,×\end{aligned}


$$

Then, we write the linear term as $-y - 6y,$ and factor the result by grouping:

$$


\begin{aligned}2𝑦^{2}−7𝑦+3 & = \\ 2𝑦^{2}−𝑦−6𝑦+3 & = \\ (2𝑦^{2}−𝑦)−(6𝑦−3) & = \\ 𝑦(2𝑦−1)−3(2𝑦−1) & = \\ (𝑦−3)(2𝑦−1) & \end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Factoring Trinomials With Negative Coefficients and Positive Constants

#### Question

Factor the expression $3x^2 - 16x + 16.$

#### Explanation

We must factor the quadratic expression

$$


3x^2 - 16x + 16.


$$

First, we find two numbers that

- multiply to the product of the quadratic coefficient and the constant ($3 \times 16 = 48$), and

- add to the linear coefficient ($-16$).

Since the linear coefficient is negative and the product of the quadratic coefficient and constant is positive, we only need to consider the negative factor pairs of $48.$

The negative factor pairs of $48$ are

$$


(-1,-48), \quad (-2,-24), \quad (-3,-16), \quad (-4,-12), \quad (-6,-8).


$$

Notice the numbers in the factor pair $(-4,-12)$ add to $-16{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}(−4)+(−12) & =−16\,✓\end{aligned}


$$

Then, we write the linear term as $-4x - 12x,$ and factor the result by grouping:

$$


\begin{aligned}3𝑥^{2}−16𝑥+16 & = \\ 3𝑥^{2}−4𝑥−12𝑥+16 & = \\ (3𝑥^{2}−4𝑥)−(12𝑥−16) & = \\ 𝑥(3𝑥−4)−4(3𝑥−4) & = \\ (𝑥−4)(3𝑥−4) & \end{aligned}


$$
