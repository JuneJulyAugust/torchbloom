## Lesson

### Introduction

In a previous lesson, we saw how to apply a percentage increase to a number to get a new, larger number. In this lesson, we’ll work backwards, finding the *original value* when we know the percentage increase and the new value.

For example, suppose that after a $35\%$ increase, a conference registration fee is $405.$ What was the original fee?

Increasing a number by $35\%$ means that we must apply a new percentage of

$$


100\% + 35\% = 135\%.


$$

Converting this percentage to a decimal, we have

$$


135\%=\frac{135}{100}=1.35.


$$

Now, let the original fee be $x.$ Then, after a $35\%$ increase, the new value is

$$


1.35x.


$$

We are told that the increased value is $405.$ So, we set them equal and solve for $x{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}1.35𝑥 & =405 \\ 𝑥 & =\frac{405}{1.35} \\ 𝑥 & =300\end{aligned}


$$

Therefore, the original fee was $300.$

We can check that this answer is correct by applying a $35\%$ increase to $300{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}300×(100\%+35\%) & =300×135\% \\ & =300×1.35 \\ & =405\,✓\end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Finding an Original Value Given a Percentage Increase

#### Question

After increasing a quantity $x$ by $20\%,$ the result is $102.$ What is the value of $x?$

#### Explanation

Increasing a number by $20\%$ means that we must apply a percentage of

$$


100\% + 20\% = 120\%.


$$

Converting this percentage to a decimal, we have

$$


120\% = \dfrac{120}{100} = 1.20.


$$

We're told that by increasing a number $x$ by $20\%,$ we get the number $102.$ So, we have the equation

$$


1.20x = 102.


$$

Now, we solve for $x{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑥 & =\frac{102}{1.20} \\ & =85\end{aligned}


$$

So, we have $x= 85.$

### Example: Finding an Original Value Given a Percentage Increase Greater Than 100%

#### Question

After increasing a number $x$ by $1{,}900\%,$ the result is $740.$ What is the value of $x?$

#### Explanation

Increasing a number by $1{,}900\%$ means that we must apply a percentage of

$$


100\% + 1{,}900\% = 2{,}000\%.


$$

Converting this percentage to a decimal, we have

$$


2{,}000\% = \dfrac{2{,}000}{100} = 20.


$$

Let $x$ be the original number. Since the increased number equals $740,$ we have the equation

$$


20x = 740.


$$

Now, we solve for $x{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑥 & =\frac{740}{20} \\ & =37\end{aligned}


$$

So, the value of $x$ is $37.$

### Example: Finding Original Values Given a Percentage Increase in Context

#### Question

A farmer counted crates of harvested produce. In week $2,$ there were $1{,}044$ crates. The percent increase from week $1$ to week $2$ was $45\%.$ How many crates were there in week $1?$

#### Explanation

Increasing a number by $45\%$ means that we must apply a new percentage of

$$


100\% + 45\% = 145\%.


$$

Converting this percentage to a decimal, we have

$$


145\% = \dfrac{145}{100} = 1.45.


$$

Let $x$ represent the number of crates in week $1.$ So, after a $45\%$ increase, the new value is

$$


1.45x.


$$

We are told the increased value is $1{,}044.$ So, we set them equal and solve for $x{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}1.45𝑥 & =1,044 \\ 𝑥 & =\frac{1,044}{1.45} \\ 𝑥 & =720\end{aligned}


$$

So, there were $720$ crates in week $1.$
