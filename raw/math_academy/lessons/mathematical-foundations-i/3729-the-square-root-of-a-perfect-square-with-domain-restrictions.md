## Lesson

### Introduction

The square root of a squared quantity can be simplified using absolute value.

$$


\sqrt{x^2} = |x|


$$

However, if the domain of the variable is restricted, some expressions might allow further simplification.

For example, suppose we know that $a>0.$ Let's use this information to fully simplify $\sqrt{36a^2}.$

We can start by simplifying the expression as follows:

$$


\begin{aligned}\sqrt{√36𝑎^{2}} & = \\ \sqrt{√(6𝑎)^{2}} & = \\ |6𝑎| & = \\ |6|⋅|𝑎| & \\ 6|𝑎| & \end{aligned}


$$

We are told that $a>0,$ and we know that the absolute value of any positive number is just itself, so we have $|a|=a.$ Therefore, our final answer is

$$


6|a| = 6a .


$$

### Example: Simplifying an Expression Given That Some Variables Are Non-Negative

#### Question

Simplify $\sqrt{\dfrac{r^2}{s^2}}$ given that $s>0.$

#### Explanation

The square root of a squared quantity can be simplified using absolute value:

$$


\sqrt{x^2} = |x|


$$

In our case, we can simplify the given expression as follows:

$$


\begin{aligned} \sqrt{\dfrac{r^2}{s^2}} &= \\\[5pt] \sqrt{\dfrac{r}{s} \cdot \dfrac{r}{s}} &= \\\[5pt] \sqrt{\left(\dfrac{r}{s}\right)^2} &= \\\[5pt] \left|\dfrac{r}{s}\right| &= \\\[5pt] \dfrac{|r|}{|s|} & \end{aligned}


$$

We are told that $s \gt 0,$ and we know that the absolute value of any positive number is just itself, so we have $|s| = s.$ Therefore, our final answer is

$$


\dfrac{|r|}{|s|} = \dfrac{|r|}{s} .


$$

### The Square Root of a Perfect Square With Negative Variables

Let's see what changes if we consider square roots of perfect squares when some variables are negative.

For example, suppose that $t \lt 0.$ Let's use this to simplify the expression $\sqrt{t^2}.$

Simplifying this expression in the usual way, we obtain

$$


\begin{aligned} \sqrt{t^2}&=\\\[5pt] \sqrt{(t)^2}&=\\\[5pt] |t|.& \end{aligned}


$$

We're given that $t \lt 0.$ To take the absolute value of a negative number, we need to flip the sign. This gives $|t| = -t.$ Therefore, our final answer is

$$


{\color{blue}\sqrt{t^2}} = {\color{red}-t}.


$$

You can convince yourself that this is correct by plugging a few values of $t$ into the above equation. Provided that $t$ is negative, this expression will always give a true statement.

For example, suppose that $t = -2.$ Substituting into the left-hand side of the equation, we get

$$


\begin{aligned}\sqrt{√𝑡^{2}} & = \\ \sqrt{√(−2)^{2}} & = \\ \sqrt{√4} & = \\ 2 & .\end{aligned}


$$

Substituting $t=-2$ into the right-hand side of the equation, we get

$$


\begin{aligned}−𝑡 & = \\ −(−2) & = \\ 2 & .\end{aligned}


$$

Since both sides give the same result, the statement is true.

### Example: Simplifying an Expression Given That Some Variables Are Negative

#### Question

Simplify $\sqrt{y^4 z^{6}}$ given that $z \lt 0.$

#### Explanation

The square root of a squared quantity can be simplified using absolute value.

$$


\sqrt{x^2} = |x|


$$

In our case, we can simplify the given expression as follows:

$$


\begin{aligned} \sqrt{y^4 z^{6}} & = \\\[5pt] \sqrt{y^2 z^3 \cdot y^2 z^3} &= \\\[5pt] \sqrt{\left(y^2 z^3\right)^2}&=\\\[5pt] |y^2 z^3| & = \\\[5pt] |y^2| \cdot |z^3| & = \\\[5pt] |y^2| \cdot |z|^3 \end{aligned}


$$

A squared number is always positive or zero, and the absolute value of any positive (or zero) number is itself, so we have $|y^2| = y^2.$

On the other hand, we are told that $z \lt 0,$ and to take the absolute value of any negative number we need to flip the sign, so we have $|z| = -z.$ Therefore, our final answer is

$$


\begin{aligned}|𝑦^{2}|⋅|𝑧|^{3}=𝑦^{2}⋅(−𝑧)^{3}=−𝑦^{2}𝑧^{3}.\end{aligned}


$$
