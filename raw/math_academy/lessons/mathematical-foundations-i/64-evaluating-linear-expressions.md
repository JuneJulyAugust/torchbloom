## Lesson

### Introduction

To **evaluate** the expression $3+x$ for $x={\color{blue}2}$, we replace $x$ with $\color{blue}2$ in the expression and simplify:

$$


\begin{aligned}3+𝑥 & = \\ 3+(2) & = \\ 3+2 & = \\ 5 & \end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Evaluating Linear Expressions

#### Question

Evaluate $11p-2$ for $p=4.$

#### Explanation

Replacing $p$ with $\color{blue}4$ in the expression, we get

$$


\begin{aligned} 11p-2 &= \\11({\color{blue}4})-2 &= \\44-2 &= \\42 . \end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Evaluating Linear Expressions Containing Fractions

#### Question

Evaluate $2-\dfrac{u}{4}$ for $u=\dfrac43.$

#### Explanation

Replacing $u$ with $\dfrac43$ in the expression, we get

$$


\begin{aligned}2−\frac{𝑢}{4} & = \\ 2−\frac{1}{4}𝑢 & = \\ 2−\frac{1}{4}⋅\frac{4}{3} & = \\ 2−\frac{4}{12} & = \\ 2−\frac{1}{3} & = \\ \frac{6−1}{3} & = \\ \frac{5}{3}. & \end{aligned}


$$

### Evaluating Expressions Containing Parentheses

The usual order of operations (PEMDAS) applies when evaluating expressions. So when an algebraic expression contains parentheses $(\ldots)$, we calculate what's inside the parentheses *first*.

For example, we evaluate $6 - (2x+3)$ at $x=3$, as follows:

$$


\begin{aligned}6−(2𝑥+3) & = \\ 6−(2⋅3+3) & = \\ 6−(6+3) & = \\ 6−9 & = \\ −3. & \end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Evaluating Expressions of a Single Variable with Parentheses

#### Question

Evaluate $2(3 x-1) +2$ for $x=-3.$

#### Explanation

We replace $x$ with $-3$ in the expression and evaluate the parentheses first:

$$


\begin{aligned}2(3𝑥−1)+2 & = \\ 2(3⋅(−3)−1)+2 & = \\ 2(−9−1)+2 & = \\ 2(−10)+2 & = \\ −20+2 & = \\ −18 & \end{aligned}


$$

### Evaluating Expressions Containing More Than One Variable

If an expression contains more than one variable, we evaluate it using the same procedure, replacing each variable with the appropriate value.

For example, to find the value of $2b + \dfrac{1}{2}a$ when $a=-2$ and $b=3,$ we replace $a$ with $-2$ and $b$ with $3\mathbin{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}2𝑏+\frac{1}{2}𝑎 & = \\ 2⋅3+\frac{1}{2}⋅(−2) & = \\ 6−1 & = \\ 5 & \end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Evaluating Expressions Containing Multiple Variables

#### Question

Find the value of $x - \dfrac{3}{2}y$ when $x=2$ and $y=-2.$

#### Explanation

Replacing $x$ with $2$ and $y$ with $-2$ in the expression, we get

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑥−\frac{3}{2}𝑦 & = \\ 2−\frac{3}{2}⋅(−2) & = \\ 2+(−\frac{3}{2})⋅(−2) & = \\ 2+3 & = \\ 5. & \end{aligned}


$$
