## Lesson

### Introduction

When simplifying an algebraic expression involving square roots, it's useful to remember that

$$


\sqrt{x^2} = |x|.


$$

It's tempting to think that $\sqrt{x^2}$ is equivalent to $x,$ but that's not true because $\sqrt{x^2}$ is always non-negative, even if $x$ is negative.

To illustrate, let's evaluate $\sqrt{x^2}$ for a couple of different values of $x\mathbin{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}\sqrt{√4^{2}} & =\sqrt{√16}=4 \\ \sqrt{√(−4)^{2}} & =\sqrt{√16}=4\end{aligned}


$$

In both cases, using absolute value gives the correct result:

$$


\begin{aligned}\sqrt{√4^{2}}=|4| & =4 & ✓ \\ \sqrt{√(−4)^{2}}=|−4| & =4 & ✓\end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Expressing the Square Root of a Perfect Square Using Absolute Value

#### Question

Simplify $\sqrt{16y^2}.$

#### Explanation

Remember that the square root of a perfect square can be simplified using absolute value.

$$


\sqrt{x^2} = |x|


$$

Also, we notice that $16$ and $y^2$ are both perfect squares.

First, we write the expression under the square root as a perfect square:

$$


\begin{aligned}\sqrt{√16𝑦^{2}} & =\sqrt{√4^{2}⋅𝑦^{2}} \\ & =\sqrt{√4⋅4⋅𝑦⋅𝑦} \\ & =\sqrt{√4⋅𝑦⋅4⋅𝑦} \\ & =\sqrt{√4𝑦⋅4𝑦} \\ & =\sqrt{√(4𝑦)^{2}}\end{aligned}


$$

Then, we take the absolute value of the squared quantity and simplify using the rules of absolute value:

$$


\begin{aligned}\sqrt{√(4𝑦)^{2}} & =|4𝑦| \\ & =|4|⋅|𝑦| \\ & =4|𝑦|\end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Simplifying the Square Root of a Perfect Square With Multiple Variables

#### Question

Simplify $\sqrt{36a^2c^2}.$

#### Explanation

Remember that the square root of a perfect square can be simplified using absolute value.

$$


\sqrt{x^2} = |x|


$$

Also, we notice that $36, a^2,$ and $c^2$ are perfect squares.

First, we write the expression under the square root as a perfect square:

$$


\begin{aligned}\sqrt{√36𝑎^{2}𝑐^{2}} & =\sqrt{√6^{2}⋅𝑎^{2}⋅𝑐^{2}} \\ & =\sqrt{√6⋅6⋅𝑎⋅𝑎⋅𝑐⋅𝑐} \\ & =\sqrt{√6⋅𝑎⋅𝑐⋅6⋅𝑎⋅𝑐} \\ & =\sqrt{√(6𝑎𝑐)⋅(6𝑎𝑐)} \\ & =\sqrt{√(6𝑎𝑐)^{2}}\end{aligned}


$$

Then, we take the absolute value of the squared quantity and simplify using the rules of absolute value:

$$


\begin{aligned}\sqrt{√(6𝑎𝑐)^{2}} & =|6𝑎𝑐| \\ & =|6|⋅|𝑎𝑐| \\ & =6|𝑎𝑐|\end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Simplifying a Square Root of a Perfect Square Containing a Fraction

#### Question

Simplify $\sqrt{\dfrac{4x^2}{y^2}}.$

#### Explanation

Remember that the square root of a perfect square can be simplified using absolute value.

$$


\sqrt{x^2} = |x|


$$

Also, we notice that $4, x^2,$ and $y^2$ are all perfect squares.

First, we write the expression under the square root as a perfect square:

$$


\begin{aligned}\sqrt{√\frac{4𝑥^{2}}{𝑦^{2}}} & =\sqrt{√\frac{2^{2}𝑥^{2}}{𝑦^{2}}} \\ & =\sqrt{√\frac{2⋅2⋅𝑥⋅𝑥}{𝑦⋅𝑦}} \\ & =\sqrt{√\frac{2⋅𝑥⋅2⋅𝑥}{𝑦⋅𝑦}} \\ & =\sqrt{√\frac{2𝑥}{𝑦}⋅\frac{2𝑥}{𝑦}} \\ & =\sqrt{√(\frac{2𝑥}{𝑦})^{2}}\end{aligned}


$$

Then, we take the absolute value of the squared quantity and simplify using the rules of absolute value:

$$


\begin{aligned}\sqrt{√(\frac{2𝑥}{𝑦})^{2}} & =\frac{2𝑥}{𝑦} \\ & =\frac{|2𝑥|}{|𝑦|} \\ & =\frac{|2|⋅|𝑥|}{|𝑦|} \\ & =\frac{2|𝑥|}{|𝑦|}\end{aligned}


$$
