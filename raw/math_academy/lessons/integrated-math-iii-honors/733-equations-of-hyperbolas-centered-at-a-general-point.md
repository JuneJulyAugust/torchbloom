### Example: Finding the Center of a Horizontal Hyperbola

#### Question

What is the center of the hyperbola $\dfrac{(x-1)^2}{9} - \dfrac{(y-2)^2}{4} = 1?$

#### Explanation

The equation of a horizontal hyperbola centered at $(h,k)$ is given by

$$


\dfrac{(x-h)^2}{a^2} - \dfrac{(y-k)^2}{b^2}=1.


$$

In this case, we have $h = 1$ and $k = 2.$

Therefore, the center of the hyperbola is $(1,2).$

### Example: Finding the Center of a Vertical Hyperbola

#### Question

What is the center of the hyperbola $\dfrac{(y+3)^2}{16} - \dfrac{(x-1)^2}{9} = 1?$

#### Explanation

The equation of a vertical hyperbola centered at $(h,k)$ is given by

$$


\dfrac{(y-k)^2}{a^2} - \dfrac{(x-h)^2}{b^2}=1.


$$

Note that the given equation can be rewritten as follows:

$$


\dfrac{(y-(-3))^2}{16} - \dfrac{(x-1)^2}{9}=1


$$

In this case, we have $h = 1$ and $k=-3.$

Therefore, the center of the hyperbola is $(1,-3).$

### Example: Finding the Center of a Vertical Hyperbola Given an Equation in Expanded Form

#### Question

What is the center of the hyperbola $4 y^2 - 10 x^2 - 8 y - 40 x = 76?$

#### Explanation

The equation of a vertical hyperbola centered at $(h,k)$ is given by

$$


\dfrac{(y-k)^2}{a^2} - \dfrac{(x-h)^2}{b^2}=1.


$$

To rewrite the equation of the hyperbola in the above form, we need to group $x$ and $y$ terms and complete the squares as follows:

$$


\begin{aligned}4𝑦^{2}−10𝑥^{2}−8𝑦−40𝑥 & =76 \\ 4𝑦^{2}−8𝑦−10𝑥^{2}−40𝑥 & =76 \\ 4[𝑦^{2}−2𝑦]−10[𝑥^{2}+4𝑥] & =76 \\ 4[(𝑦−1)^{2}−1]−10[(𝑥+2)^{2}−4] & =76 \\ 4(𝑦−1)^{2}−4−10(𝑥+2)^{2}+40 & =76 \\ 4(𝑦−1)^{2}−10(𝑥+2)^{2} & =76−40+4 \\ 4(𝑦−1)^{2}−10(𝑥+2)^{2} & =40 \\ \frac{(𝑦−1)^{2}}{10}−\frac{(𝑥+2)^{2}}{4} & =1.\end{aligned}


$$

Therefore, the center is $(-2,1)$.
