## Lesson

### Introduction

Sometimes, we might need to use more than one rule of exponents to simplify an expression. First, let's remind ourselves of all of the rules of exponents.

- Radicals as fractional exponents: $\sqrt[n]{x} = x^{1/n}$

- The product rule: $a^n \cdot a^m = a^{n+m}$

- The quotient rule: $\dfrac{a^n}{a^m} = a^{n-m}$

- The power rule: $\left(a^m \right)^n = a^{mn}$

- The power of product rule: $(ab)^n = a^nb^n$

- The power of quotient rule: $\left(\dfrac a b \right)^n = \dfrac{a^n}{b^n}$

As an example, let's simplify the following expression:

$$


\left(u^2 v\right)^3


$$

We start by using the power of product rule:

$$


\begin{aligned}(𝑢^{2}𝑣)^{3}=(𝑢^{2})^{3}⋅𝑣^{3}\end{aligned}


$$

Then, we use the power rule:

$$


\begin{aligned}(𝑢^{2})^{3}⋅𝑣^{3} & = \\ 𝑢^{(2⋅3)}⋅𝑣^{3} & = \\ 𝑢^{6}⋅𝑣^{3} & = \\ 𝑢^{6}𝑣^{3} & \end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Combining the Rules of Exponents Without Fractions

#### Question

Simplify the expression $\left(x^2y\right)^3\cdot x^2y.$

#### Explanation

We start by using the power of product rule:

$$


\begin{aligned}(𝑥^{2}𝑦)^{3}⋅𝑥^{2}𝑦= & \\ (𝑥^{2})^{3}⋅𝑦^{3}⋅𝑥^{2}𝑦 & \end{aligned}


$$

Then, we use the power rule:

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑥^{2⋅3}⋅𝑦^{3}⋅𝑥^{2}𝑦 & = \\ 𝑥^{6}⋅𝑦^{3}⋅𝑥^{2}𝑦 & \end{aligned}


$$

Finally, we use the product rule:

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑥^{6}⋅𝑦^{3}⋅𝑥^{2}𝑦= & \\ 𝑥^{6}⋅𝑥^{2}⋅𝑦^{3}⋅𝑦= & \\ 𝑥^{6+2}⋅𝑦^{3+1} & = \\ 𝑥^{8}⋅𝑦^{4} & = \\ 𝑥^{8}𝑦^{4} & \end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Combining the Rules of Exponents With Fractions

#### Question

Given that $a=2x^3$ and $b=\dfrac{x}{y},$ express $ab^2$ in terms of $x$ and $y.$

#### Explanation

First, we substitute for $a$ and $b\mathbin{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑎𝑏^{2}=2𝑥^{3}(\frac{𝑥}{𝑦})^{2}\end{aligned}


$$

Then, we apply the power of quotient rule:

$$


\begin{aligned}2𝑥^{3}(\frac{𝑥}{𝑦})^{2}=2𝑥^{3}(\frac{𝑥^{2}}{𝑦^{2}})\end{aligned}


$$

Finally, we multiply and apply the product rule:

$$


\begin{aligned}2𝑥^{3}(\frac{𝑥^{2}}{𝑦^{2}}) & = \\ \frac{2𝑥^{3}⋅𝑥^{2}}{𝑦^{2}} & = \\ \frac{2𝑥^{3+2}}{𝑦^{2}} & = \\ \frac{2𝑥^{5}}{𝑦^{2}} & \end{aligned}


$$

### Rules of Exponents and Radicals

To simplify the expression $x\cdot \sqrt[3]{x^2},$ we first rewrite the radical as a fractional exponent:

$$


x \cdot \sqrt[3]{x^2} = x \cdot \left( x^2 \right)^{1/3}


$$

Now, we apply the power rule for exponents:

$$


x \cdot \left( x^2 \right)^{1/3} = x \cdot x^{2(1/3)} = x \cdot x^{2/3}


$$

Then, we apply the product rule:

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑥⋅𝑥^{2/3} & = \\ 𝑥^{1+2/3} & = \\ 𝑥^{5/3} & \end{aligned}


$$

Finally, we express the result as a radical:

$$


x^{5/3} = \sqrt[3]{x^5}


$$

Notice that, by combining the radicals as fractional exponents and power rules, we get the following formula:

$$


\sqrt[n]{x^m} = x^{m/n}


$$

### Example: Combining the Rules of Exponents With Radicals

#### Question

Simplify the expression $\dfrac {\sqrt{y}} {\sqrt[3]{y}}.$

#### Explanation

First, we rewrite the radicals as fractional exponents:

$$


\begin{aligned}\frac{\sqrt{√𝑦}}{\sqrt[√𝑦]{3}}=\frac{𝑦^{1/2}}{𝑦^{1/3}}\end{aligned}


$$

Then, we apply the quotient rule:

$$


\begin{aligned}\frac{𝑦^{1/2}}{𝑦^{1/3}} & = \\ 𝑦^{1/2−1/3} & = \\ 𝑦^{3/6−2/6} & = \\ 𝑦^{1/6} & \end{aligned}


$$

Finally, we express the result as a radical:

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑦^{1/6}=\sqrt[√𝑦]{6}\end{aligned}


$$
