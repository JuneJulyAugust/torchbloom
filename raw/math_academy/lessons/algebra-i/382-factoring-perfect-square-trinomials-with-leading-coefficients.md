### Introduction

Sometimes, perfect square trinomials have a leading coefficient that is something other than $1.$ For example, the expression

$$


4x^2+12x+9


$$

has a leading coefficient of $4,$ and it is a perfect square trinomial because it is the square of the binomial $(2x+3)$ as we can see below:

$$


\begin{aligned}(2𝑥+3)^{2} & = \\ (2𝑥)^{2}+2(2𝑥)(3)+(3)^{2} & = \\ 4𝑥^{2}+12𝑥+9 & \end{aligned}


$$

To factor a perfect square trinomial with a leading coefficient other than $1,$ we use the square of sum formula:

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑎^{2}+2𝑎𝑏+𝑏^{2} & =(𝑎+𝑏)^{2}\end{aligned}


$$

To illustrate, let's factor the expression $4x^2+12x+9$ using the square of sum formula. We perform the following steps:

1. Write both the quadratic term and the constant term as perfect squares:

2. Factor a $2$ out of the linear term:

3. Write the middle parentheses $(6x)$ as a product of $2x$ and ${\color{blue}3}\mathbin{:}$

4. Factor the trinomial using the square of sum formula. In this case, we have $a=2x$ and $b=3\mathbin{:}$

**Note:** This method works *only* when the trinomial is a perfect square! It will *not* work for every trinomial. But we will learn more general methods in the future.

### Example: Factoring a Perfect Square Trinomial Generated by the Square of a Sum

#### Question

Factor $9x^2+30x+25.$

#### Explanation

The "square of a sum" formula states that

$$


a^2 + {\color{red}{2}}a{\color{blue}{b}} + {\color{blue}b}^2 = (a+{\color{blue}{b}})^2 .


$$

We will use this formula to factor the given trinomial. Our trinomial is

$$


9x^2+30x+25.


$$

First, we write both the quadratic term and the constant term as perfect squares:

$$


\begin{aligned}(3𝑥)^{2}+30𝑥+(5)^{2}\end{aligned}


$$

Next, we factor a $2$ out of the linear term:

$$


\begin{aligned}(3𝑥)^{2}+2(15𝑥)+(5)^{2}\end{aligned}


$$

Then, we write the middle parentheses $(15x)$ as a product of $3x$ and ${\color{blue}5}\mathbin{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}(3𝑥)^{2}+2(3𝑥⋅5)+(5)^{2}\end{aligned}


$$

Finally, we factor the trinomial using the square of sum formula with $a=3x$ and ${\color{blue}{b}} = {\color{blue}{5}}.$ This gives

$$


\begin{aligned}(3𝑥+5)^{2}\end{aligned}


$$

### Factoring a Perfect Square Trinomial Generated by the Square of a Difference

Other perfect square trinomials can be factored as the square of a difference:

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑎^{2}−2𝑎𝑏+𝑏^{2} & =(𝑎−𝑏)^{2}\end{aligned}


$$

To illustrate, let's factor the expression $9x^2-24x+16$ using the square of difference formula. We perform the following steps:

1. Write both the quadratic term and the constant term as perfect squares:

2. Factor a $2$ out of the linear term:

3. Write the middle parentheses $(12x)$ as a product of $3x$ and ${\color{blue}4}\mathbin{:}$

4. Factor the trinomial using the square of difference formula. In this case, we have $a=3x$ and $b=4\mathbin{:}$

**Note:** Once again, we note that this method works *only* when the trinomial is a perfect square! It will *not* work for every trinomial.

### Example: Factoring a Perfect Square Trinomial Generated by the Square of a Difference

#### Question

Factor $9y^2-12y+4.$

#### Explanation

The "square of a difference" formula states that

$$


a^2 - {\color{red}{2}}a{\color{blue}{b}} + {\color{blue}b}^2 = (a-{\color{blue}{b}})^2 .


$$

We will use this formula to factor the given trinomial. Our trinomial is

$$


9y^2-12y+4.


$$

First, we write both the quadratic term and the constant term as perfect squares:

$$


\begin{aligned}(3𝑦)^{2}−12𝑦+(2)^{2}\end{aligned}


$$

Next, we factor a $2$ out of the linear term:

$$


\begin{aligned}(3𝑦)^{2}−2(6𝑦)+(2)^{2}\end{aligned}


$$

Then, we write the middle parentheses $(6y)$ as a product of $3y$ and ${\color{blue}2}\mathbin{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}(3𝑦)^{2}−2(3𝑦⋅2)+(2)^{2}\end{aligned}


$$

Finally, we factor the trinomial using the square of difference formula with $a=3y$ and ${\color{blue}{b}} = {\color{blue}{2}}.$ This gives

$$


\begin{aligned}(3𝑦−2)^{2}\end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Factoring a Perfect Square Trinomial Whose Terms Have a Common Factor

#### Question

Factor the expression $7a^2+28a+28.$

#### Explanation

Before we start our normal process of factoring, notice that all of the terms have a common factor of $7.$ Therefore, we can simplify the expression by factoring out $7,$ as follows:

$$


\begin{aligned}7𝑎^{2}+28𝑎+28 & = \\ 7(𝑎^{2}+4𝑎+4) & \end{aligned}


$$

Now, let's factor the expression inside parentheses using the square of sum formula:

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑎^{2}+4𝑎+4 & = \\ 𝑎^{2}+4𝑎+2^{2} & \\ 𝑎^{2}+2⋅2𝑎+2^{2} & \\ (𝑎+2)^{2} & \end{aligned}


$$

Therefore, our original expression factors as follows:

$$


\begin{aligned}7(𝑎^{2}+4𝑎+4) & = \\ 7(𝑎+2)^{2} & \end{aligned}


$$

### Factoring a Perfect Square Trinomial With a Negative Leading Coefficient

How can we factor a trinomial that has a negative leading coefficient, like

$$


-25y^2 + 20y - 4\,?


$$

We can reduce this to a more familiar problem by factoring out $−1$ from the entire expression:

$$


-(25y^2 - 20y + 4)


$$

Now, we factor the quadratic expression inside the parentheses using the square of a difference formula:

$$


\begin{aligned}25𝑦^{2}−20𝑦+4 & = \\ (5𝑦)^{2}−20𝑦+2^{2} & = \\ (5𝑦)^{2}−2(10𝑦)+2^{2} & = \\ (5𝑦)^{2}−2(5𝑦⋅2)+2^{2} & = \\ (5𝑦−2)^{2} & \end{aligned}


$$

Therefore, our original expression factors as follows:

$$


\begin{aligned}−(25𝑦^{2}−20𝑦+4) & = \\ −(5𝑦−2)^{2} & \end{aligned}


$$
