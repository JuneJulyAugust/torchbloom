## Lesson

### Introduction

A **unit price** is the price of a single item. To compute a unit price, we divide the total cost of a group of items by the number of items in the group.

For example, suppose a store sells a pack of $3$ shirts for $12.$

To find the unit price, we divide the total cost by the number of shirts:

$$



12 \div 3 = 4



$$

Therefore, the unit price is $4$ per shirt.

### Example: Computing a Unit Price

#### Question

A $3$-pound bag of flour costs $ 6.42.$ What is the price per pound?

#### Explanation

To find the unit price, we divide the total cost by the number of pounds:

$$



6.42 \div 3 = 2.14



$$

Therefore, the unit price is $ 2.14$ per pound.

### Unit Prices With Units Conversions

Now, suppose that installing a fence costs $360$ per yard. What would be the unit price per foot?

First, notice that there are $3$ feet in $1$ yard.

To find the unit price per foot, we *divide* the price per yard by $3\mathbin{:}$

$$



360 \div 3 = 120



$$

Therefore, installing the fence costs $120$ per foot.

In reverse, if we know that installing a fence costs $120$ per foot, to find the unit price per yard, we would need to *multiply* the price per foot by $3\mathbin{:}$

$$



120 \times 3 = 360



$$

### Example: Computing a Unit Price With Conversion to Smaller Units

#### Question

A $5$-gallon jug of water costs $ 7.60.$ What is the price per quart?

#### Explanation

First, we convert from gallons to quarts. There are $4$ quarts in each gallon, so we multiply the number of gallons by $4\mathbin{:}$

$$



5 \times 4 = 20



$$

So $5$ gallons is the same as $20$ quarts. Now, to find the price per quart, we divide the total price by $20\mathbin{:}$

$$



7.60 \div 20 = 0.38



$$

Therefore, the water costs $ 0.38$ per quart.

### Example: Computing a Unit Price With Conversion to Larger Units

#### Question

A bottle contains $32$ fluid ounces of juice and costs $8.00.$ What's the unit price per cup?

Assume that there are $8$ fluid ounces in one cup.

#### Explanation

First, we convert from fluid ounces to cups. There are $8$ fluid ounces in each cup, so we divide the number of fluid ounces by $8\mathbin{:}$

$$



32 \div 8 = 4



$$

So, $32$ fluid ounces is the same as $4$ cups.

Now, to find the unit price per cup, we divide the total price by $4\mathbin{:}$

$$



8.00 \div 4 = 2



$$

Therefore, the juice costs $2.00$ per cup.
