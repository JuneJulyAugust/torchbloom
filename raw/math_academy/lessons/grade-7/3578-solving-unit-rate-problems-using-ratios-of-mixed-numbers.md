## Lesson

### Introduction

Suppose a man can walk $1\, \dfrac18$ miles in $\dfrac34$ of an hour. How long will it take the man to walk $6$ miles?

To find the number of hours it will take the man to walk $6$ miles, we first

- find the unit rate of *hours per mile*, and then

- multiply that unit rate by $6$ *miles*.

Let's see how this is done.

We begin by dividing the number of hours, $\dfrac{3}{4},$ by the number of miles traveled, $1 \, \dfrac{1}{8}\mathbin{:}$

$$



\dfrac{3}{4} \div 1 \, \dfrac{1}{8}



$$

And here is the tricky part. To calculate this division, we first need to write the mixed number $1 \, \dfrac{1}{8}$ as an improper fraction:

$$



1 \, \dfrac{1}{8} = \dfrac{(1 \times 8) + 1}{8} = \dfrac{9}{8}



$$

So, our problem now is to work out the following division, which we can evaluate by multiplying by the reciprocal:

$$



\begin{aligned}\frac{3}{4}÷\frac{9}{8} & =\frac{3}{4}×\frac{8}{9}=\frac{2}{3}\end{aligned}



$$

This means that the unit rate is $\dfrac{2}{3}$ hours per mile. Hence, to find the number of hours it will take the man to walk $6$ miles, we multiply the unit rate by $6$ miles and get

$$



\begin{aligned}\frac{2}{3}×6 & =\frac{2}{3}×\frac{6}{1}=4\end{aligned}



$$

Therefore, it will take the man $4\,$hours to walk $6$ miles.

### Example: Solving a Problem Involving a Ratio of a Fraction and a Mixed Number

#### Question

Mark is building chairs to sell online. He built $3 \, \dfrac{1}{2}$ chairs in $\dfrac{3}{4}$ of a day. How many days will it take him to build $14$ chairs?

#### Explanation

To find the number of days it will take Mark to build $14$ chairs, we can first find the unit rate of days per chair, and then multiply the unit rate by $14$ chairs.

To find the unit rate of days per chair, we divide the number of days, $\dfrac{3}{4},$ by the number of chairs, $3 \, \dfrac{1}{2},$ as follows:

$$



\dfrac{3}{4} \div 3 \, \dfrac{1}{2}



$$

First, we write $3 \, \dfrac{1}{2}$ as an improper fraction:

$$



3 \, \dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{(3 \times 2) + 1}{2} = \dfrac{7}{2}



$$

Dividing one fraction by another is equivalent to multiplying the fraction by the reciprocal of the second fraction:

$$



\begin{aligned}\frac{3}{4}÷\frac{7}{2} & =\frac{3}{4}×\frac{2}{7} \\ & =\frac{3×2}{4×7} \\ & =\frac{6}{28} \\ & =\frac{6÷2}{28÷2} \\ & =\frac{3}{14}\end{aligned}



$$

So, the unit rate is $\dfrac{3}{14}$ days per chair. To find the number of days it will take Mark to build $14$ chairs, we multiply the unit rate by $14$ chairs and get

$$



\begin{aligned}\frac{3}{14}×14 & =\frac{3}{14}×\frac{14}{1} \\ & =\frac{3×14}{14×1} \\ & =\frac{3×14}{14×1} \\ & =\frac{3}{1} \\ & =3\end{aligned}



$$

Therefore, it will take Mark $3$ days to build $14$ chairs.

### Example: Solving a Problem Involving a Ratio of a Mixed Number and a Fraction

#### Question

Jenna is painting pictures. It took her $3 \, \dfrac{1}{2}$ hours to paint $\dfrac{4}{5}$ of a picture. How long will it take Jenna to paint $3$ pictures?

#### Explanation

To find the number of hours it will take for Jenna to paint $3$ pictures, we can first find the unit rate of hours per picture, and then multiply the unit rate by $3$ pictures.

To find the unit rate of hours per picture, we divide the number of hours, $3 \, \dfrac{1}{2},$ by the number of pictures, $\dfrac{4}{5},$ as follows:

$$



3 \, \dfrac{1}{2} \div \dfrac{4}{5}



$$

First, we write $3\, \dfrac{1}{2}$ as an improper fraction:

$$



3 \, \dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{(3 \times 2) + 1}{2} = \dfrac{7}{2}



$$

Therefore, our problem now is to work out the following:

$$



\dfrac{7}{2} \div \dfrac{4}{5}



$$

Dividing one fraction by another is equivalent to multiplying the first fraction by the reciprocal of the second fraction:

$$



\begin{aligned}\frac{7}{2}÷\frac{4}{5} & =\frac{7}{2}×\frac{5}{4} \\ & =\frac{7×5}{2×4} \\ & =\frac{35}{8}\end{aligned}



$$

So, the unit rate is $\dfrac{35}{8}$ hours per picture. To find the number of hours that it will take Jenna to paint $3$ pictures, we multiply the unit rate by $3$ pictures and get

$$



\begin{aligned}\frac{35}{8}×3 & =\frac{35}{8}×\frac{3}{1} \\ & =\frac{35×3}{8×1} \\ & =\frac{105}{8}\end{aligned}



$$

Finally, we write the resulting improper fraction as a mixed number:

$$



\dfrac{105}{8} = 13 \, \textrm{R} 1 = 13 \, \dfrac{1}{8}



$$

Therefore, it will take Jenna $13 \, \dfrac{1}{8}$ hours to paint $3$ pictures.

### Example: Solving a Problem Involving a Ratio of Mixed Numbers

#### Question

A printer produces $2\,\dfrac{1}{2}$ pages in $1\,\dfrac{1}{4}$ minutes at a constant rate. How many pages will it produce in $4$ minutes at the same rate?

#### Explanation

To find the number of pages the printer will produce in $4$ minutes, we first find the unit rate of pages per minute, and then multiply that unit rate by $4$ minutes.

To find the unit rate of pages per minute, we divide the number of pages, $2\,\dfrac{1}{2},$ by the number of minutes, $1\,\dfrac{1}{4},$ as follows:

$$



2\,\dfrac{1}{2} \div 1\,\dfrac{1}{4}



$$

First, we write each mixed number as an improper fraction:

$$



\begin{aligned}2\,\frac{1}{2} & =\frac{(2×2)+1}{2}=\frac{5}{2} \\ 1\,\frac{1}{4} & =\frac{(1×4)+1}{4}=\frac{5}{4}\end{aligned}



$$

Therefore, our problem now is to compute:

$$



\dfrac{5}{2} \div \dfrac{5}{4}



$$

Dividing one fraction by another is equivalent to multiplying by the reciprocal:

$$



\begin{aligned}\frac{5}{2}÷\frac{5}{4} & =\frac{5}{2}×\frac{4}{5} \\ & =\frac{5×4}{2×5} \\ & =\frac{4}{2} \\ & =2\end{aligned}



$$

So, the unit rate is $2$ pages per minute.

Now, we multiply this unit rate by $4$ minutes:

$$



\begin{aligned}2×4 & =8\end{aligned}



$$

Therefore, the printer will produce $8$ pages in $4$ minutes.
