## Lesson

### Introduction

Recall that when one figure is a *scaled copy* of another, corresponding side lengths are multiplied by the same *scale factor*.

So, if we know one pair of corresponding side lengths, we can find the scale factor. Then, we can use that scale factor to calculate other missing lengths in the scaled copy.

For example, consider the figure below, where the triangle on the right is a scaled copy of the triangle on the left. Let's determine the length of $\overline{PQ}.$

![Instructional graphic](../../lesson-assets/grade-7/topic-1408/791648c830e59a45.png)

To find the scale factor, we compare a pair of corresponding sides. From the diagram:

- Side $\overline{AB}$ in the left triangle has length $18 \: \text{ft}.$

- The corresponding side $\overline{PR}$ in the right triangle has length $12 \: \text{ft}.$

So, the scale factor is

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑘 & =\frac{𝑃𝑅}{𝐴𝐵} \\ & =\frac{12}{18} \\ & =\frac{2}{3}.\end{aligned}



$$

Now, to find the length of $\overline{PQ},$ we multiply the length of the corresponding side in the left triangle (which is $\overline{AC}$) by the scale factor.

Therefore, we have

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑃𝑄 & =𝑘⋅𝐴𝐶 \\ & =\frac{2}{3}⋅6 \\ & =4\,ft.\end{aligned}



$$

### Example: Finding a Length Given the Scale Factor

#### Question

![Instructional graphic](../../lesson-assets/grade-7/topic-1408/5e2d1c64052a3711.png)

Consider the figure above, where the polygon on the right is a scaled copy of the polygon on the left with a scale factor of $\dfrac{5}{4}.$ What is the length of $\overline{XK}?$

#### Explanation

The polygon on the right is a ** of the polygon on the left. This means that corresponding side lengths are multiplied by the same **.

We are given that the scale factor is $k=\dfrac{5}{4}.$

To find the length of $\overline{XK},$ we multiply the length of the corresponding side in the left polygon (which is $\overline{AD}$) by the scale factor $k.$

Therefore, we have

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑋𝐾 & =𝑘⋅𝐴𝐷 \\ & =\frac{5}{4}⋅8 \\ & =10\,m.\end{aligned}



$$

### Finding Lengths on Maps

So far, we have used scale factors to compare lengths between scaled copies of geometric figures.

The same idea also appears in real-world situations such as maps, blueprints, and models. In these cases, the scale tells us how a length on the drawing relates to the actual length in real life.

For example, suppose that we created a map of a botanical garden. On the map, $2$ centimeters represents $10$ meters in real life. If a walking path is $14$ centimeters long on the map, how long is the actual path?

To find the actual length, we can use the given scale relationship. We are given that $2 \: \text{cm}$ on the map represents $10 \: \text{m}$ in reality.

We can set up a proportion comparing the map length to the actual length:

$$



\dfrac{\text{Map Length}}{\text{Actual Length}} = \dfrac{2 \: \text{cm}}{10 \: \text{m}}



$$

By finding the reciprocal of both sides, we get:

$$



\dfrac{\text{Actual Length}}{\text{Map Length}} = \dfrac{10 \: \text{m}}{2 \: \text{cm}}



$$

Let $x$ be the actual length of the path. We know the path is $14 \: \text{cm}$ long on the map, so we have:

$$



\dfrac{x}{14 \: \text{cm}} = \dfrac{10 \: \text{m}}{2 \: \text{cm}}



$$

To solve for $x$, we multiply both sides by $14 \: \text{cm}$:

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑥 & =14\,cm⋅\frac{10\,m}{2\,cm} \\ & =\frac{14⋅10}{2}\,m \\ & =7⋅10\,m \\ & =70\,m\end{aligned}



$$

Therefore, the actual length of the path is $70 \: \text{m}.$

### Example: Finding a Length From a Scale Drawing in Word Problems

#### Question

Ethan drew a map of a campground. On the map, $1$ inch represents $40$ feet in real life. If an actual trail is $600$ feet long, how long is the trail on the map?

#### Explanation

To find the length on the map, we can use the given scale relationship. We are given that $1 \: \text{in}$ on the map represents $40 \: \text{ft}$ in reality.

We can set up a proportion comparing the map length to the actual length:

$$



\frac{\text{Map Length}}{\text{Actual Length}}=\frac{1\ \text{in}}{40\ \text{ft}}



$$

Let $x$ be the length of the trail on the map. We know the actual trail is $600 \: \text{ft}$ long, so we have:

$$



\frac{x}{600 \: \text{ft}}=\frac{1 \: \text{in}}{40 \: \text{ft}}



$$

To solve for $x$, we multiply both sides by $600 \: \text{ft}{:}$

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑥 & =600\,ft⋅\frac{1\,in}{40\,ft} \\ & =\frac{600⋅1}{40}\,in \\ & =\frac{600}{40}\,in \\ & =15\,in\end{aligned}



$$

Therefore, the length of the trail on the map is $15 \: \text{in}.$
