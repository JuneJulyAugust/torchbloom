## Lesson

### Introduction

Suppose we are given the following matrix:

$$


\begin{aligned}2 & −1 & 0 \\ −5 & 0 & −2 \\ −1 & 8 & 1 \\ 0 & 3 & −7\end{aligned}


$$

We denote the entry that lies at the intersection of the $\color{blue}3$rd row and $\color{red}2$nd column using the notation $a_{{\color{blue}3}\color{red}2}.$

The first subscript represents the row and the second subscript represents the column. So $a_{{\color{blue}3}\color{red}2}=8,$ as shown below.

$$


\begin{aligned}2 & −1 & 0 \\ −5 & 0 & −2 \\ −1 & 8 & 1 \\ 0 & 3 & −7\end{aligned}


$$

We can denote every entry of a matrix in this way. For instance, $a_{{\color{blue}4}\color{red}1}$ denotes the entry that lies at the intersection of the $\color{blue}4$th row and $\color{red}1$st column.

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑎_{11} & 𝑎_{12} & 𝑎_{13} \\ 𝑎_{21} & 𝑎_{22} & 𝑎_{23} \\ 𝑎_{31} & 𝑎_{32} & 𝑎_{33} \\ 𝑎_{41} & 𝑎_{42} & 𝑎_{43}\end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Finding an Entry of a Matrix

#### Question

Find the entry $a_{23}$ of the matrix ${A},$ where

$$


[\begin{aligned}−1 & 1 & 2 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & −3 & −1\end{aligned}]


$$

#### Explanation

The entry $a_{23}$ lies at the intersection of the $2$nd row and $3$rd column. Therefore, $a_{23}={\color{blue}{-3}}.$

$$


[\begin{aligned}−1 & 1 & 2 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & −3 & −1\end{aligned}]


$$

### Example: Finding an Entry of a Square Matrix

#### Question

Find the entry $b_{22}$ of the matrix ${B},$ where

$$


[\begin{aligned}−5 & 1 \\ 0 & 2\end{aligned}]


$$

#### Explanation

The entry $b_{22}$ lies at the intersection of the $2$nd row and $2$nd column. Therefore, $b_{22}={\color{blue}{2}}.$

$$


[\begin{aligned}−5 & 1 \\ 0 & 2\end{aligned}]


$$

### General Form of a Matrix

In general, we denote the entry of a matrix $A$ that lies at the intersection of the $\color{blue}i$th row and $\color{red}j$th column by $a_{{\color{blue}i}\color{red}j}.$

If $A$ is an $m\times n$ matrix, we can write it as

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑎_{11} & 𝑎_{12} & … & 𝑎_{1𝑛} \\ 𝑎_{21} & 𝑎_{22} & … & 𝑎_{2𝑛} \\ ⋮ & ⋮ & ⋱ & ⋮ \\ 𝑎_{𝑚1} & 𝑎_{𝑚2} & … & 𝑎_{𝑚𝑛}\end{aligned}


$$

where each entry $a_{ij}$ is defined for $i = 1,2,\ldots,m$ and $j =1,2,\ldots,n.$

### Example: Describing an Entry of a Matrix in Index Notation

#### Question

Consider the highlighted entry of the matrix $C$ given below. Describe it in terms of $c_{ij}.$

$$


[\begin{aligned}0 & −2 & 5 \\ 34 & 5 & 1\end{aligned}]


$$

#### Explanation

The entry lies at the intersection of the $1$st row and $3$rd column. Therefore, we have $c_{13}={\boxed{5}}.$
