## Lesson

### Introduction

Suppose that we want to solve the following trigonometric equation:

$$



4\cos^2x-3=0, \qquad 0 \leq x < 2\pi



$$

This equation corresponds to the quadratic equation $4y^2 - 3 = 0,$ where $y=\cos x.$ To solve this equation, we perform the following three steps:

**Step 1**: Solve for the trigonometric function.

First, we rearrange the equation and isolate $\cos^2{x}{:}$

$$



\begin{aligned}4cos^{2}⁡𝑥−3 & =0 \\ 4cos^{2}⁡𝑥 & =3 \\ cos^{2}⁡𝑥 & =\frac{3}{4}\end{aligned}



$$

Taking the square root on both sides of the equation, we have

$$



\begin{aligned}cos^{2}⁡𝑥 & =\frac{3}{4} \\ cos⁡𝑥 & =±\sqrt{√\frac{3}{4}} \\ cos⁡𝑥 & =±\frac{\sqrt{√3}}{2}.\end{aligned}



$$

**Step 2**: Solve the resulting two equations.

Our equations are

$$



\cos{x} = -\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2} \qquad \textrm{and}\qquad \cos{x} = \dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}.



$$

We solve each equation separately.

- First, we solve $\cos{x} = -\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}.$ The solutions to this equation for $0 \leq x < 2\pi$ are $x=\dfrac{5\pi}{6}, \dfrac{7\pi}{6}.$

- Then, we solve $\cos{x} = \dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}.$ The solutions to this equation for $0 \leq x < 2\pi$ are $x=\dfrac{\pi}{6}, \dfrac{11\pi}{6}.$

**Step 3**: Form the solution set by combining all the solutions found in step 2.

Combining the solutions from both equations, the solutions to the original equation (in ascending order) are

$$



x_1 = \dfrac{\pi}{6}, \quad x_2 = \dfrac{5\pi}{6}, \quad x_3 = \dfrac{7\pi}{6}, \quad x_4 = \dfrac{11\pi}{6}.



$$

### Example: Solving a Quadratic Trigonometric Equation Containing Cosine Using the Square Root Method

#### Question

Given that $x_1$ and $x_2$ are the solutions to the equation $\cos^2 x=\dfrac{1}{9}$ for $-\dfrac{\pi}{2} \leq x < \dfrac{\pi}{2},$ what is the value of $x_1\cdot x_2,$ rounded to one decimal place?

#### Explanation

Taking the square root of both sides of the equation, we have

$$



\begin{aligned}cos^{2}⁡𝑥 & =\frac{1}{9} \\ cos⁡𝑥 & =±\sqrt{√\frac{1}{9}} \\ cos⁡𝑥 & =±\frac{1}{3}.\end{aligned}



$$

So, we have to solve the following two equations:

$$



\cos{x} = -\dfrac{1}{3}, \qquad \cos{x} = \dfrac{1}{3}



$$

Let's solve each equation separately.

- First, we solve $\cos{x} = -\dfrac{1}{3}.$ But since cosine is positive over $-\dfrac{\pi}{2} \leq x < \dfrac{\pi}{2},$ there are no solutions to this equation for $-\dfrac{\pi}{2} \leq x < \dfrac{\pi}{2}.$

- Then, we solve $\cos{x} = \dfrac{1}{3}.$ The solutions to this equation for $-\dfrac{\pi}{2} \leq x < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ are $x \approx -1.231\,0,\,1.231\,0.$

Combining all solutions from both equations, we get

$$



x_1 \approx -1.231\,0, \quad x_2 \approx 1.231\,0.



$$

Finally,

$$



x_1\cdot x_2 \approx -1.231\,0 \cdot 1.231\,0 \approx -1.5,



$$

rounded to one decimal place.

### Example: Solving a Quadratic Trigonometric Equation Containing Sine Using the Square Root Method

#### Question

Consider the equation $\sin^2(x)=\dfrac{3}{4}$ for $-\pi < x \leq \pi.$ If the solutions $x_1, x_2, x_3, x_4$ are written in ascending order, then what is $x_2\cdot x_3?$

#### Explanation

Taking the square root on both sides of the equation, we have

$$



\begin{aligned}sin^{2}⁡(𝑥) & =\frac{3}{4} \\ sin⁡(𝑥) & =±\sqrt{√\frac{3}{4}} \\ sin⁡(𝑥) & =±\frac{\sqrt{√3}}{2}.\end{aligned}



$$

So, we have to solve the following two equations:

$$



\sin{x} = -\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}, \qquad \sin{x} = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}



$$

Let's solve each equation separately.

- First, we solve $\sin{x} = -\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}.$ The solutions to this equation for $-\pi < x \leq \pi$ are $x = -\dfrac{2\pi}{3}, -\dfrac{\pi}{3}.$

- Then, we solve $\sin{x} = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}.$ The solutions to this equation for $-\pi < x \leq \pi$ are $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3}, \dfrac{2\pi}{3}.$

Combining all solutions from both equations, we get

$$



x_1 = -\dfrac{2\pi}{3}, \quad x_2=-\dfrac{\pi}{3},\quad x_3= \dfrac{\pi}{3},\quad x_4= \dfrac{2\pi}{3}.



$$

Finally,

$$



x_2\cdot x_3 = -\dfrac{\pi}{3} \cdot \dfrac{\pi}{3} = -\dfrac{\pi^2}{9}.



$$
