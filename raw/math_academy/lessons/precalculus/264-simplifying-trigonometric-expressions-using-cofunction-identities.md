### Example: Rewriting Trigonometric Ratios Using Cofunction Identities

#### Question

Express $\cos{\left(\dfrac \pi 5\right)}$ using the corresponding cofunction.

#### Explanation

First, notice that the cofunction of $\cos x$ (****sine) is $\sin x$ (sine). The corresponding identity is given by

$$


\cos x = \sin \left( \dfrac{\pi}{2} - x \right).


$$

Substituting $x=\dfrac{\pi}{5}$ into the above, we get

$$


\begin{aligned}cos(\frac{𝜋}{5}) & =sin(\frac{𝜋}{2}−\frac{𝜋}{5}) \\ & =sin⁡(\frac{3𝜋}{10}).\end{aligned}


$$

Therefore, we conclude that $\cos{\left(\dfrac \pi 5\right)} = \sin\left(\dfrac {3 \pi} {10}\right).$

### Example: Simplifying Trigonometric Expressions Using Cofunction Identities

#### Question

$1 - \cos \left(\dfrac \pi 2 - y \right) \sin y=$

#### Explanation

First, we recall the cofunction identity for sine and cosine:

$$


\cos \left( \dfrac{\pi}{2} - y \right) = \sin y


$$

Substituting this into our expression, we get

$$


\begin{aligned}1−cos(\frac{𝜋}{2}−𝑦)sin⁡𝑦 & =1−sin\,𝑦\,sin⁡𝑦 \\ & =1−sin^{2}⁡𝑦 \\ & =cos^{2}⁡𝑦.\end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Simplifying Expressions Using Multiple Cofunction Identities

#### Question

$\tan \left(90^\circ - x \right) \cos x- \sec\left(90^\circ - x \right)=$

#### Explanation

First, we recall the cofunction identity for tangent and contangent:

$$


\tan\left(90^\circ - x \right) = \cot x


$$

We also recall the cofunction identity for secant and cosecant:

$$


\sec( 90^\circ - x ) = \csc x


$$

Substituting these identities into our expression and using the fact that

$$


\cot x = \dfrac{\cos x}{\sin x} \qquad \text{and} \qquad \csc x = \dfrac{1}{\sin x},


$$

we get the following:

$$


\begin{aligned}tan(90^{∘}−𝑥)cos⁡𝑥−sec(90^{∘}−𝑥) & =cot\,𝑥cos⁡𝑥−csc\,𝑥 \\ & =\frac{cos\,𝑥}{sin\,𝑥}⋅cos⁡𝑥−\frac{1}{sin\,𝑥} \\ & =\frac{cos^{2}⁡𝑥−1}{sin⁡𝑥} \\ & =\frac{−sin^{2}⁡𝑥}{sin⁡𝑥} \\ & =\frac{−sin⁡𝑥sin⁡𝑥}{sin⁡𝑥} \\ & =−sin⁡𝑥\end{aligned}


$$
