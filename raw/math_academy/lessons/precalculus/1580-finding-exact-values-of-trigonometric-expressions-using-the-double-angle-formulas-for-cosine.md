## Lesson

### Introduction

If we are given that $\cos{\theta} = \dfrac{4}{5}$ and $\sin{\theta}=\dfrac{3}{5},$ then we can find the value of $\cos 2\theta$ using the double-angle formula for cosine:

$$


\cos{2\theta} = \cos^2\theta - \sin^2{\theta}


$$

Substituting $\cos \theta= \dfrac 4 5$ and $\sin \theta = \dfrac{3}{5}$ into the above, we get

$$


\begin{aligned}cos⁡2𝜃 & =(\frac{4}{5})^{2}−(\frac{3}{5})^{2} \\ & =\frac{16}{25}−\frac{9}{25} \\ & =\frac{7}{25}\end{aligned}


$$

In fact, given only $\sin\theta$ or $\cos\theta,$ we can always work out $\cos2\theta$ using an alternative form of the double-angle formula for cosine:

$$


\begin{aligned}cos⁡2𝜃 & =2cos^{2}⁡𝜃−1 \\ cos⁡2𝜃 & =1−2sin^{2}⁡𝜃\end{aligned}


$$

Let's take a look at some more examples.

### Example: Finding the Exact Value for the Cosine of a Double Angle Given the Cosine of the Angle

#### Question

Given $\cos{x}=-\dfrac{1}{5},$ find the exact value of $\cos{2x}.$

#### Explanation

We're given the value of $\cos{x},$ so we'll use the following double-angle formula for cosine to compute $\cos2x\mathbin{:}$

$$


\cos{2x} = 2 \cos^2{x}-1


$$

Substituting $\cos{x} = -\dfrac{1}{5}$ into the above, we get

$$


\eqalign { \cos 2x &= 2\cos^2 x-1 \\[3pt] &= 2\left(-\dfrac{1}{5}\right)^2-1\\[3pt] &=2 \cdot \dfrac{1}{25}-1 \\[3pt] &=\dfrac{2}{25}-1 \\[3pt] &=-\dfrac{23}{25}. }


$$

### Example: Finding the Exact Value for the Cosine of a Double Angle Given the Sine of the Angle

#### Question

If $\sin x = -\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{6}},$ find the exact value of $\cos{2x}.$

#### Explanation

We're given the value of $\sin{x},$ so we'll use the following double-angle formula for cosine to compute $\cos2x\mathbin{:}$

$$


\cos{2x} = 1 - 2 \sin^2{x}


$$

Substituting $\sin{x} = -\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{6}}$ into the above, we get

$$


\eqalign { \cos 2{x} &= 1-2\sin^2{x} \\[5pt] &= 1-2\left(-\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{6}}\right)^2\\[5pt] &= 1-2\cdot\dfrac{4}{6}\\[5pt] &= 1-\dfrac{4}{3}\\[5pt] &= -\dfrac{1}{3}. }


$$

### Example: Finding the Exact Value for the Cosine of a Double Angle Given the Secant or Cosecant of the Angle

#### Question

Given that $\sec{x} = \dfrac{\sqrt{6}}{2},$ find the exact value of $\cos{2x}.$

#### Explanation

First, notice that we can compute $\cos{x},$ as follows:

$$


\begin{aligned}cos⁡𝑥 & =\frac{1}{sec⁡𝑥} \\ & =\frac{1}{(\frac{\sqrt{√6}}{2})} \\ & =\frac{2}{\sqrt{√6}} \\ & =\frac{\sqrt{√6}}{3}\end{aligned}


$$

Now, since we know $\cos{x},$ we use the following double-angle formula for cosine to compute $\cos2x\mathbin{:}$

$$


\cos{2x}= 2\cos^2{x}-1


$$

Substituting $\cos x = \dfrac{\sqrt{6}}{3}$ into the above, we get

$$


\begin{aligned}cos⁡2𝑥 & =2cos^{2}⁡𝑥−1 \\ & =2(\frac{\sqrt{√6}}{3})^{2}−1 \\ & =\frac{4}{3}−1 \\ & =\frac{1}{3}.\end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Finding the Exact Value for Cosine of a Double Angle Given a System of Equations

#### Question

If $\cos x + \sin x = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{3}$ and $\cos x - \sin x = -\dfrac{4}{3},$ find the value of $\cos{2x}.$

#### Explanation

First, let's recall the double-angle formula for cosine:

$$


\cos{2x} = \cos^2{x} - \sin^2{x}


$$

We are given the following system of equations:

$$


\begin{aligned}cos⁡𝑥+sin⁡𝑥=\frac{\sqrt{√2}}{3} \\ cos⁡𝑥−sin⁡𝑥=−\frac{4}{3}\end{aligned}


$$

Multiplying the two equations and using the double-angle formula for cosine, we get

$$


\begin{aligned}(cos⁡𝑥+sin⁡𝑥)(cos⁡𝑥−sin⁡𝑥) & =(\frac{\sqrt{√2}}{3})⋅(−\frac{4}{3}) \\ cos^{2}⁡𝑥−sin^{2}⁡𝑥 & =−\frac{4\sqrt{√2}}{9} \\ cos⁡2𝑥 & =−\frac{4\sqrt{√2}}{9}.\end{aligned}


$$
