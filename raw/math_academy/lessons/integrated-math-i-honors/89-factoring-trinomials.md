Let's think about how to factor the following trinomial:

$$


x^2 + 7x + 12


$$

### Example: Factoring Trinomials With Positive Coefficients

#### Question

Factor the expression $z^2+13z+36.$

#### Explanation

We must factor the quadratic expression

$$


z^2+13z+36.


$$

First, we find the factor pairs of the constant term $(36)\mathbin{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}(1,36),\,(2,18),\,(3,12),\,(4,9),\,(6,6)\end{aligned}


$$

Now, we find which factor pair adds up to the coefficient of the linear term $(13)\mathbin{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}1+36 & =37 & × \\ 2+18 & =20 & × \\ 3+12 & =15 & × \\ 4+9 & =13 & ✓ \\ 6+6 & =12 & ×\end{aligned}


$$

Using the factor pair identified in the previous step, write the linear term as $13z=4z + 9z\mathbin{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑧^{2}+13𝑧+36 & = \\ 𝑧^{2}+4𝑧+9𝑧+36 & \end{aligned}


$$

Finally, we factor the first two terms and the last two terms separately, and then factor the expression completely:

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑧^{2}+4𝑧+9𝑧+36 & = \\ (𝑧^{2}+4𝑧)+(9𝑧+36) & = \\ 𝑧(𝑧+4)+9(𝑧+4) & = \\ (𝑧+9)(𝑧+4) & \end{aligned}


$$

### Trinomials With Negative Constants

Let's now consider the following trinomial:

$$


x^2 - 2x - 8


$$

Notice that the constant term $(-8)$ is *negative*. So, when writing down our factor pairs, one number will always be positive, and the other will always be negative.

So, first, we write down all factor pairs of $-8{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}(1,−8),\,(−1,8),\,(2,−4),\,(−2,4)\end{aligned}


$$

Now, we find which factor pair adds up to the coefficient of the linear term $(-2)\mathbin{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}1+(−8) & =−7 & × \\ (−1)+8 & =7 & × \\ 2+(−4) & =−2 & ✓ \\ (−2)+4 & =2 & ×\end{aligned}


$$

Using the factor pair identified above, write the linear term as $-2x = 2x-4x\mathbin{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑥^{2}−2𝑥−8 & = \\ 𝑥^{2}+2𝑥−4𝑥−8 & \end{aligned}


$$

Finally, we factor the first two terms and the last two terms separately, and then factor the expression completely:

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑥^{2}+2𝑥−4𝑥−8 & = \\ (𝑥^{2}+2𝑥)+(−4𝑥−8) & = \\ 𝑥(𝑥+2)−4(𝑥+2) & = \\ (𝑥−4)(𝑥+2) & \end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Factoring Trinomials With Negative Constants

#### Question

Factor the expression $a^2+a-20.$

#### Explanation

We must factor the quadratic expression

$$


a^2+a-20.


$$

First, we find the factor pairs of the constant term $(-20)\mathbin{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}(1,−20),\,(−1,20),\,(2,−10),\,(−2,10),\,(4,−5),\,(−4,5)\end{aligned}


$$

Now, we find which factor pair adds up to the coefficient of the linear term $(1)\mathbin{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}1+(−20) & =−19 & × \\ −1+20 & =19 & × \\ 2+(−10) & =−8 & × \\ −2+10 & =8 & × \\ 4+(−5) & =−1 & × \\ −4+5 & =1 & ✓\end{aligned}


$$

Using the factor pair identified above, write the linear term as $a=-4a+5a\mathbin{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑎^{2}+𝑎−20 & = \\ 𝑎^{2}−4𝑎+5𝑎−20 & \end{aligned}


$$

Finally, we factor the first two terms and the last two terms separately, and then factor the expression completely:

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑎^{2}−4𝑎+5𝑎−20 & = \\ (𝑎^{2}−4𝑎)+(5𝑎−20) & = \\ 𝑎(𝑎−4)+5(𝑎−4) & = \\ (𝑎+5)(𝑎−4) & \end{aligned}


$$

### Trinomials With Negative Linear Coefficients and Positive Constants

Remember that to factor a quadratic expression, we must find a factor pair of the constant term that adds to the coefficient of the linear term.

With that in mind, let's factor the following trinomial:

$$


x^2 - 7x+12


$$

Notice that,

- the constant term $(12)$ is *positive*, while

- the coefficient of the linear term $(-7)$ is *negative*.

Since the constant is *positive* and the coefficient of the linear term is *negative*, we must first consider the *negative* factor pairs of the constant term $(12).$ These are as follows:

$$


(-1,-12),\qquad (-2,-6),\qquad (-3,-4)


$$

For each pair, their *product* is positive, and their *sum* is negative, which is what we want.

Now, we find which factor pair adds up to the coefficient of the linear term $(-7)\mathbin{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}(−1)+(−12) & =−13 & × \\ (−2)+(−6) & =−8 & × \\ (−3)+(−4) & =−7 & ✓\end{aligned}


$$

Using the factor pair identified in the previous step, write the linear term as $-7x = -3x - 4x \mathbin{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑥^{2}−7𝑥+12 & = \\ 𝑥^{2}−3𝑥−4𝑥+12 & \end{aligned}


$$

Next, we factor the first two terms and the last two terms separately:

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑥^{2}−3𝑥−4𝑥+12 & = \\ (𝑥^{2}−3𝑥)+(−4𝑥+12) & = \\ 𝑥(𝑥−3)+4(−𝑥+3) & \end{aligned}


$$

Now, to get a *common* factor in both terms, we take an additional factor of $-1$ from the second term:

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑥(𝑥−3)−4(𝑥−3)\end{aligned}


$$

Now that we have a common factor of $(x-3),$ we factor the expression fully in the usual way:

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑥(𝑥−3)−4(𝑥−3) & = \\ (𝑥−3)(𝑥−4) & \end{aligned}


$$

#### Explanation

$$
