## Lesson

### Introduction

Sometimes, we may be given a sequence in terms of an unknown variable. For example, suppose we're given the arithmetic sequence

$$


x,\quad x+3,\quad 8,\quad \ldots,


$$

where the value of $x$ is unknown. How can we use our knowledge of arithmetic sequences to solve for $x?$

To solve for $x,$ we can calculate the common difference $d$ in two different ways and then set the results equal to each other.

We begin by calculating the common difference using the first and second terms:

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑑 & =𝑎_{2}−𝑎_{1} \\ & =(𝑥+3)−(𝑥) \\ & =𝑥+3−𝑥 \\ & =3\end{aligned}


$$

Then, we calculate the common difference using the second and third terms:

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑑 & =𝑎_{3}−𝑎_{2} \\ & =(8)−(𝑥+3) \\ & =8−𝑥−3 \\ & =5−𝑥\end{aligned}


$$

Now, we have two different expressions for the common difference: $d=3$ and $d=5-x.$ These expressions must be equal, so we can set them equal to each other and then solve for $x\mathbin{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}3 & =5−𝑥 \\ 𝑥 & =2\end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Solving for a Variable Given the Common Difference

#### Question

The first term of an arithmetic sequence is $x$, the fourth term is $3x-2$, and the common difference is $12.$ What is the value of $x?$

#### Explanation

We are given the following information:

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑎_{1}=𝑥,\,𝑎_{4}=3𝑥−2,\,𝑑=12\end{aligned}


$$

Using the general formula for the common difference, we construct an equation that we can solve for $x\mathbin{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑑 & =\frac{𝑎_{𝑛}−𝑎_{𝑚}}{𝑛−𝑚} \\ 12 & =\frac{𝑎_{4}−𝑎_{1}}{4−1} \\ 12 & =\frac{(3𝑥−2)−𝑥}{4−1} \\ 12 & =\frac{2𝑥−2}{3} \\ 36 & =2𝑥−2 \\ 38 & =2𝑥 \\ 19 & =𝑥\end{aligned}


$$

Therefore, $x=19.$

### Example: Solving for a Variable Given Three Consecutive Terms

#### Question

Given that

$$


\dfrac{1}{4}x,\quad x,\quad 2x-2


$$

are three consecutive terms of an arithmetic sequence, find the value of $x.$

#### Explanation

To solve for $x,$ we can calculate the common difference $d$ in two different ways and then set the results equal to each other.

We begin by calculating the common difference using the first and second terms:

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑑 & =𝑎_{2}−𝑎_{1} \\ & =𝑥−\frac{1}{4}𝑥 \\ & =\frac{3}{4}𝑥\end{aligned}


$$

Then, we calculate the common difference using the second and third terms:

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑑 & =𝑎_{3}−𝑎_{2} \\ & =(2𝑥−2)−(𝑥) \\ & =𝑥−2\end{aligned}


$$

Now, we have two different expressions for the common difference: $d=\dfrac{3}{4}x$ and $d=x-2.$ These expressions must be equal, so we can set them equal to each other and then solve for $x\mathbin{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}\frac{3}{4}𝑥 & =𝑥−2 \\ 2+\frac{3}{4}𝑥 & =𝑥 \\ 2 & =𝑥−\frac{3}{4}𝑥 \\ 2 & =\frac{1}{4}𝑥 \\ 8 & =𝑥\end{aligned}


$$

Therefore, $x=8.$

### Example: Solving for a Variable Given Three Nonconsecutive Terms

#### Question

Given that the $4$th term of an arithmetic sequence is $-x$, the $6$th term is $x-1$, and $9$th term is $x+1$, what is $x?$

#### Explanation

We are given the following information:

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑎_{4}=−𝑥,\,𝑎_{6}=𝑥−1,\,𝑎_{9}=𝑥+1\end{aligned}


$$

To solve for $x,$ we can calculate the common difference $d$ in two different ways and then set the results equal to each other.

We begin by calculating the common difference using $a_4$ and $a_6\mathbin{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑑 & =\frac{𝑎_{6}−𝑎_{4}}{6−4} \\ & =\frac{𝑥−1−(−𝑥)}{6−4} \\ & =\frac{𝑥−1+𝑥}{2} \\ & =\frac{2𝑥−1}{2}\end{aligned}


$$

Then, we calculate the common difference using $a_6$ and $a_9\mathbin{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑑 & =\frac{𝑎_{9}−𝑎_{6}}{9−6} \\ & =\frac{(𝑥+1)−(𝑥−1)}{9−6} \\ & =\frac{𝑥+1−𝑥+1}{3} \\ & =\frac{2}{3}\end{aligned}


$$

Now, we have two different expressions for the common difference: $d=\dfrac{2x-1}{2}$ and $d=\dfrac{2}{3}.$ These expressions must be equal, so we can set them equal to each other and then solve for $x\mathbin{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}\frac{2𝑥−1}{2} & =\frac{2}{3} \\ 3⋅(2𝑥−1) & =2⋅2 \\ 6𝑥−3 & =4 \\ 6𝑥 & =7 \\ 𝑥 & =\frac{7}{6}\end{aligned}


$$
