## Lesson

### Introduction

**Absolute value inequalities** combine two basic concepts: absolute value and inequality. The absolute value of a number is its distance from zero, while an inequality specifies a range of values.

Consider a simple absolute value inequality, as follows:

$$


|x| < 7


$$

This inequality specifies every number $x$ that is less than a distance of $7$ from $0$. So, it represents the same thing as the following compound inequality:

$$


-7 < x < 7


$$

To solve an absolute value inequality, we first convert it to a compound inequality to remove the absolute value. Then, we can solve the compound inequality using the usual methods.

To illustrate, let's solve the following inequality:

$$


|2x+3| \leq 5


$$

First, we interpret the inequality: the inequality specifies every number $2x+3$ whose distance from zero is less than or equal to $5.$ So, it represents the same thing as the following compound inequality:

$$


-5 \leq 2x+3 \leq 5


$$

Now, we can solve the compound inequality using the usual methods:

$$


\begin{aligned}−5≤2𝑥+3≤5 \\ −8≤2𝑥≤2 \\ −4≤𝑥≤1\end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Absolute Value Inequalities Containing a Less Than (or Equal To) Symbol

#### Question

Solve the absolute value inequality $\left\vert\dfrac{x-1}{6}\right\vert \leq 1.$

#### Explanation

We convert the absolute value inequality into a compound inequality to remove the absolute value.

The inequality specifies every number $\dfrac{x-1}{6}$ whose distance from zero is less than or equal to $1.$ This corresponds to the following compound inequality:

$$


-1 \leq \dfrac{x-1}{6} \leq 1


$$

Now, we can solve the compound inequality using the usual methods.

$$


\begin{aligned}−1 & ≤\frac{𝑥−1}{6}≤1 \\ −6 & ≤𝑥−1≤6 \\ −5 & ≤𝑥≤7\end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Cases Where the Coefficient in the Absolute Value Is Negative

#### Question

Solve the absolute value inequality $\vert 10 - 2x \vert +1 < 5$.

#### Explanation

First, we isolate the absolute value on the left side of the inequality:

$$


\begin{aligned}|10−2𝑥|+1 & <5 \\ |10−2𝑥| & <4\end{aligned}


$$

Now, we convert the absolute value inequality into a compound inequality to remove the absolute value.

The inequality specifies every number $10-2x$ whose distance from zero is less than $4.$ This corresponds to the following compound inequality:

$$


-4 < 10 - 2x < 4


$$

Now, we can solve the compound inequality using the usual methods. (Remember that whenever we multiply or divide by a negative number, we have to flip the inequality signs.)

$$


\begin{aligned}−4<10−2𝑥 & <4 \\ −14<−2𝑥 & <−6 \\ 14>2𝑥 & >6 \\ 7>𝑥 & >3\end{aligned}


$$

To make the final result easier to read, we will rewrite it so that the smaller number is on the left:

$$


3 < x < 7


$$

### Greater Than (or Equal To) Absolute Value Inequalities

We take a similar approach for absolute value inequalities involving the "greater than" symbol. To illustrate, consider the following absolute value inequality:

$$


|x| \geq 6


$$

To solve this absolute value inequality, we convert it into a compound inequality to remove the absolute value. The inequality specifies every number $x$ whose distance from zero is greater than or equal to $6.$ This corresponds to the following compound inequality:

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑥 & ≤−6 & or & & 𝑥 & ≥6\end{aligned}


$$

We use the word "or" instead of "and" since only one of the inequalities $x\leq-6$ or $x\geq 6$ needs to be true for $|x| \geq 6$ to be true.

### Example: Absolute Value Inequalities Containing a Greater Than (or Equal To) Symbol

#### Question

Solve the absolute value inequality $|12x - 5| > 7.$

#### Explanation

We convert the absolute value inequality into a compound inequality to remove the absolute value.

The inequality specifies every number $12x - 5$ whose distance from zero is greater than $7.$ This corresponds to the following compound inequality:

$$


\begin{aligned}12𝑥−5 & <−7 & or & & 12𝑥−5 & >7\end{aligned}


$$

Now, we can solve each inequality using the usual methods.

$$


\begin{aligned}12𝑥−5 & <−7 & & & 12𝑥−5 & >7 \\ 12𝑥 & <−2 & & & 12𝑥 & >12 \\ 𝑥 & <−\frac{2}{12} & & & 𝑥 & >\frac{12}{12} \\ 𝑥 & <−\frac{1}{6} & & & 𝑥 & >1\end{aligned}


$$

Therefore, the final solution is

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑥 & <−\frac{1}{6} & or & & 𝑥 & >1.\end{aligned}


$$
