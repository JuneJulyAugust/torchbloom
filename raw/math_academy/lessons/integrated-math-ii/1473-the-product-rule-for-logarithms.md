## Lesson

### Introduction

We can simplify the addition of two logarithms that share a base using the **product rule** for logarithms.

The **product rule** states that two logarithms which share a common base $\color{blue}n$ satisfy

$$


\log_{\color{blue}n}(a) + \log_{\color{blue}n}(b) = \log_{\color{blue}n}\left( ab\right).


$$

So, we can use the product rule to rewrite $\log_{\color{blue}5}(4) + \log_{\color{blue}5}(3)$ as

$$


\begin{aligned}log_{5}⁡4+log_{5}⁡(3) & =log_{5}⁡(4⋅3) \\ & =log_{5}⁡(12).\end{aligned}


$$

The product rule works exactly the same for the natural and common logarithms.

### Example: Combining Logarithms Using the Product Rule

#### Question

Express $\log_3(2)+\log_3(5)$ as a single logarithm.

#### Explanation

First, let's recall the product rule for logarithms:

$$


\log_n a + \log_n b = \log_n(ab)


$$

Using the product rule for logarithms, the given expression can be rewritten as

$$


\begin{aligned}log_{3}⁡2+log_{3}⁡5 & =log_{3}⁡(2⋅5) \\ & =log_{3}⁡10.\end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Combining Logarithms With Coefficients Using the Product Rule

#### Question

Express $3\log_{5}{7}+3\log_{5}{3}$ as a single logarithm.

#### Explanation

First, let's recall the product rule for logarithms:

$$


\log_n a + \log_n b = \log_n(ab)


$$

If we factor the given expression and use the product rule for logarithms, we get

$$


\begin{aligned}3log_{5}⁡7+3log_{5}⁡3 & =3(log_{5}⁡7+log_{5}⁡3) \\ & =3log_{5}⁡(7⋅3) \\ & =3log_{5}⁡21.\end{aligned}


$$

Therefore,

$$


3\log_{5}{7}+3\log_{5}{3} = 3\log_{5} 21 .


$$

### Example: Separating a Logarithm Into Two Logarithms Using the Product Rule

#### Question

Express $\ln\left(x^2 y\right)$ as a sum of two logarithms.

#### Explanation

First, let's recall the product rule for natural logarithms:

$$


\ln{a} + \ln{b} = \ln(ab)


$$

Using the product rule, the given expression can be rewritten as

$$


\begin{aligned}ln⁡(𝑥^{2}𝑦) & =ln⁡(𝑥^{2}⋅𝑦) \\ & =ln⁡(𝑥^{2})+ln⁡𝑦.\end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Evaluating an Expression Using the Product Rule

#### Question

If $\log_2(3)=k$ and $\log_2(x)=p,$ what is the value of $\log_2(3x)?$

#### Explanation

First, let's recall the product rule for logarithms:

$$


\log_n a + \log_n b = \log_n(ab)


$$

Using the product rule, we have

$$


\begin{aligned}log_{2}⁡(3𝑥) & =log_{2}⁡3+log_{2}⁡𝑥 \\ & =𝑘+𝑝.\end{aligned}


$$
