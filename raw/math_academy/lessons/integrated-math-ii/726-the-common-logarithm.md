## Lesson

### Introduction

Suppose we are given the logarithm $\log (100).$ If no base is specified, then by default the base is equal to $10.$

In our example, we have

$$


\log(100)=\log_{10}(100)=\log_{10}(10^2) = 2.


$$

In general, the logarithm with base $10$ is called the **common logarithm**, and is defined as follows:

$a = 10^x$ is equivalent to $\log{a} = x$

Everything we did with logarithms, we can do with the common logarithm. For example, we can

- convert between exponential and logarithm form,

- evaluate common logarithm expressions, and

- calculate specific values of $\log a$ using a calculator.

Finally, note that most calculators have a button for the common logarithm. This is usually denoted $\log_{10}$ or simply $\log.$

### Example: Converting Between Exponential and Logarithmic Form With Common Logarithms

#### Question

Express the equation $10^3 = 1\,000$ using logarithmic notation.

#### Explanation

The common logarithm of $a$ is defined as follows:

$a = 10^x$ is equivalent to $\log{a} = x$

If we compare $10^3 = 1\,000$ with $10^x = a,$ we get that $a = 1\,000$ and $x = 3.$

Therefore, the given equation can be expressed as $\log{1\,000} = 3.$

### Example: Evaluating Common Logarithms

#### Question

Evaluate $\log\left(10^{7}\right).$

#### Explanation

The common logarithm of a power of $10$ is the power itself:

$$


\log\left(10^x\right) = x


$$

In our case, we have $x =7.$ Therefore,

$$


\log\left( 10^{7}\right) = 7.


$$

### Example: Evaluating Common Logarithms of Fractions and Roots

#### Question

Evaluate $\log\left(0.1 \right).$

#### Explanation

First, we rewrite $0.1$ as a power of $10$ (the base of the common logarithm) as follows:

$$


0.1 = 10^{-1}


$$

Therefore, we have

$$


\log\left( 0.1 \right) = \log\left(10^{-1}\right).


$$

The common logarithm of a power of $10$ is the power itself:

$$


\log\left(10^x\right) = x


$$

In our case, we have $x = -1.$ Therefore,

$$


\log\left( 10^{-1} \right)= -1.


$$

### Example: Evaluating Expressions Containing Common Logarithms Using a Calculator

#### Question

Rounded to $2$ decimal places, what is the value of $2\log(7) + 5\log(5)?$

#### Explanation

We compute the required expression to $2$ decimal places using a calculator, rounding the intermediate steps to $5$ decimal places to avoid rounding error. This gives

$$


\begin{aligned}2log⁡(7)+5log⁡(5) & ≈2(0.845\,10)+5(0.698\,97) \\ & =1.690\,20+3.494\,85 \\ & =5.185\,05 \\ & ≈5.19.\end{aligned}


$$
