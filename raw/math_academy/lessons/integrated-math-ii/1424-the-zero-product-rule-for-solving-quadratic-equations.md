### Example: Solving a Factored Quadratic Equation When Both Factors are Binomials

#### Question

Calculate the values of $x$ that satisfy $(2x-1)(5x+3)=0.$

#### Explanation

Applying the zero product rule, we get

$$


2x-1=0\qquad \textrm{or}\qquad 5x+3=0.


$$

We solve each of the equations separately, as follows:

$$


\begin{aligned}2𝑥−1 & =0 & \, & \, & 5𝑥+3 & =0 \\ 2𝑥 & =1 & \, & \, & 5𝑥 & =−3 \\ 𝑥 & =\frac{1}{2} & \, & \, & 𝑥 & =−\frac{3}{5}\end{aligned}


$$

Therefore, the solutions are $x=-\dfrac{3}{5},\dfrac{1}{2}.$

### Example: Solving a Factored Quadratic Equation When One Factor is a Binomial

#### Question

Solve the equation $x(x-16) = 0.$

#### Explanation

Applying the zero product rule, we get

$$


x = 0\qquad \textrm{or}\qquad x-16=0.


$$

Solving each of these equations separately, we find that the solutions are $x=0$ and $x=16.$

### Example: Constructing a Quadratic Equation With Specified Roots

#### Question

Find a quadratic equation that has roots $x=5$ and $x=7.$

#### Explanation

The given roots $x=5$ and $x=7$ are the solutions to the equations $x-5=0$ and $x-7=0$, respectively.

So, according to the zero product rule, they are also the solutions of the product

$$


(x-5)(x-7)=0.


$$

We multiply out the product and collect like terms to produce a quadratic equation in standard form:

$$


\begin{aligned} (x-5)(x-7) &=0\\x(x-7) - 5(x-7) &=0\\x^2 - 7x - 5x + 35 &= 0 \\x^2 -12x+35 &=0 \end{aligned}


$$

Therefore, a quadratic equation that has roots $x=5$ and $x=7$ is

$$


x^2 -12x+35=0.


$$
