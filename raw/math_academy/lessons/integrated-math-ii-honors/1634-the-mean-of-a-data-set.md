## Lesson

### Introduction

When summarizing a data set, we often want to produce a *single* number that best represents *all* numbers in the data set. One way to do this is to find the **mean** of the data set. The mean can be considered the "center" of the data set.

If we have a data set that consists of the points $x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_n,$ then the mean, denoted $\overline{x},$ is given by the following formula:

$$


\overline{x} = \dfrac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n x_i


$$

To demonstrate, let's compute the mean of the data set below:

$$


1, \: 7, \: 3, \: 9


$$

Here, there are $n=4$ data points. Adding up all the points in the data set, we get

$$


\sum_{i=1}^n x_i = 1 + 7 + 3 + 9 = 20.


$$

Therefore, the mean is

$$


\begin{aligned}\overset{𝑥}{} & =\frac{1}{𝑛}\underset{\underset{𝑖=1}{∑}}{\overset{}{𝑛}}𝑥_{𝑖} \\ & =\frac{1}{4}⋅20 \\ & =5.\end{aligned}


$$

In general, to compute the mean of a set of numbers, all we need to do is add up all the numbers and divide the result by how many numbers we added.

### Example: Computing the Mean of a Data Set From the Sum

#### Question

Given a data set with $12$ data points and $\displaystyle \sum_{i=1}^{12} x_i = 96,$ compute $\overline{x}.$

#### Explanation

If we have a data set that consists of the points $x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_n,$ then the mean is denoted by $\overline{x}$ and is given by the following formula:

$$


\overline{x} = \dfrac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n x_i


$$

Substituting $n=12$ and $\displaystyle \sum_{i=1}^{12} x_i = 96,$ we get

$$


\begin{aligned}\overset{𝑥}{} & =\frac{1}{12}⋅96=8.\end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Computing the Mean of a Data Set

#### Question

What is the mean of the following data set?

$$


9, \: 10, \: 8, \: 11, \: 12


$$

#### Explanation

If we have a data set that consists of the points $x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_n,$ then the mean is denoted by $\overline{x}$ and is given by the following formula:

$$


\overline{x} = \dfrac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n x_i


$$

Here, there are $n=5$ data points. Adding up all the points in the data set, we get

$$


\begin{aligned}\underset{\underset{𝑖=1}{∑}}{\overset{}{5}}𝑥_{𝑖} & =9+10+8+11+12 \\ & =50.\end{aligned}


$$

Therefore, the mean is

$$


\begin{aligned}\overset{𝑥}{} & =\frac{1}{𝑛}\underset{\underset{𝑖=1}{∑}}{\overset{}{𝑛}}𝑥_{𝑖} \\ & =\frac{1}{5}⋅50 \\ & =10.\end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Finding Missing Values in a Data Set Given the Mean

#### Question

Given that the mean of the following data set is $9,$ find the value of $a\mathbin{:}$

$$


5, \: 3, \: a, \: a, \: a, \: 7


$$

#### Explanation

If we have a data set that consists of the points $x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_n,$ then the mean is denoted by $\overline{x}$ and is given by the following formula:

$$


\overline{x} = \dfrac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n x_i


$$

Here, there are $n=6$ data points. Adding up all the points in the data set, we get

$$


\begin{aligned}\underset{\underset{𝑖=1}{∑}}{\overset{}{6}}𝑥_{𝑖} & =5+3+𝑎+𝑎+𝑎+7 \\ & =15+3𝑎.\end{aligned}


$$

Therefore, the mean is given by the expression

$$


\begin{aligned}\overset{𝑥}{} & =\frac{1}{𝑛}\underset{\underset{𝑖=1}{∑}}{\overset{}{𝑛}}𝑥_{𝑖} \\ & =\frac{1}{6}⋅(15+3𝑎) \\ & =\frac{15+3𝑎}{6}.\end{aligned}


$$

Finally, since we're told that $\overline{x} = 9,$ we have

$$


\begin{aligned}9 & =\frac{15+3𝑎}{6} \\ 54 & =15+3𝑎 \\ 39 & =3𝑎 \\ 𝑎 & =13.\end{aligned}


$$
