### Introduction

A **quadratic equation** is an equation that can be written in the form

$$


ax^2+bx+c=0,


$$

where $a,$ $b,$ and $c$ are constants and $a \neq 0.$ The above form is called the **standard form** of a quadratic equation. The left-hand side is a quadratic polynomial, and the right-hand side is zero.

For example, the equation

$$


4x^2=5-2x


$$

is a quadratic equation because it can be rearranged into the standard form of a quadratic equation, as follows:

$$


\begin{aligned}4𝑥^{2} & =5−2𝑥 \\ 4𝑥^{2}+2𝑥 & =5−2𝑥+2𝑥 \\ 4𝑥^{2}+2𝑥 & =5 \\ 4𝑥^{2}+2𝑥−5 & =5−5 \\ 4𝑥^{2}+2𝑥−5 & =0\end{aligned}


$$

Each of the terms in a quadratic equation has a particular name:

$$


\underbrace{\color{red}4\,x^2}_{\color{red} \large\text{quadratic}} \overbrace{\color{black}+\,2\,x}^{\color{black} \large\text{linear}} \underbrace{\color{blue}-\,5}_{\color{blue} \large\text{constant}} = \: 0.


$$

So in our case,

- the **quadratic term** is $4x^2$, and its coefficient is $4$

- the **linear term** is $2x$, and its coefficient is $2$

- the **constant term** is $-5.$

### Example: Identifying the Terms of a Quadratic Equation

#### Question

What is the coefficient of the quadratic term in the equation $-5x^2+3x+7=0?$

#### Explanation

The quadratic term is $-5x^2$ and its coefficient is $-5.$

### Example: Identifying Quadratic Equations

#### Question

Is $2x^2+3-x=x^2$ a quadratic equation?

#### Explanation

Let's try to write our equation in the standard form of a quadratic equation,

$$


ax^2 + bx + c = 0,


$$

where $a,$ $b,$ and $c$ are constants and $a \neq 0.$

First, we can collect the $x^2$ terms:

$$


\begin{aligned}2𝑥^{2}+3−𝑥 & =𝑥^{2} \\ 2𝑥^{2}+3−𝑥−𝑥^{2} & =𝑥^{2}−𝑥^{2} \\ (2𝑥^{2}−𝑥^{2})+3−𝑥 & =0 \\ 𝑥^{2}+3−𝑥 & =0\end{aligned}


$$

Then, let's put the terms in order:

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑥^{2}+3−𝑥 & =0 \\ 𝑥^{2}−𝑥+3 & =0\end{aligned}


$$

We see that our equation is in the standard form of a quadratic equation with $a=1,$ $b=-1,$ and $c=3.$

Therefore, our equation is a quadratic equation.

### Example: Writing a Quadratic Equation in Standard Form

#### Question

Write the quadratic equation $(y+1)(3y+2) = y^2$ in standard form.

#### Explanation

The standard form of a quadratic equation is

$$


ax^2 + bx + c = 0,


$$

where $a,$ $b,$ and $c$ are constants and $a \neq 0.$

The variable is $y$ here, but the same idea still applies. To write this equation in standard form, we start by expanding the binomial product on the left-hand side:

$$


\begin{aligned}(𝑦+1)(3𝑦+2) & =𝑦^{2} \\ 𝑦(3𝑦+2)+1(3𝑦+2) & =𝑦^{2} \\ 3𝑦^{2}+2𝑦+3𝑦+2 & =𝑦^{2} \\ 3𝑦^{2}+5𝑦+2 & =𝑦^{2}\end{aligned}


$$

Then, we collect the $y^2$ terms:

$$


\begin{aligned}3𝑦^{2}+5𝑦+2 & =𝑦^{2} \\ 3𝑦^{2}+5𝑦+2−𝑦^{2} & =𝑦^{2}−𝑦^{2} \\ (3𝑦^{2}−𝑦^{2})+5𝑦+2 & =0 \\ 2𝑦^{2}+5𝑦+2 & =0\end{aligned}


$$

We see that our equation is in the standard form of a quadratic equation with $a=2,$ $b=5,$ and $c=2.$

Therefore, our equation is a quadratic equation.
