## Lesson

### Introduction

Suppose that we have two numbers in scientific notation, like $2 \times 10^2$ and $3.5 \times 10^4.$ How can we multiply them together?

To calculate $(2 \times 10^2)\times(3.5 \times 10^4),$ we follow three steps:

- **Step 1:** Group the decimal parts and the powers of $10$ separately.

- **Step 2:** Multiply the decimal parts.

- **Step 3:** Multiply the powers of $10$ using the product rule for exponents.

Therefore, our final answer is $7\times 10^6.$

**Note**: The decimal part of a number in scientific notation is sometimes called the **mantissa**.

### Example: Multiplying Two Numbers With Integer Mantissas

#### Question

Simplify the product $\left(-2 \times 10^{-7} \right) \left(3 \times 10^{12} \right).$

#### Explanation

To multiply two numbers in scientific notation, we separate the decimal parts from the powers of $10$ and multiply them separately.

We also recall that a positive number times a negative number gives a negative number.

So, we get

$$

\begin{aligned}(−2×10^{−7})(3×10^{12}) & =(−2×3)×(10^{−7}×10^{12}) \\ & =−6×(10^{(−7)+12}) \\ & =−6×10^{5}.\end{aligned}

$$

### Example: Multiplying Two Numbers Where One Has an Integer Mantissa

#### Question

Simplify the product $\left(-3 \times 10^{6} \right) \left(-3.3 \times 10^{2} \right).$

#### Explanation

To multiply two numbers in scientific notation, we separate the decimal parts from the powers of $10$ and multiply them separately.

We also recall that a negative number times a negative number gives a positive number.

So, we get

$$

\begin{aligned}(−3×10^{6})(−3.3×10^{2}) & =((−3)×(−3.3))×(10^{6}×10^{2}) \\ & =(3×3.3)×(10^{6}×10^{2}) \\ & =9.9×(10^{6+2}) \\ & =9.9×10^{8}.\end{aligned}

$$

### Example: Multiplying Two Numbers With Decimal Mantissas

#### Question

Write $\left(-1.2 \times 10^{3}\right)\left(2.4 \times 10^{2}\right)$ in scientific notation.

#### Explanation

To multiply two numbers in scientific notation, we separate the decimal parts from the powers of $10$ and multiply them separately.

We also recall that a negative number times a positive number gives a negative number.

So, we get

$$

\begin{aligned}(−1.2×10^{3})×(2.4×10^{2}) & =((−1.2)×2.4)×(10^{3}×10^{2}) \\ & =−(1.2×2.4)×(10^{3}×10^{2}) \\ & =−2.88×(10^{3+2}) \\ & =−2.88×10^{5}.\end{aligned}

$$

### Example: Multiplying Numbers in Scientific Notation: Advanced Cases

#### Question

Express $\left(5.8 \times 10^{-2}\right) \left(4.5 \times 10^{5}\right)$ in scientific notation.

#### Explanation

To multiply two numbers in scientific notation, we separate the decimal parts from the powers of $10$ and multiply them separately:

$$

\begin{aligned}(5.8×10^{−2})(4.5×10^{5}) & =(5.8×4.5)×(10^{−2}×10^{5}) \\ & =26.1×(10^{(−2)+5}) \\ & =26.1×10^{3}\end{aligned}

$$

This is not in scientific notation since $26.1 \gt 10.$ Writing the decimal part in scientific notation, we get

$$

\begin{aligned}26.1×10^{3} & =(2.61×10^{1})×10^{3} \\ & =2.61×(10^{1}×10^{3}) \\ & =2.61×(10^{1+3}) \\ & =2.61×10^{4}.\end{aligned}

$$

Therefore, the final answer is $2.61 \times 10^{4}.$
