## Lesson

### Introduction

To divide powers of the same base, we simply *subtract* the exponents. This is known as the **quotient rule for exponents**. For instance, in the expression

$$


{\color{blue}3}^5 \div {\color{blue}3}^2,


$$

both powers have the same base $({\color{blue}3})$. Therefore, we can simplify this expression by subtracting the exponents:

$$


{\color{blue}3}^5 \div {\color{blue}3}^2 = {\color{blue}3}^{5-2} = {\color{blue}3}^3.


$$

**Note:** The quotient rule for exponents is really just a quicker alternative to the following process:

$$


\begin{aligned}3^{5}÷3^{2} & = \\ 3^{3+2}÷3^{2} & = \\ 3^{3}×\underset{=1}{\underset{}{3^{2}÷3^{2}}} & = \\ 3^{3}×1 & = \\ 3^{3} & \end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Expressions With Integer Bases

#### Question

Express $\dfrac{(-5)^{7}}{(-5)^3}$ as a base raised to a single exponent.

#### Explanation

First, we write the fraction as a division:

$$


\dfrac{(-5)^{7}}{(-5)^3} = (-5)^{7} \div (-5)^3


$$

Now, to divide powers of the same base, we simply subtract the exponents:

$$


(-5)^{7} \div (-5)^3 = (-5)^{7-3} = (-5)^{4}


$$

### Example: Expressions With Fractional Bases

#### Question

Express $\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^4 \div \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^2$ as a base raised to a single exponent.

#### Explanation

To divide powers of the same base, we simply subtract the exponents:

$$


\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^4 \div \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^2 = \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{4-2}= \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{2}


$$

### Example: Exponents With Decimal Bases

#### Question

Express $\dfrac{0.2^5}{0.2^2}$ as a base raised to a single exponent.

#### Explanation

First, we write the fraction as a division:

$$


\dfrac{0.2^5}{0.2^2} = 0.2^5 \div 0.2^2


$$

Now, to divide powers of the same base, we simply subtract the exponents:

$$


\begin{aligned}0.2^{5}÷0.2^{2}=0.2^{5−2}=0.2^{3}\end{aligned}


$$

### The Quotient Rule With Negative Exponents

We can use the quotient rule for exponents with negative exponents too. For example, to simplify $3^{-5}\div 3^{-3},$ we subtract the exponents:

$$


3^{-5} \div 3^{-3} = 3^{-5-(-3)} = 3^{-2}


$$

Now, to evaluate $3^{-2},$ we find the reciprocal of the base $(3)$ and then raise that reciprocal to the same power but with the opposite sign:

$$


3^{-2} = \left(\dfrac{1}{3}\right)^2 =\dfrac{1}{3} \times \dfrac{1}{3} = \dfrac{1}{9}


$$

### Example: Evaluating Products with Negative Exponents

#### Question

Simplify $\dfrac{2^3}{2^5}.$

#### Explanation

To divide powers of the same base, we subtract the exponents:

$$


\dfrac{2^3}{2^5} = 2^{3-5} = 2^{-2}


$$

To evaluate $2^{-2},$ we find the reciprocal of the base $(2)$ and then raise that reciprocal to the same power but with the opposite sign:

$$


2^{-2} = \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^2 =\dfrac{1}{2} \times \dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{1}{4}


$$
