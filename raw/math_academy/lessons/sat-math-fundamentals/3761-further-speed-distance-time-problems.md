## Lesson

### Introduction

If an object moves at a constant speed for some length of time, we can calculate how far it travels using the formula

$$


\text{distance} = \text{speed} \times \text{time}.


$$

We can use this formula to determine the time it takes for one object to catch up with another when both objects travel at a constant speed.

Let's see an example.

### Example: Modeling Two Objects Traveling in the Same Direction

#### Question

Martha and Lucy jog on an Olympic track, which is $0.4 \, \textrm{km}$ long. If Martha and Lucy started running simultaneously from the same point at speeds of $7 \, \textrm{km/h}$ and $5 \, \textrm{km/h},$ respectively, how long did it take Martha to lap Lucy for the first time?

#### Explanation

Let $T$ be the time it takes Martha to lap Lucy for the first time.

We start by filling in a speed-time-distance table:

When Martha first laps Lucy, Martha will have run exactly one more lap around the track than Lucy has. Since the track is $0.4 \, \textrm{km}$ around, we must have

$$


\begin{aligned}7𝑇 & =5𝑇+0.4 \\ 2𝑇 & =0.4 \\ 𝑇 & =0.2.\end{aligned}


$$

So, Martha takes $0.2$ hours to catch up with Lucy. If we convert this time to minutes, we get

$$


\begin{aligned}0.2\,h & =0.2\,h×\frac{60\,min}{h} \\ & =0.2\,h×\frac{60\,min}{h} \\ & =0.2×60\,min \\ & =12\,min.\end{aligned}


$$

Therefore, it takes Martha $12$ minutes to lap Lucy.

### Example: Modeling Two Objects Traveling in the Same Direction With Different Start Times

#### Question

On a highway, a taxi passes a gas station at a speed of $50 \, \textrm{km/h}.$ After $2.4$ hours, another taxi passes the same gas station at $80 \, \textrm{km/h},$ moving in the same direction. How long will it take for the second taxi to catch up with the first?

#### Explanation

Let $T$ be the time the second taxi takes to catch up with the first. Then, the first taxi has been traveling for $T+2.4$ hours before the second taxi catches up with it.

We start by filling in a speed-time-distance table for both taxis:

When the second taxi catches up with the first, both have traveled the same distance from the gas station. Therefore, we can set the distances equal and solve for time:

$$


\begin{aligned}50(𝑇+2.4) & =80𝑇 \\ 50𝑇+120 & =80𝑇 \\ 120 & =30𝑇 \\ \frac{120}{30} & =𝑇 \\ 4 & =𝑇 \\ 𝑇 & =4.\end{aligned}


$$

Therefore, it takes the second taxi $4$ hours to catch up with the first.

### Example: Modeling Two Objects Traveling in the Same Direction With Different Start Times and Unit Conversions

#### Question

Bus $A$ leaves a station at $9{:}00$ am traveling $40\,\textrm{mi/h}.$ $30$ minutes later, bus $B$ leaves the same station, traveling in the same direction at $70\,\textrm{mi/h}.$ At what time does bus $B$ catch up with bus $A?$

#### Explanation

Let $T$ be the number of hours it takes bus $B$ to catch up with bus $A.$ Then bus $A$ will have been traveling for $T+\dfrac{1}{2}$ hours because bus $A$ leaves the station $30$ minutes (or $\dfrac{1}{2}$ an hour) earlier than bus $B.$

We start by filling in a speed-time-distance table:

Both will have traveled the same distance from the station when they meet. Therefore, we can set the distances equal and solve for time:

$$


\begin{aligned}40(𝑇+\frac{1}{2}) & =70𝑇 \\ 40𝑇+20 & =70𝑇 \\ 20 & =30𝑇 \\ 𝑇 & =\frac{2}{3}\end{aligned}


$$

So, bus $B$ reaches bus $A$ in $\dfrac{2}{3}$ hours. If we convert this time to minutes, we get

$$


\begin{aligned}\frac{2}{3}\,h & =\frac{2}{3}\,h×\frac{60\,min}{h} \\ & =\frac{2}{3}\,h×\frac{60\,min}{h} \\ & =\frac{2}{3}×60\,min \\ & =40\,min.\end{aligned}


$$

So, bus $B$ reaches bus $A$ in $40$ minutes after leaving the station. Since bus $B$ leaves the station at $9{:}30$ am ($30$ minutes after $A$), it catches up with bus $A$ at $10{:}10$ am.
