## Lesson

### Introduction

When a radical equation contains two square roots, like

$$


\sqrt{2-x} - \sqrt{x} = 0,


$$

we manipulate the equation so that we have one square root on each side:

$$


\sqrt{2-x} = \sqrt{x}.


$$

Then, we raise both sides to the power of $2,$ and we solve the equation as usual:

$$


\begin{aligned}(\sqrt{√2−𝑥})^{2} & =(\sqrt{√𝑥})^{2} \\ 2−𝑥 & =𝑥 \\ 2𝑥 & =2 \\ 𝑥 & =1\end{aligned}


$$

Finally, we check whether the obtained value $x=1$ is actually a solution of the original equation:

$$


\begin{aligned}\sqrt{√2−(1)} & \overset{=}{?}\sqrt{√(1)} \\ \sqrt{√1} & \overset{=}{?}\sqrt{√1} \\ 1 & =1\,✓\end{aligned}


$$

Therefore, the only solution of $\sqrt{2-x} - \sqrt{x} = 0$ is $x=1.$

### Example: Solving Equations With Radical Expressions on Both Sides

#### Question

Solve $\sqrt{2x-5} = \sqrt{4 - x}.$

#### Explanation

To solve the equation $\sqrt{2x-5} = \sqrt{4 - x},$ we raise both sides to the power of $2\mathbin{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}\sqrt{√2𝑥−5} & =\sqrt{√4−𝑥} \\ (\sqrt{√2𝑥−5})^{2} & =(\sqrt{√4−𝑥})^{2} \\ 2𝑥−5 & =4−𝑥 \\ 3𝑥 & =9 \\ 𝑥 & =3\end{aligned}


$$

Then, we check that the obtained value $x=3$ is actually a solution of the original equation:

$$


\begin{aligned}\sqrt{√2(3)−5} & \overset{=}{?}\sqrt{√4−3} \\ \sqrt{√1} & \overset{=}{?}\sqrt{√1} \\ 1 & =1\,✓\end{aligned}


$$

Therefore, the only solution of $\sqrt{2x-5} = \sqrt{4 - x}$ is $x=3.$

### Example: Solving Equations With Radical Expressions on Both Sides and an Extra Term

#### Question

Solve $\sqrt {x - 2} = \sqrt {3x} - 2.$

#### Explanation

Even though we have an extra term on the right-hand side, we proceed in the same way and raise both sides of the equation to the power of $2\mathbin{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}\sqrt{√𝑥−2} & =\sqrt{√3𝑥}−2 \\ (\sqrt{√𝑥−2})^{2} & =(\sqrt{√3𝑥}−2)^{2} \\ 𝑥−2 & =(\sqrt{√3𝑥})^{2}−2\sqrt{√3𝑥}⋅2+2^{2} \\ 𝑥−2 & =3𝑥−4\sqrt{√3𝑥}+4 \\ 4\sqrt{√3𝑥} & =3𝑥−𝑥+4+2 \\ 4\sqrt{√3𝑥} & =2𝑥+6 \\ 2\sqrt{√3𝑥} & =𝑥+3.\end{aligned}


$$

Now we have an equation with one square root on one side and a linear expression on the other side. To solve this type of equation, we raise both sides to the power of $2$ one more time:

$$


\begin{aligned}(2\sqrt{√3𝑥})^{2} & =(𝑥+3)^{2} \\ 2^{2}⋅3𝑥 & =(𝑥)^{2}+2(𝑥)⋅3+3^{2} \\ 12𝑥 & =𝑥^{2}+6𝑥+9 \\ 0 & =𝑥^{2}+6𝑥+9−12𝑥 \\ 0 & =𝑥^{2}−6𝑥+9 \\ 0 & =(𝑥−3)^{2} \\ 𝑥 & =3\end{aligned}


$$

Finally, we check that the obtained value $x=3$ is actually a solution to the original equation:

$$


\begin{aligned}\sqrt{√3−2} & \overset{=}{?}\sqrt{√3(3)}−2 \\ \sqrt{√1} & \overset{=}{?}3−2 \\ 1 & =1\,✓\end{aligned}


$$

Therefore, the equation $\sqrt {x - 2} = \sqrt {3x} - 2$ has a single solution $x=3.$

### Example: Solving Radical Equations With Radical Expressions of Binomials on Both Sides and an Extra Term

#### Question

Solve for $x$ where $\sqrt {2x +5} -\sqrt {x+6} = 1.$

#### Explanation

First, we move the radicals to separate sides of the equation:

$$


\begin{aligned}\sqrt{√2𝑥+5}−\sqrt{√𝑥+6} & =1 \\ \sqrt{√2𝑥+5} & =\sqrt{√𝑥+6}+1\end{aligned}


$$

Then, we raise both sides to the power of $2\mathbin{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}\sqrt{√2𝑥+5} & =\sqrt{√𝑥+6}+1 \\ (\sqrt{√2𝑥+5})^{2} & =(\sqrt{√𝑥+6}+1)^{2} \\ 2𝑥+5 & =(\sqrt{√𝑥+6})^{2}+2\sqrt{√𝑥+6}+1^{2} \\ 2𝑥+5 & =𝑥+7+2\sqrt{√𝑥+6} \\ 2\sqrt{√𝑥+6} & =2𝑥+5−𝑥−7 \\ 2\sqrt{√𝑥+6} & =𝑥−2\end{aligned}


$$

Now we have an equation with one square root on one side and a linear expression on the other side. To solve this type of equation, we raise both sides to the power of $2$ one more time:

$$


\begin{aligned}(2\sqrt{√𝑥+6})^{2} & =(𝑥−2)^{2} \\ 4(𝑥+6) & =𝑥^{2}−4𝑥+4 \\ 4𝑥+24 & =𝑥^{2}−4𝑥+4 \\ 𝑥^{2}−8𝑥−20 & =0 \\ 𝑥^{2}−10𝑥+2𝑥−20 & =0 \\ (𝑥−10)(𝑥+2) & =0 \\ 𝑥=10,−2 & \end{aligned}


$$

Finally, we check the obtained values by substituting them into the original equation.

- Checking $x=10,$ we get So, the value $x=10$ is indeed a solution of the original equation

- Checking $x=-2,$ we get The value $x=-2$ does ** satisfy the original equation, so we discard it.

Therefore, the equation $\sqrt {2x +5} -\sqrt {x+6} = 1$ has a single solution $x=10.$
