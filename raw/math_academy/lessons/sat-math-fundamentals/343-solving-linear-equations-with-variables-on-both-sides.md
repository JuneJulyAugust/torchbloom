## Lesson

### Introduction

To solve a linear equation with variables on both sides, we bring all variables to one side of the equation and all constants to the other side.

For example, let's solve the following equation:

$$


5x = 2x + 12


$$

Notice that we have $5x$ on the left-hand side and $2x$ on the right-hand side. Since the variable on the left-hand side has a larger coefficient, let's bring all the variables to this side.

The first step is to remove the $\color{blue}2x$ from the right-hand side. We can do this by *subtracting* it from both sides of the equation, as follows:

$$


\begin{aligned}5𝑥 & =2𝑥+12 \\ 5𝑥−2𝑥 & =2𝑥+12−2𝑥 \\ 3𝑥 & =12\end{aligned}


$$

Finally, we divide both sides of the equation by $3\mathbin{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}3𝑥 & =12 \\ \frac{3𝑥}{3} & =\frac{12}{3} \\ \frac{3𝑥}{3} & =4 \\ 𝑥 & =4\end{aligned}


$$

So $x=4$ is our answer, and we're done.

We can check that $x=4$ is correct by substituting back into the original equation:

$$


\begin{aligned}5𝑥 & \overset{=}{?}2𝑥+12 \\ 5(4) & \overset{=}{?}2(4)+12 \\ 20 & =20\,✓\end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Solving Two-Step Equations

#### Question

Given that $12 - 5m = 3m$, what is the value of $m?$

#### Explanation

We must bring all variables to one side of the equation and all constants to the other side.

Notice that we have $-5m$ on the left-hand side and $3m$ on the right-hand side. Since the variable on the right-hand side has a larger coefficient, let's bring all the variables to this side.

The first step is to remove the $-5m$ from the left-hand side. We do this by adding ${\color{blue}5m}$ to both sides:

$$


\begin{aligned}12−5𝑚 & =3𝑚 \\ 12−5𝑚+5𝑚 & =3𝑚+5𝑚 \\ 12 & =8𝑚\end{aligned}


$$

Finally, we divide both sides of the equation by $8\mathbin{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}12 & =8𝑚 \\ \frac{12}{8} & =\frac{8𝑚}{8} \\ \frac{3}{2} & =\frac{8𝑚}{8} \\ \frac{3}{2} & =𝑚 \\ 𝑚 & =\frac{3}{2}\end{aligned}


$$

### Solving Equations With Variables and Constants on Both Sides

Let's now solve the following equation:

$$


9x -9= 1+4x


$$

To solve this equation, we must bring all variables to one side of the equation and all constants to the other side.

Notice that we have $9x$ on the left-hand side and $4x$ on the right-hand side. Since the variable on the left-hand side has a larger coefficient, let's bring all the variables to this side.

The first step is to remove the $4x$ from the right-hand side of the equation. We do this by subtracting ${\color{blue}4x}$ from both sides:

$$


\begin{aligned}9𝑥−9 & =1+4𝑥 \\ 9𝑥−9−4𝑥 & =1+4𝑥−4𝑥 \\ 5𝑥−9 & =1\end{aligned}


$$

Next, we remove the $-9$ from the left-hand side. We do this by adding ${\color{red}9}$ to both sides:

$$


\begin{aligned}5𝑥−9 & =1 \\ 5𝑥−9+9 & =1+9 \\ 5𝑥 & =10\end{aligned}


$$

Finally, we divide both sides of the equation by $5\mathbin{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}5𝑥 & =10 \\ \frac{5𝑥}{5} & =\frac{10}{5} \\ \frac{5𝑥}{5} & =2 \\ 𝑥 & =2\end{aligned}


$$

So $x=2$ is our answer. We can check that this is correct by substituting back into the original equation:

$$


\begin{aligned}9𝑥−9 & =1+4𝑥 \\ 9(2)−9 & \overset{=}{?}1+4(2) \\ 18−9 & \overset{=}{?}9 \\ 9 & =9\,✓\end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Solving Three-Step Equations

#### Question

Solve the equation $3x-12 = 5x + 8.$

#### Explanation

We must bring all variables to one side of the equation and all constants to the other side.

Notice that we have $3x$ on the left-hand side and $5x$ on the right-hand side. Since the variable on the right-hand side has a larger coefficient, let's bring all the variables to this side.

The first step is to remove the $3x$ from the left-hand side of the equation. We do this by subtracting ${\color{blue}3x}$ from both sides:

$$


\begin{aligned}3𝑥−12 & =5𝑥+8 \\ 3𝑥−12−3𝑥 & =5𝑥+8−3𝑥 \\ −12 & =2𝑥+8\end{aligned}


$$

Next, we remove the $8$ from the right-hand side. We do this by subtracting ${\color{red}8}$ from both sides:

$$


\begin{aligned}−12 & =2𝑥+8 \\ −12−8 & =2𝑥+8−8 \\ −20 & =2𝑥\end{aligned}


$$

Finally, we divide both sides of the equation by $2\mathbin{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}−20 & =2𝑥 \\ \frac{−20}{2} & =\frac{2𝑥}{2} \\ −10 & =\frac{2𝑥}{2} \\ −10 & =𝑥 \\ 𝑥 & =−10\end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Solving Equations Using the Distributive Law

#### Question

Solve for $t$ where $2(t+3)= 5t-3.$

#### Explanation

First, we expand the parentheses on the left-hand-side:

$$


\begin{aligned}2(𝑡+3) & =5𝑡−3 \\ 2⋅𝑡+2⋅3 & =5𝑡−3 \\ 2𝑡+6 & =5𝑡−3\end{aligned}


$$

Now, we must now bring all variables to one side of the equation and all constants to the other side.

Notice that we have $2t$ on the left-hand side and $5t$ on the right-hand side. Since the variable on the right-hand side has a larger coefficient, let's bring all the variables to this side.

The first step is to remove the $2t$ from the left-hand side of the equation. We do this by subtracting ${\color{blue}2t}$ from both sides:

$$


\begin{aligned}2𝑡+6 & =5𝑡−3 \\ 2𝑡+6−2𝑡 & =5𝑡−3−2𝑡 \\ 6 & =3𝑡−3\end{aligned}


$$

Next, we remove the $-3$ from the right-hand side. We do this by adding ${\color{red}3}$ to both sides:

$$


\begin{aligned}6 & =3𝑡−3 \\ 6+3 & =3𝑡−3+3 \\ 9 & =3𝑡\end{aligned}


$$

Finally, we divide both sides of the equation by $3\mathbin{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}9 & =3𝑡 \\ \frac{9}{3} & =\frac{3𝑡}{3} \\ 3 & =\frac{3𝑡}{3} \\ 3 & =𝑡 \\ 𝑡 & =3\end{aligned}


$$
