## Lesson

### Introduction

Suppose that we want to simplify the following expression:

$$


2+\dfrac{5}{2}x -5 + \dfrac{7}{2}x.


$$

We begin by collecting like terms.

$$


\begin{aligned}(\frac{5}{2}𝑥+\frac{7}{2}𝑥)+(2−5)\end{aligned}


$$

Then, we combine the like terms, simplifying the coefficients as much as possible.

$$


\begin{aligned}(\frac{5}{2}𝑥+\frac{7}{2}𝑥)+(2−5) & = \\ (\frac{5+7}{2})𝑥+(−3) & = \\ \frac{12}{2}𝑥−3 & = \\ 6𝑥−3 & .\end{aligned}


$$

Thus, the simplified expression is $6x-3.$

### Example: Simplifying Fractions with Like Denominators

#### Question

Simplify $\dfrac{1}{3}k +1 + \dfrac{4}{3}k - 3.$

#### Explanation

We collect and combine the like terms, simplifying the coefficients as much as possible.

$$


\begin{aligned}\frac{1}{3}𝑘+1+\frac{4}{3}𝑘−3 & = \\ (\frac{1}{3}𝑘+\frac{4}{3}𝑘)+(1−3) & = \\ (\frac{1+4}{3})𝑘+(−2) & = \\ \frac{5}{3}𝑘−2 & \end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Simplifying Fractions with Unlike Denominators

#### Question

Simplify the expression $-\dfrac{1}{2}u + \dfrac{7}{8}u.$

#### Explanation

We combine the like terms by adding the coefficients.

$$


\begin{aligned}−\frac{1}{2}𝑢+\frac{7}{8}𝑢 & = \\ (−\frac{1}{2}+\frac{7}{8})𝑢 & \end{aligned}


$$

In order to add the coefficients, we have to convert them to have the same denominator.

$$


\begin{aligned}(−\frac{1}{2}+\frac{7}{8})𝑢 & = \\ (−\frac{4}{8}+\frac{7}{8})𝑢 & = \\ (\frac{−4+7}{8})𝑢 & = \\ \frac{3}{8}𝑢 & \end{aligned}


$$

Thus, the expression simplifies to $\dfrac{3}{8}u.$

### Example: Using the Distributive Law With Fractions

#### Question

Simplify $\dfrac{1}{2}(p-4q).$

#### Explanation

Applying the distributive law and simplifying, we have:

$$


\begin{aligned}\frac{1}{2}(𝑝−4𝑞) & = \\ (\frac{1}{2})⋅𝑝−(\frac{1}{2})⋅4𝑞 & = \\ \frac{1}{2}𝑝−\frac{4𝑞}{2} & = \\ \frac{1}{2}𝑝−2𝑞 & \end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Simplifying Expressions Using the Distributive Law

#### Question

Simplify $\dfrac{1}{3}(3a+6b)+2a.$

#### Explanation

First, we apply the distributive law.

$$


\begin{aligned}\frac{1}{3}(3𝑎+6𝑏)+2𝑎 & = \\ (\frac{1}{3})⋅3𝑎+(\frac{1}{3})⋅6𝑏+2𝑎 & = \\ \frac{3𝑎}{3}+\frac{6𝑏}{3}+2𝑎 & = \\ 𝑎+2𝑏+2𝑎 & \end{aligned}


$$

Then, we collect and combine the like terms, simplifying the coefficients as much as possible.

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑎+2𝑏+2𝑎 & = \\ 𝑎+2𝑎+2𝑏 & = \\ 3𝑎+2𝑏 & \end{aligned}


$$
