## Lesson

### Introduction

In this lesson, we will learn how to determine unknown parameters in exponential functions using information about points on their graphs.

Often, we are given a function with an unknown constant, and by knowing where the graph passes through a particular point, we can solve for that constant.

Let's look at an example to make this concrete.

A function is defined by $f(x) = 7^{x} + a,$ where $a$ is a constant. The graph of $y = f(x)$ has its $y$-intercept at $(0,4).$ Let's use this information to find the value of $a.$

Since the graph passes through the point $(0,4),$ we have $f(0) = 4.$ So, we have the following equation, which we solve for $a{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑓(0) & =4 \\ 7^{0}+𝑎 & =4 \\ 1+𝑎 & =4 \\ 𝑎 & =3\end{aligned}


$$

Therefore, $a = 3.$

Now, let’s look at a few more examples.

### Example: Determining a Vertical Shift Given the Y-Intercept of an Exponential Curve

#### Question

A function is defined by $f(x) = 6 \cdot 3^{x} + n,$ where $n$ is a constant. The graph of $y = f(x)$ has its $y$-intercept at $(0,15).$ What is the value of $n?$

#### Explanation

Since the graph passes through the point $(0,15),$ we have $f(0) = 15.$ So, we have the following equation, which we solve for $n{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑓(0) & =15 \\ 6⋅3^{0}+𝑛 & =15 \\ 6⋅1+𝑛 & =15 \\ 6+𝑛 & =15 \\ 𝑛 & =9\end{aligned}


$$

Therefore, $n =9.$

### Example: Determining the Base of an Exponential Function Given a Point on the Curve

#### Question

An exponential function is defined by $f(x) = 15 \cdot c^{x},$ where $c$ is a positive constant. What is the value of $c$ if $f(2) = 135?$

#### Explanation

We are told that $f(x) = 15 \cdot c^{x}$ and that $f(2) = 135.$

Substituting $x = 2$ into the function $f$ and solve for $c,$ we obtain the following:

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑓(2) & =15⋅𝑐^{2} \\ 135 & =15𝑐^{2} \\ 15𝑐^{2} & =135 \\ \frac{15𝑐^{2}}{15} & =\frac{135}{15} \\ 𝑐^{2} & =9 \\ 𝑐 & =±3\end{aligned}


$$

Therefore, taking into account that $c$ is positive, we get $c = 3.$
