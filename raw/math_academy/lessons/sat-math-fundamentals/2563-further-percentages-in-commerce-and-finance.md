## Lesson

### Introduction

Remember that when items are sold or purchased, the final bill is not always the same as the original price of the item.

- When there is a **markup**, the final bill is greater than the original price because some extra amount is added to the original price.

- When there is a **markdown**, the final bill is less than the original price because some extra amount is subtracted from the original price.

To calculate the percentage of a markup, we can use the following formula:

$$


\textrm{percentage markup} = \dfrac{\textrm{amount of markup}}{\textrm{original amount}}


$$

This is simply the formula for percentage *increase*, rephrased in terms of markups.

Likewise, to calculate the percentage of a markdown, we can use the following formula:

$$


\textrm{percentage markdown} = \dfrac{\textrm{amount of markdown}}{\textrm{original amount}}


$$

This is simply the formula for percentage *decrease*, rephrased in terms of markdowns.

Note that sales taxes, commissions, gratuities, and fees are all examples of markups. Discounts and rebates are examples of markdowns.

### Example: Calculating a Percentage Markup

#### Question

At the supermarket, a particular brand of olive oil normally costs $12.$ However, the supermarket is running low on oil this week, so the prices are marked up to $15.$ What is the markup, as a percentage?

#### Explanation

We will use the formula

$$


\textrm{percent markup} = \dfrac{\textrm{amount of markup}}{\textrm{original price}}.


$$

The original price is $12$ dollars. The amount of markup is $15-12 = 3$ dollars. Substituting these values into the formula, we get:

$$


\begin{aligned}percent markup & =\frac{3}{12} \\ & =\frac{3÷3}{12÷3} \\ & =\frac{1}{4} \\ & =1÷4 \\ & =0.25\end{aligned}


$$

Finally, we convert the result to a percent:

$$


0.25 = 0.25 \times 100 \% = 25 \%


$$

So, the percent markup is $25\%.$

### Example: Calculating a Percentage Markdown

#### Question

A swimsuit store marks down their prices after summer. If the price of a swimsuit is $40$ during the summer and $15$ after summer, what is the markdown as a percentage?

#### Explanation

We will use the formula

$$


\textrm{percent markdown} = \dfrac{\textrm{amount of markdown}}{\textrm{original price}}.


$$

The original price is $40$ dollars. The amount of markdown is $40-15 = 25$ dollars. Substituting these values into the formula, we get:

$$


\begin{aligned}percent markdown & =\frac{25}{40} \\ & =\frac{25÷5}{40÷5} \\ & =\frac{5}{8} \\ & =5÷8 \\ & =0.625\end{aligned}


$$

Finally, we convert the result to a percent:

$$


0.625 = 0.625 \times 100 \% = 62.5 \%


$$

Therefore, the percent markdown is $62.5\%.$

### Calculating a Price Before or After a Markdown Was Applied

Sometimes, we might know the final price of an item after a markup or markdown has been applied. If we also know the percentage markup or markdown, we can then solve for the original price using the following formula:

$$


\textrm{percent paid} \times \textrm{original price} = \textrm{final price}


$$

Note the following:

- For a markup, the percent paid is $100\%$ *plus* the percent of the markup.

- For a markdown, the percent paid is $100\%$ *minus* the percent of the markdown.

Let's see some examples.

### Example: Calculating a Price Before a Markdown Was Applied

#### Question

Meredith bought a lawnmower on sale for $150.$ If the markdown was $20\%,$ what was the original price of the lawnmower?

#### Explanation

We will use the formula

$$


\textrm{percent paid} \times \textrm{original price} = \textrm{final price}.


$$

The markdown was $20\%,$ so the percent that Meredith paid was

$$


100\% - 20\% = 80\%.


$$

We also know that the final price was $150.$ So, if $p$ was the original price, then we have the following equation which we can solve for $p\mathbin{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}0.8𝑝 & =150 \\ 𝑝 & =\frac{150}{0.8} \\ 𝑝 & =187.5\end{aligned}


$$

Therefore, the original price of the lawnmower was $187.50.$

### Example: Calculating a Price Before a Markup Was Applied

#### Question

Cayden bought a shirt online. He requested that the shirt be shipped to him the very next day in exchange for a $40\%$ markup on the price. If the final price of the shirt was $35,$ then what was the original price of the shirt?

#### Explanation

We will use the formula

$$


\textrm{percent paid} \times \textrm{original price} = \textrm{final price}.


$$

The markup was $40\%,$ so the percent that Cayden paid was

$$


100\% + 40\% = 140\%.


$$

Expressing $140\%$ as a decimal, we have

$$


140\% = 140 \div 100 = 1.4 \, .


$$

We also know that the final price was $35.$ So, if $p$ was the original price, then we have the following equation which we can solve for $p\mathbin{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}1.4𝑝 & =35 \\ 𝑝 & =\frac{35}{1.4} \\ 𝑝 & =25\end{aligned}


$$

Therefore, the original price of the shirt was $25.$
