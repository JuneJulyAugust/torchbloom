## Lesson

### Introduction

Some linear equations can have **no solutions.** This means there is no value of $x$ that can be substituted into the equation to give a true statement.

For example, let's consider the following equation:

$$


x+1 = x


$$

Let's attempt to solve this equation. We start by subtracting $x$ from both sides.

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑥+1 & =𝑥 \\ 𝑥+1\,−\,𝑥 & =𝑥\,−\,𝑥 \\ (𝑥−𝑥)+1 & =(𝑥−𝑥) \\ 0+1 & =0 \\ 1 & =0\end{aligned}


$$

Notice that the final equation, $1=0,$ is always **false**. In other words, there is no value of $x$ that we can substitute to make the equation true.

Therefore, our equation has no solutions.

### Example: Identifying an Equation With No Solutions

#### Question

1. $4z + 3 = 4z + 9$

3. $3z - 2 = 3z - 2$

### Conditions for an Equation To Have No Solutions

In general, suppose we have an equation in the variable $x$ of the form

$$


ax = b,


$$

where

- $a$ and $b$ are constants, and

- $b$ is *not* equal to zero, which we write as $b \neq 0.$

Then, if $a = 0,$ the equation has **no solutions.**

Indeed, by substituting $a = 0$ into $ax=b,$ we get

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑎𝑥 & =𝑏 \\ (0)𝑥 & =𝑏 \\ 0 & =𝑏\end{aligned}


$$

which is a *contradiction* (i.e., a statement that's always false) because we were told that $b \neq 0.$ Therefore, the equation has no solution.

### Problems Involving Unknown Parameters

Consider the equation in the variable $x,$ given by

$$


7qx = -5,


$$

where $q$ is a constant. Given that this equation has no solutions, what is the value of $q?$

Notice that this equation is in the form $ax = b{:}$

$$


\underbrace{7q}_{\large a} \ x = \underbrace{-5}_{\large b}


$$

In this case,

$$


a = 7q, \qquad b = -5.


$$

We know that an equation of the form $ax=b$ with $b \neq 0$ has no solutions when $a = 0.$ Therefore, the equation has no solution if

$$


7q = 0.


$$

Solving this for $q,$ we get

$$


\begin{aligned}7𝑞 & =0 \\ \frac{7𝑞}{7} & =\frac{0}{7} \\ 𝑞 & =0.\end{aligned}


$$

Therefore, we conclude that the equation $7qx = -5$ has no solutions if $q = 0.$

**Note:** The constant $q$ in this problem is sometimes called a **parameter.**

### Example: Solving For an Unknown Parameter

#### Question

Consider the equation in the variable $y,$ given by

$$


4(r - 2)y = -8


$$

where $r$ is a constant. Given that this equation has no solution, what is the value of $r?$

#### Explanation

Suppose we have an equation of the form

$$


ax = b


$$

where $a$ and $b$ are constants, and $b\neq 0.$ If $a = 0,$ then the equation has no solution.

Notice that this equation is in the form $ay = b.$ In this case,

$$


a = 4(r - 2), \qquad b = -8.


$$

Therefore, the equation has no solution if

$$


4(r - 2) = 0.


$$

Solving this for $r,$ we get

$$


\begin{aligned}4(𝑟−2) & =0 \\ \frac{4(𝑟−2)}{4} & =\frac{0}{4} \\ 𝑟−2 & =0 \\ 𝑟 & =2.\end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Solving For an Unknown Parameter - Constants on Both Sides

#### Question

Consider the equation in the variable $z,$ given by

$$


(r - 9)z + 2 = -1


$$

where $r$ is a constant. Given that this equation has no solution, what is the value of $r?$

#### Explanation

Suppose we have an equation of the form

$$


ax = b


$$

where $a$ and $b$ are constants, and $b\neq 0.$ If $a = 0,$ then the equation has no solution.

First, let's write our equation in the form $az = b{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}(𝑟−9)𝑧+2 & =−1 \\ (𝑟−9)𝑧+2−2 & =−1−2 \\ \underset{𝑎}{\underset{}{(𝑟−9)}}𝑧 & =\underset{𝑏}{\underset{}{−3}}\end{aligned}


$$

Therefore, the equation has no solution if

$$


r - 9 = 0.


$$

Solving this for $r,$ we get

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑟−9 & =0 \\ 𝑟 & =9.\end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Solving For an Unknown Parameter - Variables and Constants on Both Sides

#### Question

Consider the equation in the variable $x,$ given by

$$


2nx + 7 = -4x + 5


$$

where $n$ is a constant. Given that this equation has no solution, what is the value of $n?$

#### Explanation

Suppose we have an equation of the form

$$


ax = b


$$

where $a$ and $b$ are constants, and $b\neq 0.$ If $a = 0,$ then the equation has no solution.

First, we rewrite the equation in the form $ax = b.$

We start by adding $4x$ to both sides of the equation:

$$


\begin{aligned}2𝑛𝑥+7 & =−4𝑥+5 \\ 2𝑛𝑥+4𝑥+7 & =−4𝑥+5+4𝑥 \\ 2𝑛𝑥+4𝑥+7 & =5\end{aligned}


$$

Then, we subtract $7$ from both sides:

$$


\begin{aligned}2𝑛𝑥+4𝑥+7 & =5 \\ 2𝑛𝑥+4𝑥+7−7 & =5−7 \\ 2𝑛𝑥+4𝑥 & =−2\end{aligned}


$$

Next, we factor $x$ on the left-hand side. This gives

$$


(2n + 4)x = -2.


$$

Notice that this equation is now in the form $ax = b{:}$

$$


\underbrace{(2n + 4)}_{a}x = \underbrace{-2}_{b}


$$

Therefore, the equation has no solution if

$$


2n + 4= 0.


$$

Solving this for $n,$ we get

$$


\begin{aligned}2𝑛+4 & =0 \\ 2𝑛+4−4 & =0−4 \\ 2𝑛 & =−4 \\ \frac{2𝑛}{2} & =\frac{−4}{2} \\ 𝑛 & =−2.\end{aligned}


$$
