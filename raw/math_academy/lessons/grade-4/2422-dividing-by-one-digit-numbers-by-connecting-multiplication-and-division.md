## Lesson

### Introduction

We can use multiplication as repeated addition and the relationship between multiplication and division to solve division problems.

For example, suppose we are given the following multiplication problem:

$$
20 \times 4 = {\color{blue}{80}}
$$

As we've seen, we can generate more multiplication problems using repeated addition. For example:

$$
\begin{aligned}21×4 & =80+4=84 \\ 22×4 & =84+4=88\end{aligned}
$$

Let's write these results a little more clearly (without the intermediate step):

$$
\begin{aligned}21×4 & =84 \\ 22×4 & =88\end{aligned}
$$

Each of these new multiplication problems can be used to generate *two* new division problems!

$$
\begin{aligned}84 & ÷4=21,\, & & 84÷21=4 \\ 88 & ÷4=22,\, & & 88÷22=4\end{aligned}
$$

We can use this idea to help us divide large numbers. Let's see how.

### Example: Dividing Two-Digit Numbers by One-Digit Numbers

#### Question

You're given the following multiplication problem:

$$
10 \times 7 = 70
$$

Use the equation above and the relationship between multiplication and division to determine which of the following is the solution to $98 \div 7.$

1. $11$

2. $12$

3. $13$

4. $14$

#### Explanation

If $0$ then by the relationship between multiplication and division, we have

$$
0
$$

We start with the given multiplication problem:

$$
10 \times 7 = {\color{blue}{70}}
$$

Let's now try the numbers in the list, **. We can use repeated addition to compute the products.

$$
\begin{aligned}11×7 & =70+7=77\,× \\ 12×7 & =77+7=84\,× \\ 13×7 & =84+7=91\,× \\ 14×7 & =91+7=98\,✓\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, $98 \div 7 = 14.$

### Using Repeated Subtraction

We can also use repeated *subtraction* and the relationship between multiplication and division to solve division problems.

We once again consider the following multiplication problem:

$$
20 \times 4 = {\color{blue}{80}}
$$

Let's generate some more multiplication problems using repeated subtraction:

$$
\begin{aligned}19×4 & =80−4=76 \\ 18×4 & =76−4=72\end{aligned}
$$

Writing these results a little more clearly (without the intermediate step), we get the following:

$$
\begin{aligned}19×4 & =76 \\ 18×4 & =72\end{aligned}
$$

Each of these new multiplication problems can be used to generate two new division problems:

$$
\begin{aligned}76 & ÷4=19,\, & & 76÷19=4 \\ 72 & ÷4=18,\, & & 72÷18=4\end{aligned}
$$

Let's now use repeated subtraction to help us divide some large numbers.

### Example: Dividing a Two or Three-Digit Number Using Repeated Subtraction

#### Question

You're given the following multiplication problem:

$$
70 \times 4 = 280
$$

Use the equation above and the relationship between multiplication and division to find which of the following is the solution to $268 \div 4.$

1. $69$

2. $68$

3. $67$

4. $66$

#### Explanation

If $0$ then by the relationship between multiplication and division, we have

$$
0
$$

We start with the given multiplication problem:

$$
70 \times 4 = {\color{blue}{280}}
$$

Let's now try the numbers in the list, starting with the **. We can use repeated subtraction to compute the products.

$$
\begin{aligned}69×4 & =280−4=276\,× \\ 68×4 & =276−4=272\,× \\ 67×4 & =272−4=268\,✓\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, $268 \div 4 = 67.$
