### Introduction

Whenever we write a mixed number as a decimal, our decimal will have

- a **whole number part** to the *left* of the decimal point, corresponding to the whole number part of the mixed number, and

- a **decimal part** to the *right* of the decimal point, corresponding to the fractional part of the mixed number.

$$

### Converting Fractions to Decimals Using Equivalent Fractions

We sometimes need to find an equivalent fraction for the fractional part when expressing a mixed number as a decimal.

For example, let's consider the following mixed number:

$$
{\color{blue}2}\,{\color{red}\dfrac{1}{5}}
$$

Notice the denominator of the fractional part $({\color{red}{5}})$ is not $10,100,$ or $1,000.$ However, we can still express this fraction as a decimal, as follows:

- The whole number part is ${\color{blue}2}.$

- The fractional part is $\dfrac{1}{5}.$ We can make a denominator of $10$ by multiplying the numerator and denominator by $2{:}$ So, the fractional part can be represented as a decimal with $1$ decimal place:

Therefore, we can express our mixed number as a decimal as follows:

$$
{\color{blue}{2}}\,{\color{red}{\dfrac{1}{5}}} = {\color{blue}{2.}}{\color{red}{2}}
$$
