### Introduction

Sometimes, we may have two equations with two variables. However, we can often combine the two equations into a single equation that can be solved by the usual methods.

For example, suppose that $y = 2x$ and $x=3,$ and we want to find the value of $y.$

To combine these two equations, we can *substitute* the value ${\color{blue}x} = {\color{blue}3}$ into the equation $y=2x,$ as follows:

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑦 & =2𝑥 \\ 𝑦 & =2×3 \\ 𝑦 & =6\end{aligned}



$$

Therefore, $y=6.$

### Example: Substituting into a Two-Variable Equation

#### Question

Alex has $m$ marbles, and Jason has $n$ marbles. The relationship between $m$ and $n$ is given by the equation

$$



m = n + 10.



$$

If Jason has $15$ marbles, how many does Alex have?

#### Explanation

To find the number of marbles that Alex has, we substitute ${\color{blue}{n}}={\color{blue}{15}}$ into the equation:

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑚 & =𝑛+10 \\ 𝑚 & =15+10 \\ 𝑚 & =25\end{aligned}



$$

Therefore, Alex has $25$ marbles.

### Example: Substituting into Two-Step Equations

#### Question

Find $p,$ if $p = 4q - 1$ and $q = 6.$

#### Explanation

To find the value of $p,$ we substitute $\color{blue}{q}=6$ into the equation:

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑝 & =4𝑞−1 \\ 𝑝 & =4×6−1 \\ 𝑝 & =24−1 \\ 𝑝 & =23\end{aligned}



$$

### Constructing a Two-Variable Equation

Let's now learn how to construct equations from context.

Suppose Lena earns four times as much money per hour as Marcus.

If $l$ represents Lena's hourly wage and $m$ represents Marcus's hourly wage, what is the relationship between $l$ and $m?$

Assume that Marcus earns $8$ dollars per hour. Then, Lena earns

$\qquad$ $4 \cdot {\color{blue}8} = {\color{red}32}$ dollars per hour

If Marcus earns $9$ dollars per hour, then Lena earns

$\qquad$ $4 \cdot {\color{blue}9} = {\color{red}36}$ dollars per hour

If Marcus earns $10$ dollars per hour, then Lena earns

$\qquad$ $4 \cdot {\color{blue}10} = {\color{red}40}$ dollars per hour

As we can see from the above examples, in order to find out how much Lena earns per hour, we must multiply $4$ by Marcus's hourly wage.

So, if Marcus earns ${\color{blue}m}$ dollars per hour and Lena earns ${\color{red}l}$ dollars per hour, then we must have

$$



4{\color{blue}m} = {\color{red}l}.



$$

Therefore, the relation between $l$ and $m$ is

$$



l = 4m.



$$

### Dependent and Independent Variables

When modeling a real-life situation with variables, one variable often depends on another.

For example, suppose that each gallon of ice cream at a store costs $5$ dollars. If $g$ is the number of gallons of ice cream and $p$ is the corresponding price, then we have the following equation connecting the two:

$$



p = 5g



$$

Here, the price ($p$) *depends* on the number of gallons ($g$) of ice cream we purchase. So, we call $p$ the **dependent variable**.

Since we are free to choose the number of gallons ($g$), we call $g$ the **independent variable**.

### Example: Classifying Dependent and Independent Variables

#### Question

Johana gets $8$ identical pieces of cake for each cake she makes.

If $p$ represents the number of pieces of cake and $c$ represents the number of cakes, then which of the following statements are true?

1. $c = 8p$

2. $p$ is the dependent variable

3. $c$ is the independent variable

#### Explanation

First, let's construct a two-variable equation for our situation.

- If Johana makes ${\color{blue}1}$ cake, she gets $8 \times {\color{blue}1} = 8$ pieces of cake.

- If Johana makes $\color{blue}2$ cakes, she gets $8 \times {\color{blue}2} = 16$ pieces of cake.

- If Johana makes $\color{blue}3$ cakes, she gets $8 \times {\color{blue}3} = 24$ pieces of cake. $\qquad\ldots$

- If Johana makes $\color{blue}c$ cakes, she gets $8 \times {\color{blue}c} = 8c$ pieces of cake.

Therefore, we get the equation

$$



p = 8c.



$$

Thus, statement I is false.
