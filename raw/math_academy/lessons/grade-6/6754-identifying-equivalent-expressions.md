### Introduction

Two expressions are **equivalent** if they always have the same value, even if they look different.

A useful way to check whether two expressions are equivalent is to simplify each one. If they can be written in the same form, they are equivalent. This often involves using techniques like *distributing*, *collecting like terms*, or *factoring*.

For example, let's check if $2(3x+4)$ and $8x+8-2x$ are equivalent.

The first expression, $2(3x+4),$ is already simplified in a factored form. We will now simplify the second expression, $8x+8-2x,$ to see if we can get it into the same form.

First, we simplify the second expression by collecting like terms:

$$



\begin{aligned}8𝑥+8−2𝑥 & = \\ 8𝑥−2𝑥+8 & = \\ (8−2)𝑥+8 & = \\ 6𝑥+8 & \end{aligned}



$$

Then, we factor out the greatest common factor. Since $\text{GCF}(6x,8)=2,$ we get

$$



\begin{aligned}6𝑥+8 & = \\ (2⋅3𝑥)+(2⋅4) & = \\ 2(3𝑥+4) & .\,✓\end{aligned}



$$

The simplified form, $2(3x+4),$ matches the first expression.

Since the expressions can be written in the same form, they are equivalent.

### Example: Identifying Equivalent Expressions by Distributing, Collecting Like Terms, and Factoring

#### Question

$8b+5+3(4b-6)=$

#### Explanation

First, using the distributive law, we multiply each term inside the parentheses by $3{:}$

$$



\begin{aligned}8𝑏+5+3(4𝑏−6) & = \\ 8𝑏+5+3⋅4𝑏−3⋅6 & = \\ 8𝑏+5+12𝑏−18 & \end{aligned}



$$

The resulting expression has two groups like terms, $8b$ and $12b,$ and $5$ and $-18.$ Combining the terms in each group, we have

$$



\begin{aligned}8𝑏+5+12𝑏−18 & = \\ 8𝑏+12𝑏+5−18 & = \\ (8+12)𝑏+(5−18) & = \\ 20𝑏−13 & .\end{aligned}



$$

Finally, we factor out the greatest common factor. Since $\text{GCF}(20b,13)=1,$ there is nothing to factor out.

$$



20b - 13



$$

Therefore, the expression is equivalent to $20b-13.$
