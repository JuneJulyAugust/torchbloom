## Lesson

### Introduction

**Speed** is a special unit rate applied to moving objects. It is defined as *the distance traveled per unit of time*.

We can calculate the speed of a moving object using the following equation:

$$



\text{speed} = \dfrac{\text{distance}}{\text{time}}



$$

For example, suppose that a car travels $80$ miles in $2$ hours at a constant rate. What is the speed of the car?

To find the speed (in miles per hour), we divide the number of miles, $80,$ by the number of hours, $2.$ This gives

$$



\dfrac{80}{2} = 40.



$$

Therefore, the speed of the car is $40$ miles per hour.

### Example: Calculating the Speed of an Object

#### Question

At full speed, a fully grown cheetah can cover a distance of $998$ feet in $10$ seconds. What is the speed of the cheetah when running at full speed?

#### Explanation

Speed is a special unit rate applied to moving objects. It is defined as **:

$$



\text{speed} = \dfrac{\text{distance}}{\text{time}}



$$

To find the speed in feet per second, we divide the number of feet, $998,$ by the number of seconds, $10.$ This gives

$$



\begin{aligned}\frac{998}{10} & =99.8.\end{aligned}



$$

Therefore, the speed of the cheetah is $99.8$ feet per second.

### Calculating the Distance Traveled by an Object

Let's return to the example of a car traveling at ${\color{red}{40}}$ miles per hour.

If the car travels at this speed, it covers a distance of ${\color{red}{40}}$ miles *every hour*. Therefore, we can deduce the following:

- after ${\color{blue}{1}}$ hour, the car covers a distance of ${\color{red}{40}}\times{\color{blue}{1}} = 40$ miles

- after ${\color{blue}{2}}$ hours, the car covers a distance of ${\color{red}{40}}\times{\color{blue}{2}} = 80$ miles

- after ${\color{blue}{3}}$ hours, the car covers a distance of ${\color{red}{40}}\times{\color{blue}{3}} = 120$ miles

- after ${\color{blue}{4}}$ hours, the car covers a distance of ${\color{red}{40}}\times{\color{blue}{4}} = 160$ miles

- $\cdots$

This suggests the following formula:

$$



\text{distance} = \text{speed} \times \text{time}



$$

We can use this formula to calculate the distance traveled by an object moving at a constant speed.

For instance, suppose an elevator traveled for $1.5$ minutes at a constant speed of $120$ feet per minute. What distance is covered by the elevator?

The speed is $120$ feet per minute. To find the distance covered in $1.5$ minutes, we multiply the speed by $1.5.$ This gives

$$



\begin{aligned}distance & =speed×time \\ & =120×1.5 \\ & =180.\end{aligned}



$$

Therefore, the elevator covered a distance of $180$ feet.

### Example: Calculating Distance Given Speed and Time

#### Question

Moving at a constant rate, it takes $8$ seconds for a particular type of fish to swim a distance of $200$ centimeters. Find the distance this fish can cover in $3$ seconds.

#### Explanation

Speed is a special unit rate applied to moving objects. It is defined as **:

$$



\text{speed} = \dfrac{\text{distance}}{\text{time}}



$$

To find the speed, in centimeters per second, we divide the number of centimeters, $200,$ by the number of seconds, $8.$ This gives

$$



\begin{aligned}\frac{200}{8} & =200÷8 \\ & =25.\end{aligned}



$$

So, the speed of the fish is $25$ centimeters per second.

To find the distance covered in $3$ seconds, we multiply the speed by $3.$ This gives

$$



\begin{aligned}distance & =speed×time \\ & =25×3 \\ & =75.\end{aligned}



$$

Therefore, the fish can cover $75$ centimeters in $3$ seconds.

### Calculating the Total Time an Object is in Motion

Let's recall the formula relating distance to speed and time:

$$



\text{speed} \times \text{time} = \text{distance}



$$

Using the relationship between multiplication and division, we can write this equation as

$$



\text{time} = \text{distance} \div \text{speed}



$$

which is equivalent to the formula

$$



\text{time} = \dfrac{\text{distance}}{\text{speed}}.



$$

We can use this formula to calculate the time taken for an object moving at a particular speed to cover a certain distance.

For example, suppose a plane flew for $3\,120$ kilometers at a constant speed of $520$ kilometers per hour. For how long was the plane in motion?

To determine the time the plane was in motion, we divide the distance, $3\,120$ kilometers, by the speed, $520$ kilometers per hour. This gives

$$



\begin{aligned}time & =\frac{distance}{speed} \\ & =\frac{3\,120}{520} \\ & =\frac{3\,120÷10}{520÷10} \\ & =\frac{312}{52} \\ & =312÷52 \\ & =6.\end{aligned}



$$

Therefore, the plane was in motion for $6$ hours.

### Example: Calculating Time Given Distance and Speed

#### Question

It takes $5$ days for the Earth to travel $13$ million kilometers around the Sun. How much time does it take for the Earth to travel $52$ million kilometers around the Sun?

#### Explanation

Speed is a special unit rate applied to moving objects. It is defined as **:

$$



\text{speed} = \dfrac{\text{distance}}{\text{time}}



$$

To find the speed, in millions of kilometers per day, we divide the number of million kilometers, $13,$ by the number of days, $5.$ This gives

$$



\begin{aligned}\frac{13}{5} & =13÷5 \\ & =2.6.\end{aligned}



$$

So, the speed of the Earth is $2.6$ million kilometers per day.

To find the time it takes the Earth to cover $52$ million kilometers, we divide the distance by $2.6.$ This gives

$$



\begin{aligned}time & =\frac{distance}{speed} \\ & =\frac{52}{2.6} \\ & =\frac{52×10}{2.6×10} \\ & =\frac{520}{26} \\ & =520÷26 \\ & =20.\end{aligned}



$$

Therefore, it takes the Earth $20$ days to travel $52$ million kilometers around the Sun.
