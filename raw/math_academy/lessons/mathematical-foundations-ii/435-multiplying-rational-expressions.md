## Lesson

### Introduction

Multiplying rational expressions is just like multiplying fractions. After all, fractions are just rational expressions with constants!

For example, to multiply the rational expressions $\dfrac{3}{x}$ and $\dfrac{y}{2},$ we multiply the numerators and the denominators as follows:

$$


\dfrac{3}{x} \cdot \dfrac{y}{2} = \dfrac{3 \cdot y}{x \cdot 2} = \dfrac{3y}{2x}


$$

### Example: Multiplying Rational Expressions With No Common Factor

#### Question

Simplify $\dfrac 2 x \cdot \dfrac 3 {y^2}.$

#### Explanation

We multiply the numerators and the denominators as follows:

$$


\begin{aligned} \dfrac 2 x \cdot \dfrac 3 {y^2} &=\dfrac {2 \cdot 3} {x \cdot y^2} &=\dfrac {6} {xy^2} \end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Multiplying Rational Expressions and Simplifying the Result

#### Question

Given that $\dfrac {6a} {b^2} = x$ and $\dfrac {3b} {2a}= y,$ what is $x\cdot y?$

#### Explanation

We multiply the numerators and the denominators as follows:

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑥⋅𝑦 & =\frac{6𝑎}{𝑏^{2}}⋅\frac{3𝑏}{2𝑎} \\ & =\frac{6𝑎⋅3𝑏}{𝑏^{2}⋅2𝑎} \\ & =\frac{18𝑎𝑏}{2𝑎𝑏^{2}} \\ & =\frac{9}{𝑏}.\end{aligned}


$$

**** In the above example, we performed some cancellations after multiplying. However, with more complex expressions, it's often easier to cancel ** multiplying.

### Example: Multiplying Rational Expressions by Canceling Common Terms

#### Question

Simplify $\dfrac {(n + 1)(n + 3)} {4n^2} \cdot \dfrac {8n}{(n + 3)(n - 2)}.$

#### Explanation

First, we cancel any terms that appear in both a numerator and a denominator. Then, we multiply the numerators and the denominators as follows:

$$


\begin{aligned}\frac{(𝑛+1)(𝑛+3)}{4𝑛^{2}}⋅\frac{8𝑛}{(𝑛+3)(𝑛−2)} & =\frac{(𝑛+1)(𝑛+3)}{4𝑛⋅𝑛}⋅\frac{2⋅4𝑛}{(𝑛+3)(𝑛−2)} \\ & =\frac{𝑛+1}{𝑛}⋅\frac{2}{𝑛−2} \\ & =\frac{2𝑛+2}{𝑛^{2}−2𝑛}\end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Multiplying Rational Expressions by Factoring and Canceling Common Terms

#### Question

Simplify $\dfrac {3p - 12}{p^2 - 9} \cdot \dfrac {p + 3} {p - 4}.$

#### Explanation

First, we factor all of the expressions and cancel any terms that appear in both a numerator and a denominator. Then, we multiply the numerators and the denominators as follows:

$$


\begin{aligned} \dfrac {3p - 12}{p^2 - 9} \cdot \dfrac {p + 3} {p - 4} &=\dfrac {3(p - 4)} {(p + 3)(p - 3)} \cdot \dfrac {p + 3} {p - 4} \\\[5pt] &=\dfrac {3(p - 4)} {(p + 3)(p - 3)} \cdot \dfrac {p + 3} {p - 4} \\\[5pt] &=\dfrac {3}{p - 3} \end{aligned}


$$
