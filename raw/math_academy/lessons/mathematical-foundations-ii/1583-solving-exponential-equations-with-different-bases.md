### Introduction

Suppose we are given the equation $3^{3x} = 81^{x-1},$ which has two exponential terms. How do we solve it?

To solve this equation, we need both exponential terms to have a common base. To do this, we rewrite the equation using the fact that $81 = {\color{red}3}^4,$ as follows:

$$


{\color{red}3}^{3x}=\left({\color{red}3}^4\right)^{x-1}


$$

Then, we apply the power rule for exponents:

$$


\begin{aligned}3^{3𝑥} & =3^{4(𝑥−1)}\end{aligned}


$$

Now, since both sides of the equation share the same base $({\color{red}3}),$ we equate the exponents and solve for $x\mathbin{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}3𝑥 & =4(𝑥−1) \\ 3𝑥 & =4𝑥−4 \\ 𝑥 & =4\end{aligned}


$$

Whenever we can rewrite an equation so that both sides have a common base, we can solve the equation by equating the exponents.

### Example: Solving Exponential Equations Containing Different Bases by Rewriting One Exponent

#### Question

Solve for $x$ where $3^{3x-3}=9^x.$

#### Explanation

To solve this equation, we need both exponential terms to have a common base.

To do this, we write $9$ as ${\color{red}3}^2,$ and then rewrite the right-hand side using the power rule for exponents:

$$


\begin{aligned}3^{3𝑥−3} & =9^{𝑥} \\ 3^{3𝑥−3} & =(3^{2})^{𝑥} \\ 3^{3𝑥−3} & =3^{2𝑥}\end{aligned}


$$

Now, since both sides of the equation share the same base $({\color{red}3}),$ we equate the exponents and solve for $x\mathbin{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}3𝑥−3 & =2𝑥 \\ 𝑥 & =3\end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Solving Exponential Equations Containing Different Bases by Rewriting Two Exponents

#### Question

Solve for $x$ where $9^{3x}=27^{x+2}.$

#### Explanation

To solve this equation, we need both exponential terms to have a common base. To do this, we rewrite the equation using $9={\color{red}3}^2$ and $27 = {\color{red}3}^3,$ as follows:

$$


\left({\color{red}3}^2\right)^{3x}=\left({\color{red}3}^3\right)^{x+2}


$$

Then, we apply the power rule for exponents:

$$


\begin{aligned}3^{2(3𝑥)}=3^{3(𝑥+2)}\end{aligned}


$$

Now, since both sides of the equation share the same base $({\color{red}3}),$ we equate the exponents and solve for $x\mathbin{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}2(3𝑥) & =3(𝑥+2) \\ 6𝑥 & =3𝑥+6 \\ 3𝑥 & =6 \\ 𝑥 & =2\end{aligned}


$$
