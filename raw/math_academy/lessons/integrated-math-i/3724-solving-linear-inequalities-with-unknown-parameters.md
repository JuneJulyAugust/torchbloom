## Lesson

### Introduction

Suppose we wish to solve the following inequality for the variable $x\mathbin{:}$

$$



5x + 4 < 8k



$$

We solve this inequality using the addition and multiplication principles, treating $k$ like any other number.

First, we apply the addition principle, subtracting $4$ from both sides of the inequality:

$$



\begin{aligned}5𝑥+4 & <8𝑘 \\ 5𝑥+4−4 & <8𝑘−4 \\ 5𝑥 & <8𝑘−4\end{aligned}



$$

Then, we apply the multiplication principle, dividing both sides of the inequality by $5{:}$

$$



\begin{aligned}5𝑥 & <8𝑘−4 \\ \frac{5𝑥}{5} & <\frac{8𝑘−4}{5} \\ 𝑥 & <\frac{8𝑘−4}{5}\end{aligned}



$$

Therefore, the solution to our inequality is $x < \dfrac{8k-4}{5}.$

### Example: Solving Linear Inequalities

#### Question

Solve the inequality $\dfrac{3x-2k}{5} \ge -1$ for the variable $x.$

#### Explanation

We solve the inequality using the addition and multiplication principles, treating $k$ like any other number.

First, we multiply both sides of the inequality by $5$ to eliminate the fraction:

$$



\begin{aligned}\frac{3𝑥−2𝑘}{5} & ≥−1 \\ 5⋅(\frac{3𝑥−2𝑘}{5}) & ≥5⋅(−1) \\ 3𝑥−2𝑘 & ≥−5\end{aligned}



$$

Now, we apply the addition principle, adding $2k$ to both sides of the inequality:

$$



\begin{aligned}3𝑥−2𝑘 & ≥−5 \\ 3𝑥−2𝑘+2𝑘 & ≥−5+2𝑘 \\ 3𝑥 & ≥−5+2𝑘 \\ 3𝑥 & ≥2𝑘−5\end{aligned}



$$

Finally, we apply the multiplication principle, dividing both sides of the inequality by $3{:}$

$$



\begin{aligned}3𝑥 & ≥2𝑘−5 \\ \frac{3𝑥}{3} & ≥\frac{2𝑘−5}{3} \\ 𝑥 & ≥\frac{2𝑘−5}{3}\end{aligned}



$$

### Example: Solving Inequalities Where Flipping is Required

#### Question

Solve the inequality $1-3x \leq 13-6k$ for the variable $x.$

#### Explanation

We solve the inequality using the addition and multiplication principles, treating $k$ like any other number.

First, we apply the addition principle, subtracting $1$ from both sides of the inequality:

$$



\begin{aligned}1−3𝑥 & ≤13−6𝑘 \\ 1−3𝑥−1 & ≤13−6𝑘−1 \\ −3𝑥 & ≤12−6𝑘\end{aligned}



$$

Then, we apply the multiplication principle, dividing both sides of the inequality by $-3.$ Note that since we are dividing by a negative number, we need to flip the inequality.

$$



\begin{aligned}−3𝑥 & ≤12−6𝑘 \\ \frac{−3𝑥}{(−3)} & ≥\frac{12−6𝑘}{(−3)} \\ 𝑥 & ≥\frac{12−6𝑘}{(−3)}\end{aligned}



$$

Finally, we further simplify the right-hand side, as follows:

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑥 & ≥\frac{12−6𝑘}{(−3)} \\ 𝑥 & ≥\frac{3(4−2𝑘)}{(−3)} \\ 𝑥 & ≥\frac{3(4−2𝑘)}{(−3)} \\ 𝑥 & ≥\frac{4−2𝑘}{(−1)} \\ 𝑥 & ≥2𝑘−4\end{aligned}



$$

### Example: Solving Inequalities Containing Unknown Coefficients

#### Question

Given that $k< 0,$ solve the inequality $\dfrac{kx +3}3 \geq 2k$ for the variable $x.$

#### Explanation

We solve the inequality using the addition and multiplication principles, treating $k$ like any other number.

First, we multiply both sides of the inequality by $3$ to eliminate the fraction:

$$



\begin{aligned}\frac{𝑘𝑥+3}{3} & ≥2𝑘 \\ 3⋅(\frac{𝑘𝑥+3}{3}) & ≥3⋅2𝑘 \\ 𝑘𝑥+3 & ≥6𝑘\end{aligned}



$$

Now, we apply the addition principle, subtracting $3$ from both sides of the inequality:

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑘𝑥+3 & ≥6𝑘 \\ 𝑘𝑥 & ≥6𝑘−3\end{aligned}



$$

Then, we divide by $k.$ Since $k < 0,$ we need to flip the inequality:

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑘𝑥 & ≥6𝑘−3 \\ \frac{𝑘𝑥}{𝑘} & ≤\frac{6𝑘−3}{𝑘} \\ 𝑥 & ≤\frac{6𝑘−3}{𝑘}\end{aligned}



$$
