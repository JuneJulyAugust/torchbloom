## Lesson

### Introduction

The **power of quotient rule** states that if we want to raise the quotient $\dfrac{x}{y}$ to some power $n,$ we must raise the numerator and denominator to the power $n$ as follows:

$$



\left(\dfrac{x}{y}\right)^n=\dfrac{x^n}{y^n}



$$

For example,

$$



\left(\dfrac{x}{y}\right)^4=\dfrac{x^4}{y^4}.



$$

To see why the power of quotient rule is true, let's take the above example and work it out again the long way. We get the same result:

$$



\begin{aligned}(\frac{𝑥}{𝑦})^{4} & = \\ \frac{𝑥}{𝑦}⋅\frac{𝑥}{𝑦}⋅\frac{𝑥}{𝑦}⋅\frac{𝑥}{𝑦} & = \\ \frac{𝑥⋅𝑥⋅𝑥⋅𝑥}{𝑦⋅𝑦⋅𝑦⋅𝑦} & = \\ \frac{𝑥^{4}}{𝑦^{4}} & \end{aligned}



$$

The power of quotient rule is just a shortcut for the process above.

### Example: Using the Power of Quotient Rule with One Fraction

#### Question

Calculate $\left(\dfrac{2x}{3}\right)^4.$

#### Explanation

First, we apply the power of quotient rule:

$$



\begin{aligned}(\frac{2𝑥}{3})^{4}=\frac{(2𝑥)^{4}}{3^{4}}\end{aligned}



$$

Then, we apply the power rule and simplify:

$$



\begin{aligned}\frac{(2𝑥)^{4}}{3^{4}} & = \\ \frac{2^{4}𝑥^{4}}{3^{4}} & = \\ \frac{16𝑥^{4}}{81} & \end{aligned}



$$

### Example: Using the Power of Quotient Rule with Two Fractions

#### Question

Simplify $\left(\dfrac{y}{2}\right)^3 \left(\dfrac{2y}{3}\right)^2.$

#### Explanation

First, we apply the power of quotient rule:

$$



\begin{aligned}(\frac{𝑦}{2})^{3}(\frac{2𝑦}{3})^{2}=\frac{𝑦^{3}}{2^{3}}⋅\frac{(2𝑦)^{2}}{3^{2}}\end{aligned}



$$

Next, we apply the power rule:

$$



\begin{aligned}\frac{𝑦^{3}}{2^{3}}⋅\frac{(2𝑦)^{2}}{3^{2}} & = \\ \frac{𝑦^{3}}{8}⋅\frac{2^{2}𝑦^{2}}{9} & = \\ \frac{𝑦^{3}}{8}⋅\frac{4𝑦^{2}}{9} & \end{aligned}



$$

Finally, we apply the product rule and simplify:

$$



\begin{aligned}\frac{𝑦^{3}}{8}⋅\frac{4𝑦^{2}}{9} & = \\ \frac{4𝑦^{(3+2)}}{72} & = \\ \frac{4𝑦^{5}}{72} & = \\ \frac{𝑦^{5}}{18} & \end{aligned}



$$
