## Lesson

### Introduction

How do we solve an equation with a variable raised to an even exponent, such as

$$



x^6 = 64?



$$

We can solve this equation by taking the $6$th root of each side and solving for $x.$

Since the exponent $(6)$ is even, there will be two real solutions (positive and negative):

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑥^{6} & =64 \\ \sqrt[√𝑥^{6}]{6} & =±\sqrt[√64]{6} \\ 𝑥 & =±\sqrt[√2^{6}]{6} \\ 𝑥 & =±2\end{aligned}



$$

Therefore, the solutions are $x=\pm 2.$

In general, we have the following rule of thumb:

*When $n$ is even, and we take the $n$th root of both sides of $x^n = a$ with $a \geq 0,$ we get two solutions (positive and negative):*

$$



x = \pm\sqrt[n]{a}



$$

**Watch out!** If $a<0$ and $n$ is even, equation $x^n = a$ has no solution since, in this case, $x^n \geq 0$ for any real $x.$

### Example: Solving Equations With Even Exponents

#### Question

Find all of the real solutions to the equation $9x^4 = \dfrac{1}{9}.$

#### Explanation

We can solve this equation by isolating the $x^4$ term and then taking the $4$th root of each side.

First, let's isolate the $x^4$ term on the left-hand side:

$$



\begin{aligned}9𝑥^{4} & =\frac{1}{9} \\ 𝑥^{4} & =\frac{1}{81}\end{aligned}



$$

Now, we take the $4$th root of each side and solve for $x.$ Since the exponent $(4)$ is even, we consider both the positive and negative solutions:

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑥^{4} & =\frac{1}{81} \\ \sqrt[√𝑥^{4}]{4} & =±\sqrt[√\frac{1}{81}]{4} \\ 𝑥 & =±\sqrt[√(\frac{1}{3})^{4}]{4} \\ 𝑥 & =±\frac{1}{3}\end{aligned}



$$

Therefore, the solutions are $x=\pm \dfrac13.$

### Example: Solving Equations Containing an Expression Raised to an Even Power

#### Question

Find all of the real solutions to the equation $(x-3)^4=-\dfrac{1}{81}.$

#### Explanation

Notice that $(x-3)^4 \geq 0$ for all values of $x$ because the exponent ($4$) is even. This expression can never be negative.

Therefore, there are no real solutions to this equation.
