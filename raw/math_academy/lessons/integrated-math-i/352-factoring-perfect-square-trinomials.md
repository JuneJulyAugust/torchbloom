### Introduction

A **perfect square trinomial** is a three-term expression that is the result of squaring a binomial.

For example, the expression

$$



x^2 + 6x + 9



$$

is a perfect square trinomial because it is the result of squaring the binomial $(x+3)$, as shown below:

$$



\begin{aligned}(𝑥+3)^{2} & = \\ 𝑥^{2}+2⋅3𝑥+3^{2} & = \\ 𝑥^{2}+6𝑥+9 & \end{aligned}



$$

To factor a perfect square trinomial, we have to figure out what binomial it is the square of. We can often do this by relating the perfect square trinomial to the **square of a sum**:

$$



x^2 + {\color{red}2} {\color{blue}a}x + {\color{blue}a}^2 = (x + {\color{blue}a})^2



$$

To illustrate, let's factor the expression $x^2+6x+9$ using the square of sum formula. We perform the following steps:

1. Write the constant term as a perfect square:

2. Factor a $2$ out of the linear term:

3. Factor the trinomial using the "square of a sum formula". In this case, we have ${\color{blue}{a}}={\color{blue}{3}}\mathbin{:}$

This method works *only* when the trinomial is a perfect square! It will *not* work for every trinomial. We will learn more general methods in the future.

### Example: Factoring a Trinomial Generated by the Square of a Sum

#### Question

Factor $x^2 + 12x + 36.$

#### Explanation

The "square of a sum" formula states that

$$



x^2 + {\color{red}{2}}{\color{blue}{a}} x + a^2 = (x+{\color{blue}{a}})^2 .



$$

We will use this formula to factor the given trinomial. Our trinomial is

$$



x^2 + 12x + 36.



$$

First, we write the constant term as a perfect square:

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑥^{2}+12𝑥+6^{2} & \end{aligned}



$$

Then, we factor a $2$ out of the linear term:

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑥^{2}+2⋅6𝑥+6^{2}\end{aligned}



$$

Finally, we factor the trinomial using the square of sum formula with ${\color{blue}{a}} = {\color{blue}{6}}.$ This gives

$$



\begin{aligned}(𝑥+6)^{2}.\end{aligned}



$$

### Example: Factoring a Trinomial Generated by the Square of a Difference

#### Question

Factor $p^2 - 10p + 25.$

#### Explanation

The "square of a difference" formula states that

$$



p^2 - {\color{red}{2}}{\color{blue}{a}} p + a^2 = (p-{\color{blue}{a}})^2 .



$$

We will use this formula to factor the given trinomial. Our trinomial is

$$



p^2 - 10p + 25.



$$

First, we write the constant term as a perfect square:

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑝^{2}−10𝑝+5^{2}\end{aligned}



$$

Then, we factor a $2$ out of the linear term:

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑝^{2}−2⋅5𝑝+5^{2}\end{aligned}



$$

Finally, we factor the trinomial using the square of difference formula with ${\color{blue}{a}} = {\color{blue}{5}}.$ This gives

$$



\begin{aligned}(𝑝−5)^{2}.\end{aligned}



$$

### Example: Factoring a Trinomial with Fractions

#### Question

Factor the expression $y^2 + \dfrac{2}{5}y + \dfrac{1}{25}.$

#### Explanation

The "square of a sum" formula states that

$$



x^2 + {\color{red}{2}}{\color{blue}{a}} x + a^2 = (x+{\color{blue}{a}})^2 .



$$

We will use this formula to factor the given trinomial. Our trinomial is

$$



y^2 + \dfrac{2}{5}y + \dfrac{1}{25}.



$$

First, we write the constant term as a perfect square:

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑦^{2}+\frac{2}{5}𝑦+(\frac{1}{5})^{2} & \end{aligned}



$$

Then, we factor a $2$ out of the linear term:

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑦^{2}+2⋅\frac{1}{5}𝑦+(\frac{1}{5})^{2}\end{aligned}



$$

Finally, we factor the trinomial using the square of sum formula with ${\color{blue}{a}} = {\color{blue}{\dfrac 1 5}}.$ This gives

$$



\begin{aligned}(𝑦+\frac{1}{5})^{2}.\end{aligned}



$$
