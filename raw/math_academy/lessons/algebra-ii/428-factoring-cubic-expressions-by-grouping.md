## Lesson

### Introduction

We have learned how to factor quadratics. However, we can also factor higher-degree expressions, such as cubics. When factoring a cubic, the goal is exactly the same: we want to transform it into a product of two or three binomials.

To factor a cubic, we can often **factor by grouping**. To demonstrate, let's use this technique to factor the following cubic:

$$


3x^3-6x^2+2x-4


$$

**Step 1:** Group the two larger terms and the two smaller terms.

$$


(3x^3-6x^2)+(2x-4)


$$

**Step 2:** Factor the two larger terms and the two smaller terms separately, so that the leftover binomials inside the parentheses match. Here, we factor out $3x^2$ from the two larger terms and $2$ from the two smaller terms, and the leftover expressions $(x-2)$ match.

$$


3x^2(x -2)+2(x-2)


$$

**Step 3:** Rearrange the expression so that the binomial factors in parentheses appear at the front of each of the two terms.

$$


(x - 2)3x^2+(x-2)2


$$

**Step 4:** Factor out the binomial in parentheses from the entire expression.

$$


(x - 2)(3x^2+2)


$$

And that's it! The cubic $3x^3-6x^2+2x-4$ factors into $(x - 2)(3x^2+2).$

**Note:** Just like with quadratics, not all cubics can be factored. However, of the cubics that can be factored, many can be factored by grouping.

### Example: Factoring a Cubic Expression by Grouping

#### Question

Factor $x^3 + 3x^2 + 4x + 12.$

#### Explanation

First, we group the two larger terms and the two smaller terms:

$$


(x^3 + 3x^2) + (4x + 12)


$$

Next, we factor the two larger terms and the two smaller terms separately, so that the leftover binomials inside the parentheses match:

$$


x^2(x + 3) + 4(x + 3)


$$

Then, we rearrange the expression so that the binomial factors in parentheses appear at the front of each of the two terms:

$$


(x + 3)x^2 + (x + 3)4


$$

Finally, we factor out the binomial in parentheses from the entire expression:

$$


(x + 3)(x^2 + 4)


$$

### Example: Factoring a Cubic Expression With Leading Coefficients by Grouping

#### Question

Factor $15n^3 + 5n^2 + 3n + 1.$

#### Explanation

First, we group the two larger terms and the two smaller terms:

$$


(15n^3 + 5n^2) + (3n + 1)


$$

Next, we factor the two larger terms and the two smaller terms separately, so that the leftover binomials inside the parentheses match:

$$


5n^2(3n + 1) + (3n + 1)


$$

Then, we rearrange the expression so that the binomial factors in parentheses appear at the front of each of the two terms:

$$


(3n + 1)5n^2 + (3n + 1)


$$

Finally, we factor out the binomial in parentheses from the entire expression:

$$


(3n + 1)(5n^2 + 1)


$$

### Example: Factoring by Grouping when the Leading Coefficient is One: Factoring Out a Negative

#### Question

Factor $a^3 + 4a^2 - 3a - 12.$

#### Explanation

First, we group the two larger terms and the two smaller terms:

$$


(a^3 + 4a^2) - (3a + 12)


$$

Notice that when grouping the smaller terms, the negative sign was factored out.

Next, we factor the two larger terms and the two smaller terms separately, so that the leftover binomials inside the parentheses match:

$$


a^2(a + 4) - 3(a + 4)


$$

Then, we rearrange the expression so that the binomial factors in parentheses appear at the front of each of the two terms:

$$


(a + 4)a^2 - (a + 4)3


$$

Finally, we factor out the binomial in parentheses from the entire expression:

$$


(a + 4)(a^2 - 3)


$$

### Example: Factoring by Grouping when the Leading Coefficient is Not One: Factoring Out a Negative

#### Question

Factor $2p^3 +5p^2 -8p - 20.$

#### Explanation

First, we group the two larger terms and the two smaller terms:

$$


(2p^3 +5p^2) - (8p +20)


$$

Next, we factor the two larger terms and the two smaller terms separately, so that the leftover binomials inside the parentheses match:

$$


p^2(2p +5) -4(2p +5)


$$

Then, we rearrange the expression so that the binomial factors in parentheses appear at the front of each of the two terms:

$$


(2p+5)p^2-(2p+5)4


$$

Finally, we factor out the binomial in parentheses from the entire expression:

$$


(2p + 5)(p^2-4)


$$

However, we need to make sure that we factor **. Here, the factor $p^2-4$ is a difference of squares and can itself be factored into $(p - 2)(p + 2).$ So, our final result is

$$


(2p + 5)(p-2)(p+2).


$$
