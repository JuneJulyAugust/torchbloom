## Lesson

### Introduction

Remember that in order to add fractions with different denominators, like $\dfrac 1 4 + \dfrac 1 8,$ we need to put the fractions over a **common denominator**, as follows:

$$



\begin{aligned}\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{8} & = \\ \frac{2}{8}+\frac{1}{8} & = \\ \frac{3}{8} & \end{aligned}



$$

It works the same in algebra! To add two fractions, we need to put each fraction over a common denominator. Then, we add the numerators.

### Example: Adding and Subtracting a Number and a Rational Expression

#### Question

Write $2 + \dfrac{2}{k}$ as a single fraction.

#### Explanation

Using the fact that $2=\dfrac 2 1,$ we can write the expression as

$$



\dfrac 2 1 + \dfrac{2}{k}.



$$

In order to add the two fractions, we need them to have a common denominator. So, we put both fractions over a common denominator of $k$ by multiplying the top and bottom of the first fraction by $k\mathbin{:}$

$$



\begin{aligned}\frac{2}{1}+\frac{2}{𝑘} & = \\ \frac{𝑘}{𝑘}⋅\frac{2}{1}+\frac{2}{𝑘} & = \\ \frac{2𝑘}{𝑘}+\frac{2}{𝑘} & \end{aligned}



$$

Finally, we add the fractions and simplify the result:

$$



\begin{aligned}\frac{2𝑘}{𝑘}+\frac{2}{𝑘} & = \\ \frac{2𝑘+2}{𝑘} & = \\ \frac{2(𝑘+1)}{𝑘} & \end{aligned}



$$

### Example: Adding and Subtracting Rational Expressions With No Common Factors in the Denominators

#### Question

Write $\dfrac{1}{q} + \dfrac{1}{p+1}$ as a single fraction.

#### Explanation

In this case, we need to put the fractions over a common denominator of $(p+1)q,$ since there are no common factors in either denominator that we can exploit.

To do this, we multiply the top and bottom of the first fraction by $p+1,$ and the top and bottom of the second fraction by $q\mathbin{:}$

$$



\begin{aligned}\frac{1}{𝑞}+\frac{1}{𝑝+1} & = \\ \frac{𝑝+1}{𝑝+1}⋅\frac{1}{𝑞}+\frac{𝑞}{𝑞}⋅\frac{1}{𝑝+1} & = \\ \frac{𝑝+1}{(𝑝+1)𝑞}+\frac{𝑞}{(𝑝+1)𝑞} & = \\ \frac{𝑝+𝑞+1}{(𝑝+1)𝑞} & \end{aligned}



$$
