## Lesson

### Introduction

When we take a power of a trigonometric function, we write the power after the function but before the argument.

For instance $\sin^2 30^\circ$ is the same as $(\sin30^\circ)^2.$ Therefore,

$$



\begin{aligned}sin^{2}⁡30^{∘} & =(sin⁡30^{∘})^{2} \\ & =(\frac{1}{2})^{2} \\ & =\frac{1}{4}.\end{aligned}



$$

Similarly, $\tan^4 \left(\dfrac\pi 3\right)$ is the same as $\left(\tan \left(\dfrac\pi 3\right)\right)^4.$ Therefore,

$$



\begin{aligned}tan^{4}⁡(\frac{𝜋}{3}) & =(tan⁡(\frac{𝜋}{3}))^{4} \\ & =(\sqrt{√3})^{4} \\ & =(\sqrt{√3})^{2}⋅(\sqrt{√3})^{2} \\ & =3⋅3 \\ & =9.\end{aligned}



$$

### Example: Evaluating an Expression Involving Special Angles in Degrees

#### Question

Evaluate $\sin^2\left(150^\circ\right) + 2\csc^2\left(240^\circ\right).$

#### Explanation

Let's remind ourselves of the special trigonometric ratios, as given by the unit circle.

![Instructional graphic](../../lesson-assets/algebra-ii/topic-3766/940341c5c0ce3451.png)

- Let's first consider the angle $150^\circ.$ From the unit circle, we see that the angle $150^\circ$ corresponds to the point Since the sine of the angle corresponds to the $y$-coordinate, we have

- Now, let's consider the angle $240^\circ.$ From the unit circle, we see that the angle $240^\circ$ corresponds to the point Since the sine of the angle corresponds to the $y$-coordinate, we have

Using the fact that $\csc{\theta} = \dfrac{1}{\sin\theta},$ we have

$$



\begin{aligned}csc⁡240^{∘} & =\frac{1}{sin⁡240^{∘}} \\ & =\frac{1}{(−\frac{\sqrt{√3}}{2})} \\ & =−\frac{2}{\sqrt{√3}}.\end{aligned}



$$

Finally, we combine the results:

$$



\begin{aligned}sin^{2}⁡(150^{∘})+2csc^{2}⁡(240^{∘}) & =(\frac{1}{2})^{2}+2(−\frac{2}{\sqrt{√3}})^{2} \\ & =\frac{1}{4}+2(\frac{4}{3}) \\ & =\frac{1}{4}+\frac{8}{3} \\ & =\frac{35}{12}\end{aligned}



$$

### Example: Evaluating an Expression Involving Special Angles in Radians

#### Question

Evaluate $4\cos^2 \left(\dfrac \pi 6\right) - \sin \left(\dfrac \pi 4\right).$

#### Explanation

Let's remind ourselves of the special trigonometric ratios, as given by the unit circle.

![Instructional graphic](../../lesson-assets/algebra-ii/topic-3766/2849e6fb50979f66.png)

- Let's first consider the angle $\dfrac{\pi}{6}.$ From the unit circle, we see that the angle $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ corresponds to the point Since the cosine of the angle corresponds to the $x$-coordinate, we have

- Now, let's consider the angle $\dfrac \pi 4.$ From the unit circle, we see that the angle $\dfrac \pi 4$ corresponds to the point Since the sine of the angle corresponds to the $y$-coordinate, we have

Finally, we combine the results:

$$



\begin{aligned}4cos^{2}⁡(\frac{𝜋}{6})−sin⁡(\frac{𝜋}{4}) & =4(\frac{\sqrt{√3}}{2})^{2}−\frac{\sqrt{√2}}{2} \\ & =3−\frac{\sqrt{√2}}{2}\end{aligned}



$$
