## Lesson

### Introduction

When finding the roots of a transformed radical function, we need to consider whether the radical's index is odd or even.

Let's start with the odd case first. As an example, we will find the root of the function $f(x),$ given by

$$


f(x) = \sqrt[{\color{blue}{3}}]{10-4x} + 2\,.


$$

Notice that we're dealing with a cube root, and so the index of the radical is ${\color{blue}{3}},$ which is odd.

To find the root of $f(x),$ we must solve $f(x)=0\mathbin{:}$

$$


\sqrt[3]{10 - 4x} + 2 = 0


$$

We solve this equation by making $x$ the subject, as follows:

$$


\begin{aligned}\sqrt[√10−4𝑥]{3}+2 & =0 \\ \sqrt[√10−4𝑥]{3} & =−2 \\ (\sqrt[√10−4𝑥]{3})^{3} & =(−2)^{3} \\ 10−4𝑥 & =−8 \\ −4𝑥 & =−18 \\ 𝑥 & =\frac{9}{2}.\end{aligned}


$$

Therefore, the root of $f(x)$ is $x=\dfrac92.$

A transformed radical function with an odd index will always have one real root.

### Example: Finding the Roots of a Transformed Radical Function With an Odd Index

#### Question

Find the root of $f(x) = \sqrt[3]{4x-5} - 3.$

#### Explanation

To find the roots of $f(x),$ we must solve $f(x)=0\mathbin{:}$

$$


\sqrt[3]{4x-5} - 3 = 0


$$

We solve this equation by making $x$ the subject, as follows:

$$


\begin{aligned}\sqrt[√4𝑥−5]{3}−3 & =0 \\ \sqrt[√4𝑥−5]{3} & =3 \\ (\sqrt[√4𝑥−5]{3})^{3} & =3^{3} \\ 4𝑥−5 & =27 \\ 4𝑥 & =32 \\ 𝑥 & =8\end{aligned}


$$

Therefore, the root of $f(x)$ is $x=8.$

### Finding the Roots of a Transformed Radical Function: Even Case

Let's now learn how to find the root of a radical function with an even index. For example, let's try to find the root of

$$


f(x) = -3\sqrt[4]{10x+1} + 9.


$$

To find the root of $f(x),$ we must solve $f(x)=0\mathbin{:}$

$$


-3\sqrt[4]{10x+1} + 9 = 0


$$

We solve this equation by making $x$ the subject, as follows:

$$


\begin{aligned}−3\sqrt[√10𝑥+1]{4}+9 & =0 \\ −3\sqrt[√10𝑥+1]{4} & =−9 \\ \sqrt[√10𝑥+1]{4} & =3 \\ (\sqrt[√10𝑥+1]{4})^{4} & =3^{4} \\ 10𝑥+1 & =81 \\ 10𝑥 & =80 \\ 𝑥 & =8\end{aligned}


$$

Finally, we must check the answer by substituting it back into the initial equation:

$$


\begin{aligned}−3\sqrt[√10⋅8+1]{4}+9 & =0 \\ −3\sqrt[√81]{4}+9 & =0 \\ −3⋅3+9 & =0 \\ 0 & =0\,✓\end{aligned}


$$

Therefore, the root of $f(x)$ is $x=8.$

When the index of the radical function is even, we should always check for extraneous roots by substituting them back into the original equation.

Sometimes, a radical function with an even index has no root. Let's see an example.

### Example: Finding the Roots of a Transformed Radical Function With an Even Index

#### Question

Find the root of $f(x) = 3\sqrt[4]{15- 8x} + 3.$

#### Explanation

To find the root of $f(x),$ we must solve $f(x)=0\mathbin{:}$

$$


3\sqrt[4]{15- 8x} + 3 = 0


$$

We solve this equation by making $x$ the subject, as follows:

$$


\begin{aligned}3\sqrt[√15−8𝑥]{4}+3 & =0 \\ 3\sqrt[√15−8𝑥]{4} & =−3 \\ \sqrt[√15−8𝑥]{4} & =−1\end{aligned}


$$

This equation does not have any solutions since its left-hand side is always nonnegative, while the right-hand side is negative.

Therefore, $f(x)$ has no roots.
