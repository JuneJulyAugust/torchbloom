## Lesson

### Introduction

Suppose that we want to factor the following trinomial:

$$



t^4+4t^2+3.



$$

This trinomial is a quartic, but it can also be viewed as a quadratic "in disguise." To see this, notice that if we introduce a new variable $z=t^2,$ then we can rewrite the trinomial as a quadratic in terms of $z\mathbin{:}$

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑡^{4}+4𝑡^{2}+3 & = \\ (𝑡^{2})^{2}+4(𝑡^{2})+3 & = \\ 𝑧^{2}+4𝑧+3 & \end{aligned}



$$

Now, we can factor the quadratic, and then rewrite the final expression in terms of $t\mathbin{:}$

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑧^{2}+4𝑧+3 & = \\ (𝑧+1)(𝑧+3) & = \\ (𝑡^{2}+1)(𝑡^{2}+3) & \end{aligned}



$$

Therefore, $t^4+4t^2+3$ factors into $(t^2+1)(t^2+3).$ We can double-check this result by multiplying it out:

$$



\begin{aligned}(𝑡^{2}+1)(𝑡^{2}+3) & = \\ 𝑡^{2}(𝑡^{2}+3)+1(𝑡^{2}+3) & = \\ 𝑡^{4}+3𝑡^{2}+𝑡^{2}+3 & = \\ 𝑡^{4}+4𝑡^{2}+3 & \,✓\end{aligned}



$$

### Example: Factoring Biquadratic Expressions as Perfect Squares

#### Question

Factor $x^4-4x^2+4.$

#### Explanation

We can introduce a new variable $z=x^2$ and then rewrite the trinomial as a quadratic in terms of $z\mathbin{:}$

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑥^{4}−4𝑥^{2}+4 & = \\ (𝑥^{2})^{2}−4(𝑥^{2})+4 & = \\ 𝑧^{2}−4𝑧+4 & \end{aligned}



$$

Now, we can factor the quadratic as the square of a difference, and then rewrite the final expression in terms of $x\mathbin{:}$

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑧^{2}−4𝑧+4 & = \\ (𝑧−2)^{2} & = \\ (𝑥^{2}−2)^{2} & \end{aligned}



$$

### Example: Factoring Biquadratic Expressions as Quadratic Trinomials

#### Question

Factor $t^4 - t^2 - 2.$

#### Explanation

We can introduce a new variable $z = t^2$ and then rewrite the trinomial as a quadratic in terms of $z\mathbin{:}$

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑡^{4}−𝑡^{2}−2 & = \\ (𝑡^{2})^{2}−𝑡^{2}−2 & = \\ 𝑧^{2}−𝑧−2 & \end{aligned}



$$

Now, we can factor the quadratic, and then rewrite the final expression in terms of $t\mathbin{:}$

$$



\begin{aligned}𝑧^{2}−𝑧−2 & = \\ (𝑧+1)(𝑧−2) & = \\ (𝑡^{2}+1)(𝑡^{2}−2) & \end{aligned}



$$

### Example: Factoring Biquadratic Expressions as Quadratic Trinomials With Leading Coefficients

#### Question

Factor $2p^4 + 5p^2 + 3.$

#### Explanation

We can introduce a new variable $t = p^2$ and then rewrite the trinomial as a quadratic in terms of $t\mathbin{:}$

$$



\begin{aligned}2𝑝^{4}+5𝑝^{2}+3 & = \\ 2(𝑝^{2})^{2}+5𝑝^{2}+3 & = \\ 2𝑡^{2}+5𝑡+3 & \end{aligned}



$$

Now, we can factor the quadratic, and then rewrite the final expression in terms of $p\mathbin{:}$

$$



\begin{aligned}2𝑡^{2}+5𝑡+3 & = \\ (𝑡+1)(2𝑡+3) & = \\ (𝑝^{2}+1)(2𝑝^{2}+3) & \end{aligned}



$$
