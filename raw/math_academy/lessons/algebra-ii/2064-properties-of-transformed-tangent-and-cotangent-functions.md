## Lesson

### Introduction

Given the equation of a general transformed tangent function

$$


y={\color{red}A}\tan\left( {\color{blue}B} x + C\right) +{\color{green}D},


$$

some properties of the graph of the function are as follows:

- The period is $\dfrac{\pi}{\color{blue}B}.$

- The vertical shift is ${\color{green}D}.$

We obtain the horizontal shift by factoring the argument of the function, as follows:

$$


y={\color{red}A}\tan\left( {\color{blue}B}\left[ x + \dfrac{C}{{\color{blue}B}} \right]\right) +{\color{green}D}


$$

From here, we can see that the phase (horizontal shift) is $-\dfrac{C}{{\color{blue}B}}.$

We can apply the same ideas to work out the properties of a transformed cotangent function of the form

$$


y={\color{red}A}\cot\left( {\color{blue}B} x + C\right) +{\color{green}D}.


$$

### Example: Determining the Period of a Transformed Tangent or Cotangent Function

#### Question

Calculate the period of the function $y = \cot\left(2x-\dfrac\pi 6\right).$

#### Explanation

Given a function of the form $y = A\cot(Bx +C) + D,$ the period is $\dfrac{\pi}{B}.$

In our case, we have that $B=2.$ Therefore, the period is $\dfrac{\pi}{2}.$

### Example: Finding a General Expression for the Zeros of a Transformed Tangent or Cotangent Function

#### Question

Which expression gives all of the zeros of the function $f(x) = \cot \left(2x + \dfrac \pi 6\right)?$

#### Explanation

The zeros of $y=\cot x$ are given by

$$


x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + n\pi,


$$

where $n$ is an integer.

To find the zeros of $f(x) = \cot \left(2x + \dfrac \pi 6\right),$ we replace the $x$ on the left-hand side of the above with $\left(2x + \dfrac \pi 6\right).$ This gives

$$


2x + \dfrac \pi 6 = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + n\pi.


$$

Solving for $x,$ we get

$$


\begin{aligned}2𝑥+\frac{𝜋}{6} & =\frac{𝜋}{2}+𝑛𝜋 \\ 2𝑥 & =\frac{𝜋}{2}+𝑛𝜋−\frac{𝜋}{6} \\ 2𝑥 & =\frac{𝜋}{3}+𝑛𝜋 \\ 𝑥 & =\frac{𝜋}{6}+\frac{𝑛𝜋}{2}.\end{aligned}


$$

Therefore, we conclude that the zeros are $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6} + \dfrac{n\pi}{2},$ where $n$ is an integer.

### Example: Finding a General Expression for the Asymptotes of a Transformed Tangent or Cotangent Function

#### Question

What expression gives all of the vertical asymptotes of the function $f(x)=\tan\left(2x\right)?$

#### Explanation

The asymptotes of $y=\tan x$ are given by the expression

$$


x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + n \pi,


$$

where $n$ is an integer.

To find the asymptotes of $\tan\left(2x\right),$ we replace the $x$ on the left-hand side of the above with $2x.$ This gives

$$


2x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + n\pi.


$$

Solving for $x,$ we get

$$


\begin{aligned}2𝑥 & =\frac{𝜋}{2}+𝑛𝜋 \\ 𝑥 & =\frac{𝜋}{4}+\frac{𝑛𝜋}{2}.\end{aligned}


$$

Therefore, we conclude that the expression giving all the vertical asymptotes is $x= \dfrac{\pi}{4} + \dfrac{n\pi}{2},$ where $n$ is an integer.

### Example: Finding a General Expression for the Domain of a Transformed Tangent or Cotangent Function

#### Question

What is the domain of the function $y=\tan\left(\dfrac x 2\right)?$

#### Explanation

The domain of $y=\tan x$ is given by the expression

$$


x \neq \dfrac\pi 2 + n\pi ,


$$

where $n$ is an integer.

To find the domain of $\tan\left(\dfrac x 2\right),$ we replace the $x$ on the left-hand side of the above with $\left(\dfrac x 2\right).$ This gives

$$


\dfrac x 2 \neq \dfrac\pi 2 + n\pi .


$$

Solving for $x,$ we get

$$


\begin{aligned}\frac{𝑥}{2} & ≠\frac{𝜋}{2}+𝑛𝜋 \\ 𝑥 & ≠𝜋+2𝑛𝜋.\end{aligned}


$$

Therefore, the domain of the function is $x \neq \pi + 2n\pi,$ where $n$ is an integer.
