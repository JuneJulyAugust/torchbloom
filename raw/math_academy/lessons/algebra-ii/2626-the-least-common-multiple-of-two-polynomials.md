## Lesson

### Introduction

Just as we can find the least common multiple ($\textrm{LCM}$) of two monomials, we can also find the least common multiple of two polynomials.

To find the least common multiple ($\textrm{LCM}$) of two polynomials that are made up of products of binomials, we find the least common multiple of the constants and the least common multiple of the variable part. Then, we multiply all of the results together.

For example, let's find the least common multiple of $6(x+3)^2$ and $8(x+3).$

First, we find the least common multiple of the constants, $\textrm{LCM}(6,8)\mathbin{:}$

- Multiples of $6 \mathbin{:}$ $\quad 6, \, 12, \, 18, \, {\color{blue}24}, \, 30, \ldots$

- Multiples of $8 \mathbin{:}$ $\quad 8, \, 16, \, {\color{blue}24}, \, 32, \ldots$

So, $\textrm{LCM}(6,8) = {\color{blue}{24}}.$

To find the $\textrm{LCM}$ of $(x+3)^2$ and $(x+3),$ we raise $(x+3)$ to the largest power that appears in the expressions, which is $2.$ Therefore,

$$



\textrm{LCM}\left((x+3)^2, \, (x+3) \right) = {\color{red}{(x+3)^2}}.



$$

Finally, we multiply all of our results together:

$$



\textrm{LCM}\left(6(x+3)^2, \, 8(x+3) \right) = {\color{blue}{24}}\cdot {\color{red}{(x+3)^2}} = 24(x+3)^2.



$$

### Example: Finding the LCM of Two Polynomials that Differ by a Constant Factor

#### Question

Find the least common multiple of $4(x+1)$ and $6(x+1).$

#### Explanation

To find the least common multiple ($\textrm{LCM}$) of two polynomials that are made up of products of binomials, we find the least common multiple of the constants and the least common multiple of the variable part. Then, we multiply all of the results together.

First, we find $\textrm{LCM}(4,6)\mathbin{:}$

- Multiples of $4\mathbin{:}$ $\quad 4, \, 8, \, {\color{blue}12}, \ldots$

- Multiples of $6\mathbin{:}$ $\quad 6, \, {\color{blue}12}, \, 18, \ldots$

So, $\textrm{LCM}(4,6) = {\color{blue}{12}}.$

Since the variable part is the same in the expressions, we have

$$



\textrm{LCM}\left((x+1), \, (x+1)\right) = {\color{red}{(x+1)}}.



$$

Finally, we multiply all of our results together:

$$



\textrm{LCM}\left(4(x+1), \, 6(x+1)\right) = {\color{blue}{12}}\cdot {\color{red}{(x+1)}} = 12(x+1).



$$

### Example: Finding the LCM of Two Single-Variable Polynomials

#### Question

Find the least common multiple of $9(x-1)^4$ and $12(x-1)^2.$

#### Explanation

To find the least common multiple ($\textrm{LCM}$) of two polynomials that are made up of products of binomials, we find the least common multiple of the constants and the least common multiple of the variable part. Then, we multiply all of the results together.

First, we find $\textrm{LCM}(9,12)\mathbin{:}$

- Multiples of $9 \mathbin{:}$ $\quad 9, \, 18, \, 27, \, {\color{blue}36}, \, 45, \ldots$

- Multiples of $12 \mathbin{:}$ $\quad 12, \, 24, \, {\color{blue}36}, \, 48, \ldots$

So, $\textrm{LCM}(9,12) = {\color{blue}{36}}.$

To find the $\textrm{LCM}$ of $(x-1)^4$ and $(x-1)^2,$ we raise $(x-1)$ to the largest power that appears in the expressions, which is $4.$ Therefore,

$$



\textrm{LCM}\left((x-1)^4, \, (x-1)^2\right) = {\color{red}{(x-1)^4}}.



$$

Finally, we multiply all of our results together:

$$



\textrm{LCM}\left(9(x-1)^4, \, 12(x-1)^2\right) = {\color{blue}{36}}\cdot {\color{red}{(x-1)^4}} = 36(x-1)^4.



$$

### Example: Finding the LCM of Two Multivariable Polynomials

#### Question

Find the least common multiple of the polynomials $6(x+3)^2(y+1)^2$ and $9(x+3)(y+1)^4.$

#### Explanation

To find the least common multiple ($\textrm{LCM}$) of two polynomials that are made up of products of binomials, we find the least common multiple of the constants and the least common multiple of each variable part. Then, we multiply all of the results together.

First, we find $\textrm{LCM}(6,9)\mathbin{:}$

- Multiples of $6\mathbin{:}$ $\quad 6, \, 12, \, {\color{blue}18}, \, 24, \ldots$

- Multiples of $9\mathbin{:}$ $\quad 9, \, {\color{blue}18}, \, 27, \ldots$

So, $\textrm{LCM}(6,9) = {\color{blue}{18}}.$

To find the $\textrm{LCM}$ of $(x+3)^2(y+1)^2$ and $(x+3)(y+1)^4,$ we find the largest powers for $(x+3)$ and $(y+1)$ that appear in the expressions:

- For $(x+3)^2$ and $(x+3),$ the largest power is ${\color{blue}{2}}.$

- For $(y+1)^2$ and $(y+1)^4,$ the largest power is ${\color{red}{4}}.$

Now, we raise $(x+3)$ and $(y+1)$ to the corresponding largest powers. Therefore,

$$



\textrm{LCM}\left((x+3)^2(y+1)^2, \,(x+3)(y+1)^4\right) =(x+3)^{\color{blue}{2}}\cdot (y+1)^{\color{red}{4}}.



$$

Finally, we multiply all of our results together:

$$



\textrm{LCM}\left(6(x+3)^2(y+1)^2, \, 9(x+3)(y+1)^4\right) = 18(x+3)^2 (y+1)^4.



$$

### Example: Finding the LCM of Three Polynomials

#### Question

Find the least common multiple of the polynomials $12(x+2)^4 (y+1),$ $\,9(x+2) (y+1)^2,$ and $6(x+2)^2 (y+1)^3.$

#### Explanation

To find the least common multiple ($\textrm{LCM}$) of three polynomials that are made up of products of binomials, we find the least common multiple of the constants and the least common multiple of each variable part. Then, we multiply all of the results together.

First, we find $\textrm{LCM}(12, 9, 6)\mathbin{:}$

- Multiples of $12\mathbin{:}$ $\quad 12, \, 24, \, {\color{blue}36}, \, 48, \ldots$

- Multiples of $9\mathbin{:}$ $\quad 9, \, 18, \, 27, \, {\color{blue}36}, \, 45, \ldots$

- Multiples of $6 \mathbin{:}$ $\quad 6, \, 12, \, 18, \, 24, \, 30, \, {\color{blue}36}, \ldots$

So, $\textrm{LCM}(12, 9, 6) = {\color{blue}{36}}.$

To find the $\textrm{LCM}$ of $(x+2)^4 (y+1), (x+2) (y+1)^2,$ and $(x+2)^2 (y+1)^3,$ we find the largest powers for $(x+2)$ and $(y+1)$ that appear in all these expressions:

- For $(x+2)^4,(x+2),$ and $(x+2)^2,$ the largest power is ${\color{blue}{4}}.$

- For $(y+1),(y+1)^2,$ and $(y+1)^3,$ the largest power is ${\color{red}{3}}.$

Now, we raise $(x+2)$ and $(y+1)$ to the corresponding largest powers. Therefore,

$$



\textrm{LCM}\left((x+2)^4 (y+1), \,(x+2) (y+1)^2,\,(x+2)^2 (y+1)^3\right) =(x+2)^{\color{blue}{4}}\cdot (y+1)^{\color{red}{3}}.



$$

Finally, we multiply all of our results together:

$$



\begin{aligned} & LCM(12(𝑥+2)^{4}(𝑦+1),\,9(𝑥+2)(𝑦+1)^{2},\,6(𝑥+2)^{2}(𝑦+1)^{3}) \\ & =36⋅(𝑥+2)^{4}⋅(𝑦+1)^{3} \\ & =36(𝑥+2)^{4}(𝑦+1)^{3}.\end{aligned}



$$
