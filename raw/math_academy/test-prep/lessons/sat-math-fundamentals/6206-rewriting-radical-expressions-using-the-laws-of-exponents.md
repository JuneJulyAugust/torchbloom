### Introduction

Recall that an exponential expression such as $x^{1/2}$ can be written as $\sqrt x,$ the square root of $x.$

Similarly $z^{1/3}$ can be written as $\sqrt[3]{z},$ the cube root of $z.$

In general, the fractional exponent $x^{1/n}$ can be written as the $n$th root $\sqrt[n]{x}.$

So, how might we rewrite the following exponential expression as a radical?

$$


y^{4/7}


$$

Notice that the exponent is a non-unit fraction because the numerator $(4)$ does not equal $1.$

To rewrite $y^{4/7}$ as a radical, we use the rules of exponents.

First, using the power law for exponents, we can write our expression as

$$


y^{4/7} = \left(y^4\right)^{1/7}.


$$

Notice that we now have an expression $(y^4)$ raised to a unit fraction $(1/7).$ So, we can write our expression as

$$


\left(y^4\right)^{1/7} = \sqrt[7]{y^4}.


$$

Therefore, we conclude that

$$


y^{4/7} = \sqrt[7]{y^4}.


$$

Let's see some more examples.

### Example: Converting a Fractional Exponent to a Radical

#### Question

Convert $\left(6n\right)^{2/3}$ into a radical expression.

#### Explanation

First, using the power law for exponents, we can write our expression as

$$


\left(6n\right)^{2/3} = \left((6n)^2\right)^{1/3}.


$$

In general, the fractional exponent $x^{1/n}$ can also be written as the $n$th root $\sqrt[n]{x}.$

In this particular case, $n=3$ and $x=(6n)^2.$ Therefore, we have

$$


\left((6n)^2\right)^{1/3} = \sqrt[3]{(6n)^2} = \sqrt[3]{36\,n^2}.


$$

### Example: Converting a Radical to a Fractional Exponent

#### Question

Write $\sqrt[11]{\left(\dfrac{5}{a}\right)^{2}}$ using a fractional exponent.

#### Explanation

In general, the $n$th root $\sqrt[n]{x}$ can also be written using a fractional exponent as $x^{1/n}.$

In this particular case, $n=11$ and $x=\left(\dfrac{5}{a}\right)^2.$ Therefore, we have

$$


\sqrt[11]{\left(\dfrac{5}{a}\right)^{2}}=\left(\left(\dfrac{5}{a}\right)^{2}\right)^{1/11}.


$$

Finally, using the power law for exponents, we have

$$


\left(\left(\dfrac{5}{a}\right)^{2}\right)^{1/11}=\left(\dfrac{5}{a}\right)^{2/11}.


$$

### Example: Converting a Negative Fractional Exponent to a Radical

#### Question

Convert $(8y)^{-3/2}$ into a radical.

#### Explanation

Remember that in general, a negative exponent can be converted to a positive exponent if we take the reciprocal. Here, we have

$$


(8y)^{-3/2} = \dfrac{1}{(8y)^{3/2}}.


$$

Next, using the power law for exponents, we can write our expression as

$$


\dfrac{1}{(8y)^{3/2}} = \dfrac{1}{\left((8y)^3\right)^{1/2}}.


$$

Now, we can proceed as usual to convert the fractional exponent to a radical:

$$


\dfrac{1}{\left((8y)^3\right)^{1/2}} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{(8y)^3}}


$$

Therefore, we have

$$


(8y)^{-3/2} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{(8y)^3}} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{512y^3}}.


$$

### Example: Converting a Radical to a Negative Fractional Exponent

#### Question

Express $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt[8]{(4r)^3}}$ using power notation.

#### Explanation

First, we convert the radical to a fractional exponent:

$$


\dfrac{1}{\sqrt[8]{(4r)^3}}=\dfrac{1}{\left((4r)^3\right)^{1/8}}=\dfrac{1}{(4r)^{3/8}}


$$

Then, we take the reciprocal and convert the exponent to a negative exponent:

$$


\dfrac{1}{(4r)^{3/8}}=(4r)^{-3/8}


$$

Therefore, we have

$$


\dfrac{1}{\sqrt[8]{(4r)^3}}=(4r)^{-3/8}.


$$
