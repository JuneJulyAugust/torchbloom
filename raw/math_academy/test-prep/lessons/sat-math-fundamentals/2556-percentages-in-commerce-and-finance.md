## Lesson

### Introduction

When items are sold or purchased, the final bill is not always the same as the original price of the item.

- Sometimes the final bill is *greater than* the original price because some extra amount is added to the original price. Examples include markups, taxes, commissions, gratuities, and fees.

- Other times, the final bill is *less than* the original price because some amount is subtracted from the original price. Examples include markdowns and discounts.

For example, suppose that Jane buys a shirt from the store for $20.$

- If there is a $10\%$ tax, then an extra $10\%$ of $20$ is added to the bill.

- On the other hand, if there is a $25\%$ discount, then $25\%$ of $20$ is subtracted from the bill.

### Example: Calculating a Tax or Commission Amount

#### Question

Sergey is a car salesman. Every time he sells a car, he gets a commission of $4\%.$ If Sergey sells a car for $15,000,$ then how much does he earn in commission?

#### Explanation

Sergey's commission is $4\%$ of the price of the car. In this case, the price of the car is $15,000.$ So, to calculate how much Sergey earns in commission, we need to find $4\%$ of $15,000.$

To do this, we first write $4\%$ as a decimal:

$$


4\% = 4 \div 100 = 0.04


$$

Then, we multiply by $15,000\mathbin{:}$

$$


0.04 \times 15,000 = 600


$$

Therefore, Sergey earns $600$ in commission.

### Example: Calculating a Markdown or Discount Amount

#### Question

John's Jeans is having a sale in which a $20\%$ discount is applied to all pairs of jeans. If a pair of jeans cost $35$ before the discount is applied, how much is the discount?

#### Explanation

There is a $20\%$ discount on the price of the jeans. Here, the price of the jeans is $35.$ So to calculate the amount of the discount, we need to find $20\%$ of $35.$

To do this, we first write $20\%$ as a decimal:

$$


20\% = 20 \div 100 = 0.2


$$

Then, we multiply by $35\mathbin{:}$

$$


0.2 \times 35 = 7


$$

So, the amount of the discount is $7.$

### Example: Calculating a Total Bill After a Tax or Commission

#### Question

Larry bought a computer for $800$ before tax. If the sales tax was $7\%,$ then what was Larry's total bill after tax?

#### Explanation

First, we need to find the amount of tax that Larry must pay. Then, we will add that amount to the price of the computer.

The tax rate is $7\%$ of the price of the computer, and the price of the computer is $800.$ So, to calculate how much tax Larry must pay, we need to find $7\%$ of $800.$

To do this, we first write $7\%$ as a decimal:

$$


7\% = 7 \div 100 = 0.07


$$

Then, we multiply by $800\mathbin{:}$

$$


0.07 \times 800 = 56


$$

So Larry must pay $56$ in tax. We add this amount to the price of the computer:

$$


800 + 56 = 856


$$

Therefore, Larry's total bill after tax is $856.$

### Example: Calculating a Total Bill After a Markdown or Discount

#### Question

Otto's Outdoorswear is having a sale in which a $15\%$ markdown is applied to all items in the store. If Dillon buys a winter jacket for $120$ before the markdown is applied, what is Dillon's total bill after the markdown?

#### Explanation

First, we need to find the amount of the markdown. Then, we will subtract that amount from the price of the jacket.

The markdown is $15\%$ of the price of the jacket, and the price of the jacket is $120.$ So, to calculate the amount of the markdown, we need to find $15\%$ of $120.$

To do this, we first write $15\%$ as a decimal:

$$


15\% = 15 \div 100 = 0.15


$$

Then, we multiply by $120\mathbin{:}$

$$


0.15 \times 120 = 18


$$

So the amount of the markdown is $18.$ We subtract this amount from the price of the jacket:

$$


120 - 18 = 102


$$

Therefore, Dillon's total bill after the markdown is $102.$
