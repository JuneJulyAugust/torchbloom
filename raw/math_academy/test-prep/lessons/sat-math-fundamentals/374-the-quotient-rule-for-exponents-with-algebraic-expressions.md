### Introduction

The **quotient rule for exponents** states that when we divide two exponents that have the same base, we take the difference of the exponents in the numerator and denominator:

$$


\dfrac{a^n}{a^m}=a^{n-m}


$$

For example,

$$


\begin{aligned}\frac{𝑎^{5}}{𝑎^{2}} & = \\ 𝑎^{(5−2)} & = \\ 𝑎^{3}. & \end{aligned}


$$

To see why the quotient rule is true, let's take the above example and work it out again, the long way.

For the two terms $a^5$ and $a^2$ we have $a^5 = a \cdot a \cdot a \cdot a \cdot a$ and $a^2 = a \cdot a.$ If we now substitute these two expressions into $\dfrac{a^5}{a^2},$ we get the same result:

$$


 \begin{aligned} \dfrac{a^{5}}{a^2}&=\\\dfrac {a \cdot a \cdot a \cdot a \cdot a} {a \cdot a}&=\\\dfrac {a \cdot a \cdot a \cdot a \cdot a} {a \cdot a }&=\\a \cdot a \cdot a &=\\a^3 \end{aligned}


$$

The quotient rule is just a shortcut for the process above.

### Example: Applying the Quotient Rule for Exponents

#### Question

Simplify $\dfrac{x^2}{x^3}.$

#### Explanation

We subtract the exponents, as follows:

$$


\begin{aligned}\frac{𝑥^{2}}{𝑥^{3}} & = \\ 𝑥^{(2−3)} & = \\ 𝑥^{−1} & \end{aligned}


$$

Then, we convert the negative exponent to a positive exponent by taking the reciprocal:

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑥^{−1} & =\frac{1}{𝑥}\end{aligned}


$$

Therefore,

$$


\dfrac{x^2}{x^3} = \dfrac 1 x.


$$

### Example: Applying the Quotient Rule with Negative Exponents

#### Question

Simplify $\dfrac{z^5}{z^{-3}}.$

#### Explanation

We subtract the exponents, as follows:

$$


\begin{aligned}\frac{𝑧^{5}}{𝑧^{−3}} & = \\ 𝑧^{5−(−3)} & = \\ 𝑧^{5+3} & = \\ 𝑧^{8} & \end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Applying the Quotient Rule and Product Rule

#### Question

Simplify the expression $\dfrac {p^7} {p^2} \cdot p^3.$

#### Explanation

First we apply the quotient rule, subtracting exponents in the fraction:

$$


\begin{aligned}\begin{matrix}\frac{𝑝^{7}}{𝑝^{2}}⋅𝑝^{3} & = \\ 𝑝^{(7−2)}⋅𝑝^{3} & = \\ 𝑝^{5}⋅𝑝^{3} & \end{matrix}\end{aligned}


$$

Then we apply the product rule, adding exponents in the product:

$$


\begin{aligned}\begin{matrix}𝑝^{5}⋅𝑝^{3} & = \\ 𝑝^{(5+3)} & = \\ 𝑝^{8} & \end{matrix}\end{aligned}


$$

Therefore,

$$


\begin{aligned}\begin{matrix}\frac{𝑝^{7}}{𝑝^{2}}⋅𝑝^{3}=𝑝^{8}.\end{matrix}\end{aligned}


$$
