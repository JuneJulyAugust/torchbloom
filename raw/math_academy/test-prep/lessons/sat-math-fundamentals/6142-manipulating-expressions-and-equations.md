### Introduction

Often in algebra, we’re so used to solving for a variable that we overlook faster, simpler paths to an answer. In many problems, the quantity we’re asked to find already contains a piece of information we’ve been given. Recognizing this can save time, reduce the possibility of errors, and sharpen our pattern-recognition skills.

For example, suppose we have the equation

$$


5y = 32,


$$

and we want to determine the value of

$$


5y - 6.


$$

What is the fastest way to do this? Let's compare two different methods.

- **Method 1 (Slow)** The standard approach is to solve for $y$ using the first equation, and then substitute that value into the expression we want to evaluate. First, we solve for the variable $y{:}$ Then, we evaluate $5y-6{:}$

- **Method 2 (Fast)** Notice that the term $\color{blue}5y$ appears in *both* the equation *and* the expression we wish to evaluate. So, there's no need to solve for $y.$ Instead, we can substitute ${\color{blue}{5y}} = {\color{red}{32}}$ directly into the expression ${\color{blue}{5y}} - 6,$ and evaluate:

Let's see some more examples.

### Example: Evaluating an Expression

#### Question

If $\dfrac{6p}{3} = 10,$ then find the value of $6p - 4$.

#### Explanation

Notice that $6p - 4$ contains $6p.$

So, we isolate the term $6p$ from the first equation, as follows:

$$


\begin{aligned}\frac{6𝑝}{3} & =10 \\ 3⋅\frac{6𝑝}{3} & =3⋅10 \\ 6𝑝 & =30\end{aligned}


$$

Now, we substitute $6p = 30$ into $6p - 4$ and simplify:

$$


\begin{aligned}6𝑝−4 & =(30)−4 \\ & =26\end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Evaluating an Expression Using Multiplication and Division

#### Question

If $12 + 4x = 28,$ what is the value of $3 + x$?

#### Explanation

Notice that the target expression $3 + x$ can be obtained by multiplying the expression $12 + 4x$ by $\dfrac14{:}$

$$


\dfrac{1}{4} \cdot (12 + 4x) = 3 + x.


$$

Since we are already told that $12 + 4x = 28,$ we can multiply both sides of the equation by $\dfrac{1}{4}{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}12+4𝑥 & =28 \\ \frac{1}{4}⋅(12+4𝑥) & =\frac{1}{4}⋅28 \\ 3+𝑥 & =7\end{aligned}


$$

Therefore, the value of $3 + x$ is $\boxed{7}.$

### The Reciprocal of an Equation

Suppose that $a,b,c,d \neq 0.$ Then, the equation

$$


\dfrac{\color{blue}a}{\color{red}b} = \dfrac{\color{blue}c}{\color{red}d}


$$

is equivalent to the equation obtained by taking the reciprocal of both sides:

$$


\dfrac{\color{red}b}{\color{blue}a} = \dfrac{\color{red}d}{\color{blue}c}


$$

For example, suppose we have the equation

$$


\dfrac{w}{2} = 3.


$$

Provided that $w\neq 0,$ we can determine the value of $\dfrac2w$ *without* solving for $w$ first.

First, we express the right-hand side as a fraction:

$$


\begin{aligned}\frac{𝑤}{2} & =\frac{3}{1}\end{aligned}


$$

Finally, we take the reciprocal of both sides, which gives

$$


\begin{aligned}\frac{2}{𝑤} & =\frac{1}{3}.\end{aligned}


$$

Note the the condition $w\neq 0$ is important. If $w = 0$ then the expression $\dfrac2w$ is undefined!

### Example: Evaluating an Expression Using the Reciprocal

#### Question

If $\dfrac{3x}{2} + 5 = 11$ and $x\neq 0,$ what is the value of $\dfrac{2}{3x}?$

#### Explanation

Notice that $\dfrac{2}{3x}$ is the reciprocal of $\dfrac{3x}{2}.$

First, we isolate the term $\dfrac{3x}{2}$ from the first equation, as follows:

$$


\begin{aligned}\frac{3𝑥}{2}+5 & =11 \\ \frac{3𝑥}{2}+5−5 & =11−5 \\ \frac{3𝑥}{2} & =6\end{aligned}


$$

Now, it is sufficient to take the reciprocal on both sides of the equation, as follows:

$$


\begin{aligned}\frac{3𝑥}{2} & =6 \\ \frac{3𝑥}{2} & =\frac{6}{1} \\ \frac{2}{3𝑥} & =\frac{1}{6}\end{aligned}


$$
