### Introduction

**Factoring** is the opposite of distributing. To illustrate, consider the expression $4(a-2b).$ If you consider distributing as going forward,

$$


\begin{aligned}4(𝑎−2𝑏) & = \\ 4⋅𝑎−4⋅2𝑏 & = \\ 4𝑎−8𝑏, & \end{aligned}


$$

then factoring would be going in reverse:

$$


\begin{aligned}4𝑎−8𝑏 & = \\ (4⋅𝑎)\,−\,(4⋅2𝑏) & = \\ 4\,(𝑎−2𝑏) & \end{aligned}


$$

In this case, you would say that $\color{red}4$ was **factored out** of $4a-8b.$

To factor any expression, we just need to follow these two steps:

1. Separate the greatest common factor from each of the terms (ignoring any negative signs). In this case, $\text{GCF}(4a,8b) = {\color{red}{4}},$ so:

2. Place the common factor in front of the parentheses, leaving whatever remains inside the parentheses:

### Example: Factoring Out a Positive Number

#### Question

Factor the following expression: $6a+30.$

#### Explanation

We factor in two steps:

1. Separate the greatest common factor from each of the terms. In this case, $\text{GCF}(6a,30) = 6,$ so:

2. Place the common factor in front of the parentheses, leaving whatever remains inside the parentheses:

Thus, the factored form of the expression is $6(a+5).$

### Factoring Out a Negative Sign

When the terms of an expression are negative, we can also factor out a negative sign. For example, the expression $-x-1$ can be factored into $-(x+1).$

By the same token, the expression $-2x-2$ can be factored by first factoring out a negative sign, and then factoring out the greatest common factor of $2{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}−2𝑥−2 & = \\ −(2𝑥+2) & = \\ −2(𝑥+1) & \end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Factoring Out a Negative Number

#### Question

Factor the following expression: $-5a-35.$

#### Explanation

Both terms are negative, so we begin by factoring out a negative sign:

$$


\begin{aligned}−5𝑎−35 & = \\ −(5𝑎+35) & \end{aligned}


$$

Then, we continue factoring in two steps:

1. Separate the greatest common factor from each of the terms. In this case, $\text{GCF}(5a,35) = 5,$ so

2. Place the common factor in front of the parentheses, leaving whatever remains inside the parentheses:

Thus, the factored form of the expression is $-5(a+7).$

### Example: Factoring Expressions With More Than Two Terms

#### Question

Factor the expression $27a+33b-30.$

#### Explanation

We factor in two steps:

1. Separate the greatest common factor from each of the terms (ignoring any negative signs). In this case, $\text{GCF}(27a, 33b, 30) = 3,$ so:

2. Place the common factor in front of the parentheses, leaving whatever remains inside the parentheses:

Thus, the factored form of the expression is $3(9a+11b-10).$
