### Introduction

When collecting data, we sometimes find values that are much larger or much smaller than all the other values in the data set. We call these values **outliers**.

For example, consider the following data set, which records the heights (in feet) of a group of saplings:

$$


5, \quad 5.5, \quad 1, \quad 4.5, \quad 5


$$

Let's begin by writing our data set in ascending order:

$$


{\color{blue}\underline{1}}, \quad 4.5, \quad 5, \quad 5, \quad 5.5


$$

In this data set, the value $\color{blue}1$ is an outlier because it is much smaller than all other values.

### Example: Finding a Single Outlier

#### Question

What is the outlier of the data set given below?

$$


28, \quad 11, \quad 17, \quad 841, \quad 33, \quad 41, \quad 50


$$

#### Explanation

An outlier is a value that is either much larger or much smaller than all other values of a data set.

Let's begin by writing our data set in ascending order:

$$


11, \quad 17, \quad 28, \quad 33, \quad 41, \quad 50, \quad {\color{blue}\underline{841}}


$$

In the given data set, the value $\color{blue}841$ is much larger than all other values. So, $\color{blue}841$ is an outlier.

### Example: Finding Multiple Outliers

#### Question

What are the outliers of the data set given below?

$$


289, \quad 927, \quad 221, \quad 201, \quad 209, \quad 11, \quad 291, \quad 217


$$

#### Explanation

An outlier is a value that is either much larger or much smaller than all other values of a data set.

Let's begin by writing our data set in ascending order.

$$


{\color{blue}\underline{11}}, \quad 201, \quad 209, \quad 217, \quad 221, \quad 289, \quad 291, \quad{\color{blue}\underline{927}}


$$

In the given data set, the value $\color{blue}11$ is much smaller than all of the other values, and the value ${\color{blue}927}$ is much larger than all other values. So, $\color{blue}11$ and ${\color{blue}927}$ are outliers.
