## Lesson

### Introduction

In this lesson, we will learn how to compare radical expressions. This skill is important because radicals frequently appear in algebra and geometry, and we often need to determine which values are larger or smaller without using a calculator. This task is not always straightforward because there are many ways of writing radical expressions.

The key fact that we will use throughout this lesson is the following:

*For any positive numbers $a$ and $b,$ if $a < b,$ then $\sqrt{a} < \sqrt{b}.$*

For example, suppose we want to arrange the following radicals in ascending order (smallest to largest):

$$


\sqrt{7},\quad \sqrt{2},\quad \sqrt{5}


$$

By comparing the numbers under the radical sign, we get

$$


2 < 5 < 7.


$$

Therefore, the correct order of the radicals is

$$


\sqrt{2} < \sqrt{5} < \sqrt{7}.


$$

We can check that this result is correct by comparing their approximate decimal values:

$$


\sqrt{2} \approx 1.41, \qquad \sqrt{5} \approx 2.24, \qquad \sqrt{7} \approx 2.65


$$

Indeed, these match the inequality we obtained.

$$


1.41 < 2.24 < 2.65


$$

Let's see some more examples.

### Example: Ordering Radicals

#### Question

Express the following radicals in ascending order

$$


\sqrt{39}, \quad \sqrt{19}, \quad \sqrt{28}


$$

#### Explanation

Recall that for any positive numbers $a$ and $b,$ if $a < b,$ then $\sqrt{a} < \sqrt{b}.$

Notice that $19 < 28 < 39.$ Therefore, the correct order of the radicals is

$$


\sqrt{19} < \sqrt{28} < \sqrt{39}.


$$

### Using the Product Rule

Certain radical expressions can be rewritten using the product rule for radicals:

$$


\sqrt{a} \cdot \sqrt{b} = \sqrt{a \cdot b}


$$

This allows us to write multiples of radicals as a single radical, making it easier to compare them with other radicals.

To illustrate this method, let's write the following radicals in ascending order.

$$


3\sqrt{6},\qquad \sqrt{51}


$$

**Watch out!** You might be tempted to say that $\sqrt{51}$ is larger than $3\sqrt{6},$ but is that really the case? Let's find out.

To compare these two radicals, we need to express $3\sqrt{6}$ as a single radical using the product rule. We'll also use the fact that if $a$ is positive, then

$$


\sqrt{a^2} = a.


$$

Now, we apply the product rule for radicals in reverse to express $3\sqrt{6}$ as a single radical, as follows:

$$


\begin{aligned}3\sqrt{6} & =\sqrt{3^{2}}⋅\sqrt{6} \\ & =\sqrt{9}⋅\sqrt{6} \\ & =\sqrt{9⋅6} \\ & =\sqrt{54}\end{aligned}


$$

Since $51 < 54,$ we have $\sqrt{51} < \sqrt{54}.$

Therefore, we conclude that

$$


\sqrt{51} < 3\sqrt{6}.


$$

Let's see some more examples.

### Example: Comparing Radicals Using the Product Rule

#### Question

Fill in the blanks so that the following reasoning is correct:

$\qquad$ The radical $6\sqrt{5}$ is equivalent to $𝐼180$

$\qquad$ The radical $5\sqrt{7}$ is equivalent to $𝐼175$

$\qquad$ Therefore, $6\sqrt{5}$ $𝐼𝑥𝑥$ $5\sqrt{7}.$

#### Explanation

Recall that for any positive numbers $a$ and $b,$ if $a < b,$ then $\sqrt{a} < \sqrt{b}.$

First, we use the product rule of radicals in reverse:

$$


\begin{aligned}6\sqrt{5} & =\sqrt{6^{2}}⋅\sqrt{5} \\ & =\sqrt{36}⋅\sqrt{5} \\ & =\sqrt{36⋅5} \\ & =\sqrt{180} \\ 5\sqrt{7} & =\sqrt{5^{2}}⋅\sqrt{7} \\ & =\sqrt{25}⋅\sqrt{7} \\ & =\sqrt{25⋅7} \\ & =\sqrt{175}\end{aligned}


$$

Since $180 > 175,$ we have $\sqrt{180} > \sqrt{175}.$

Therefore, we conclude that

$$


6\sqrt{5} > 5\sqrt{7}.


$$

### Using the Quotient Rule

We can also rewrite certain radical expressions using the quotient rule for radicals:

$$


\dfrac{\sqrt{a}}{ \sqrt{b}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{a}{ b}\,\!},


$$

for any real numbers $a \geq 0$ and $b > 0.$

Just like with the product rule, this rule also helps us combine radicals into a single square root, making it easier to compare them directly. Once we apply the rule, we simply compare the radicands.

To illustrate this method, let's compare the following radicals:

$$


\dfrac{\sqrt{96}}{4}, \qquad \sqrt{7}


$$

First, we use the quotient rule of radicals in reverse:

$$


\begin{aligned}\frac{\sqrt{96}}{4} & =\frac{\sqrt{96}}{\sqrt{4^{2}}} \\ & =\frac{\sqrt{96}}{\sqrt{16}} \\ & =\sqrt{\frac{96}{16}} \\ & =\sqrt{6}\end{aligned}


$$

Since $6 < 7,$ we have $\sqrt{6} < \sqrt{7}.$

Therefore, we conclude that

$$


\dfrac{\sqrt{96}}{4} < \sqrt{7}.


$$

We can also compare radicals by combining the product *and* quotient rules. Let's see some examples.

### Example: Comparing Radicals Using the Product and Quotient Rules

#### Question

Fill in the blanks so that the following reasoning is correct:

$\qquad$ The radical $\dfrac{3}{8}\sqrt{128}$ is equivalent to $𝐼𝑥𝑥$ Therefore, $\dfrac{3}{8}\sqrt{128}$ $𝐼𝑐𝑥$ $\sqrt{19}.$

#### Explanation

Recall that for any positive numbers $a$ and $b,$ if $a < b,$ then $\sqrt{a} < \sqrt{b}.$

First, we use the quotient rule of radicals in reverse:

$$


\begin{aligned}\frac{3}{8}\sqrt{128} & =\frac{\sqrt{3^{2}}}{\sqrt{8^{2}}}⋅\sqrt{128} \\ & =\frac{\sqrt{9}⋅\sqrt{128}}{\sqrt{64}} \\ & =\sqrt{\frac{9⋅128}{64}} \\ & =\sqrt{9⋅\frac{128}{64}} \\ & =\sqrt{9⋅2} \\ & =\sqrt{18}\end{aligned}


$$

Since $18 < 19,$ we have $\sqrt{18} < \sqrt{19}.$

Therefore, we conclude that

$$


\dfrac{3}{8}\sqrt{128} < \sqrt{19}.


$$

### Example: Comparing Expressions Containing Radical Denominators

#### Question

Fill in the blanks so that the following reasoning is correct:

$\qquad$ The radical $\dfrac{12}{\sqrt{20}}$ is equivalent to $𝐼𝑥𝑥$ Therefore, $\dfrac{12}{\sqrt{20}}$ $𝐼𝑐𝑥$ $\sqrt{7}.$

#### Explanation

Recall that for any positive numbers $a$ and $b,$ if $a < b,$ then $\sqrt{a} < \sqrt{b}.$

First, we use the quotient rule of radicals in reverse:

$$


\begin{aligned}\frac{12}{\sqrt{20}} & =\frac{\sqrt{12^{2}}}{\sqrt{20}} \\ & =\frac{\sqrt{144}}{\sqrt{20}} \\ & =\sqrt{\frac{144}{20}} \\ & =\sqrt{\frac{36}{5}}\end{aligned}


$$

Also, note that

$$


\sqrt{7} = \sqrt{\dfrac{7}{1}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{35}{5}}


$$

Since

$$


\dfrac{36}{5} > \dfrac{35}{5}


$$

we have that

$$


\sqrt{\dfrac{36}{5}} > \sqrt{\dfrac{35}{5}}.


$$

Therefore, we conclude that

$$


\dfrac{12}{\sqrt{20}} > \sqrt{7}.


$$
