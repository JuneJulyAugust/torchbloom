### Introduction

Suppose we wish to factor the expression

$$


(x - 4)(x + 1) + (x - 4)(x + 3).


$$

You might be tempted to multiply the binomials and then factor the resulting quadratic expression. However, there's a shortcut that makes factoring this expression much easier!

First, notice that both terms contain a common factor of $(x-4).$

$$


{\color{blue}(x - 4)}(x + 1) + {\color{blue}(x - 4)}(x + 3)


$$

Therefore, we can factor out the common factor ${\color{blue}(x - 4)}$ as follows:

$$


{\color{blue}(x - 4)}\left[(x + 1) + (x + 3)\right]


$$

Finally, we simplify the expression inside the square brackets. This gives

$$


(x - 4)(2x + 4).


$$

And we're done! This useful trick is used quite often in higher-level math. The trick is to spot the common factor and factor right away *instead of* multiplying.

### Example: Factoring Linear Expressions Containing a Common Binomial

#### Question

Factor the following expression:

$$


\dfrac{1}{2}(5y-3) - \dfrac{1}{5}(5y-3)


$$

#### Explanation

We are asked to factor the expression

$$


\dfrac{1}{2}(5y-3) - \dfrac{1}{5}(5y-3).


$$

Notice that both terms in this expression contain a common factor of $(5y-3).$ So, we can factor the expression as follows:

$$


\begin{aligned}(\frac{1}{2}−\frac{1}{5})(5𝑦−3)\end{aligned}


$$

Now, we subtract the numbers inside the first brackets. This gives

$$


\begin{aligned}(\frac{1}{2}−\frac{1}{5})(5𝑦−3) & = \\ (\frac{5}{10}−\frac{2}{10})(5𝑦−3) & = \\ \frac{5−2}{10}(5𝑦−3) & = \\ \frac{3}{10}(5𝑦−3) & .\end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Factoring Quadratic Expressions Containing a Common Binomial

#### Question

Factor the following expression:

$$


(2y - 5)(y - 4) - (2y - 5)(3y + 2)


$$

#### Explanation

We're asked to factor the expression

$$


(2y - 5)(y - 4) - (2y - 5)(3y + 2).


$$

Notice that both terms in this expression contain a common factor of $(2y - 5).$ So, we can factor the expression as follows:

$$


(2y - 5)\left[(y - 4) - (3y + 2)\right]


$$

Now, we simplify the expression inside the square brackets. This gives

$$


\begin{aligned} & =(2𝑦−5)(𝑦−4−3𝑦−2) \\ & =(2𝑦−5)(−2𝑦−6).\end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Factoring Expressions Containing Squared Binomials

#### Question

Factor the following expression:

$$


(2y - 1)(y + 4) + 3(2y - 1)^2


$$

#### Explanation

We're asked to factor the expression

$$


(2y - 1)(y + 4) + 3(2y - 1)^2.


$$

Notice that both terms in this expression contain a common factor of $(2y - 1).$ So, we can factor the expression as follows:

$$


(2y - 1)\left[(y + 4) + 3(2y - 1)\right]


$$

Now, we simplify the expression inside the square brackets. This gives

$$


\begin{aligned} & =(2𝑦−1)(𝑦+4+6𝑦−3) \\ & =(2𝑦−1)(7𝑦+1).\end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Factoring Expressions Containing Cubic Binomials

#### Question

Consider the expression

$$


(y - 1)^3(y + 2) + (y + 4)(y - 1)^2.


$$

This expression can be written in the form

$$


(y - 1)^2\cdot g(y).


$$

Find the polynomial $g(y).$

#### Explanation

We're asked to factor the expression

$$


(y - 1)^3(y + 2) + (y + 4)(y - 1)^2.


$$

Notice that both terms in this expression contain a common factor of $(y - 1)^2.$ So, we can factor the expression as follows:

$$


(y - 1)^2\left[(y - 1)(y + 2) + (y + 4)\right]


$$

Now, we simplify the expression inside the square brackets. This gives

$$


\begin{aligned} & =(𝑦−1)^{2}[(𝑦−1)(𝑦+2)+(𝑦+4)] \\ & =(𝑦−1)^{2}[𝑦^{2}+𝑦−2+𝑦+4] \\ & =(𝑦−1)^{2}⋅\underset{𝑔(𝑦)}{\underset{}{(𝑦^{2}+2𝑦+2)}}.\end{aligned}


$$

Therefore, we have

$$


g(y) = y^2 + 2y + 2.


$$
