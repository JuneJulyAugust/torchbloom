## Lesson

### Introduction

Suppose that we want to solve the following quadratic equation:

$$


(x+1)^2 = 4.


$$

Notice that the left-hand side is a perfect square and the right-hand side is a constant:

$$


\underbrace{(x+1)^2}_{\large\color{blue}\text{perfect square}} = \overbrace{\!\!\!\!\!4\!\!\!\!\!}^{\large\color{red}\text{constant}} \!\!\!\!\!


$$

In this case, we can solve the equation using the square root method. First, we take the square root of both sides:

$$


\begin{aligned}(𝑥+1)^{2} & =4 \\ \sqrt{(𝑥+1)^{2}} & =\sqrt{4} \\ |𝑥+1| & =2\end{aligned}


$$

Then, we solve the resulting absolute value equation:

$$


\begin{aligned}|𝑥+1| & =2 \\ 𝑥+1 & =±2 \\ 𝑥 & =−1±2\end{aligned}


$$

Finally, we simplify the two solutions:

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑥 & =−1+2=1 \\ 𝑥 & =−1−2=−3\end{aligned}


$$

Therefore, the solutions are $x=-3$ and $x=1.$

### Example: Equations Where the Perfect Square is Isolated

#### Question

Solve $(3y +2)^2 = 4.$

#### Explanation

We solve the equation using the square root method:

$$


\begin{aligned}(3𝑦+2)^{2} & =4 \\ \sqrt{(3𝑦+2)^{2}} & =\sqrt{4} \\ |3𝑦+2| & =2 \\ 3𝑦+2 & =±2 \\ 3𝑦 & =−2±2 \\ 𝑦 & =\frac{−2±2}{3}\end{aligned}


$$

Next, we simplify the two solutions:

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑦 & =\frac{−2+2}{3}=0 \\ 𝑦 & =\frac{−2−2}{3}=−\frac{4}{3}\end{aligned}


$$

Therefore, the solutions are $y=-\dfrac{4}{3}$ and $y=0.$

### Example: Equations Where the Perfect Square Is Not Isolated

#### Question

If $-2(3x - 6)^2 + 1 = -9$, then calculate $x.$

#### Explanation

First, we isolate the perfect square:

$$


\begin{aligned}−2(3𝑥−6)^{2}+1 & =−9 \\ −2(3𝑥−6)^{2} & =−10 \\ (3𝑥−6)^{2} & =5\end{aligned}


$$

Then, we solve the equation using the square root method:

$$


\begin{aligned}\sqrt{(3𝑥−6)^{2}} & =\sqrt{5} \\ |3𝑥−6| & =\sqrt{5} \\ 3𝑥−6 & =±\sqrt{5} \\ 3𝑥 & =6±\sqrt{5} \\ 𝑥 & =\frac{6±\sqrt{5}}{3} \\ 𝑥 & =\frac{6}{3}±\frac{\sqrt{5}}{3} \\ 𝑥 & =2±\frac{\sqrt{5}}{3}\end{aligned}


$$

The two solutions are

$$


x= 2 + \dfrac{\sqrt{5}}{3}\quad \text{and}\quad x= 2 - \dfrac{\sqrt{5}}{3}.


$$

However, because these solutions cannot be simplified any further, we will keep the "$\pm$" symbol in our final answer. So our final answer is

$$


x = 2 \pm \dfrac{\sqrt{5}}{3}.


$$

### Example: Solving Equations by Factoring

#### Question

Given that $t^2 -10t +25 = 121$, what are the values of $t?$

#### Explanation

First, notice that the left-hand side is a perfect square. It just needs to be factored:

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑡^{2}−10𝑡+25 & =121 \\ 𝑡^{2}−2⋅5⋅𝑡+5^{2} & =121 \\ (𝑡−5)^{2} & =121\end{aligned}


$$

From here, we solve the equation using the square root method:

$$


\begin{aligned}\sqrt{(𝑡−5)^{2}} & =\sqrt{121} \\ |𝑡−5| & =11 \\ 𝑡−5 & =±11 \\ 𝑡 & =5±11\end{aligned}


$$

Next, we simplify the two solutions:

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑡 & =5+11=16 \\ 𝑡 & =5−11=−6\end{aligned}


$$

Therefore, the solutions are $t=-6$ and $t=16.$
