### Introduction

The **standard form** equation of a line is

$$


ax+by=c,


$$

where

- $a,b,$ and $c$ are all integers,

- $a \geq 0,$ and

- $a,b,$ and $c$ have no common factors.

Note that we always write $x$ *before* $y$ on the left-hand side!

Let's see some examples:

- The equation $2x - y= -5$ is in standard form. Indeed, the variables are on the left-hand side, while the constant term is on the right-hand side: All coefficients are integers (namely, $2,$ $-1,$ and $-5$) and don't have common factors greater than $1.$ Also, the coefficient of $x$ is non-negative ($2$).

- The equation $-3x + y = 1$ is *not* in standard form because the coefficient of $x$ is negative ($-3$).

- The equation $2x+4y = -10$ is *not* in standard form because the coefficients $(2,4,$ and $-10)$ have a common factor of $2.$

- The equation $\dfrac12x+\dfrac13y = 6$ is *not* in standard form because the coefficients of $x$ and $y$ are not integers.

### Example: Identifying Equations Written in Standard Form

#### Question

Consider the equations of the following lines.

1. $-5x + y=3$

2. $3x - y= -2$

3. $3x= 5$

4. $2x- 6y= 4$

Which of these lines is written in standard form?

#### Explanation

The ** equation of a line is

$$


ax+by=c,


$$

where

- $a,b,$ and $c$ are all integers,

- $a\geq 0,$ and

- $a,b,$ and $c$ have no common factors greater than $1.$

With that in mind, let's examine our equations.

- Equation I is ** in standard form because the coefficient of $x$ is negative ($-5$).

- Equation II is in standard form. Indeed, the variables are on the left-hand side, while the constant term is on the right-hand side: All coefficients are integers (namely, $3,$ $-1,$ and $-2$) and don't have common factors greater than $1.$ Also, the coefficient of $x$ is positive ($3$).

- Equation III is in standard form. Indeed, the variables are on the left-hand side, while the constant term is on the right-hand side: All coefficients are integers (namely, $3$ and $5$) and don't have common factors greater than $1.$ Also, the coefficient of $x$ is positive ($3$).

- Equation IV is ** in standard form because the coefficients $(2,-6,$ and $4)$ have a common factor of $2.$

Therefore, the correct answer is "II and III only."

### Converting a Line to Standard Form

Let's now discuss how to convert the equation of a line to standard form.

To illustrate, we will take the line $y = 2x-1$ and convert it into standard form. We begin by moving all of the variables to the left-hand side and all of the constants to the right-hand side.

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑦 & =2𝑥−1 \\ 𝑦−2𝑥 & =−1 \\ −2𝑥+𝑦 & =−1\end{aligned}


$$

Now the equation is in the form $ax+by=c.$ However, it violates the rule $a \geq 0.$ We can fix this by multiplying the equation by $-1$ as follows:

$$


\begin{aligned}−2𝑥+𝑦 & =−1 \\ (−1)⋅(−2𝑥+𝑦) & =(−1)⋅(−1) \\ 2𝑥−𝑦 & =1\end{aligned}


$$

Now, $a,b,$ and $c$ are integers with no common factors, and $a \geq 0.$ So, we are done!

The standard form of the line is $2x-y=1.$

### Example: Converting from Slope-Intercept Form to Standard Form

#### Question

Write $y = 7x+\dfrac{2}{5}$ in standard form.

#### Explanation

Our goal is to write the equation in the form $ax+by = c,$ where $a,b,$ and $c$ are integers with no common factors, and $a\geq 0.$

We begin by moving all of the variables to the left-hand side and all of the constants to the right-hand side.

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑦 & =7𝑥+\frac{2}{5} \\ 𝑦−7𝑥 & =\frac{2}{5} \\ −7𝑥+𝑦 & =\frac{2}{5}\end{aligned}


$$

Now the equation is in the form $ax+by=c.$ However, it violates the rule $a \geq 0.$ We can fix this by multiplying the equation by $-1$ as follows:

$$


\begin{aligned}−7𝑥+𝑦 & =\frac{2}{5} \\ (−1)⋅(−7𝑥+𝑦) & =(−1)⋅(\frac{2}{5}) \\ 7𝑥−𝑦 & =−\frac{2}{5}\end{aligned}


$$

However, $c$ is a fraction, not an integer. To fix this, we multiply by $5$ to get rid of the fraction:

$$


\begin{aligned}7𝑥−𝑦 & =−\frac{2}{5} \\ 5⋅(7𝑥−𝑦) & =5⋅(−\frac{2}{5}) \\ 35𝑥−5𝑦 & =−2\end{aligned}


$$

Now, $a,b,$ and $c$ are integers with no common factors, and $a\geq 0.$ So we are done!

The standard form of the line is $35x-5y=-2.$

### Example: Converting an Arbitrary Line to Standard Form

#### Question

Write $y-1=x$ in standard form.

#### Explanation

Our goal is to write the equation in the form $ax+by = c,$ where $a,b,$ and $c$ are integers with no common factors and $a\geq 0.$

We begin by moving all of the variables to the left-hand side and all of the constants to the right-hand side. This gives

$$


-x+y=1.


$$

Then, we multiply by $-1$ to make the $x$-coefficient positive:

$$


\begin{aligned}−𝑥+𝑦 & =1 \\ (−1)(−𝑥+𝑦) & =−1 \\ 𝑥−𝑦 & =−1\end{aligned}


$$

Now, $a,b,$ and $c$ are integers with no common factors, and $a \geq 0.$ So we are done!

The standard form of the line is $x-y=-1.$

### Example: Finding the Standard Equation of a Line Passing Through Two Points

#### Question

Calculate the equation of the straight line that passes through the points $(-1,3)$ and $(2,4).$ Write the answer in standard form.

#### Explanation

First, we will construct the point-slope form of the line. Then, we will simplify it into standard form.

To construct the point-slope form of the line, we need a point and a slope. We already have two points, so we calculate the slope $m$ using the given points:

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑚=\frac{𝑦_{2}−𝑦_{1}}{𝑥_{2}−𝑥_{1}}=\frac{4−3}{2−(−1)}=\frac{1}{3}\end{aligned}


$$

Then, we use this slope along with one of the points to write the point-slope form equation of the line. It doesn't matter which point we choose, but let's choose $(2,4).$ The point-slope equation is as follows:

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑦−𝑦_{1} & =𝑚(𝑥−𝑥_{1}) \\ 𝑦−4 & =\frac{1}{3}(𝑥−2)\end{aligned}


$$

To simplify the point-slope formula into standard form, we first multiply by $3$ to get rid of the fraction:

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑦−4 & =\frac{1}{3}(𝑥−2) \\ 3⋅(𝑦−4) & =3⋅\frac{1}{3}(𝑥−2) \\ 3𝑦−12 & =𝑥−2\end{aligned}


$$

Then, we move the variables to the left side and the constant to the right side:

$$


\begin{aligned}3𝑦−12 & =𝑥−2 \\ 3𝑦 & =𝑥+10 \\ −𝑥+3𝑦 & =10\end{aligned}


$$

Finally, we multiply by $-1$ to make the $x$-coefficient positive:

$$


\begin{aligned}−𝑥+3𝑦 & =10 \\ (−1)⋅(−𝑥+3𝑦) & =(−1)⋅10 \\ 𝑥−3𝑦 & =−10\end{aligned}


$$

So, the standard form of the equation is $x-3y = -10.$
