## Lesson

### Introduction

To determine the slope of a line that is given in standard form, we need to convert the equation to slope-intercept form.

To demonstrate, let's calculate the slope of the following line:

$$


2x + y = 8


$$

To write this equation in slope-intercept form, we solve for $y\mathbin{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}2𝑥+𝑦 & =8 \\ 2𝑥+𝑦−2𝑥 & =8−2𝑥 \\ 𝑦 & =8−2𝑥 \\ 𝑦 & =−2𝑥+8\end{aligned}


$$

From the slope-intercept equation, we can tell that the slope of the line is $m=-2.$

### Example: Finding the Slope of a Line Given Standard Form

#### Question

What is the slope of the line $4x-2y=1?$

#### Explanation

We can find the slope of the line if we can express its equation in slope-intercept form. To do this, we solve for $y,$ as follows:

$$


\begin{aligned}4𝑥−2𝑦 & =1 \\ −2𝑦 & =−4𝑥+1 \\ 2𝑦 & =4𝑥−1 \\ \frac{2𝑦}{2} & =\frac{4𝑥−1}{2} \\ 𝑦 & =\frac{4𝑥}{2}−\frac{1}{2} \\ 𝑦 & =2𝑥−\frac{1}{2}\end{aligned}


$$

From the slope-intercept equation, we can tell that the slope of the line is $m=2.$

### Determining Intercepts of Lines Given in Standard Form

Suppose we want to find the $x$- and $y$-intercepts of the following line:

$$


2x + y = 8


$$

Let's find each of them in turn.

- To find the $x$-intercept, we substitute $y=0$ into the equation and solve for $x{:}$ Therefore, the line intersects the $x$-axis at the point $(4,0).$

- To find the $y$-intercept, we substitute $x=0$ into the equation and solve for $y{:}$ Therefore, the line intersects the $y$-axis at the point $(0,8).$

### Example: Finding the X-Intercept of a Line

#### Question

Find the point where the line $4x-3y=8$ intersects the $x$-axis.

#### Explanation

To find the $x$-intercept, we substitute $y=0$ into the equation and solve for $x.$

$$


\begin{aligned} 4x-3y&=8\\\[5pt] 4x-3(0)&=8\\\[5pt] 4x&=8\\\[5pt] \dfrac{4x}{4}&=\dfrac{8}{4}\\\[5pt] x&=2 \end{aligned}


$$

Therefore, the $x$-intercept is $2,$ and we conclude that the line intersects the $x$-axis at $\left(2,0\right).$

### Example: Finding the Y-Intercept of a Line

#### Question

Find the point where the line $5x -4y=-8$ intersects the $y$-axis.

#### Explanation

To find the $y$-intercept, we substitute $x=0$ into the equation and solve for $y.$

$$


\begin{aligned}5𝑥−4𝑦 & =−8 \\ 5(0)−4𝑦 & =−8 \\ −4𝑦 & =−8 \\ 𝑦 & =2\end{aligned}


$$

Therefore, the $y$-intercept is $2,$ and we conclude that the line intersects the $y$-axis at $\left(0,2\right).$
