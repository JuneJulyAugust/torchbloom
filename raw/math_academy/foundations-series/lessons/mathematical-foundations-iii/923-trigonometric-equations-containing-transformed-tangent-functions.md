### Introduction

Suppose we want to find all of the solutions to the equation

$$


\tan\left(x-\dfrac{\pi}{6}\right) = \sqrt 3, \qquad 0 \leq x < 2\pi.


$$

Notice that this equation contains a transformed tangent function.

We know that the general solution to the elementary equation $\tan{X} = \sqrt 3$ is given by

$$


X = \dfrac{\pi}3 + n\pi,


$$

where $n$ is assumed to be an integer.

To find the general solution of the transformed tangent equation, we replace $X$ with the tangent function's input, which is $x-\dfrac{\pi}6$ in this case. This gives the general solution

$$


x-\dfrac{\pi}6 = \dfrac{\pi}3 + n\pi .


$$

We now solve this equation for $x,$ as follows:

$$


\begin{aligned}𝑥 & =\frac{𝜋}{6}+\frac{𝜋}{3}+𝑛𝜋 \\ 𝑥 & =\frac{𝜋}{6}+\frac{2𝜋}{6}+𝑛𝜋 \\ 𝑥 & =\frac{3𝜋}{6}+𝑛𝜋 \\ 𝑥 & =\frac{𝜋}{2}+𝑛𝜋 \\ 𝑥 & =\frac{𝜋}{2}+\frac{2𝑛𝜋}{2} \\ 𝑥 & =\frac{(1+2𝑛)𝜋}{2}\end{aligned}


$$

Now, we set $n=0,\pm1,\pm2,\ldots$ until we have generated all solutions in the domain $0\leq x < 2\pi.$

- Setting $n=0$ gives $x=\dfrac{\pi}2.$

- Setting $n=1$ gives $x=\dfrac{3\pi}2.$

All other values of $n$ generate solutions outside the domain $0\leq x < 2\pi.$ So, the solutions $x_1$ and $x_2$ (written in ascending order) are

$$


x_1 = \dfrac{\pi}2, \qquad x_2= \dfrac{3\pi}2.


$$

### Example: Solving a Trigonometric Equation Containing the Tangent Function Transformed by Multiple Operations

#### Question

Solve the equation $2\sqrt{3}\tan\left(2x+\dfrac{\pi}{3}\right) - 2 = 0$ for $0\leq x < 2\pi.$

#### Explanation

First, we rearrange the equation as follows:

$$


\begin{aligned}2\sqrt{√3}tan⁡(2𝑥+\frac{𝜋}{3})−2 & =0 \\ 2\sqrt{√3}tan⁡(2𝑥+\frac{𝜋}{3}) & =2 \\ tan⁡(2𝑥+\frac{𝜋}{3}) & =\frac{1}{\sqrt{√3}} \\ tan⁡(2𝑥+\frac{𝜋}{3}) & =\frac{\sqrt{√3}}{3}\end{aligned}


$$

We will find the general solution to the equation and then generate all of the solutions in the required domain.

The general solution to our equation is

$$


\begin{aligned}2𝑥+\frac{𝜋}{3} & =\frac{𝜋}{6}+𝑛𝜋.\end{aligned}


$$

Solving the equation above for $x,$ we get

$$


\begin{aligned}2𝑥 & =−\frac{𝜋}{3}+\frac{𝜋}{6}+𝑛𝜋 \\ 2𝑥 & =−\frac{𝜋}{6}+𝑛𝜋 \\ 𝑥 & =−\frac{𝜋}{12}+\frac{𝑛𝜋}{2} \\ 𝑥 & =\frac{(6𝑛−1)𝜋}{12}.\end{aligned}


$$

We set $n=0, \pm 1, \pm 2,\ldots$ until we have generated all solutions in the domain $0 \leq x \lt 2\pi.$ Notice that setting $n=0$ gives a result outside of the domain.

- Setting $n=1$ gives $x=\dfrac{5\pi}{12}.$

- Setting $n=2$ gives $x=\dfrac{11\pi}{12}.$

- Setting $n=3$ gives $x=\dfrac{17\pi}{12}.$

- Setting $n=4$ gives $x=\dfrac{23\pi}{12}.$

All other values of $n$ generate solutions outside the domain $0 \leq x \lt 2\pi.$ So, the solutions $x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4$ (written in ascending order) are

$$


x = \dfrac{5\pi}{12}, \dfrac{11\pi}{12}, \dfrac{17\pi}{12}, \dfrac{23\pi}{12} .


$$

### Example: Solving a Trigonometric Equation Containing a Transformed Tangent Function Under a Modified Domain

#### Question

Solve the equation $\tan\left(3x+\dfrac{\pi}{4}\right) = 1$ for $-\pi < x \leq \pi.$

#### Explanation

We will find the general solution to the equation, and then generate all of the solutions in the required domain.

The general solution to our equation is

$$


\begin{aligned}3𝑥+\frac{𝜋}{4} & =\frac{𝜋}{4}+𝑛𝜋.\end{aligned}


$$

Solving the equation above for $x,$ we get

$$


\begin{aligned}3𝑥 & =−\frac{𝜋}{4}+\frac{𝜋}{4}+𝑛𝜋 \\ 3𝑥 & =𝑛𝜋 \\ 𝑥 & =\frac{𝑛𝜋}{3}.\end{aligned}


$$

We set $n=0, \pm 1, \pm 2,\ldots,$ until we have generated all solutions in the domain $-\pi < x \leq \pi.$

- Setting $n=0$ gives $x=0.$

- Setting $n=\pm1$ gives $x=\pm\dfrac{\pi}{3}.$

- Setting $n=\pm2$ gives $x=\pm\dfrac{2\pi}{3}.$

- Setting $n=3$ gives $x=\pi.$

All other values of $n$ generate solutions outside the domain $-\pi < x \leq \pi.$ So, the solutions are

$$


x = 0,\, \pm \dfrac{\pi}{3},\, \pm\dfrac{2\pi}{3}, \, \pi.


$$
