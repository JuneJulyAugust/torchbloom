### Introduction

The first few prime numbers are

$$


2,\quad 3, \quad 5, \quad 7, \quad 11, \quad 13, \quad 17, \quad 19, \quad 23, \quad 29, \quad \ldots\,.


$$

Remember that $1$ is *not* a prime number.

A number's **prime factors** are the prime numbers that divide the number with no remainder.

Let's list the prime factors of $45{:}$

- $2$ is *not* a prime factor of $45$ since dividing $45$ by $2$ gives a remainder of $1. \quad{\color{red}{\times}}$

- $3$ *is* a prime factor of $45$ since $45 \div 3 = 15.\quad{\color{green}{\checkmark}}$

- $5$ *is* a prime factor of $45$ since $45 \div 5 = 9.\quad{\color{green}{\checkmark}}$

- $7$ is *not* a prime factor of $45$ since dividing $45$ by $7$ gives a remainder of $3. \quad{\color{red}{\times}}$

There are no other prime numbers that divide $45.$ Therefore, the prime factors of $45$ are $3$ and $5.$

We often wish to find a number's prime factors. We'll discuss this more in a future lesson.

### Divisibility Rules Revisited

To determine the prime factors of some larger numbers, it helps to recall some divisibility rules with primes:

Recall the following divisibility rules:

- A number is divisible by $2$ if the last digit is even.

- A number is divisible by $3$ if the sum of its digits is divisible by $3.$

- A number is divisible by $5$ if the last digit is either $5$ or $0.$

- A number is divisible by $7$ if, when we double the last digit and subtract the result from the remaining digits, the difference is divisible by $7.$

Let's use these rules to find the *smallest* prime factor of $715.$ We consider each prime in turn, starting with the smallest, until we find a factor:

- $2$ is *not* a factor of $715$ since the last digit $(5)$ is not even.

- $3$ is *not* a factor of $715$ since the sum of the digits is not divisible by $3.$

- $5$ *is* a factor of $715$ since the last digit equals $5.$

Therefore, the *smallest* prime factor of $715$ is $5.$

### Example: Finding the Smallest Prime Factor of a Three-Digit Integer

#### Question

What is the smallest prime factor of $133?$

#### Explanation

The smallest prime factor of a number is the smallest prime that divides the number with no remainder.

The first few prime numbers are given below.

$$


2, \quad 3, \quad 5, \quad 7, \quad 11, \quad \ldots


$$

It also helps to recall some divisibility rules with primes:

- A number is divisible by $2$ if the last digit is even.

- A number is divisible by $3$ if the sum of its digits is divisible by $3.$

- A number is divisible by $5$ if the last digit is either $5$ or $0.$

- A number is divisible by $7$ if, when we double the last digit and subtract the result from the remaining digits, the difference is divisible by $7.$

With that in mind, let's consider each prime in turn, starting with the smallest, until we find a factor:

- $2$ is ** a factor of $133$ since the last digit $(3)$ is not even.

- $3$ is ** a factor of $133$ since the sum of its digits is not divisible by $3.$

- $5$ is ** a factor of $133$ since its last digit $(3)$ is neither $5$ nor $0.$

- $7$ ** a factor of $133$ because if we subtract the double of the last digit ($3$) from the remaining digits ($13$), the result is divisible by $7.$

Therefore, the smallest prime factor of $133$ is $7.$
