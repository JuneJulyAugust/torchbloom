## Lesson

### Introduction

Whenever we have a fraction in an equation, we apply the multiplication principle to get rid of it. For example, consider the equation below:

$$


\dfrac{2x+12}{3} =6


$$

Since the opposite of dividing by $3$ is multiplying by $3$, we apply the multiplication principle and multiply both sides of the equation by $3$:

$$


 \begin{aligned} \dfrac{2x+12}{3} &=6\\3 \cdot \left(\dfrac{2x+12}{3}\right) &= 3 \cdot 6\\3 \cdot \left(\dfrac{2x+12}{3}\right) &= 18 \\2x+12 &= 18 \end{aligned}


$$

The process of removing the fraction is called **clearing the rational expression**. We now have an equation that we can solve using the usual methods:

$$


\begin{aligned} 2x+12 &= 18\\2x+12-12 &=18-12 \\2x &=6 \\\dfrac{2x}{2} &=\dfrac{6}{2} \\x &=3 \end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Clearing a Rational Expression With a Variable on One Side

#### Question

Solve the equation $13 = \dfrac{6y+8}{2}.$

#### Explanation

First, we clear the rational expression by multiplying both sides by $2\mathbin{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned} 13 &= \dfrac{6y+8}{2} \\2 \cdot 13 &= 2 \cdot \left(\dfrac{6y+8}{2}\right) \\2 \cdot 13 &= 2 \cdot \left(\dfrac{6y+8}{2}\right) \\26 &= 6y+8 \end{aligned}


$$

Next, we solve the remaining equation using the addition and multiplication principles:

$$


\begin{aligned} 26 &= 6y+8 \\26-8 &= 6y+8-8 \\18 &= 6y \\\dfrac{18}{6} &= \dfrac{6y}{6} \\3 &= y \end{aligned}


$$

Thus, $y=3.$

### Example: Clearing a Rational Expression With a Variable on Both Sides

#### Question

Solve the equation $\dfrac{a+2}{3} =a.$

#### Explanation

First, we clear the rational expression by multiplying both sides by $3\mathbin{:}$

$$


 \begin{aligned} \dfrac{a+2}{3} &=a \\3 \cdot \left( \dfrac{a+2}{3} \right) &= 3 \cdot a \\3 \cdot \left( \dfrac{a+2}{ 3 } \right) &= 3a \\a+2 &= 3a \end{aligned}


$$

Next, we solve the remaining equation using the addition and multiplication principles:

$$


 \begin{aligned} a+2 &= 3a \\a + 2 - a &= 3a - a \\2 &= 2a \\\dfrac{2}{2} &= \dfrac{2a}{2} \\1 &= a \end{aligned}


$$

Thus, $a=1.$

### Example: Clearing a Rational Expression With Binomials on Both Sides

#### Question

Solve the equation $\dfrac{2n+6}{3} = 4n+1.$

#### Explanation

First, we clear the rational expression by multiplying both sides by $3.$ We must make sure that ** of the terms on the right-hand side are multiplied by $3.$

$$


\begin{aligned}\frac{2𝑛+6}{3} & =4𝑛+1 \\ 3⋅(\frac{2𝑛+6}{3}) & =3⋅(4𝑛+1) \\ 3⋅(\frac{2𝑛+6}{3}) & =3⋅4𝑛+3⋅1 \\ 2𝑛+6 & =12𝑛+3\end{aligned}


$$

Next, we solve the remaining equation using the addition and multiplication principles:

$$


\begin{aligned}2𝑛+6 & =12𝑛+3 \\ 2𝑛+6−2𝑛 & =12𝑛+3−2𝑛 \\ 6 & =10𝑛+3 \\ 6−3 & =10𝑛+3−3 \\ 3 & =10𝑛 \\ \frac{3}{10} & =\frac{10𝑛}{10} \\ \frac{3}{10} & =𝑛\end{aligned}


$$

Thus, $n = \dfrac{3}{10}.$

### Example: Clearing a Rational Expression That Includes a Factored Expression

#### Question

Solve $\dfrac{5p+10}{6} =2(p-5).$

#### Explanation

First, we clear the rational expression by multiplying both sides by $6\mathbin{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned} \dfrac{5p+10}{6} &=2(p-5)\\6 \cdot \left(\dfrac{5p+10}{6}\right) &=6\cdot 2 (p-5) \\6 \cdot \left(\dfrac{5p+10}{6}\right) &=12(p-5) \\5p+10 &=12p-60 \end{aligned}


$$

Then, we solve the remaining equation using the addition and multiplication principles:

$$


\begin{aligned} 5p+10 &=12p-60 \\5p+10-5p &=12p-60-5p \\10 &=7p-60 \\10+60 &=7p-60+60 \\70 &=7p \\\dfrac{70}{7} &=\dfrac{7p}{7} \\10 &= p \end{aligned}


$$

Thus, $p=10.$
