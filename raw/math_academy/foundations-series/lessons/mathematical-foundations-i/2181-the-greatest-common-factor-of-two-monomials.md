### Introduction

The **greatest common factor** (or $\text{GCF}$) of the two monomials $12x^2$ and $54x$ is the largest expression that will divide *both* $12x^2$ and $54x$ with no remainder.

To find the greatest common factor, we follow three steps:

1. First, decompose each expression into its separate factors. We decompose each constant into its prime factors, and we treat each variable in the product as a separate factor.

2. Now, we pair off the individual factors as much as possible:

3. The greatest common factor is the product of these paired factors:

$$


\text{GCF}(12x^2, 54x) = {\color{red}2} \cdot {\color{blue}3} \cdot {\color{green}x} = 6x


$$

### Example: Finding the Greatest Common Factor With One Variable

#### Question

What is the greatest common factor of $10y^2$ and $25y^4?$

#### Explanation

First, we decompose each expression into its separate factors. We express each constant as a product of its prime factors, and we treat each variable in the product as a separate factor.

$$


\begin{aligned}10𝑦^{2} & =2⋅5⋅𝑦⋅𝑦 \\ 25𝑦^{4} & =5⋅5⋅𝑦⋅𝑦⋅𝑦⋅𝑦\end{aligned}


$$

Now, we pair off the individual factors as much as possible:

$$


\begin{aligned}10𝑦^{2} & =2⋅\underset{}{5}⋅\underset{}{𝑦}⋅\underset{}{𝑦} \\ 25𝑦^{4} & =\underset{}{5}⋅5⋅\underset{}{𝑦}⋅\underset{}{𝑦}⋅𝑦⋅𝑦\end{aligned}


$$

The greatest common factor is the product of these paired factors:

$$


\text{GCF}(10y^2, 25y^4) = {\color{red}5} \cdot {\color{blue}y} \cdot {\color{green}y} = 5y^2


$$

### Example: Finding the Greatest Common Factor With Two Variables

#### Question

What is the greatest common factor of $6x^2y^2$ and $3xy?$

#### Explanation

First, we decompose each expression into its separate factors. We express each constant as a product of its prime factors, and we treat each variable in the product as a separate factor.

$$


\begin{aligned}6𝑥^{2}𝑦^{2} & =2⋅3⋅𝑥⋅𝑥⋅𝑦⋅𝑦 \\ 3𝑥𝑦 & =3⋅𝑥⋅𝑦\end{aligned}


$$

Now, we pair off the individual factors as much as possible:

$$


\begin{aligned}6𝑥^{2}𝑦^{2} & =2⋅\underset{}{3}⋅\underset{}{𝑥}⋅𝑥⋅\underset{}{𝑦}⋅𝑦 \\ 3𝑥𝑦 & =\underset{}{3}⋅\underset{}{𝑥}⋅\underset{}{𝑦}\end{aligned}


$$

The greatest common factor is the product of these paired factors:

$$


\text{GCF}(6x^2y^2, 3xy) = {\color{red}3} \cdot {\color{blue}x} \cdot {\color{green}y} = 3xy


$$

### Example: Finding the Greatest Common Factor With Three Variables

#### Question

What is the greatest common factor of $-18p^2qr$ and $24p^3qr^2?$

#### Explanation

The terms are $-18p^2qr$ and $24p^3qr^2.$ When finding the greatest common factor, we ignore the negative sign.

First, we decompose each expression into its separate factors. We express each constant as a product of its prime factors, and we treat each variable in the product as a separate factor.

$$


\begin{aligned}18𝑝^{2}𝑞𝑟 & =2⋅3⋅3⋅𝑝⋅𝑝⋅𝑞⋅𝑟 \\ 24𝑝^{3}𝑞𝑟^{2} & =2⋅2⋅2⋅3⋅𝑝⋅𝑝⋅𝑝⋅𝑞⋅𝑟⋅𝑟\end{aligned}


$$

Now, we pair off the individual factors as much as possible:

$$


\begin{aligned}18𝑝^{2}𝑞𝑟 & =\underset{}{2}⋅\underset{}{3}⋅3⋅\underset{}{𝑝}⋅\underset{}{𝑝}⋅\underset{}{𝑞}⋅\underset{}{𝑟} \\ 24𝑝^{3}𝑞𝑟^{2} & =\underset{}{2}⋅2⋅2⋅\underset{}{3}⋅\underset{}{𝑝}⋅\underset{}{𝑝}⋅𝑝⋅\underset{}{𝑞}⋅\underset{}{𝑟}⋅𝑟\end{aligned}


$$

The greatest common factor is the product of these paired factors:

$$


\text{GCF}(-18p^2qr, 24p^3qr^2) = {\color{red}2} \cdot {\color{blue}3} \cdot {\color{green}p} \cdot {\color{green}p} \cdot {\color{YellowOrange}q} \cdot {\color{Magenta}r} = 6p^2qr


$$

### Example: Finding the Greatest Common Factor of Three Monomials

#### Question

What is the greatest common factor of $30ab^3,$ $10a^3b,$ and $15a^2b^2?$

#### Explanation

First, we decompose each expression into its separate factors. We express each constant as a product of its prime factors, and we treat each variable in the product as a separate factor.

$$


\begin{aligned}30𝑎𝑏^{3} & =2⋅3⋅5⋅𝑎⋅𝑏⋅𝑏⋅𝑏 \\ 10𝑎^{3}𝑏 & =2⋅5⋅𝑎⋅𝑎⋅𝑎⋅𝑏 \\ 15𝑎^{2}𝑏^{2} & =3⋅5⋅𝑎⋅𝑎⋅𝑏⋅𝑏.\end{aligned}


$$

Now, we pair off the individual factors as much as possible:

$$


\begin{aligned}30𝑎𝑏^{3} & =2⋅3⋅\underset{}{5}⋅\underset{}{𝑎}⋅\underset{}{𝑏}⋅𝑏⋅𝑏 \\ 10𝑎^{3}𝑏 & =2⋅\underset{}{5}⋅\underset{}{𝑎}⋅𝑎⋅𝑎⋅\underset{}{𝑏} \\ 15𝑎^{2}𝑏^{2} & =3⋅\underset{}{5}⋅\underset{}{𝑎}⋅𝑎⋅\underset{}{𝑏}⋅𝑏\end{aligned}


$$

The greatest common factor is the product of these paired factors:

$$


\text{GCF}\left(30ab^3, 10a^3b, 15a^2b^2\right) = {\color{red}5} \cdot {\color{blue}a} \cdot {\color{violet}b} = 5ab.


$$
