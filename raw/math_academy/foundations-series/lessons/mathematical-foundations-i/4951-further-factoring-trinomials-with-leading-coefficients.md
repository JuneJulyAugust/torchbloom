## Lesson

### Introduction

When factoring a trinomial, we should always begin by factoring out any constant factors. Then, we factor whatever quadratic remains using the techniques we've studied.

For example, let's factor the following trinomial:

$$


6y^2+21y+9


$$

The first thing to notice is that all the terms have a common factor of $3.$ So, we can simplify the calculation by first factoring out $3\mathbin{:}$

$$


3(2y^2 + 7y + 3)


$$

So, our goal now is to factor the trinomial in parentheses:

$$


2y^2 + 7y + 3


$$

To do this, we find two numbers that

- multiply to the product of the quadratic coefficient and the constant ($2 \times 3 = 6$), and

- add to the linear coefficient ($7$).

Also, since all coefficients are positive, we only need to consider the *positive* factor pairs of $6.$

The positive factor pairs of $6$ are

$$


(1,6),\quad (2,3).


$$

Notice the numbers in the factor pair $(1,6)$ add to $7{:}$

$$


1+6 =7 \quad{\color{green}\checkmark}


$$

Then, we write the linear term as $y+6y,$ and factor the result by grouping:

$$


\begin{aligned}2𝑦^{2}+7𝑦+3= & \\ 2𝑦^{2}+𝑦+6𝑦+3 & = \\ (2𝑦^{2}+𝑦)+(6𝑦+3) & = \\ 𝑦(2𝑦+1)+3(2𝑦+1) & = \\ (𝑦+3)(2𝑦+1) & \end{aligned}


$$

Therefore, the original trinomial factors as follows:

$$


\begin{aligned}6𝑦^{2}+21𝑦+9 & = \\ 3(2𝑦^{2}+7𝑦+3) & = \\ 3(𝑦+3)(2𝑦+1) & \end{aligned}


$$

And we're done!

Whenever the coefficient of the quadratic term is negative, we should always factor this out first. That way, we're left with the task of factoring a trinomial containing a positive quadratic coefficient. Let's see an example.

### Example: Quadratics With Positive Coefficients (After Initial Factoring)

#### Question

Factor the trinomial $-4w^2-30w-50.$

#### Explanation

We need to factor the following expression:

$$


-4w^2-30w-50


$$

First, notice that all the terms in the trinomial have a common factor of $-2.$ So, we can simplify the calculation by first factoring out $-2\mathbin{:}$

$$


-2(2w^2+15w+25)


$$

Now, we need to factor the trinomial $2w^2+15w+25$ in parentheses. To do this, we find two numbers that

- multiply to the product of the quadratic coefficient and the constant ($2 \times 25 = 50$), and

- add to the linear coefficient ($15$).

Since all coefficients are positive, we only need to consider the positive factor pairs of $50.$

The positive factor pairs of $50$ are

$$


(1,50),\quad (2,25),\quad (5,10).


$$

Notice the numbers in the factor pair $(5,10)$ add to $15{:}$

$$


5+10 =15 \quad{\color{green}\checkmark}


$$

Then, we write the linear term as $5w+10w,$ and factor the result by grouping:

$$


\begin{aligned}2𝑤^{2}+15𝑤+25 & = \\ 2𝑤^{2}+5𝑤+10𝑤+25 & = \\ 2𝑤^{2}+10𝑤+5𝑤+25 & = \\ (2𝑤^{2}+10𝑤)+(5𝑤+25) & = \\ 2𝑤(𝑤+5)+5(𝑤+5) & = \\ (2𝑤+5)(𝑤+5) & \end{aligned}


$$

Therefore, the original trinomial factors as follows:

$$


\begin{aligned}−2(2𝑤+5)(𝑤+5)\end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Quadratics With Negative Constants (After Initial Factoring)

#### Question

Factor the trinomial $-10t^2 - 35t + 20.$

#### Explanation

We must factor the following expression:

$$


-10t^2 - 35t +20


$$

First, notice that all the terms have a common factor of $-5.$ So, we can simplify the calculation by first factoring out $-5\mathbin{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}−5(2𝑡^{2}+7𝑡−4)\end{aligned}


$$

Now, we just need to factor the trinomial $2t^2 + 7t - 4$ in parentheses. To do this, we find two numbers that

- multiply to the product of the quadratic coefficient and the constant ($2 \times (-4) = -8$), and

- add to the linear coefficient ($7$).

The factor pairs of $-8$ are

$$


(1,-8),\quad (-1,8),\quad (2,-4),\quad (-2,4).


$$

Notice the numbers in the factor pair $(-1,8)$ add to $7{:}$

$$


-1+8 = 8-1 = 7 \quad{\color{green}\checkmark}


$$

Then, we write the linear term as $-t+8t,$ and factor the result by grouping:

$$


\begin{aligned}2𝑡^{2}+7𝑡−4 & = \\ 2𝑡^{2}−𝑡+8𝑡−4 & = \\ (2𝑡^{2}−𝑡)+(8𝑡−4) & = \\ 𝑡(2𝑡−1)+4(2𝑡−1) & = \\ (2𝑡−1)(𝑡+4) & \end{aligned}


$$

Therefore, the original trinomial factors as follows:

$$


\begin{aligned}−5(2𝑡−1)(𝑡+4)\end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Quadratics With Negative Linear Coefficients and Positive Constants (After Initial Factoring)

#### Question

Factor the trinomial $15x^2-40x+20.$

#### Explanation

We need to factor the following expression:

$$


15x^2-40x+20


$$

First, notice that all the terms have a common factor of $5.$ So, we can simplify the calculation by first factoring out $5{:}$

$$


\begin{aligned}5(3𝑥^{2}−8𝑥+4)\end{aligned}


$$

Now, we just need to factor the trinomial $3x^2 - 8x + 4$ in parentheses. To do this, we find two numbers that

- multiply to the product of the quadratic coefficient and the constant (which is $3 \times 4 = 12$), and

- add to the linear coefficient (which is $-8$).

Since the linear coefficient is negative and the constant is positive, we need to consider the ** factor pairs of $12.$

The negative factor pairs of $12$ are

$$


(-1,-12),\quad (-2,-6),\quad (-3,-4).


$$

Notice the numbers in the factor pair $(-2,-6)$ add to $-8{:}$

$$


-2-6= -8 \quad{\color{green}\checkmark}


$$

Then, we write the linear term as $-2x-6x,$ and factor the result by grouping:

$$


\begin{aligned}3𝑥^{2}−8𝑥+4 & = \\ 3𝑥^{2}−2𝑥−6𝑥+4 & = \\ 3𝑥^{2}−6𝑥−2𝑥+4 & = \\ (3𝑥^{2}−6𝑥)−(2𝑥−4) & = \\ 3𝑥(𝑥−2)−2(𝑥−2) & = \\ (3𝑥−2)(𝑥−2) & \end{aligned}


$$

Therefore, the original trinomial factors as follows:

$$


\begin{aligned}5(3𝑥−2)(𝑥−2)\end{aligned}


$$
