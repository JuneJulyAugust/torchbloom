## Lesson

### Introduction

When we want to calculate the **square of a binomial**, like

$$


(a+b)^2,


$$

there's a formula that we can use instead of working out the product $(a+b)(a+b)$ manually. The formula is

$$


(a + b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2.


$$

It might be helpful to remember this as *"the square of the first term, plus the square of the second term, plus twice the product."*

Suppose we need to calculate the square $(x+3)^2.$ In this case, we have $a= x$ and $b=3.$ We substitute into the formula and simplify:

$$


\begin{aligned}(𝑥+3)^{2} & = \\ (𝑥)^{2}+2(𝑥)(3)+(3)^{2} & = \\ 𝑥^{2}+6𝑥+9 & \end{aligned}


$$

We can check that the formula gives the correct result by working out the multiplication manually:

$$


\begin{aligned} (x + 3)^2 &= \\[3pt] (x + 3)(x + 3) &= \\[3pt] x(x + 3) + 3(x + 3) &= \\[3pt] x^2 + 3x + 3x + 9 &= \\[3pt] x^2 + 6x + 9 &\, {\color{green}{\checkmark}} \end{aligned}


$$

So we get the same result! But by using the formula, we save a lot of time and effort.

### Example: Squaring Linear Binomials

#### Question

Expand $(4+3y)^2.$

#### Explanation

We will use the following formula:

$$


(a+b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2


$$

Substituting $a=4$ and $b=3y,$ we have

$$


\begin{aligned} (4 + 3y)^2 &= \\(4)^2 + 2(4) ( 3y) + (3y)^2 &= \\16 + 24y + 9y^2. \end{aligned}


$$

### Example: Squaring Nonlinear Binomials

#### Question

Expand $(2n^2 + 3)^2.$

#### Explanation

We will use the following formula:

$$


(a+b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2


$$

Substituting $a=2n^2$ and $b=3,$ we have

$$


\begin{aligned} (2n^2 + 3)^2 &= \\[2pt] (2n^2)^2 + 2(2n^2) ( 3) + (3)^2 &= \\[2pt] 2^2\cdot (n^2)^2 + 2(2n^2) (3) + (3)^2 &= \\[2pt] 4n^4 + 12n^2 + 9. \end{aligned}


$$

### Binomials With Subtraction

So far, we've been using the formula for the square of a binomial involving addition:

$$


(a + b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2


$$

There is also a similar formula that we can use to compute the square of a binomial involving subtraction. The formula is

$$


(a - b)^2 = a^2 - 2ab + b^2.


$$

For example, to compute the square $(x-3)^2,$ we can substitute $a=x$ and $b=3$ into the formula and simplify:

$$


\begin{aligned}(𝑥−3)^{2} & = \\ (𝑥)^{2}−2(𝑥)(3)+(3)^{2} & = \\ 𝑥^{2}−6𝑥+9 & \end{aligned}


$$

We can check that the formula gives the correct result by working out the multiplication manually:

$$


\begin{aligned} (x - 3)^2 &= \\(x - 3)(x - 3) &= \\x(x - 3) - 3(x - 3) &= \\x^2 - 3x - 3x + 9 &= \\x^2 - 6x + 9 &\, {\color{green}{\checkmark}} \end{aligned}


$$

Again, we get the same result.

### Example: Squaring Binomials With Subtraction

#### Question

Expand $(3p - 5)^2.$

#### Explanation

We will use the following formula:

$$


(a-b)^2 = a^2 - 2ab + b^2


$$

Substituting $a=3p$ and $b=5,$ we have

$$


\begin{aligned} (3p - 5)^2 &= \\(3p)^2 - 2(3p)(5) + 5^2 &= \\9p^2 - 30p + 25. \end{aligned}


$$
