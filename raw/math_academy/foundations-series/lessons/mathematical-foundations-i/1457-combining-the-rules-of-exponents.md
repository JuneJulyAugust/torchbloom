## Lesson

### Introduction

Let's recap some of the rules for exponents.

- **Product rule**: to multiply powers of the same base, add the exponents.

- **Quotient rule**: to divide powers of the same base, subtract the exponents.

- **Power rule**: to raise a power to another power, multiply the exponents.

- **Power of product rule**: to raise the product of two numbers to a power, raise each number to that power, and then multiply.

- **Power of quotient rule**: to raise a fraction to a power, raise both the numerator and the denominator to that power.

We can use a combination of these rules to simplify expressions containing exponents. Let's see an example of this.

### Example: Combining the Product and Quotient Rules of Exponents

#### Question

Express $\dfrac{5^4\cdot 5^2}{5^3}$ as a base raised to a single number.

#### Explanation

First, we rewrite the numerator using the product rule for exponents:

$$


\dfrac{5^4\cdot 5^2}{5^3}= \dfrac{5^{4+2}}{5^3} = \dfrac{5^{6}}{5^3}


$$

Then, we use the quotient rule for exponents:

$$


\dfrac{5^6}{5^3} = 5^{6-3} = 5^{3}


$$

### Example: Combining Various Product and Quotient Rules of Exponents

#### Question

Express $4^{8} \cdot \left(\dfrac{7}{4}\right)^5$ as the product of two exponentiated integers.

#### Explanation

First, we rewrite the second factor using the power of quotient rule:

$$


\begin{aligned}4^{8}⋅(\frac{7}{4})^{5}=4^{8}⋅\frac{7^{5}}{4^{5}}\end{aligned}


$$

Next, we rewrite the fractional part of our expression as follows:

$$


4^{8} \cdot \dfrac{7^5}{4^5} = \dfrac{4^8}{4^5} \cdot 7^5


$$

Finally, we simplify using the quotient rule for exponents:

$$


\dfrac{4^8}{4^5} \cdot 7^5 =4^{8-5}\cdot 7^5=4^{3}\cdot 7^5


$$

### Example: Combining the Power Rule of Exponents With Other Rules

#### Question

Express $\left(6^4\right)^2\cdot 6$ as a base raised to a single power.

#### Explanation

First, we rewrite the first factor using the power rule for exponents:

$$


\left(6^4\right)^2=6^{4\cdot 2}=6^8


$$

Then we use the product rule for exponents:

$$


6^8 \cdot 6= 6^8 \cdot 6^1=6^{8+1}=6^9


$$
