## Lesson

### Introduction

In this lesson, we'll consider real-life situations where we have to multiply fractions by whole numbers.

Suppose that Justin, a chef, has $8$ large tins of tomatoes. If each tin of tomatoes weighs $\dfrac{5}{4}\,\text{lb},$ how many pounds of tomatoes does Justin have in total?

To find out the total weight of tomatoes, we need to multiply $\dfrac{5}{4}\,\text{lb}$ by $8.$

To multiply a fraction by a whole number, we multiply the fraction's numerator by the whole number and keep the denominator the same:

$$


8 \times \dfrac54= \dfrac{8\times 5}{4} = \dfrac{40}4


$$

We can simplify this fraction by interpreting the fraction as a division:

$$


\dfrac{40}{4} = 40 \div 4 = 10


$$

So, in total, Justin has $10\,\text{lb}$ of tomatoes.

### Example: Multiplying a Fraction by a Whole Number: Result Is a Whole Number

#### Question

Larry filled his water bottle with $12\, \mathrm{oz}$ of drinking water. If he drank $\dfrac34$ of the bottle's contents after exercising, how much water did Larry drink?

#### Explanation

To find out how much water Larry drank, we need to multiply $12$ by $\dfrac{3}{4}.$

To multiply a fraction by a whole number, we multiply the fraction's numerator by the whole number and keep the denominator the same:

$$


\dfrac34 \times 12 = \dfrac{3\times 12}{4} = \dfrac{36}4


$$

Finally, we simplify:

$$


\dfrac{36}{4} = 36 \div 4=9


$$

So, Larry drank $9\,\text{oz}$ of water.

### Example: Multiplying a Fraction by a Whole Number: Result Is a Mixed Number

#### Question

Greg stacked $5$ cardboard boxes on top of each other. How tall was his pile if each box is $\dfrac{3}{4}\,\text{ft}$ high?

#### Explanation

To find out the height of the pile of boxes, we have to multiply $\dfrac{3}{4}$ by $5.$

To multiply a fraction by a whole number, we multiply the fraction's numerator by the whole number and keep the denominator the same:

$$


5 \times \dfrac34 = \dfrac{5\times 3}{4} = \dfrac{15}{4}


$$

Finally, we then convert $\dfrac {15}{4}$ to a mixed number:

$$


15 \div 4 = 3\,\text{R}\,3 = 3\,\dfrac{3}{4}


$$

So, Greg's stack of boxes was $3\,\dfrac{3}{4}\,\text{ft}$ high.

### Example: Determining Whole Number Bounds of a Multiplication Problem

#### Question

Mr. Stewart spends $\dfrac56$ hours watching an episode of his favorite TV show. If $5$ episodes are played every week, find the total time he spends watching the show every week. Between what two whole numbers does your answer lie?

#### Explanation

To find out how long Mr. Stewart watches the show each week, we need to multiply $\dfrac{5}{6}$ by $5.$

To multiply a fraction by a whole number, we multiply the fraction's numerator by the whole number and keep the denominator the same:

$$


5\times\dfrac56 = \dfrac{5\times 5}{6} = \dfrac{25}6


$$

Finally, we then convert $\dfrac {25}{6}$ to a mixed number:

$$


25 \div 6 = 4\,\text{R}\,1 = 4\,\dfrac{1}{6}


$$

So, Mr. Stewart spends a total of $4\,\dfrac{1}{6}$ hours watching the show, which lies between $4$ and $5$ hours.
