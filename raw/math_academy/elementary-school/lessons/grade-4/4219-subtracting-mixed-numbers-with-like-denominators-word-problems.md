## Lesson

### Introduction

Suppose Bethany promised to work for $9\,\dfrac23\,\rm{hr}$ this week in her mother's store. If she worked for $2\,\dfrac13\,\rm{hr}$ in the first two days, how many hours does she have left to work over the remaining days?

To find the number of hours she has remaining, we need to calculate the following difference:

$$

9\,{\color{black}\dfrac23} - 2\,{\color{black}\dfrac13}

$$

To subtract mixed numbers with like denominators, we subtract

- whole numbers from whole numbers, and

- fractions from fractions.

Subtracting the whole numbers, we get

$$

9-2={\color{blue}7}.

$$

Subtracting the fractions, we get

$$

\dfrac23 - \dfrac13 = \dfrac{2-1}3 = {\color{red}\dfrac13}.

$$

Finally, we combine the resulting whole number and the fraction:

$$

9\,\dfrac23 - 2\,\dfrac13 = {\color{blue}7}\,{\color{red}\dfrac13}

$$

Therefore, this week, Bethany has to work a further $7\,\dfrac13\,\rm{hr}$.

### Subtracting Mixed Numbers Using Improper Fractions

Sometimes, we must write each mixed number as an improper fraction before subtracting.

To demonstrate, let's return to the example of Bethany and her mother's store.

Suppose Bethany promised to work for $8\,\dfrac25\,\rm{hr}$ next week in her mother's store. If she plans to work for $3\,\dfrac45\,\rm{hr}$ on Monday, how many hours must she work over the remaining days next week?

To determine the number of hours Bethany must work over the remaining days, we need to calculate the following difference:

$$

8\,{\color{blue}\dfrac25} - 3\,{\color{red}\dfrac45}

$$

Now, notice that

$$

{\color{blue}\dfrac25} < {\color{red}\dfrac45}.

$$

Therefore, Bethany has to work a further $4\,\dfrac35\,\rm{hr}.$

### Example: Subtracting Mixed Numbers Using Improper Fractions in Context

#### Question

At the beginning of the day, a grocery store had $25\,\dfrac{1}{6}\,\textrm{kg}$ of sugar for sale. If $14\,\dfrac{5}{6}\,\textrm{kg}$ of sugar were sold during the day, how much sugar is left?

#### Explanation

To find the weight of the remaining sugar, we need to calculate the difference $25\,\dfrac{1}{6} - 14\,\dfrac{5}{6}.$

We cannot subtract the fractional parts because $\dfrac{5}{6}$ is larger than $\dfrac{1}{6}.$ So, instead, we write each number as an improper fraction.

We can now carry out the subtraction:

$$

\dfrac{151}{6} - \dfrac{89}{6} = \dfrac{151 - 89}{6} = \dfrac{62}{6}

$$

We can simplify $\dfrac{62}{6}$ by dividing the numerator and denominator by $2{:}$

$$

\dfrac{62}{6} = \dfrac{62 \div 2}{6 \div 2} = \dfrac{31}{3}

$$

Finally, we convert it back to a mixed number:

$$

31 \div 3 = 10\,\textrm{R}1

$$

Therefore, there is $10\,\dfrac{1}{3}\,\textrm{kg}$ of sugar left.
